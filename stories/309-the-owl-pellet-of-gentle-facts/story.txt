"Come see the sunny stripe," Paz said, and their soft voice drifted along the quiet library aisle where a slice of sun lay warm across the carpet like a golden path.

Paz stepped closer and felt the heat of that light on the tops of their shoes while Nana Ruth walked just behind them with her canvas bag that smelled faintly of cinnamon from the morning bread she had baked. The shelves rose high on both sides, full of spines in blue and green and soft brown, and Paz ran one careful finger along a lower shelf without tugging any books free. Dust motes floated in the bright slice of sun and turned slowly, and Paz watched them and felt the calm of the room settle on their shoulders like a light blanket. Dahlia arrived a moment later, her sneakers making almost no sound, and she stood shoulder to shoulder with Paz so they could both look at the same spot on the floor where the sun made everything look clean and still.

On a low table beside the aisle sat a clear box with a neat paper label, and inside the box rested an owl pellet the library kept only for looking. Paz leaned in until their nose nearly touched the cool plastic and studied the gray oval shape that held tiny bones and bits of fur pressed together like a secret puzzle no one was meant to open. The wish to pocket it or poke it rose quick and real in Paz’s chest, a small flutter of wanting to hold the mystery closer, yet Paz kept both hands flat on the table edge and only looked. Dahlia pressed close too, and Paz could hear the tiny hush of her breathing. Nana Ruth set her bag down gently and waited without rushing anyone.

“We can look with our eyes,” Paz told Dahlia in a steady voice, “and leave every piece exactly where it is.”

Dahlia nodded and clasped her hands behind her back the way Paz showed her, and together they tilted their heads to see how the soft light from the window made the pellet’s surface look almost silver in places. Nana Ruth bent lower so her cheek nearly touched Paz’s hair and said, “I see the little curves of bone, Paz. How neat the owl kept them.” The three of them stayed still while the slice of sun inched a little farther across the carpet, and Paz felt proud that the box stayed closed and the pellet stayed safe. Somewhere deeper in the building a gate latch clicked once with a clean metal sound, then quiet returned, and Paz liked how the sound felt finished and tidy.

Paz invited Dahlia to move one step to the side so the sun would fall across the label letters, and they read the words slowly together without touching the lid. Nana Ruth opened her bag and took out a small wooden spoon she kept for stirring tea later, and the handle felt walnut-smooth when Paz held it for a moment just to feel its gentle curve before handing it back. The spoon had nothing to do with the pellet except that it reminded Paz how some things are made for careful holding and some things are made only for careful seeing. Paz set their hands on their own knees again and watched the pellet’s quiet shape, and the wanting to poke faded into a softer kind of wonder that did not need fingers.

“It looks like a tiny gray moon,” Dahlia whispered, and Paz smiled because the words fit the quiet mood of the aisle.

Nana Ruth rested one warm hand on Paz’s shoulder and said, “You showed us the best way to visit it, Paz.” The light on the carpet cooled a little as a cloud passed outside, yet the aisle still felt friendly, and Paz rocked gently on their heels and hummed four notes and then left a rest so the silence could sit with them. Dahlia copied the hum and the rest, and the three of them shared that small pause like a shared breath. Paz glanced once more at the labeled box and decided the pellet belonged right there for the next child who might walk down the same aisle tomorrow.

They turned together toward the end of the shelves where the reading chairs waited in softer light, and Paz kept thinking about the careful bones and the way looking had been enough. Nana Ruth carried her bag again, and the cinnamon scent rose faintly when she shifted it higher on her arm. Paz walked between Nana Ruth and Dahlia, matching their slow steps, and felt the carpet’s low pile underfoot and the cool air near the tall windows. No one hurried. The library held them kindly while they moved away from the table, and Paz looked back only once to be sure the box sat exactly as they had found it, closed and peaceful in its place of sun.

Later, when they reached the corner near home where the evening had already begun to settle, Paz sat by the wide window and told the whole visit again in a quiet voice while Nana Ruth listened and Dahlia rested her chin on her folded arms. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Paz felt the last warmth of the day on their cheeks and closed their eyes for a moment, still seeing the pellet safe in its box and the three of them looking together without taking, and the calm of that choice stayed with Paz as the room grew dim and kind.
