"Would you like to rest with a book while the shelves grow quiet?" Mr. Ade asked, and Noemi nodded as they stepped into the library corner where a low bed waited beneath a tall window.

The late sun lay across the mattress in gentle stripes, and Noemi felt the cool air from the open pane mingle with the faint lemon soap that clung to Mr. Ade’s clean hands as he straightened a pillow. On the side table sat an open picture book marked with a pale paper ribbon that fluttered once when Noemi breathed near it. Noemi ran one finger along the soft cover and smiled at the quiet of the room. Outside, the wind in the fig leaves made a slow shushing sound that seemed to match the turning of pages somewhere deeper in the stacks. Mr. Ade moved without hurry, checking the lamp and the blanket fold, and Noemi watched him the way they always watched when something kind was happening without a big announcement.

Noemi settled onto the edge of the bed and discovered the surprise at once. A smooth warm stone rested beneath the top blanket, already slipped into place near where Noemi’s feet would go, holding a steady heat that spread through the cloth like a small sun. Noemi pressed their palm against the coverlet and felt that careful warmth rising. Mr. Ade had done this before Noemi arrived, and the simple gift made Noemi’s chest feel full and light at the same time. A quick thank-you rose to Noemi’s lips, yet it felt too thin for the quiet work already finished. Noemi looked at the paper ribbon resting in the open book and then at Mr. Ade’s calm face and decided that noticing would need something more than short words alone.

Noemi stood again and glanced around the little corner for one useful thing Mr. Ade had not asked for. The side table held a small plate with crumbs from an earlier snack and a floured towel left folded beside a tin of biscuits. Noemi lifted the floured towel, felt its dry powdery texture against their fingers, and used it to gather the crumbs into a neat pile before wiping the wood clean. Then Noemi took a soft cloth from the nearby basket and began to wipe the table in slow circles, watching the last dusty streaks disappear under the steady motion. The lemon soap smell drifted again from the cloth Mr. Ade had used earlier, mixing with the clean wood scent, and Noemi kept the circles quiet so the peace of the corner stayed unbroken. Outside the window the wind in the fig leaves continued its soft rush, and Noemi timed the wipes to that gentle rhythm until the surface shone in the fading light.

When the table looked ready, Noemi returned the floured towel to its place and smoothed the paper ribbon flat inside the open book so it would not slip. Mr. Ade noticed the tidy surface and the settled ribbon but said nothing yet, only raised his eyebrows in mild surprise. Noemi sat back on the bed, tucked their feet near the warm stone, and felt the heat travel up through the blanket like a slow hug. The stone’s warmth stayed even and kind, and Noemi thought about how Mr. Ade must have heated it carefully and carried it here without being asked. The library corner felt safer and softer because of that small plan, and Noemi wanted Mr. Ade to know the plan had been seen.

"I saw the warm stone you put in the bed," Noemi said plainly, looking straight at Mr. Ade. "I wiped the table and set the ribbon straight because you already took care of the rest." Mr. Ade’s smile arrived slow and bright. "That was thoughtful of you, Noemi," he answered, his voice low enough not to startle the quiet. Noemi felt the blanket’s soft weave under their hands and the stone’s gentle heat under their toes, and the words they had chosen settled comfortably between them. Noemi reached for the picture book and held the paper ribbon while Mr. Ade adjusted the pillow once more. The light on the floor had turned the color of apricot jam, and the air stayed mild and still inside the corner even as the fig leaves whispered outside.

Noemi opened the book to the marked page and began to look at the pictures of a river and a wooden bridge while Mr. Ade sat in the low chair nearby. The pages smelled faintly of old paper and the lemon soap that still lingered on Noemi’s own hands from the cleaning cloth. Every so often Noemi glanced down to make sure the warm stone had not cooled, and each time the heat remained steady and friendly. "The stone feels just right," Noemi murmured, almost to themselves. Mr. Ade nodded. "I hoped it would," he said. Noemi turned another page carefully so the paper ribbon stayed in place as a gentle bookmark for later. The library corner held only the soft rustle of paper and the distant hush of the wind in the fig leaves, and Noemi felt the day folding itself into evening without any rush.

After a few more pictures, Noemi closed the book with the ribbon still marking the spot and lay back against the pillow. The warm stone pressed lightly against the soles of their feet through the blanket, and Noemi wriggled once to settle deeper into the heat. Mr. Ade rose and dimmed the brighter lamp so only the smaller light remained. "Would you like me to stay while the story rests with you?" he asked. Noemi answered, "Yes, please," and the simple exchange felt complete. Noemi’s eyes followed the last pale stripes of sun as they climbed the wall and then faded. The floured towel rested neatly where Noemi had returned it, and the wiped table caught a thin gleam of remaining light. Everything in the corner looked cared for, both by Mr. Ade’s earlier work and by the quiet help Noemi had added without being told.

Noemi breathed in the mild air and listened again to the wind in the fig leaves sliding past the window screen. The lemon soap scent had grown softer now, almost like a memory of clean hands and careful work. Noemi thought of the warm stone slipped into the bed and of the clean table and of the paper ribbon waiting patiently in the book, and a calm fullness stayed with them. Mr. Ade’s chair creaked once as he shifted, and the sound was friendly rather than loud. Noemi’s eyelids grew heavier while the blanket held its gentle weight and the stone kept its secret heat. The library corner seemed to breathe with them, slow and even, and Noemi felt ready for sleep without any leftover worry.

Noemi’s breathing matched the quiet of the shelves as the last light left the floorboards. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Mr. Ade stayed nearby in the chair, and Noemi slept with the warm stone still holding its kindness under the blanket while the paper ribbon waited in the book for morning.
