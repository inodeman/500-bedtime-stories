Auntie June stood already at the cottage hearth with the clock unhurried, smoothing the front of her cotton apron while the fire sent steady warm light across the worn stones. Yael came in from the hall and felt that same gentle heat rise to meet their face and hands. Auntie June looked up and said, “Sit here with me, Yael, the evening is just beginning.” Yael set the chair square to the table so it faced the flames properly, then settled onto the seat. The unhurried tick of the clock mixed with the soft crackle of wood, and Yael rested both palms on the smooth tabletop while the light flickered gold on their sleeves.

The smell of toasted sesame drifted from the baking tin Auntie June had left to cool on the sideboard, warm and nutty in the air. Auntie June lifted a folded paper from the mantel shelf and opened it near the glow. “I carried home this report from the lost and found at the village hall,” she said. “We can go through it item by item while the fire keeps us company.” Yael felt a quick wish to tell bigger news about the tall block tower they had built with Idris that afternoon, yet the calm rhythm of the room held them quiet. The paper edges caught the light as Auntie June held them steady, and Yael watched without speaking.

“The first item was a single blue mitten left on the park bench after the morning games,” Auntie June read in her even voice. “It waited there until someone claimed it by the shape of the thumb.” Yael nodded and listened as the hearth light painted soft shapes on the walls. They pressed their lips together against the urge to describe how high the tower had climbed, higher than the bookshelf even. Instead Yael watched a bright ember settle deeper and felt the quiet air of the cottage rest against their skin. Auntie June turned the paper a little so both could see the neat handwriting.

“Next came a wooden toy boat with a yellow sail that had drifted into the pond reeds,” Auntie June continued. She paused and glanced at Yael with a small smile before reading on. The heat from the fire made Yael’s toes tingle pleasantly inside their slippers, a steady comfort that spread upward. Yael almost told how Idris had clapped when the last block stayed balanced, yet they kept the words inside and listened all the way. Outside the window the last daylight faded to a gentle violet, and the cottage stayed snug around them both.

“Then there was a set of colored pencils tied with a thin ribbon, and after that a small brass key on a leather string,” Auntie June said. Yael leaned forward a little and heard, far off, the clear click of a gate latch as a neighbor closed their fence for the night. The sound arrived cleanly through the still air and faded again. Yael pictured the pencils and the key while the wish to interrupt rose once more and then settled. Another log shifted in the hearth and the air near Yael’s knees grew even warmer, wrapping them in quiet patience.

Auntie June turned the page with care. “A scarf printed with little moons came next, found folded under the library steps,” she read. “Someone had left it tidy so it would not be missed.” Yael watched the orange flicker of the fire move across Auntie June’s kind face and felt the cushion beneath them hold steady. They remembered Idris laughing when the tower swayed yet stayed upright, but Yael remained silent and let the list continue. The clock kept its unhurried pace, and the light from the hearth stayed soft and golden on the table.

“After the scarf came a pair of shiny rain boots two sizes too small, then a lunch box covered in star stickers,” Auntie June went on. “Near the bottom of the list was one bright green button from a coat.” Yael sat very still and listened through every word until the final sentence stated that each item had found its owner again by the week’s end. A thin cool draft brushed Yael’s wrist for a moment before the fire’s warmth returned and covered it. Yael kept their hands folded and waited through the last calm line without breaking the quiet flow.

Yael drew a slow breath and asked one real question. “Auntie June, how did the people know which things belonged to them?” Auntie June nodded as if the question pleased her. “They came and looked with care, and sometimes they knew by a special mark or simply by the way it felt in their hands,” she answered. Only then did Yael share something small of their own. “Today Idris and I found a smooth white stone by the path, and I kept it in my pocket,” Yael said. The hearth light shone gently across Yael’s hair while they spoke, and the room seemed to listen with them.

They stayed side by side a little longer while the cottage grew stiller and the fire settled into deep red coals. Auntie June folded the report once more and laid it on the mantel where the clock continued without hurry. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Yael felt the even temperature of the room hold them in quiet comfort, warm enough for rest and bright enough to see Auntie June’s peaceful face. Outside the night deepened, yet inside Yael leaned closer to the gentle heat, ready whenever sleep arrived.
