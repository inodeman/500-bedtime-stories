The jar of lemon curd rested near the center of the broad ceramic studio table, its clear glass walls letting the pale yellow cream shine under the low afternoon light that slipped across the wood. Tiny drops of cool moisture beaded on the inside, and the surface of the curd looked almost still, only the softest slow ripple showing it was still settling into something thicker. Kira bent close until the cool glass nearly touched their cheek and watched the way the light made the yellow look deep and calm. The tin lid sat loosely on top, its metal surface smooth and cool under the soft room air. All around the jar lay boards holding soft slip and newly attached clay handles that needed the same patient quiet so they could firm without hurry.

Kira kept their eyes on the jar and felt the quiet weight of wanting to lift the cover just to see if the curd had grown ready. The air near the table carried a gentle warmth from the drying clay, and Kira’s fingertips brushed the edge of the wood as they leaned. Nana Ruth stood a few steps away, her hands dusted lightly with dried slip, and the soft smell of chamomile rose from the small cup she had set on a side stool. Kira straightened a little and said, “Nana Ruth, may I look under the lid now and check the lemon curd?” Nana Ruth turned with a kind smile and answered in a low voice, “It still needs its own slow time to thicken, Kira, so we leave the cover settled.”

Kira nodded and let their hand rest for a moment on the tin lid, feeling its cool ridged texture against their palm before they drew back. The handles on the nearest board looked pale and soft, and the slip shone with a dull wet light that asked not to be touched. Kira slid onto the wooden stool beside Nana Ruth and watched a thin curl of steam lift from the chamomile cup. The table surface felt smooth under Kira’s free hand, and they kept their knees still so no sudden bump could shake the setting clay. Soft afternoon light pooled on the floorboards and made the whole studio feel hushed and safe.

Nana Ruth wiped her hands on a clean cloth and said, “Would you like a quiet task while we wait together, Kira?” Kira answered, “Yes, I will leave the jar covered and stay near you.” They glanced once more at the lemon curd and saw that the yellow surface still held its slow calm. Near the chair a small warm heap of clean laundry waited, socks and soft cloths still holding heat from the dryer. Kira reached into the heap and drew out two socks that felt cozy and slightly fuzzy against their fingers. They smoothed each sock flat on their lap and matched the pair by size and color with careful slow movements.

The tin clock on the shelf gave a steady soft tick that marked the quiet minutes without rush. Kira matched another pair from the warm heap, pressing the fabric together so the heels lined up neatly, and the gentle heat of the cloth warmed their hands. Nana Ruth sat nearby and watched the slip begin to lose its wet shine as it set. Kira kept their breathing even and said, “The handles look a little firmer already.” Nana Ruth replied, “They are finding their shape in their own time, just as the curd is.” The light in the room had turned a deeper gold, and the air held a mild temperature that felt kind on bare arms.

Kira finished matching two more socks and set the neat pairs aside in a small stack that felt soft under their palm. They looked toward the jar again but did not reach for it, choosing instead to rest both hands in their lap. The boards of clay handles sat still, their edges no longer shiny with wet slip, and a faint dry earth smell mixed with the remaining warmth in the studio. Kira felt the stool steady beneath them and listened to the quiet of the room broken only by the soft sounds of cloth being folded. Nana Ruth poured a little more warm water into her cup, and the steam rose in a thin friendly ribbon.

Time moved in easy stretches while Kira stayed close to the table and the covered jar. They smoothed a clean cloth across their knees and watched how the light shifted across the glass of the lemon curd without lifting anything. The clay pieces continued their slow firming, and Kira noticed that the surface of the curd now looked denser and more settled when viewed through the glass. Nana Ruth set her cup down and said in a gentle tone, “You have been very still and kind with the waiting, Kira.” Kira smiled and answered, “I wanted the jar to finish in its own time.” The air near the window felt cooler now as evening began to gather.

Kira rose carefully so the stool made no scrape and walked once around the far side of the table, keeping far from the setting handles. They returned to the stool and rested their chin on their folded hands, eyes calm on the jar. The tin lid still sat in place, and the yellow cream inside no longer showed any ripple at all. Soft shadows lengthened across the floor, and the wooden table held a pleasant coolness under Kira’s elbow. Nana Ruth checked the clay with a light look only and then turned back with a quiet nod that meant everything was finding its readiness together.

Kira and Nana Ruth sat side by side while the last of the light softened across the studio. The jar of lemon curd had thickened at last into a smooth rich cream ready for later toast, and the slip and handles had set firm enough to leave untouched until morning. Kira felt the peaceful stillness settle over their shoulders like a light blanket. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
