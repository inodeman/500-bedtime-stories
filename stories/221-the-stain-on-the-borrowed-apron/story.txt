"Grandpa Sol, the bowls on this sill feel almost ready," Yuri said, their voice soft as they leaned on the wide ceramic studio sill above the quiet plaza. Warm evening light brushed Yuri's cheeks and turned the clay rims the color of honey while a gentle breeze cooled the backs of their hands. Yuri smoothed the borrowed apron across their lap and felt the unexpected sticky patch near the pocket, a deep berry stain from the jam tart they had shared earlier on the steps. For a moment Yuri thought of folding the fabric over so the mark would vanish from sight, yet the idea sat heavy in their chest like an unshaped lump of clay. The plaza below stayed hushed except for far-off footsteps, and Yuri stayed close to the sill, fingers tracing the cool ceramic edge.

Yuri stayed quiet a little longer, watching a pale moth drift past the open studio window while the air held a faint chalky coolness from the drying shelves. The borrowed apron still smelled faintly of soap and clay dust, and the berry stain looked darker now in the fading gold light. Yuri pictured slipping the apron off and hanging it so the mark faced the wall, yet that picture felt crooked, like a pot set off-center on the wheel. They pressed one palm flat against the sill's smooth grit and listened to their own slow breathing. Somewhere a bicycle bell chimed once and faded, leaving the studio peaceful again. Yuri decided the truth would sit better than any folded corner.

Grandpa Sol stepped through the studio doorway with a tray of clean sponges, the wooden floor creaking under his careful shoes. He set the tray down and rested a kind hand on Yuri's shoulder, the touch steady and warm through the apron strap. "You have been working so patiently today," Grandpa Sol said, smiling at the neat row of little cups Yuri had pressed. Yuri felt the weight of the stain again and nearly stayed silent, yet the quiet between them felt safe. The light inside the studio had turned softer, blue-gray at the edges, and the temperature dropped just enough that Yuri pulled the apron closer without thinking. Grandpa Sol waited without hurry, eyes on the plaza below where evening lamps began to glow like small moons.

"I spilled berry jam on your apron, Grandpa Sol, and I almost hid the stain," Yuri said, the words coming out plain and steady. Their fingers stayed open on the fabric so the dark mark showed clearly under the window light. Grandpa Sol nodded once, no frown, no teasing laugh, only a gentle hum of understanding. "Thank you for telling me straight away," he answered. "We can mend what can be mended together." Yuri felt their shoulders loosen as if a string had been untied. The air near the sill carried a clean mineral scent from the damp sponges, and the borrowed apron now felt less heavy against Yuri's knees. They both looked at the stain the way they might look at a cracked handle that still deserved care.

Together they carried the apron to the deep sink in the corner where cool water ran in a silver ribbon. Yuri held the stained cloth under the stream while Grandpa Sol guided their hands, showing how to dab rather than scrub. The water turned a soft pink and then clear again, and the berry mark lightened to a gentle shadow that no longer shouted. Yuri noticed a satin ribbon that had been used to tie a bundle of clean towels; they ran one fingertip along its smooth cool length before looping it loosely around the apron strings so the cloth could hang to finish drying. The ribbon's slippery texture surprised Yuri in a pleasant way, like touching the inside of a petal. Soft lamp light filled the sink corner and kept the space snug.

While the apron dripped, Yuri and Grandpa Sol returned to the sill and watched the quiet plaza settle into evening. A light shower had passed earlier, and now the smell of rain on dust rose from the stones below, earthy and fresh and almost sweet. Yuri breathed it in deeply and felt calm spread through their chest. "The air smells like the garden after clouds," Yuri said. Grandpa Sol agreed with a quiet "Yes, exactly that." They rinsed two small sponges and pressed them gently along the drying bowls so no water spots would form. The temperature near the open window stayed mild, neither hot nor cold, perfect for sitting side by side without jackets. Yuri's hands moved slowly, matching Grandpa Sol's easy rhythm.

A gate latch clicked shut somewhere at the edge of the plaza, a clean metal sound that carried up clearly through the still air. Yuri tilted their head to catch it and smiled because the sound felt like the day folding itself neatly away. Grandpa Sol handed Yuri a dry cloth and they wiped the sill together until the ceramic shone under the gathering dusk. No one rushed. Yuri noticed a faint fog of breath on the lower corner of the window glass and, with one dry finger, traced a slow careful circle there, watching the clear line appear. The simple motion felt right in the peaceful room. Light from the plaza lamps climbed higher and painted faint gold on the studio walls.

Paz appeared briefly at the studio doorway holding two mugs of warm milk, steam curling like tiny clouds. "I brought these for the end of the work," Paz said, setting them on a low table so the heat could be felt even before tasting. Yuri thanked Paz and wrapped both hands around one mug, feeling the pleasant warmth seep into their palms. Grandpa Sol lifted the other mug and they all three sat near the sill for a few quiet sips. The milk tasted mild and comforting, and the berry stain on the hanging apron looked only like a soft memory now. Outside, the plaza stayed peaceful, and inside the studio the clay bowls rested safe and round. Yuri leaned a little against Grandpa Sol's arm, content.

Later Yuri helped hang the nearly dry apron on its peg, the satin ribbon still loosely tied so the cloth fell straight. The last of the daylight slipped away and the studio filled with a gentle hush broken only by the soft clink of mugs returned to the tray. Yuri stood once more at the sill, palms resting on the cool ceramic, and watched the quiet plaza lamps glow like friendly fireflies. Grandpa Sol dimmed the overhead bulbs until only one small light remained. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Yuri breathed in the settled air, felt the mild night temperature on their skin, and knew the borrowed apron and the honest words had found their easy place together before sleep.
