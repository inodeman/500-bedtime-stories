Grandpa Sol knelt on the cool wooden floor of the school hall and hummed while he sorted bright bunting into neat piles, the soft light from high windows warming his shoulders as he worked. Ilaria stepped inside and felt the mild air of the big room brush their cheeks, noticing at once how Grandpa Sol already had ribbons and signs laid out for the gentle fair. The hall smelled faintly of polished floors mixed with something new, and Ilaria walked closer so their shoes made quiet taps. “The tent pieces are over by the wall,” Grandpa Sol said without looking up, his voice calm and steady. Ilaria saw the small tent’s pole and pegs resting beside a rolled canvas and felt glad Grandpa Sol had begun early. They touched the smooth edge of a table and watched dust motes drift in a shaft of afternoon light.

Ilaria called softly for Opal, who soon appeared carrying a box of paper flowers that rustled like dry leaves. Together Ilaria and Opal studied the long pole and the short metal pegs that would hold the little tent steady for children to sit inside and hear stories later. The canvas felt cool under Ilaria’s fingers when they unrolled a corner, and the whole hall seemed hushed except for distant footsteps. Opal smiled and said, “We can make a cozy reading corner if we take our time.” Ilaria nodded and smelled a brief drift of rain on dust when someone opened the far door for a moment, the outdoor scent slipping in and fading. Grandpa Sol kept sorting, and Ilaria felt the steady temperature of the room settle around them like a quiet blanket.

They realized the pole would lean wrong and the pegs would scatter if anyone hurried, so Ilaria and Opal decided to split the work into two slow jobs. Ilaria would hold and steady the pole while Opal placed each peg with care, and then they would switch. The wooden floor felt smooth and solid beneath Ilaria’s knees as they knelt. Soft light slanted across the pegs and made them gleam faintly. “I’ll count the spaces slowly,” Ilaria said, and Opal answered, “I’ll press each one just deep enough.” Grandpa Sol glanced over and offered only a quiet name for the simple knot they would need later on the guy lines. Ilaria listened and felt ready to begin without rush.

Ilaria lifted the light pole and felt its smooth painted surface against their palms while Opal lined up the first peg. The air near the floor stayed cool, and Ilaria heard a gate latch click once somewhere outside the open side door, a brief metal sound that faded into the calm hall. They moved together, Ilaria keeping the pole upright with both hands while Opal tapped a peg gently into place. No one spoke for a while, only the soft scrape of peg against wood sounding now and then. Ilaria watched Opal’s careful hands and felt the quiet pride of shared work settle in their chest. Grandpa Sol continued his own slow tasks nearby, never crowding them.

When the pole needed a slight turn, Ilaria and Opal switched jobs without hurry. Now Opal held the pole steady and Ilaria chose the next peg, feeling its cool metal weight. A length of satin ribbon from the fair decorations brushed Ilaria’s wrist as they reached, the slick soft texture sliding past their skin before they tucked it aside. Grandpa Sol said, “That knot is called a simple half hitch if you need the name,” and Ilaria repeated the words quietly so they would remember. The high windows let in a gentle glow that warmed the side of Ilaria’s face. They worked on, placing peg after peg in a slow circle around the base.

Ilaria paused once to breathe and noticed how the canvas smelled clean and new when a breeze stirred it. Opal held the pole true while Ilaria finished the last few pegs, each one seated firmly yet without force. The temperature in the hall stayed pleasantly even, neither warm nor chilly. “We are almost there,” Opal whispered, and Ilaria answered, “The tent will stand nicely for the fair.” Grandpa Sol nodded from his place by the bunting and offered a soft count of four when they prepared the short ropes. Ilaria looped each rope slowly, using the named knot, and felt the satisfaction of every careful motion.

Together they raised the canvas over the pole, Ilaria guiding one side while Opal smoothed the other. The fabric settled with a gentle sigh of air, and soft light filtered through its pale color to make a quiet glowing space inside. Ilaria crawled in for a moment and felt the still coolness of the shaded floor under their hands. Outside the little tent the hall waited peacefully, filled with the small sounds of fair preparations. Grandpa Sol brought two cushions and placed them just inside so visitors could sit comfortably later. Ilaria backed out and stood beside Opal, both of them looking at the finished shape with calm delight.

They checked every peg one last time, Ilaria running a finger along the taut ropes while Opal tested the pole’s balance. Everything held firm because they had refused to rush. The air carried a faint lingering freshness, and Ilaria heard only the distant quiet of the school settling toward evening. Grandpa Sol smiled and said nothing more, simply handing Ilaria a spare satin scrap to mark the entrance if they wished. Ilaria tied it loosely and felt the day’s work rest easy in their mind. Opal gave a small happy nod, and the three of them stood together for a peaceful minute in the open hall.

Ilaria walked to the tall window where a faint fog of breath had gathered on the cool glass and traced a circle of fog with a dry finger, watching the clear line appear. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Outside the light had grown gentle and blue, and inside the school hall the little tent waited ready for the gentle fair, its pole straight and its pegs secure because Ilaria and Opal had shared the slow jobs and Grandpa Sol had given only the quiet help of a count and a knot name, all of them finishing side by side in the calm room.
