The bottom edge of the wooden door pressed hard against the curled corner of the thick mat, and Femi leaned so close that they could see the individual fibers twisted tightly together in the stubborn roll. In the market lane crowded with baskets of fresh vegetables, tall glass jars filled with spices, and rows of colorful flower pots, the door stayed caught as if the mat had decided to hold it. Warm golden light from the afternoon sun streamed down and heated the wooden surface near Femi's face. They reached out and felt the rough raised texture of the curled mat under their fingertips while a gentle warmth surrounded them. The air held a mild stillness right at that spot even as the rest of the lane continued its easy pace. Femi stayed kneeling there, examining the stuck place with quiet attention before anyone else arrived.

Grandma Nia came along the lane with her woven basket hanging from one arm and tried to push the door open so she could store her new jars inside the little stall at the end of the row. The door moved a short way then stuck firmly once more on the curled mat, and the effort looked awkward for her alone. She placed the basket down on the sun-warmed stones with a quiet sound. The smell of basil floated over from a nearby seller who had bundles of the green leaves tied ready, and it filled Femi's nose with its fresh herbal note. "This door will not swing free while the mat stays curled underneath," Grandma Nia said in a calm tone. Femi felt the heat of the day lingering on their arms and shoulders as they rose from the ground to stand beside her.

Femi understood that the stuck door made the whole task difficult for one person and chose to take a useful half of the work right away. They moved closer and placed both hands on the side of the door. "I will hold it up so you can smooth the mat, Grandma Nia," Femi said in a clear steady voice. Their grip kept the bottom edge lifted just enough. Bright light continued to shine on the painted panels of the door and made little sparkles dance near Femi's eyes. The temperature remained pleasantly warm without becoming too hot. Flower pots stood in neat lines nearby with their leaves catching the same light that touched Femi as they waited ready to help.

Grandma Nia knelt down and used both hands to press and roll the curled mat until every part lay flat and smooth against the ground again. While she worked, Femi held their half of the job with patient strength, keeping the door from dropping back onto the fibers. The wood felt smooth and slightly cool now under their palms where shade had begun to reach it. A tin clock ticked regularly from a wooden counter across the market lane, its clear metallic sound counting out the peaceful seconds. "Now we can let it rest gently," Grandma Nia said. Femi eased the door down carefully and watched it settle without any catch on the newly flat mat. A soft draft of cooler air moved past their ankles from under a nearby awning.

With the door free at last, Femi and Grandma Nia carried the jars from the basket into the shaded stall, and Femi took several of them without being asked so the load stayed light for both. Inside the stall the light came dimmer through a cloth curtain and felt gentle on their skin. They set each jar in place on the wooden shelf. "That made everything easier," Grandma Nia said with a soft smile. Femi arranged the final jar and noticed how the clay felt firm and dry against their hands. They stepped back outside together once the stall looked neat and ready, the market lane still full of its baskets and pots under the continuing afternoon glow.

Near the mat Femi noticed a polished pebble that had come to rest against the edge, and they bent to pick it up. The texture of the polished pebble was sleek and cool in their palm, rounded by long time in water perhaps, and they moved it carefully beside one of the flower pots so the walking space stayed open. Evening began to soften the light overhead as thin clouds passed. Grandma Nia tested the door one more time and it opened and closed without sticking at all. Soft voices of other people drifted from farther along the lane of jars and baskets, yet Femi felt only calm in that moment of finished work. They wiped their hands on their shirt and stood ready for the next part of the day.

Grandma Nia packed her empty basket and they left the market lane behind, walking home along the quieter street where trees cast longer shadows. Femi stayed close and matched the unhurried pace. The ground under their shoes felt solid and even. "The sun must have dried the mat and made it lift up like that," Femi said thoughtfully. "Yes, exactly so," Grandma Nia answered. A mild coolness settled into the air as the day turned toward evening and brushed lightly against their faces and bare arms. Birds sang short songs from the branches above them while they continued their steady walk toward home with the helpful afternoon still fresh in mind.

Once they reached home Grandma Nia began to prepare a light supper in the kitchen, and Femi helped by folding the cloth napkins one by one so that the stitched pear on every napkin faced neatly upward on the table. The fabric felt soft and slightly worn from many washings under their careful fingers. Warm light from the setting sun came through the kitchen window and laid a golden path across the floor near their feet. They sat together and ate bread with a little cheese that tasted mild and comforting in the quiet room. After the meal Femi helped stack the plates by the basin, feeling the cool water when they rinsed their hands. Everything moved at a peaceful speed with no rush at all.

Bedtime came softly after they had tidied the last crumbs. Femi climbed into bed and pulled the blanket up to their chin where it rested with a smooth clean feel against their arms. Grandma Nia paused beside the bed and spoke a quiet good night. The lantern by the window glowed softly. A faint draft of cool night air moved the curtain just a little, and the room held a steady gentle temperature that invited rest. Femi listened to the far-off settling sounds of the house and closed their eyes, holding the calm of the market lane and the shared work close as sleep began to arrive.
