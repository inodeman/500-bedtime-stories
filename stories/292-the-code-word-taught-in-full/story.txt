The code word rested complete on the small cream card Uma cradled in both palms, every letter inked dark and steady so nothing trailed off half-finished. Warm light from the round window poured across the card and turned the edges a soft gold while Uma traced the final syllable with one careful fingertip. The paper felt dry and slightly ridged under their skin, and the quiet library air held a faint coolness that brushed Uma’s wrists. Grandma Nia had spoken the whole word slowly earlier, never stopping midway, and Uma practiced it once more in a tiny breath so the full sound stayed whole inside them.

On the wide library rug under the round window, Uma sat cross-legged with the card resting on their knee while the soft wool fibers pressed gently against their bare ankles. Grandma Nia settled nearby with a stack of picture books, and the afternoon brightness painted a pale circle on the floor around them. Tove stood just beyond the edge of the rug, fingers brushing the spines of nearby shelves, close enough to hear but not yet inside the circle. Uma noticed the way Tove’s shoulders angled toward the group and felt the mild temperature of the room shift as a draft from the window stirred the air. The code word belonged to the quiet game they were about to begin, and Uma kept the card face-up so its full message stayed visible.

Grandma Nia smiled and said, “Uma, you may teach the code word all the way through whenever you are ready.” Uma nodded and turned the card so the light caught every letter again. The tin clock on the nearby shelf gave a steady soft tick that marked the calm seconds without hurry. Uma could smell the fresh basil leaves in the little clay pot resting on the windowsill, their green scent rising whenever the air moved. Tove took one small step closer, still outside the woven border of the rug, and Uma felt the polished pebble they kept in their pocket, its cool smooth surface warming slowly against their palm as they held it for comfort.

Uma looked at the open space on the rug and then at Tove’s quiet face. “Tove, would you like to sit right here beside me so we can share the code word together?” Uma asked in a gentle voice that matched the hush of the room. The round window let in a honey-colored glow that touched the tops of the books and made the rug fibers look almost silver in places. Grandma Nia stayed quiet and simply folded her hands, letting the children find their own way. Uma shifted two cushions sideways to open a clear spot that faced the window, changing the seating without any announcement or prize.

Tove stepped onto the rug and settled into the new place Uma had made, the wool feeling springy and kind under both of them. “Thank you, Uma,” Tove whispered, voice barely louder than the turning of a page. Uma answered, “We can say the whole code word slowly so it stays complete for both of us.” The basil scent drifted again from the windowsill pot and mixed with the dry paper smell of open books. Grandma Nia watched with calm eyes and said nothing more, trusting the small circle to arrange itself. Uma placed the cream card between them so the full letters stayed easy to see in the gentle light.

They practiced the code word together, Uma speaking each part clearly and waiting while Tove repeated it all the way to the end without rushing. The tin clock kept its quiet ticking from the shelf, a steady companion to their soft voices. Uma felt the polished pebble once more as they rolled it lightly between their fingers, its surface silky and cool before it warmed again. Light from the round window slid lower and painted longer shapes across the rug, warming the backs of their hands. Grandma Nia opened a book but left the pages unread for now, giving the children time to settle the rules at their own pace.

Uma decided the game would move more slowly so everyone could join the turns without hurry. “We can pass the card after each full word, and no one has to race,” Uma said, voice steady and kind. Tove smiled and replied, “That feels right for sitting here together.” The air near the floor stayed pleasantly mild while the basil leaves released another light wave of green fragrance whenever a small breeze arrived. Uma adjusted the cushions one last time so both of them faced the window equally, making the circle open instead of closed. Grandma Nia nodded once in quiet approval and let the moment belong to the children.

They took turns whispering the complete code word, listening to every sound land fully before the next voice began. Uma kept the polished pebble in an open palm so Tove could see its smooth shine if they wished, though neither spoke of it. The library around them held only gentle page turns and distant soft footsteps, and the temperature under the round window remained even and calm. Grandma Nia finally read a short page aloud in a low voice that matched the ticking of the tin clock, then closed the book and rested it nearby. Uma and Tove sat side by side on the changed rug, the full code word now shared between them like a quiet bridge.

Shadows lengthened in friendly shapes across the floor while Uma and Tove rested the card between their knees and simply breathed the peaceful air. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Uma felt the last warmth of the afternoon light on their shoulders and heard the tin clock settle into an even softer rhythm as evening approached. Grandma Nia rose without hurry and gathered the cushions into a neat stack, leaving the little basil pot where its scent could linger. Uma slipped the polished pebble back into their pocket and glanced at Tove with a small shared smile, the whole code word safe and finished inside the quiet library glow.
