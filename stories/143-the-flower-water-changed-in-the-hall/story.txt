The warm smell of lemon soap rose from the cloth in Kavi’s busy hands as they wiped the library return desk in slow circles. Cool wood met their palms through the damp fabric, and pale afternoon light pooled across the counter like spilled milk. Kavi pressed gently, clearing smudges left by many books, and listened to the soft squeak of the cloth. Rain whispered against the windows, yet the air inside stayed mild and clean. Kavi’s wrists turned in steady loops, careful around the stamp pad and the neat pile of return slips. They liked the quiet work and the way the desk began to shine. A little more polishing, and every corner looked ready for the next visitor. Kavi breathed in the bright scent and kept their hands moving without hurry.

Near the return desk stood a tall vase of hall flowers whose water had gone cloudy, the stems leaning as if tired. Kavi noticed the dull water first, then the soft petals that still held a faint green fragrance. Mr. Ade appeared at the rainy steps outside, his arms full of books he meant to return, the heavy doors swinging with a low creak. Rain beaded on his coat and dripped onto the stone, making the steps glisten under the gray sky. Kavi watched him shift the books from one arm to the other, trying also to steady the vase that blocked part of the doorway. The cloudy water sloshed when the vase wobbled. Kavi set the cloth aside and stepped closer, feeling the cooler air that drifted in from the open door.

“These books are ready to come home,” Mr. Ade said, his voice calm though his elbows looked full. Kavi nodded and reached for the vase without waiting to be asked. “I can hold the flowers steady while you set the books down,” Kavi answered, already lifting the heavy glass with both hands. The cloudy water chilled their fingers, and the stems brushed softly against their wrists. Mr. Ade placed the books on the return desk with a grateful sigh. Together they carried the vase a few steps toward the sink alcove beside the desk, where fresh water waited in a clean pitcher. Kavi kept the vase level so nothing spilled on the floor.

Mr. Ade poured out the old water while Kavi held the stems gently in a loose bundle. Fresh cool water filled the vase again, clear as a quiet pond, and the petals lifted a little as if pleased. “Thank you for the extra hands,” Mr. Ade said, smiling as he dried the outside of the glass. Kavi only shrugged and helped slide the vase back to its place in the hall near the return desk. The rainy steps still shone outside, and a soft draft carried the sound of wind in the fig leaves that grew along the library wall. Kavi listened to that gentle rustle while they both checked that every book sat neatly in the return slot.

They worked side by side without fuss. Kavi took the useful half of the job—rinsing the vase rim, wiping stray drops from the floor tiles, and straightening the flower stems so they stood tall again. Mr. Ade handled the heavier books and the final placement of the vase. No one needed to give orders. The desk lamp cast a warm circle of light over their hands, and the rain kept a steady hush on the steps. Kavi felt the smooth glass under their palms and the slight chill that lingered from the fresh water. Everything smelled cleaner now, and the hall looked peaceful once more.

When the last book rested in its place, Mr. Ade found a floured towel on a nearby cart left from the morning story-time baking craft. He handed one end to Kavi so they could dry the last damp spots on the counter together. The floured towel felt soft and slightly powdery against Kavi’s fingers, catching every drop without a sound. They folded it afterward and set it aside. Outside, the rainy steps steamed a little where warmer air met the wet stone. Kavi glanced through the glass and saw the fig branches sway, their leaves still whispering. Mr. Ade stacked the return cards in a tidy row.

“The flowers look happy again,” Kavi said, almost to themselves. Mr. Ade nodded and replied, “They do, and the desk is ready for whoever comes next.” Kavi smiled at the clear water and the open petals. They both stepped back to admire the calm order they had made. The library lights hummed softly overhead, and the air felt just right—not too warm, not too cool. Kavi’s hands rested at their sides, no longer busy, simply quiet. Rain continued its gentle pattern on the steps, and the whole room seemed to breathe more slowly.

Kavi walked with Mr. Ade toward the reading nook near the windows, where cushions waited in soft piles. They sat for a moment on the low bench, listening to the distant drip of rain and the hush of turning pages from other quiet corners. Mr. Ade spoke once more in a low voice: “You noticed exactly what was needed.” Kavi looked at the flowers one last time and felt only the simple gladness of work shared. The light outside had grown softer, painting the wet steps in silver. Inside, everything stayed still and kind. Kavi’s shoulders relaxed as the day settled around them like a blanket.

Later, when the library grew even quieter, Kavi helped straighten one final cushion and watched the rain ease on the steps. The hall flowers stood bright in their clear water, and the return desk gleamed under the lamp. Kavi’s hands remembered the cool glass and the smooth wood. Mr. Ade locked the front latch with a gentle click. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Kavi felt the peaceful weight of evening and knew the shared work had left everything ready for sleep.
