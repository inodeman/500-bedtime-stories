The story disc rested high on the wooden shelf above the stacked flower pots, its round silver face catching every scrap of late-day light that spilled between the hanging baskets. Tiny rainbows flickered across the plastic whenever a breeze moved, and Golda tipped their chin up to study the small printed pictures of smiling animals dancing around the center hole. The air near the shelf felt warmer than the shaded ground, carrying a dry dusty warmth from the wood itself. Golda could almost hear the soft crackle the disc made when it spun inside Grandma Nia’s old player at home. Jars of honey and bright spoons glittered on the lower tables, yet Golda’s eyes stayed fixed on that one shiny circle waiting just out of easy reach.

Grandma Nia stood beside the flower pots with one hand on her hip and the other stretched toward the high shelf, her woven shawl brushing the rims of the clay jars. The market lane stretched long and quiet under the canvas awnings, filled with the cool evening air that smelled faintly of woodsmoke from a distant stove. Golda walked closer, sneakers soft on the packed earth, and noticed how Grandma Nia’s fingers just missed the edge of the disc. Baskets of dried flowers leaned against one another, their petals papery when Golda’s sleeve touched them. “The shelf is taller than I remembered today,” Grandma Nia said gently, her voice calm as she lowered her arm for a moment. Golda nodded and stayed near, watching the way the light turned the glass jars into soft glowing lanterns of their own.

A small orange cat padded past the nearest basket, and the bright sound of a cat's collar bell chimed once as it slipped under a low table. Golda breathed in slowly and caught the clean sharp scent of mint leaves rising from a bunch someone had set beside a jar of tea. Grandma Nia tried again, rising onto the balls of her feet, yet the disc stayed just beyond her fingertips. Golda felt the smooth cool side of a wooden crate against their palm as they leaned nearer. “I can steady the stool if we find one,” Golda said, already glancing along the lane for something sturdy. The temperature under the awning stayed mild, neither hot nor chilly, perfect for standing still and thinking.

Golda spotted a low wooden box used for empty pots and dragged it carefully across the ground until it sat steady beneath the shelf. The box made a soft scraping sound that blended with the distant murmur of other shoppers packing their baskets. Grandma Nia smiled and stepped onto it while Golda held both sides firm with both hands, feeling the rough grain of the wood under their fingers. “You take the top half and I’ll keep everything balanced down here,” Golda said without looking up for praise. The light from the sinking sun painted long gentle stripes across the flower pots, turning their green leaves almost golden. Together they made a quiet team, Grandma Nia reaching higher while Golda kept the box from tipping even a little.

Grandma Nia’s fingers closed around the edge of the story disc at last, and she lifted it free with a soft satisfied sigh. Golda kept holding the box until both of Grandma Nia’s feet were safely back on the ground. The disc felt cool and perfectly round when Grandma Nia passed it down into Golda’s waiting hands for a moment. Golda ran one careful fingertip along the rim and then felt the tiny raised bump of a glass bead that had been glued long ago as a decorative spot near the label. “We did it together,” Grandma Nia said, her voice warm as she took the disc back and slipped it into a cloth bag. The air cooled another degree as evening settled deeper over the market lane of baskets, jars, and flower pots.

Eshe appeared from behind a tall stack of empty jars, carrying a small bunch of leftover flowers, and stopped to watch the pair with bright curious eyes. Golda waved once and kept one hand lightly on the cloth bag so it would not swing. “Grandma Nia needed an extra set of hands for the high shelf, so I held the box steady,” Golda explained simply. Eshe nodded and offered the flowers, their petals soft against Golda’s wrist. The three of them stood close while the last warm light of day filtered through the hanging baskets and made everything look peaceful. Golda noticed the gentle hush that had settled over the lane as most of the other stalls finished packing.

They walked slowly along the market lane together, Golda carrying a light basket of jars while Grandma Nia held the cloth bag with the story disc safe inside. The packed earth felt firm and cool under Golda’s shoes, and the scent of the remaining flower pots still lingered sweetly. Eshe skipped once beside them and then matched their slower pace. “Will you play the disc after supper?” Eshe asked. Golda answered, “Yes, we will sit by the window and listen,” their voice quiet and sure. The temperature dropped just enough that Grandma Nia’s shawl felt welcome when she draped one corner over Golda’s shoulders for a moment of shared warmth.

Back at the small house at the end of the lane, Golda helped set the jars on the kitchen table and watched Grandma Nia slide the story disc into the player. Soft music began, the first notes floating through the room like calm water. Golda sat on the thick rug and ran their hand over the woven texture while the stories unfolded in familiar voices. Eshe curled nearby with a cushion, listening as the room filled with gentle sound. The air inside smelled clean and safe, and the walls held the day’s quiet kindness without anyone needing to name it. Golda closed their eyes for a stretch of time and simply let the words wash over them.

Later Golda climbed into bed while the last story finished its final soft notes. Grandma Nia tucked the blanket snug around their shoulders, and the cotton felt smooth and comforting against Golda’s chin. Eshe had already gone home with a wave and a promise to meet again soon. Golda breathed evenly and watched the familiar shadows on the ceiling. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Outside the market lane rested under the quiet night, baskets and jars and flower pots all still, while inside the room stayed warm and the story disc waited ready for another evening whenever they wanted it.
