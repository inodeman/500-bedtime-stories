The mild evening breeze touched the walls of the workshop gently, slipping cool fingers of air through the cracks and around the open door. Violeta stepped inside after finishing with their towel, their damp hair cool and heavy on their shoulders as they looked about the familiar space. Tools hung ready on a low peg along the wall, metal and wood gleaming a little in the last of the daylight that filtered in softly. Violeta felt the smooth worn boards under their bare feet, still holding a trace of afternoon warmth. The whole place smelled clean and safe, and Violeta was glad Papa was here as usual. They walked farther in without hurry, noticing how the shadows lengthened slowly across the floor. Everything felt just right for a quiet time before sleep.

Papa sat at the long bench with a small piece of wood in his hands, shaping it carefully. "Come in, Violeta," Papa said in his calm voice. "I have been working while you dried your hair." Violeta came close and then saw it clearly: the little bed tucked in the corner of the workshop had its covers turned down neatly, sheets folded back in a welcoming way and the blanket smoothed flat. Papa must have done that for them while they were still busy with the towel and the warm water. Soft light from the high window rested on the pillow, making it look inviting and ready. Violeta touched the edge of the bed frame lightly and felt how solid and steady it was. The air around it was cool and fresh.

A simple thank-you rose in Violeta's mind but felt too thin for the kindness of the ready bed. They wanted to offer something useful that Papa had not asked them to do. Violeta glanced around the workshop and spotted the remains of a shared snack. The bright smell of orange peel floated up from the scrap of peel left on a plate, fresh and citrusy in the mild air. Violeta picked up the peels and carried them to the bin. Then they reached for a linen napkin that lay folded on the shelf, its fine texture crisp and cool between their fingers as they shook it out. With careful strokes Violeta wiped the bench clear of shavings and bits, making the wood clean and pleasant again. They worked steadily in the quiet.

"You are very kind to tidy that, Violeta," Papa said, watching them with a gentle expression. Violeta just smiled and kept going until every speck was gone. They folded the linen napkin once more and set it back neatly where it belonged. Near the wall a little sink waited with its faucet, a drop forming slowly at the tip. Violeta decided the calm action of counting would fit here perfectly. They closed their eyes and counted twelve drips from the tap without peeking, listening to each soft plink as it fell into the basin below. One through twelve passed in peaceful order while the cool sensation of the damp air touched their cheeks. When they opened their eyes again the workshop seemed even more settled and kind. Papa had not moved from his spot.

Violeta turned toward Papa and stood straight. The tools on the low peg shone dully now as the light faded more. "Papa, I noticed you turned down the bed when I was still drying my hair," Violeta said in a plain clear voice. They wanted him to know they had seen the care. Papa set his wood piece aside and looked at them fully. "Yes, I did that so you would find it ready," Papa answered. The temperature in the room stayed mild and even, neither warm nor chilly. Violeta felt happy they had spoken the words out loud. A soft rustle came from outside as leaves moved in the breeze, adding to the calm. They both paused in the shared quiet of the workshop.

After speaking, Violeta helped put away one more small tool that had been left out, sliding it onto the low peg where the others hung. The handle felt firm and smooth in their grip. Papa smiled at the extra care. The tin clock ticked once more loudly enough to notice, its clear metallic sound marking time in the workshop. Soft shadows stretched longer across the floor as the day ended gently. Violeta felt the air growing a touch cooler now but still comfortable. They looked at the ready bed and felt content that they had noticed and helped in return without being asked.

Papa stood and stretched his arms, then came over to the corner. "Shall we leave the work for morning?" he asked. Violeta nodded. "Yes, Papa, that sounds good." They rose from the bed and together they checked that all tools sat safely on the low peg. The wood of the pegs felt dry and sturdy when Violeta brushed a hand along them. Outside the light had turned to deep blue, and inside a quiet glow began to take over. Violeta's damp hair had mostly dried by now, feeling lighter and softer. The whole scene stayed calm and unhurried. No rush marked their movements at all.

They walked slowly toward the door of the workshop but paused once more near the little bed. Violeta smoothed one corner of the turned-down sheet with a careful hand, enjoying the soft cool fabric there. The evening air drifted cooler through the door now. Papa rested a hand on Violeta's shoulder lightly. "You noticed carefully," he said. Violeta leaned a little into the touch, feeling safe. The floorboards creaked softly under their shifted weight, a friendly sound. Night birds called faintly from beyond the walls. Everything was in its place.

Violeta and Papa stepped back into the main part of the room for a last look around the workshop with its tools on the low peg and the bed waiting turned down. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Its warm light spread across the clean bench and the ready covers, making the space feel complete and kind. Violeta yawned a little, the sound small in the quiet. They felt the gentle temperature of the air on their skin and knew it was time to rest soon. Papa guided them toward home with a steady step. The calm of the evening held them both as they left the tools and the bed behind for the night.
