The sweet smell of pear skins drifted from the little bowl near the glue pots as Opal's busy hands pressed cool damp soil into neat furrows on the wooden workshop bench. Afternoon light poured across the smooth planks and made every grain of wood look golden. Opal's fingertips felt the tiny round seeds slip between them while labels waited in a tidy stack and the glue brush rested sticky-side up. Outside the open window the air stayed mild and still, and Opal kept their elbows tucked close so nothing would tip. They hummed a soft tune only they could hear and patted each little mound flat with careful pats. The workshop smelled clean and ready, full of quiet purpose, and Opal's shoulders stayed loose and happy as they worked.

Opal had already marked one long row with a neat paper strip that said beans in clear letters, and the next row waited for peas, yet the packets had somehow switched in the busy moment. Their palms stayed warm from holding the open seed envelopes, and the wooden edges of the bench felt solid and friendly under their wrists. A faint creak sounded when they leaned forward to reach the glue again. Uncle Idris moved at the far end sorting extra labels, his quiet footsteps soft on the floorboards. Opal felt the soil crumbs cling to their nails and brushed them off against their apron. The light shifted a little as a cloud passed, turning the whole bench the soft color of fresh toast for a moment.

For a short while Opal thought of sliding a spare label over the mix-up so no one would notice the peas resting where beans belonged. Their stomach fluttered once like a small moth, and they almost tucked the wrong packet out of sight behind the glue bottle. The air near their face felt cooler when they bent lower over the tray. Then Opal remembered how kind Uncle Idris always sounded, and the idea of hiding faded as quickly as it came. They straightened slowly, feeling the solid wood against their knees. Outside a gentle hush began among the leaves, and Opal took one calm breath that smelled faintly of the leftover pear skins still in the bowl.

Opal turned toward the taller figure and spoke in a clear steady voice. "Uncle Idris, I planted the pea seeds in the row marked beans by mistake," they said. The words came out plain and honest, and the workshop felt warmer right away. Uncle Idris set down his stack of tags and walked over with easy steps. "Thank you for telling me straightaway, Opal," he answered with a smile that reached his eyes. He rested one large hand lightly on the bench edge so the wood stayed steady for both of them. Opal felt relief loosen their shoulders like a soft blanket settling.

Together they began to mend the little rows with no hurry and no fuss at all. Uncle Idris showed Opal how to loosen the soil with a small wooden spoon so the seeds could lift free without harm. "We can place each one where it belongs and the garden will still grow fine," he said gently. Opal nodded and answered, "I almost hid it, but I wanted you to know the plain truth." Their voices stayed low and peaceful while the afternoon light turned a deeper gold across the labels and glue pots. The soil felt cool and crumbly again between Opal's fingers as they worked side by side. Quinn appeared in the doorway carrying a fresh cup of water for the tray and offered a quiet wave before settling on a stool to watch.

Opal reached into their pocket for a clean cloth and their fingers brushed a warm coin that had rested there since morning. The smooth metal felt comforting against their skin for a second before they drew out the cloth instead. They wiped a smear of glue from one corner of a label while Uncle Idris carefully resettled the pea seeds into their proper row. The wooden bench held steady under every small movement, and the air stayed mild enough that no one needed a sweater. Outside the wind in the fig leaves made a soft rushing sound like distant pages turning. Opal listened and smiled, glad the mistake was already becoming something neat and right again.

Quinn leaned closer and whispered, "The new labels look even better now," and Opal grinned back without any leftover worry. Uncle Idris handed Opal a fresh strip of paper and the glue brush so they could write peas in careful letters. The brush bristles felt springy and damp, and the ink smelled clean as it dried in the gentle light. They pressed the new label flat beside the corrected row and patted the soil one last time. Everything on the workshop bench of wood, glue, and labels looked orderly once more. Opal's hands moved slowly and surely, enjoying the simple work and the quiet company.

When the last seed sat snug in its true place, Opal stepped back and used one toe to straighten the corner of the braided rug that had curled near the bench legs. The rug felt rough yet friendly under their sock, and the whole floor seemed neater afterward. Uncle Idris washed the little spoon at the side sink while Quinn carried the water cup away. Soft shadows stretched longer across the wood as evening came closer, and the temperature inside the workshop stayed perfectly comfortable. Opal wiped their palms on the apron and felt only calm pride at having spoken truthfully and helped set things right.

Later they all sat for a moment on the low stools while the last light faded to blue. Opal rested their elbows on their knees and watched the tidy seed tray gleam faintly. Uncle Idris told a short cheerful story about a fig tree that once grew taller than the roof, and everyone listened with easy smiles. The workshop held the faint leftover scent of wood and clean soil. Opal felt sleepy in the best way, eyelids growing heavier with each peaceful breath. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
