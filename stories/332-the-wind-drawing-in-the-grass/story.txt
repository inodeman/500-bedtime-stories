The tin clock on the shelf ticked with a soft steady sound that filled the quiet room, and Greta stopped what they were doing to notice every gentle tick. Cool evening air slipped through the open window and touched Greta's face with a fresh feeling while the warm light from the table lamp made golden pools on the floorboards. Greta listened carefully and then looked over at Suri who waited nearby with quiet patience. "Do you hear how the tin clock keeps time so patiently?" Greta asked Suri in a low voice. The sound continued without hurry and Greta felt calm as they stood there in the comfortable temperature of the room before deciding it was a good moment to go outside for a short walk down toward the water.

Greta led the way down the path with Suri close beside them as the light of the setting sun painted long soft shadows across the ground. Along the way Greta bent and picked up a polished pebble that lay smooth among the others and felt its cool rounded texture rest perfectly in the center of their palm. Greta rolled the polished pebble slowly and enjoyed how fine and even it was under their fingertips while a mild breeze brushed their hair. "This pebble feels like a little moon in my hand," Greta said to Suri. They continued walking and the air stayed pleasantly warm as the harbor came into view ahead with its wide wall waiting.

When Greta and Suri reached the harbor wall the tide sat very low revealing dark wet sand and clusters of stones that gleamed under the slanted light. Greta climbed carefully onto the wide flat top of the wall and stood looking out over the quiet scene with Suri right beside them. Just beyond the far side of the wall a meadow of short green grass spread out in a gentle slope that the high water never quite covered because of the way the land lifted there above the usual tide line. The wind passed over that meadow and drew silver lines across the grass tips where the light caught the dew and made it shine like thin bright threads. Greta felt the remaining warmth of the day on their shoulders as they watched the living drawings form and change with every soft breath of air.

Greta stepped down from the wall a few paces so they could see the meadow more closely and the silver lines seemed even more delicate up near the green. A real wish rose in Greta to reach out and try to pocket one of those shining lines or to poke gently at them to feel if they would stay. But Greta stayed still and breathed in slowly instead. From the herb pots that lined the short walk to the landing the fresh smell of basil drifted over on the breeze and mixed with the sea air in a clean pleasant way. Suri stood next to Greta and waited to see what they would do while the soft light continued to make the silver shift and sparkle across the grass.

"Look carefully, Suri," Greta said in a hushed tone that matched the calm of the place. "The wind is drawing silver lines in the meadow right now. We can watch them without touching so they stay just as they are." Greta showed Suri how to lean forward a little but keep hands folded and eyes open wide to take in every change. Suri watched the lines move and reform and nodded in agreement. The grass itself made a whispery sound as the wind stroked it again and again in long gentle passes. Greta felt glad inside that they could share the sight this way while the temperature around them stayed gentle and even against their skin.

Just then the ferryman came along the harbor wall from where his boat rested on the exposed mud flats at low tide. His boots made soft sounds on the stone and he stopped when he saw Greta and Suri standing so quietly by the meadow edge. "What holds your attention so kindly this evening?" the ferryman asked with a friendly smile that reached his eyes. "The wind draws silver lines in the meadow and we are learning to look without disturbing them," Greta told him while pointing toward the shining grass. The last light of day gave a rosy glow to everything and touched the ferryman's face as well as Greta's hands while a cool edge began to enter the air around them all.

The three of them stood side by side in peaceful silence for several minutes while the wind continued its silver drawings across the green meadow grass without pause. Greta felt the soft movement of air against their neck and noticed how the fading light kept changing the brightness of each thin line. Suri shifted slightly closer and Greta put a steady hand on their friend's shoulder to keep the quiet mood steady and kind. No one reached toward the meadow or tried to take anything away from the place where it belonged. The temperature dropped just a little more but stayed comfortable as they all shared the same quiet sight of the moving light on the grass blades.

After a while Greta spoke again in the same gentle way that fitted the hour. "I wanted to pocket the silver or poke it at first but it is better to leave the wind's drawing right where it belongs," Greta said. Suri answered softly, "Yes it looks freer that way when no one touches." The ferryman nodded slowly and added in his deep calm voice, "You chose well to show how to look only and leave it be." They all took one last long look as the silver lines faded a bit with the fading light over the meadow. The stones under their feet held a leftover warmth that Greta could feel through their shoes as they prepared to go.

Greta Suri and the ferryman turned together and walked back along the harbor wall and up the path toward home as dusk settled fully around them with its gentle blue tones. Greta kept their hands free and open while they moved with slow easy steps that matched the slowing day. The walk felt peaceful and the air had grown cooler yet still kind on their faces and arms. When they reached the room again Greta sat by the familiar window and rested with Suri nearby in the hush. Soft shadows stretched across the floor in the growing quiet of evening. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Greta listened to the distant tiny sounds outside and felt ready for sleep with the silver lines still clear in their mind like a gentle lasting picture.
