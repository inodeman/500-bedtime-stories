Soft evening air slipped through the window and rested cool on the bedroom rug of cushions, puppets, and paper, making the paper roads flutter like quiet leaves. Eko sat cross-legged among the cushions and felt the mild temperature against their bare arms while they smoothed a paper path with careful hands. Papa sat nearby in the chair with a calm smile, watching without speaking yet. Eko picked up a small puppet and set it at the start of the paper road. “We can walk home together after the picnic on the cushions,” Eko said in a low voice that matched the peaceful room. The light from the window lay soft and golden over everything, turning the puppet faces gentle and ready.

Casper waited just outside the circle of cushions, shoes half on and eyes watching the paper roads that Eko had drawn. Eko noticed how the cooler air near the doorway touched Casper’s sleeves and how Casper’s fingers rested still on the edge of a cushion. The tin clock on the shelf ticked once with a clear bright sound that marked the quiet moment. Eko turned toward Casper and patted an empty cushion right beside their own. “Come sit here so the walk can wait for both of us,” Eko said. Casper stepped closer and settled onto the cushion, feeling its give under them, and the room stayed warm and steady around the two friends.

Eko decided the pretend walk home would not start until every shoe was ready, so they slowed the whole game and moved the puppets back to the beginning of the paper path. Papa nodded once from his chair and let the children arrange it themselves. Eko handed Casper a puppet whose cloth shoe still needed tying and showed how to loop the string slowly. The smell of orange peel rose from a small bowl on the low table where Papa had left a snack earlier, mixing sweetly with the air near the cushions. Eko breathed it in while they worked and said, “We will go only when your puppet’s shoe is on, Casper.” Casper smiled and finished the tiny knot with careful fingers.

The paper roads stretched between the cushions like gentle trails, and Eko placed more cushions so Casper’s spot stayed open and comfortable instead of leftover. Warm light from the lamp mixed with the last daylight and made the puppet clothes glow softly. Eko shifted a stack of paper so the path curved past Casper’s knee and said, “This way the walk belongs to both of us at the same time.” Casper answered, “Thank you, Eko, now I can keep up.” Papa remained quiet in his chair, hands folded, letting the two of them choose the new pace without any hurry from him.

Eko and Casper lined the puppets side by side on the starting cushion and practiced taking turns moving them one slow step at a time along the paper. The texture of a linen napkin that Papa had set nearby for snack time felt smooth when Eko brushed it while reaching for a puppet. Eko used the napkin to wipe a tiny crumb from the paper road so the path stayed clean for the journey. Cool air still drifted from the window and kept the room pleasant while the friends counted quiet steps together. “One step for you and one step for me,” Eko whispered, and Casper repeated the same words with a soft laugh.

They made the rules simpler so no one had to rush ahead: every puppet waited until both shoes were on and both children said ready. Eko moved their own cushion a little closer so shoulders almost touched and the game felt shared. Papa watched the change with quiet eyes and did not interrupt. The puppets traveled the paper road past crumpled hills of cushions, and the light stayed gentle across the floorboards. Eko felt happy warmth in their chest while they guided the slow parade and kept glancing at Casper to make sure the pace felt right.

Casper’s puppet finally stood ready with both tiny shoes tied, and Eko clapped once very softly so the sound stayed kind. “Now the walk home can begin,” Eko said, and they moved the puppets together at the new slower speed. Outside the window the evening air stayed mild and the curtains swayed once. Papa rose just enough to pour a little water into two cups and set them near the rug without a word, then returned to his chair. Eko and Casper sipped and tasted cool water while the puppets rested halfway along the paper path, everything calm and fair.

When the pretend walk reached the far cushion that stood for home, Eko and Casper arranged the puppets side by side on a soft landing place made of folded paper. Eko felt the cushion fabric under their palms and the steady temperature of the room around them. Casper leaned against Eko’s shoulder for a moment and the two stayed quiet together. Papa smiled wider now that the children had finished solving the start on their own. The game ended gently with the puppets tucked under a small blanket of paper, and Eko patted Casper’s knee once in thanks for waiting so patiently while everything became shared.

Eko and Casper stacked the remaining cushions into a low nest and rested there while the last light faded into softer tones across the walls. Papa dimmed the bright lamp and left only the smaller glow. Eko listened to the quiet room and felt the cool floor near their toes where the rug ended. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Eko closed their eyes for a moment beside Casper and knew the walk home had waited just long enough for everyone.
