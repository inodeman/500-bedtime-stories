The ferryman knelt beside his boat against the harbor wall at low tide, rubbing a soft cloth along the painted side in long careful strokes that left a faint sheen. Cosima walked closer over the sun-warmed stones and felt the pleasant heat soak through the soles of their shoes while a light breeze brushed their arms. The air held the quiet scent of warm bread drifting from a nearby basket the ferryman had brought for later, and Cosima breathed it in deeply as they stopped a few steps away. Zola hurried up beside Cosima, their steps light on the dry upper stones, and together they watched the ferryman work without interrupting him. The low water had left broad stretches of smooth rock shining in the light, and Cosima liked how the whole harbor felt open and peaceful under the clear sky.

Cosima noticed a small piece of lantern glass resting in a shallow hollow of the wall where the tide had left it. Sunlight poured straight through the clear curve and made the glass light up from within with a soft golden glow, even though no match had ever touched it. Cosima crouched down and felt the cool stone under their knees while the bright spot seemed almost alive with light. Zola leaned in close too, and Cosima heard the wind in the fig leaves rustling gently from the tree that grew just beyond the wall. The glowing glass sat quiet and still, catching every bit of the afternoon sun, and Cosima felt a small excited wish rise to pick it up or poke it just to see what would happen.

“Look how it shines all by itself,” Cosima said softly to Zola, keeping their hands folded in their lap. Zola nodded and whispered back, “It looks like a little sun sitting right there.” Cosima wanted very much to pocket the lantern glass or tap it with one finger, yet they stayed still and showed Zola how to lean close without touching. They pointed with an open hand held far back so the light stayed pure and undisturbed. The ferryman paused his work and turned toward them, the cloth still in his fingers, and the three of them watched the bright glass together while the air stayed mild and calm around their shoulders.

Cosima spoke again in a gentle voice. “We can see it best if we leave it just where it is.” The ferryman smiled and answered, “That is a fine way to look at something special.” Zola reached out as if to touch, then pulled their hand back when Cosima showed them once more how to cup their palms in the air near the glow without letting fingers meet the glass. Cosima felt proud of the careful choice and kept their own hands quiet against their knees. The light inside the lantern glass stayed steady and warm-looking, and Cosima noticed how the stones around it held a faint coolness from the earlier tide.

They all stayed quiet for a long moment, simply watching the sun make the glass shine. Cosima shifted a little and rested one hand against a cool ceramic rim of an old jar that someone had left on the wall nearby, feeling its smooth rounded edge under their fingertips. The texture grounded them while they kept gazing at the glowing lantern glass. Zola settled cross-legged on a flat stone, and the ferryman set his cloth aside so he could kneel at their level. No one hurried, and the golden light inside the glass seemed to grow even softer as a thin cloud drifted past the sun for a breath and then moved on.

“It feels like the glass is sharing the day with us,” Cosima said, their voice calm and happy. The ferryman nodded slowly and replied, “Some things shine brightest when we let them stay.” Cosima smiled at that and showed Zola again how to tilt their head just so, catching every sparkle without moving closer. The wish to pocket the lantern glass or poke it had been real, yet Cosima found it even better to leave the glow untouched for anyone else who might pass later. They listened to the small sounds of the harbor and felt content sitting side by side on the warm wall.

After a while Cosima stood and brushed the fine grit from their palms, still careful not to step near the hollow. Zola rose too, and the ferryman returned to his cloth with an easy motion. Cosima took one last long look at the lantern glass lighting up from the sun before any match, then turned with Zola to walk back along the stones. The ferryman called a quiet goodbye, and Cosima answered with a wave that felt light and free. They left the glowing glass exactly where it rested, knowing the tide would come and go and the sun would keep finding it for a time.

Later at home Cosima helped set the table while the rooms filled with gentle evening light. They carried an empty bowl from the table over to the counter and came back to sit near the window with Zola. The air inside felt soft and safe, and Cosima thought again of the harbor wall and the bright glass they had chosen to leave alone. Outside the sky turned a deeper blue, and Cosima felt sleepy and glad. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
