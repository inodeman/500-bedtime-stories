Cool evening air drifted through the open bakery window and brushed the warm counters like a soft breath, carrying the faint sweetness of cooling jam. Yuri stood on the wooden step stool in the middle of the kitchen and felt the gentle breeze tickle their cheeks while the last orange light of day slipped across the flour-dusted table. Dough rested under clean cloths in two big bowls, puffed and quiet, and little jars of ruby jam sat in a neat row waiting for their lids. Near the sink, a small wooden screen held a layer of paper pulp that Mama had mixed from soft scraps earlier, the wet fibers settling slowly into an even sheet. Yuri watched the pulp gleam under the low kitchen lamp and smelled the leftover warmth of baked bread still hanging in the room.

Yuri reached one careful finger toward the edge of the screen, wanting to lift the thin cloth that covered it so they could see if the pulp had finished settling. The cloth felt damp and cool under their fingertip, and the pulp beneath looked like a pale cloud resting in a shallow frame. “Mama, may I peek now?” Yuri asked in a hushed voice. Mama stood close by the counter, her hands resting lightly on a clean towel, and the soft yellow glow from the overhead light made her smile look warm. She shook her head gently and kept her voice calm. “Not yet, Yuri. The pulp needs its own slow time to settle flat and strong.” Yuri pulled their hand back and nodded, feeling the smooth wood of the stool under their bare feet.

The kitchen stayed peaceful while the dough continued its quiet rising and the jam cooled without a sound. Yuri looked again at the covered screen and felt a small wish to check just once more, yet they remembered Mama’s words and left the cloth right where it was. Instead Yuri turned toward the open drawer of polished spoons that shone under the lamp light. “I can line the spoons while we wait,” Yuri said softly. Mama nodded and answered, “That sounds like a lovely quiet task, Yuri.” The metal spoons felt cool and smooth as Yuri began to lift them one by one, and the air near the window carried a faint chill that made the whole room feel settled and safe.

Yuri worked slowly, placing the tiniest teaspoon first, then the next size, then the larger ones, until a neat silver line stretched across the counter like a little staircase of metal. Each spoon made a tiny bright clink when it touched the wood, a sound so soft it almost blended with the hush of the kitchen. The leather lace that tied Mama’s old recipe book closed brushed against Yuri’s wrist as they reached past it, feeling sturdy and worn smooth from many evenings of use. Yuri kept their movements careful so nothing would jostle the nearby screen of pulp. Mama watched from her place by the dough bowls and said, “Your line of spoons looks calm and ready, Yuri.” Yuri smiled and answered, “They are waiting just like the pulp.”

A soft meow floated from the doorway, and then the clear tiny ring of a cat’s collar bell sounded once as the kitchen cat wandered in and curled near the warm stove. Yuri glanced over and saw the cat’s eyes half closed in the gentle heat rising from the floor tiles. The pulp stayed covered and still, doing its slow work while Yuri finished adjusting the last spoon so every handle pointed the same way. The light in the room had turned a deeper gold, and the temperature near the window felt cooler than the cozy center of the kitchen. Yuri stood quietly for a moment and simply listened to the small sounds around them, feeling patient without needing to rush.

Mama stepped closer and rested a hand on Yuri’s shoulder, the fabric of her sleeve carrying the clean smell of soapy wool from the sweater she had washed that morning. Yuri leaned into the soft warmth and kept their eyes on the covered screen, trusting that the fibers inside were settling into place grain by grain. The dough under its cloths had risen higher now, round and ready for later, and the jam jars no longer sent up any steam. Yuri whispered, “I think the pulp is almost finished, Mama.” Mama replied in the same quiet tone, “We will know when the time is right, Yuri. Everything here finishes when it is ready.”

Yuri returned to the spoons for one last gentle check, sliding the middle one a finger-width so the row looked even under the lamp’s glow. The counter felt smooth and slightly cool beneath their palms, and the whole kitchen seemed wrapped in a peaceful hush broken only by the distant drip of a faucet and the cat’s soft breathing. Yuri felt glad they had left the pulp covered instead of lifting the cloth too soon. The paper fibers needed darkness and stillness the same way the dough needed its warm spot and the jam needed its cooling shelf. Yuri’s shoulders relaxed as they stood beside Mama and simply shared the quiet waiting.

After a long peaceful stretch of minutes the air near the screen felt different, drier and lighter, and Mama nodded toward it with a small smile. Yuri carefully lifted one corner of the covering cloth and saw that the pulp had settled into a smooth pale sheet, no longer wet and shifting but firm enough to hold its shape. “It finished all by itself,” Yuri said with quiet delight. Mama answered, “Yes, Yuri, it took the time it needed.” They left the new paper sheet resting safely on its screen so it could dry the rest of the way while the dough and jam waited for morning. Yuri climbed down from the stool and felt the cool floor tiles under their feet as they walked with Mama toward the hallway.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Yuri and Mama paused together in that gentle light, listening to the quiet kitchen settle around the finished pulp, the risen dough, and the cooled jam, while the evening air drifted in one last cool breath and the cat’s soft fur brushed Yuri’s ankle before everyone headed toward bed.
