Miss Dalca was already kneeling beside the garden row when Yael stepped onto the warm soil that afternoon. Soft light spilled through the bean leaves in shifting patches and rested on Yael's shoulders like a light blanket. Miss Dalca's fingers traced the taut string between the wooden stakes while two watering cans waited side by side, their cool metal rims glinting. The mild air carried a gentle scent of toasted sesame from the small cloth pouch tucked near her knee. Yael crouched close and watched the careful way Miss Dalca straightened each loop. “These plants like a steady guide,” Miss Dalca said, her voice even and low. Yael nodded and felt the earth crumble softly under their palms as they settled in to help.

Yael noticed the wide rain barrel lid leaning against the far stake, its smooth surface pale in the filtered glow. It was not heavy at all, only broad and a little clumsy to hold alone. Beside it the two watering cans stood ready, and the string waited for its final fastening. Yael ran one hand along the lid’s edge and felt how the wood had dried into a pleasant grain. A quiet breeze moved the nearby leaves and made a soft rustle that mixed with the distant click of a gate latch closing somewhere beyond the fence. “We could rest the cans on this lid so they stay steady while we water,” Yael said. Miss Dalca smiled and kept her eyes on the string.

Idris arrived along the path with slow steps and joined Yael beside the awkward lid. The three of them looked at the wide circle of wood that needed to settle flat across the row without tipping the string or the cans. Warm sunlight touched Idris’s hair and then slipped across Yael’s wrists. “If we hurry it will wobble and bump everything,” Idris murmured. Yael agreed at once. The lid smelled faintly of clean rainwater still clinging to its underside, and Yael breathed it in while deciding. Miss Dalca rose and smoothed the front of her cotton apron, the fabric soft and familiar under her hand.

Yael and Idris chose two slow jobs instead of one fast rush. Yael would hold the near edge of the lid at waist height while Idris guided the far edge into place above the string. The air felt pleasantly cool against Yael’s bare arms as they lifted together. “We go only as far as our count,” Yael said clearly. Idris answered, “Yes, and we stop whenever either of us needs a breath.” Miss Dalca stood ready but did not take the lid; she simply watched with quiet attention. The watering cans shone nearby like twin silver moons on the ground.

They began the first lift with careful knees bent. Yael felt the wood press evenly into both palms while Idris matched the height on the other side. A soft warmth rose from the soil and brushed Yael’s ankles. Miss Dalca offered only a gentle count: “One for the left, two for the right, pause.” Her voice floated like the light itself. Yael and Idris moved the lid a short distance and set it down again to rest. The cotton apron brushed lightly against the stake as Miss Dalca stepped closer to name the knot they would need next. “This is a simple half hitch, easy and sure,” she said.

After the pause Yael and Idris lifted once more, still slow. The lid floated between them without a single wobble because neither person hurried. Light danced across the wet beads that clung to the wood and made tiny rainbows for a moment. Yael kept their elbows soft and their breathing steady. Idris hummed a quiet note that matched the pace. When the lid hovered exactly above the string, Miss Dalca spoke the count again so they could lower it without a bump. The two watering cans waited untouched and ready.

With the lid resting safely across the row, Yael knelt and held one end of the string while Idris looped the half hitch Miss Dalca had named. The knot slid smooth and firm under their fingers. A cooler shadow fell across Yael’s back as a cloud drifted past the sun, and the change in temperature felt soothing. “It sits just right,” Yael whispered. Idris answered, “We did each part without rush.” Miss Dalca only nodded and kept her hands free, letting the children finish the work they had chosen together. The garden row now looked tidy and calm.

They filled each watering can in turn and set them on the steady lid so no spill reached the new knot. Yael poured a thin silver stream along the base of the nearest plant and watched the water darken the soil in a slow circle. The sound of the gate latch clicked once more in the distance, soft and final. Idris watered the next plant while Yael held the can steady. Miss Dalca stood nearby with the cotton apron still smooth over her dress, offering no more than a quiet smile. When the last drop fell, Yael and Idris set both cans upright again side by side. Everything felt finished and peaceful.

Later Yael carried the empty pouch that still held a trace of toasted sesame into the quiet house while Idris walked beside them. Miss Dalca followed and helped Yael set a small chair square to the table so the three of them could sit. The room held a gentle hush. Outside the garden row rested under the evening sky with its string, its lid, and its two cans all in place. Yael felt the day’s warmth lingering on their skin and the soft satisfaction of work shared without hurry. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
