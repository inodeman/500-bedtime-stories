"Ines, would you like a one-minute story about feeling a little lost inside a very kind store?" Auntie Luz asked as the lamp poured warm yellow light across the bedroom quilt and the wooden floor. Ines sat propped on two pillows and felt the cool evening air drift across their bare ankles while they nodded yes, even though bigger news already pressed against their teeth and wanted to spill out first. The soft beeswax scent still hung near the nightstand where Auntie Luz had pinched out the little candle earlier. Ines tucked the blanket edge under their chin and watched Auntie Luz settle into the low chair, the light turning her silver hair strands to quiet gold. Outside the open window a gentle hush moved through the dark garden. Ines wanted to jump in with their own news right then, yet the calm way Auntie Luz folded her hands made them hold still. The pillowcase felt smooth and cool under Ines’s cheek as they waited. Auntie Luz smiled and began in a voice no louder than a shared secret, and Ines stayed quiet though their toes wiggled under the covers.

Auntie Luz said the story took place on an ordinary afternoon when a child very much like Ines stepped into a store that smelled of fresh paper and polished wood and felt as friendly as a neighbor’s kitchen. The child wandered past tall shelves of soft scarves and bright buttons and then turned a corner and suddenly could not see the door or the grown-up who had come inside with them. Soft overhead lights glowed on the child’s hair and made every aisle look the same for one brief minute. Ines listened with both ears and felt the mattress springs give a tiny friendly creak each time they shifted. Auntie Luz kept her voice slow and steady so the feeling of being lost stayed small and safe. Ines almost spoke up about the snail they had watched earlier, but they pressed their lips together and let the tale keep unfolding. The room stayed warm around Ines’s shoulders like a second blanket. Auntie Luz described how the child stood still and breathed and looked at the floorboards that shone like honey in the store light.

Then Auntie Luz told how a kind shopkeeper with a green apron noticed the child right away and walked over without hurry. “Are you looking for someone?” the shopkeeper asked in the story, and the child’s shoulders dropped with relief. Ines heard Auntie Luz’s real voice shape those gentle words and felt the cool edge of the sheet brush their wrist. The child in the story nodded and pointed toward the front of the store, and the shopkeeper simply held out a hand and walked with them past the soft scarves again until the grown-up came into view near the door. Ines kept listening all the way, even though their own news felt bright and ready. Warm lamp light rested on Ines’s knees and made the blanket look like melted butter. Auntie Luz paused for half a breath so Ines could picture the kind store clearly, and the quiet filled the bedroom like soft cotton. Outside, wind in the fig leaves made a low shushing sound that matched the peaceful end of the little tale.

Auntie Luz finished the one-minute story by saying the child and the grown-up waved thank you to the shopkeeper and stepped back into the sunny street together, no longer lost at all. Ines still held their own words inside and felt the cool night air touch the tips of their ears. The whole short story had lasted only as long as it takes to count slowly to sixty, yet it left a calm shape in the room. Ines watched Auntie Luz’s hands rest open on her knees and noticed how the lamp light caught the silver ring she always wore. Ines wanted to interrupt with bigger news about the garden path, but they remembered the child in the store had been found because someone listened and walked slowly. The pillow under Ines’s head stayed cool and smooth. Auntie Luz looked at Ines and waited without rushing, the quiet stretching gently between them like a soft scarf.

Ines took one deep breath of the room’s warm air and asked their real question in a clear small voice. “Auntie Luz, what did the kind store smell like inside, besides the paper?” Auntie Luz smiled and answered that it smelled a little like clean cotton and a little like the lemon polish on the wooden counters. Ines nodded and felt the textured weave of the blanket under their fingertips as they thought about that answer. The lamp’s glow stayed steady and gold on the wall beside the bed. Ines still kept their own small news tucked away for one more moment. Auntie Luz reached over and smoothed a wrinkle from the quilt near Ines’s elbow, her hand warm and light. The bedroom felt snug and safe around both of them while the question settled. Ines listened to the answer all the way to its last word before they prepared to speak.

Only then did Ines share something small of their own. “I watched a snail cross one whole brick on the garden path today,” Ines said, and their voice sounded quiet and pleased. Auntie Luz leaned closer so the lamp light fell across both their faces together. Ines described how the snail moved so slowly that they could count every soft push of its body, and how the brick felt warm from the afternoon sun when they touched it lightly with one fingertip. The air in the bedroom had grown a little cooler near the window, yet the bed stayed cozy under Ines. Auntie Luz listened without adding a single extra word until Ines finished. Ines felt glad they had waited through the whole store story first. The quiet after their own small news felt round and complete, like a pebble resting in a pocket.

Auntie Luz asked if Ines wanted to hold one of the smooth fig leaves she had brought in earlier from the tree outside. Ines took the leaf and turned it over carefully, feeling a leaf's soft underside against their thumb while the lamp light showed the pale green veins. The texture surprised Ines in a gentle way, cooler and silkier than the top side. Auntie Luz said the leaf had come from the same tree whose branches brushed the bedroom wall. Ines traced the veins slowly and smelled the clean green scent that rose from the leaf when they held it close. The bedroom stayed quiet except for their soft breathing. Ines placed the leaf on the nightstand beside the snuffed candle so it could rest overnight. Auntie Luz smiled and tucked the blanket higher around Ines’s shoulders with careful hands.

Ines wriggled deeper into the pillows and felt the mattress hold them the way a nest holds a bird. Auntie Luz began to hum a tune without words, low and slow, and the sound mixed with the faint hush still coming through the window. Ines closed their eyes halfway and watched the gold lamp light dance behind their lashes like tiny stars. The air near their face felt cooler now, carrying a clean night freshness. Auntie Luz stayed in the chair and kept humming until Ines’s breathing slowed. Ines thought once more of the kind store and the snail on the brick and felt both pictures sitting peacefully side by side inside their mind. The quilt grew heavier and warmer across Ines’s chest as sleep began to gather at the edges of the room. Auntie Luz whispered that tomorrow they could look for more snails together if the path was dry.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Ines opened their eyes one last time and saw Auntie Luz standing to leave, the lantern’s gentle light painting a pale circle on the floorboards and the wall. Ines felt the cool pillow under their cheek and the warm weight of the blanket over their legs while Auntie Luz’s footsteps whispered across the room. The lantern light stayed steady and kind, just bright enough to keep the dark from feeling empty. Ines’s eyelids grew heavy again and the last sound they heard was the quiet click of the bedroom door closing. The air smelled clean and still. Ines drifted toward sleep with the one-minute story and their own small snail news resting together like two friendly hands.
