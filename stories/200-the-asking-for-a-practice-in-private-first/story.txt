The gentle tick from the shelf reached Jorie first, a thin metal sound that marked each second like a quiet friend. Jorie noticed it right away and sat still on the low cushion, listening while the evening air felt pleasantly cool against their bare arms. Soft yellow light from the side lamp painted the floorboards in long stripes, and Jorie watched the stripes shift when a curtain stirred. The music corner waited just ahead, its wooden edges smooth under Jorie’s palm as they scooted closer. Auntie Luz hummed somewhere in the next room, and the humming mixed with the clock in a way that made Jorie’s shoulders drop. They breathed in and out, feeling ready to stay right there.

Jorie rested one hand on the round drum that sat in the corner and felt the tight skin give a little under their fingers. The recorder lay beside it on a folded cloth, its wooden body warm from the lamp. Jorie picked up the recorder and turned it slowly, listening to the faint hollow note that escaped when their breath brushed the mouthpiece. The corner smelled clean like polished wood, and the cool floor kept Jorie’s toes comfortable. They set the recorder down again and tapped the drum once, soft enough that the sound stayed close. Everything in the music corner seemed to wait patiently for whatever Jorie decided next.

A bright scent of orange peel drifted from the small table near the doorway where Auntie Luz had left a snack earlier. Jorie breathed it in and smiled because the citrus smell made the whole corner feel friendly. They looked at the drum and the recorder again and felt the shyness rise like a small warm wave in their chest. Jorie wanted to try the songs alone before any showing, yet the words sat heavy on their tongue. The lamp light stayed steady and golden across the instruments, and Jorie traced a circle on the drum skin while the orange scent lingered. They knew Auntie Luz would understand if they could only speak.

Auntie Luz stepped into the doorway with quiet feet and a kind face. “Jorie, I see you found the music corner,” she said in a voice as soft as the lamp glow. Jorie nodded and kept one hand on the drum. The air between them felt still and safe, and Jorie could hear their own breathing mix with the distant kitchen quiet. Auntie Luz waited without rushing, her hands folded loosely. Jorie swallowed and felt the cool floor under their heels while they gathered the smallest bit of courage. The light stayed gentle on Auntie Luz’s smile, inviting without pressing.

Jorie’s voice came out clear enough to hear, though it stayed gentle. “Auntie Luz, may I have one private practice with the drum and the recorder before any showing?” they asked, looking right at her. Auntie Luz’s eyes crinkled happily. “Of course you may, Jorie. Take all the quiet time you need,” she answered. Jorie felt relief spread through their arms like warm tea. The corner seemed brighter for a moment as the lamp light caught the shiny edge of the drum. Jorie thanked her with a soft “Thank you” and watched Auntie Luz step back without any push toward a performance.

Alone now in the music corner, Jorie lifted the recorder and blew a long slow note that floated low and sweet. They switched to the drum and tapped a steady pattern that matched their heartbeat. The wooden floor held a comfortable coolness under Jorie’s crossed legs, and the instruments answered every careful touch. Jorie practiced the short song twice, then three times, letting each sound settle before the next. No one watched, and that freedom made the notes feel freer too. Jorie’s fingers moved with growing ease across the recorder holes and the drum skin.

After a while Jorie set both instruments down and noticed a folded linen napkin resting on the nearby stool. They picked it up and rubbed the crisp woven texture between their fingers, enjoying how the cloth felt both smooth and slightly bumpy. The texture grounded them while they sat quietly and listened to their own calm breathing. Soft light continued to pool around the music corner, and Jorie folded the napkin neatly again. They felt proud of the private practice without needing anyone else to say so. The whole room seemed to hum with the leftover notes.

Jorie stayed a few more minutes, tapping one last soft rhythm on the drum just for themselves. The temperature in the corner remained pleasantly cool, and the lamp light never flickered. They thought of Auntie Luz waiting kindly in the other room and felt glad they had asked out loud. Eitan might come tomorrow to hear the song, but tonight belonged only to this quiet corner. Jorie whispered “I did it” to the empty space and smiled at the drum. Everything felt settled and complete inside the golden light.

Jorie rose slowly, smoothed the cushion, and walked toward the hallway with light steps. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Auntie Luz met them with open arms and another gentle “Well done, Jorie,” and Jorie answered “I liked the private time.” Together they left the music corner behind for sleep, the cool evening air following them down the hall while the last orange scent faded and the instruments rested safely.
