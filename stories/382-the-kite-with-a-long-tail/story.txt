A tiny drip sounded from the faucet in the still bakery kitchen. Remy heard it right away and turned toward the deep sink, watching one clear drop fall and land with a soft plink that seemed louder than usual in the early hush. Pale blue light from the high window rested cool on Remy’s cheeks as they stood beside the long wooden counter before any tables had been pulled out for the day. The air held a gentle chill that made Remy tuck their sleeves closer, and they breathed in the quiet while Ms. Okonkwo checked the ovens with careful steps. Remy pressed a palm to the polished counter and felt the smooth grain under their skin, glad for this calm moment before the bakery woke fully.

Remy noticed the long kite tail of bright bows lying carefully across a clean baking sheet near the flour bins, brought inside so the silk ribbons would stay neat while the morning bread rose. Each bow needed to be tied the same distance apart along the trailing string, or the whole tail would look uneven when it flew later beside the bakery window for the spring display. Remy touched one silky bow and felt how light it was, then glanced at Gio who had just arrived still rubbing sleep from their eyes. The kitchen smelled faintly of warm dough already, and Remy’s shoulders eased as they looked at the neat row of bows waiting for careful hands.

“We could each take half of the string and work slowly,” Remy said to Gio in a voice no louder than the low oven hum. Gio nodded and answered, “Yes, you start from this end and I will start from the other so we meet in the middle.” Ms. Okonkwo glanced over from the mixing bowls and offered only a gentle count under her breath while she measured mint leaves for the morning tea cakes, her calm tone steady as a clock. Remy felt the cool air still touching their wrists and smiled because the plan felt easy when shared. The pale light shifted a little brighter across the flour-dusted floor as they began.

Remy and Gio split the kite tail into two slow jobs, Remy holding one end steady while Gio measured the first equal space with a small wooden spoon for a guide. They worked side by side without hurry, knowing a rush would make the bows crowd together or stretch too far. Remy whispered the distances as they tied, and the ribbons made a soft rustle like dry leaves each time a bow settled into place. Warmth from the nearest oven reached Remy’s knees in a friendly wave, and they kept their fingers patient on the string. Gio’s quiet breathing matched Remy’s own, and the kitchen stayed peaceful around them.

Ms. Okonkwo paused beside them long enough to name the knot they needed. “That one is called a double loop,” she said kindly, then returned to her bowls without taking over. Remy practiced the double loop once on a spare scrap and felt how the ribbon slid smoothly before it locked snug. Gio tried the same knot on their half and grinned when it held. Remy noticed the faint scent of mint leaves rising from the cutting board where Ms. Okonkwo had just chopped them for the cakes, and the fresh green smell drifted gently past as they tied the next bow. The equal spaces began to look even and true under their careful hands.

A glass bead at the very tip of the kite string caught Remy’s finger as they smoothed the last stretch of ribbon; it felt cool and perfectly round against their skin, smooth as a tiny frozen drop of water. Remy showed it to Gio with a quiet smile, and they both made sure the bead hung free so it could catch sunlight later. They kept measuring with the spoon, counting the same distance each time so nothing went crooked. Soft golden light now filled more of the kitchen, warming the air around Remy’s ears while the ovens sighed. Remy and Gio stayed close, passing the string between them whenever one section was ready.

Remy heard the light tinkle of a cat’s collar bell as the bakery cat wandered in from the back hallway and settled near the warm oven bricks. The gentle sound chimed once more when the cat stretched, then fell quiet again. Remy kept tying, feeling calm because the work was shared and no one needed to hurry. Gio finished their last bow at the same moment Remy finished theirs, and the two halves of the kite tail met perfectly in the middle with every bow spaced the same. Ms. Okonkwo looked over and gave a small nod of approval while she wiped her hands on her apron. Remy felt the counter’s cool surface still under one elbow and knew the tail was ready.

Together Remy and Gio lifted the finished kite tail and laid it carefully along the clean counter so the bows rested flat and bright. Remy ran one finger along the even spaces and felt proud of the steady work they had done side by side. Gio helped coil the string loosely so it would not tangle before the display later. The kitchen air had grown a little warmer now, soft against Remy’s neck, and the pale light turned golden across the bowls of rising dough. Ms. Okonkwo set out the early tablecloths nearby without a word, simply smiling at the neat row of bows. Remy breathed slowly and felt the quiet joy of finishing something beautiful with a friend.

Remy and Gio stood together a moment longer, looking at the kite tail that now hung straight and balanced because they had refused to rush. Remy touched one final bow and felt the silk cool and smooth under their fingertip. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Outside the sky lightened another shade, and the bakery kitchen filled with the gentle promise of the day ahead while Remy rested both hands on the counter and smiled.
