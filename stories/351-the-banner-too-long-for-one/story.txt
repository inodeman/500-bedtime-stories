The warm smell of cedar drifted through the school hall as Nils’s small hands already smoothed the folded edge of a long blue banner that stretched across the floor like a quiet river of cloth. Soft afternoon light slanted through the high windows and warmed the dust motes that danced near their busy fingers. Nils pressed the fabric flat again and again, feeling how cool and smooth it lay against their palms while the gentle bustle of preparing the fair went on all around. Empty baskets waited on tables, paper flowers rested in open boxes, and the air held a calm hush broken only by quiet footsteps. Grandpa Sol sat on a low stool nearby, sorting ribbons with slow care. Nils looked along the banner’s full length and saw at once that it was far too long for one person to lift without the middle sagging down to touch the ground. “This needs careful hands,” Nils whispered to the cloth itself, their voice nearly lost in the soft shuffle of shoes on wood.

Nils kept one hand on the banner while they stood and studied the bright hall. Sunlight painted pale gold squares across the floorboards and made the blue cloth glow like shallow water. They knew that if anyone rushed the hanging, the whole length would twist or droop and the fair decorations would look tumbled instead of neat. Dori came walking over with quiet steps, carrying a small coil of soft string, and the light caught the freckles on their cheeks. “Nils, that banner is longer than both of us put together,” Dori said, smiling as they set the string down. Nils nodded and touched the cool fabric once more. The two friends looked at each other and understood without hurry that the work wanted slow hands and shared lifting, nothing fast.

Together Nils and Dori decided to treat the banner as two calm jobs instead of one hurried one. Nils took the left half and began to gather the cloth into gentle loops while Dori took the right half and did the same. Cool air moved through an open side door and brushed their bare arms with a pleasant chill. “We can walk it up together when both ends are ready,” Nils said, keeping their voice low and steady. They moved the loops with careful fingers so no crease stayed sharp. Grandpa Sol watched from his stool and offered no hurry, only a quiet presence that made the hall feel safe and measured. The banner never left the floor yet, but already it looked more ready.

When the loops were even, Nils and Dori lifted their ends at the same moment and carried the long cloth toward the far wall where hooks waited high above a doorway. The temperature near the open windows felt cooler still, and the cloth brushed lightly against Nils’s knees with each step. They walked slowly so the middle stayed clear of the ground. “Count with us if you like, Grandpa Sol,” Nils called softly. Grandpa Sol smiled and spoke in his warm gravelly voice. “One slow step, two slow steps, three,” he counted, naming the pace they already kept. The counting helped the two friends match each other without strain, and the banner rose like a soft blue sky being lifted into place.

Halfway to the wall they paused to rest the middle of the banner across a low bench so it would not drag. Nils felt the rough weave of a straw mat under their bare feet where someone had set a resting spot for helpers, and the dry texture grounded them while they breathed. Dori held their end steady and waited. Grandpa Sol came closer, still holding a length of soft ribbon. “The knot you need is called a double loop,” he said gently, showing the shape with his fingers without taking over the work. Nils watched closely and practiced the motion in the air first. The hall remained filled with quiet light and the soft sounds of other fair preparations far across the room.

Nils and Dori raised the banner the rest of the way, sliding the ends onto the waiting hooks with care. Outside, the sound of wind in the fig leaves drifted in through the open windows like a soft whisper, and Nils smiled at how peaceful it felt. They stepped back together and checked that the middle hung evenly. “It looks just right now,” Dori said, their words calm and glad. Nils reached up once more to smooth a small wrinkle near the left hook. Grandpa Sol nodded and handed them the ribbon so they could finish the double-loop knots themselves. Nils tied the left side slowly while Dori tied the right, each knot firm but not tight, each movement matched to the other.

With the banner secure, Nils and Dori stepped farther back to see the whole hall. Warm light now poured more golden through the western windows and lit the blue cloth from below so it seemed to float. Tables waited for jars of jam and baskets of apples that would arrive later. Nils’s hands still remembered the cool slide of the fabric, and they wiped a tiny thread from their sleeve. “We finished it without any rush,” Nils said, glancing at Dori with quiet pride that needed no loud words. Grandpa Sol set a small wooden cup of water on a nearby shelf and Nils turned the cup so the handle faced the room, making it easy for the next helper to find. Everything in the hall felt settled and ready.

They spent a few more minutes walking the edges of the space, straightening a paper flower here and shifting a basket there. The air had grown a touch cooler as the sun lowered, and Nils felt the pleasant change on their cheeks and bare arms. Dori hummed a soft tune that matched the earlier counting. Grandpa Sol packed the leftover ribbons into a neat box and closed the lid with a gentle click. Nils looked once more at the long blue banner hanging straight and true above the doorway. No part of it touched the floor now, and the double-loop knots held firm. The friends had shared every step, and the work felt complete in the kindest way.

Later the hall grew quieter still as the last helpers finished their small tasks and the light turned softer and pinker near the windows. Nils sat for a moment on the low stool beside Grandpa Sol, feeling the smooth wood under their hands and the fading warmth of the day on their shoulders. Dori rested nearby, and all three shared a peaceful silence filled only by the gentle settling of the building. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Nils leaned their head against Grandpa Sol’s arm and watched the blue banner hang steady in the quiet air, knowing the gentle fair would open tomorrow under its calm length, and their eyelids grew heavy with the good tiredness that follows slow shared work.
