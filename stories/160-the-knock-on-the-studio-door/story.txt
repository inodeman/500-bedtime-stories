A soft evening breeze drifted through the open window and touched the music corner with a cool, gentle hush that made the wooden floorboards feel smooth under Eko’s bare feet. Eko settled cross-legged beside the round drum and the slender recorder, listening to the quiet room while the breeze carried a faint freshness across their cheeks and the warm lamp light pooled in a golden circle around them. Auntie Luz sat nearby on a low cushion, her presence calm and steady, and Eko watched the way the light made the drum skin glow like soft sand. The air felt pleasant and still, perfect for small sounds and quiet thoughts, and Eko rested their hands on their knees while the breeze kept stroking the space with its kind touch.

Eko ran one careful finger along the smooth wooden rim of the drum and noticed how the recorder lay waiting on a folded cloth, its holes dark and round in the gentle lamp glow. The music corner smelled faintly sweet just then because Auntie Luz had peeled an orange earlier and left the bright orange peel on a little plate near the window sill, filling the air with its clean citrus scent that made Eko’s nose tingle happily. Eko breathed in slowly and felt the quiet settle deeper into their chest. Auntie Luz smiled without rushing anything, and the light stayed soft across the floorboards as Eko stayed close to the instruments, enjoying the peaceful weight of the evening around them.

A soft knock sounded against the studio door that stood already ajar, the wood giving a low, friendly tap that floated into the music corner without startling anyone. Eko’s shoulders lifted a little at the unexpected sound, and they felt a warm flush of shyness rise in their cheeks while the lamp light stayed steady and kind. The door was only partly open, showing a thin slice of the hallway beyond, and Eko looked at it while the cool evening air continued to drift past. Auntie Luz glanced toward the door but did not move quickly, giving Eko space to notice the knock and the way it made the quiet room feel suddenly a little wider.

Eko pressed their palms against the cool wooden floor and felt shy about the knock on the studio door that was already ajar, wishing for a moment that the sound had not come at all. Their heart beat a gentle extra thump, and they looked down at the drum instead of the door while the soft light from the lamp made everything look safe and ordinary. Auntie Luz stayed right where she was, her voice low and warm when she finally spoke. “Someone is saying hello very softly,” Auntie Luz said. “We can answer whenever we feel ready, Eko.” Eko nodded without words yet, still feeling the shyness like a small warm blanket around their shoulders, and the room stayed quiet enough to hear the tiny tick of a tin clock resting on the shelf above the recorder.

Eko listened to that steady tin clock ticking once, twice, three times, and the familiar sound helped their breath slow down again while Auntie Luz waited with kind patience and never asked for a big show. The light from the lamp touched the edge of a linen napkin folded beside the orange peel plate, and Eko reached out to feel its dry, slightly rough weave under their fingertips, the simple texture calming them further. Casper, who had been sitting quietly on the far cushion with a picture book, looked up and smiled a small smile that asked nothing. Eko felt the shyness still present but softer now, like a cloud that had thinned, and they kept one hand on the linen napkin while they thought about the ajar door and the gentle knock that had already happened.

Eko decided to try the smallest brave version they could manage, a voice that could be heard without needing to be loud or perfect. They stood up slowly, feeling the cool floor under their feet again, and took three quiet steps toward the studio door that remained ajar. “Hello,” Eko said in a clear but gentle tone that carried just far enough. “We are in the music corner.” Auntie Luz’s eyes shone with quiet pride that asked for nothing more, and she answered softly, “That was a fine hello, Eko.” Casper added from the cushion, “I heard you really well,” and Eko felt the warmth of those words settle around them like another soft blanket while the lamp light continued to glow steadily across the drum and recorder.

Eko stayed near the door for a moment longer and listened, but no further knock came, only the peaceful hush of the evening and the distant quiet of the hallway beyond. They returned to the music corner and sat once more beside the drum, resting one hand lightly on its smooth top while the cool air from the window brushed their hair. Auntie Luz picked up the recorder and held it out without pressure. “Would you like to blow one soft note, or shall we just sit?” she asked. Eko considered and chose to sit, feeling glad that no one pushed, and they answered, “Just sit is good right now.” The room accepted that choice completely, and Eko felt the last bits of shyness melt into ordinary comfort while Casper turned a page in the picture book with a quiet rustle.

Eko and Auntie Luz and Casper stayed together in the calm circle of light, and Eko let their fingers drum a single slow tap on the drum skin, listening to the low warm sound it made against the quiet. The orange peel scent had faded into the background, the tin clock had already done its friendly work, and the linen napkin still rested nearby where Eko could see its folded edges. Nothing needed to be bigger or louder than it already was. Auntie Luz poured a little water into a cup and set it within easy reach, and Eko took a small sip that tasted clean and cool. The evening felt complete in its gentleness, and Eko leaned their shoulder lightly against Auntie Luz’s arm, enjoying the steady warmth there while Casper hummed a tiny made-up tune that barely rose above a whisper.

Eko yawned a slow yawn and stretched their arms above their head before settling back down, ready for the quiet that came before sleep. Auntie Luz smoothed a stray bit of hair from Eko’s forehead with a careful hand, and Casper closed the picture book with a soft thump that sounded friendly. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Eko watched that mild light for a long peaceful minute, feeling the cool night air still drifting in, and they closed their eyes while the drum and recorder rested nearby, the whole music corner wrapped in safety and the memory of a small brave hello that had been just right.
