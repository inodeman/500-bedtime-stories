The painted pebble rested in the middle of a bright square of sun on the wide wooden counter of the bakery kitchen, its blue waves and yellow dots catching the light so that each color seemed to breathe. Warmth rose from the stone in a gentle pool that touched Brisa’s cheeks when they leaned near, and the air carried the slow heat of the low oven. Brisa’s eyes stayed fixed on the tiny painted shorelines, noticing how the sun made the edges of the paint soft and almost glowing. A faint dusting of flour lay around the pebble like pale snow, and Brisa could hear the quiet settling of dough in its bowl not far away. They held their breath for a moment just to study the way the light turned the pebble into a small sunny island of its own.

Mama stood at the far end of the counter, her hands resting lightly on a cloth that covered a mound of dough still finishing its rise, while jars of dark jam waited with their lids set loosely aside. The kitchen felt cozy and still, filled with the mild warmth that came from the cooled baking stones. Brisa turned toward Mama and said, “I want to check the painted pebble now to see if the colors have set.” Mama smiled and answered in a soft voice, “It needs the sun a little longer, Brisa, so leave it covered for now.” Brisa nodded slowly, feeling the cool air near the window brush their wrists, and they looked once more at the square of light holding the pebble safe.

Brisa found a clean square of thin paper and lowered it carefully over the painted pebble so the sun could still reach through in a gentle way. Their fingertips brushed a paper edge that felt finely ridged and dry, and they smoothed it flat without pressing hard. The covering settled like a quiet tent, and Brisa stepped back, the kitchen air warm against their arms. Mama watched with kind eyes as Brisa moved away from the counter. “I can wait until it is truly ready,” Brisa said, and the words felt calm in the golden light.

Instead of lifting the paper, Brisa walked over to the side table where a small tin clock sat ticking with a clear, steady sound that marked each moment without hurry. They stood still and listened, letting the rhythm settle into their breathing while the light from the high window fell across the floor in long soft stripes. Mama poured water into the kettle and set it on the quiet stove, and Brisa breathed in and out in time with the first faint tick of heat beginning inside the metal. The kitchen smelled clean and yeasty, and Brisa’s shoulders dropped as they matched their breath to the gentle pace around them.

Anouk had left a small wool scarf hanging on the chair earlier after walking through the morning damp, and now the scarf gave off a mild scent of wet wool that drifted faintly when Brisa passed. Brisa picked up a wooden spoon and began to stir the waiting jam in slow circles, feeling the thick sweetness pull against the spoon. The texture of the jam was smooth and cool at first, warming only a little as they turned it. Mama said, “That is a good quiet task, Brisa,” and Brisa answered, “I like helping the jam finish while the pebble stays in its sun.” The spoon made soft scraping sounds against the jar, and Brisa kept their eyes on the deep red swirl rather than the covered counter.

Time moved in the easy way of a bakery kitchen in late afternoon, with dough rising under its cloth and jam settling into its final shine. Brisa wiped a drop of jam from the rim with a clean corner of cloth, noticing how the cloth felt slightly rough under their thumb. The square of sun on the counter had shifted only a little, still holding the covered pebble in its warm place. Brisa felt the mild heat from the nearby oven on their bare knees and stayed close to Mama without rushing. They thought of the painted blue waves waiting patiently beneath the paper and decided the pebble could take all the time it needed.

Brisa carried two clean bowls to the drying rack, placing them down with care so they made only the softest clink. The air near the rack was cooler, touched by the breeze from the half-open window, and Brisa paused there to feel the change on their face. Mama folded a fresh towel and said, “Everything finishes in its own good time here.” Brisa smiled and replied, “The pebble will tell us when the paint is ready.” They returned to the jam jars and twisted each lid just snug enough, the metal cool under their palms, while the square of sun continued its slow work.

When the light on the counter grew softer and more golden, Brisa knew enough minutes had passed. They lifted the paper covering with both hands and set it aside, revealing the painted pebble now fully dry and bright in its square of sun. The colors held firm and true, blue against yellow without any stickiness left. Brisa picked up the pebble and felt its smooth warmth rest in their open palm. Mama came beside them and together they admired the tiny painted scene, the kitchen quiet except for the low creak of settling wood. Brisa held the pebble close and breathed in the remaining sweetness of jam and dough.

Brisa carried the painted pebble to the wide sill and placed it where the last of the daylight could touch it one more time. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Mama dimmed the brighter lamps so only that gentle light remained, and Brisa felt the cool evening air drift across their bare feet while the pebble stayed warm in their hand. They sat on the low stool with Mama nearby, turning the stone slowly to watch every painted line catch the lantern shine, and the bakery kitchen held them both in its calm finished hush as the dough rested and the jam cooled completely in the quiet.
