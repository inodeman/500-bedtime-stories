Idris heard a faint tapping against the baseboard near the foot of the bed, no louder than a pencil rolling once across wood. The hall light still shone through the half-open door, laying a soft golden stripe across the carpet and warming the cooler air that drifted in from the corridor. Idris sat up carefully, the quilt sliding down with a quiet rustle, and blinked at the familiar shapes of the dresser and the little desk. The tapping stopped as soon as they noticed it, leaving only the hush of the house and the mild glow that kept the dark from feeling complete. Idris swung their legs over the side of the mattress and let bare feet touch the floorboards, which held a faint leftover warmth from the day. Curiosity pulled them toward the desk where a round tin sat waiting in the edge of the light.

Inside the tin the pencils stood upright and ready, their points fine and clean as if freshly cut, wood pale and fragrant where the sharpener had shaved them smooth. Idris lifted one and rolled it between their fingers, feeling how evenly Grandma Nia had prepared each one before Idris ever woke. The hall light reached just far enough to show the neat points and the soft graphite dust that still clung to the rim of the tin like gray snow. Idris smiled at the quiet gift left without any note or fuss. The air near the desk carried a cool stillness that made the small discovery feel even kinder. Idris set the pencil back among its companions and thought of Grandma Nia moving softly through the house while they slept.

A simple thank-you spoken later would feel too thin for something so thoughtful and early. Idris decided then to do one useful thing Grandma Nia had not asked for at all. They padded across the room to the low shelf where picture books leaned in uneven stacks and began to straighten them by size so the spines lined up clean and easy to reach. The carpet under their knees felt springy and a little cool as they worked. Outside the open door the hall light hummed its steady low note and painted the same gentle stripe across the floor. Idris stacked the tallest books first, then the medium ones, and finally the thin board books, smoothing each cover with the flat of a hand until the shelf looked calm and ready for morning stories.

While the books settled into order, a soft jingling carried from the hallway where the family cat wandered past, its collar bell ringing once and then fading. Idris paused and listened, glad the house felt so peaceful. They turned next to the small round table by the window and noticed the water glass Grandma Nia liked to keep nearby. Idris carried it carefully to the bathroom sink, filled it with cool clear water that made a gentle glugging sound, and brought it back without spilling a drop. The glass felt smooth and solid in their hands. They set it down exactly where it belonged so Grandma Nia would find it waiting. The room stayed quiet except for the distant hush of the rest of the house settling into night.

On the corner of the desk lay a loose glass bead that had rolled free from an old craft box, smooth and cool under Idris’s fingertip when they picked it up and returned it to the little wooden tray. Idris liked the way the bead caught a spark of the hall light and threw a tiny rainbow fleck onto the wood. After that they opened the window a few inches more so fresh air could drift in, and with it came the clean scent of mint leaves from the pot Grandma Nia kept on the sill outside. Idris breathed the green smell and felt the room grow even calmer. They wiped a faint smudge from the desk surface with the edge of a sleeve until the wood shone dull and clean under the spill of light.

Idris returned to the tin of pencils and arranged them so the sharpened points all faced the same direction, a small finishing touch no one had requested. The graphite tips looked like dark stars ready for morning drawings. The temperature near the window stayed pleasantly cool against their wrists while they worked. When everything felt settled Idris walked to the doorway and looked down the softly lit hall toward Grandma Nia’s chair where she sometimes sat with her knitting late. The chair creaked once as Grandma Nia shifted, and Idris knew she was still awake and near.

“Grandma Nia,” Idris said in a voice just above a whisper, “I saw the pencils you sharpened. They are perfect.”  
Grandma Nia’s gentle answer floated back. “I thought you might want them ready first thing.”  
Idris stepped a little closer so the hall light fell across both of them. “I straightened the books on the shelf and filled your water glass too.”  
“That was kind of you, Idris,” Grandma Nia said, her tone warm as flannel. “Come give me a hug before you climb back in.”

Idris crossed the short space of hallway and leaned into the hug, smelling the familiar clean scent of Grandma Nia’s sweater and feeling the steady warmth of her arms. The hug lasted long enough for both of them to breathe slowly together. Then Idris walked back into the bedroom, feet quiet on the carpet, and pulled the quilt up to their chin. The hall light still reached across the floor in its same soft stripe, and the mint smell lingered near the open window. Everything felt finished and peaceful, the way a room does when small careful things have been done without hurry.

Idris closed their eyes and listened to the house settle around them, the faint warmth of the quilt matching the leftover glow from the corridor. The sharpened pencils waited patiently in their tin for morning pictures. The straightened books stood ready on the shelf. The water glass shone clear on the little table. Idris felt glad they had noticed and glad they had answered the noticing with one quiet useful deed of their own. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
