The ink spot rested in the middle of the kitchen table, a small dark circle with edges that looked soft and wet in the gentle yellow light pouring from the ceiling lamp. Golda bent very near and watched the way the wood grain held the blue-black color, almost like a tiny lake that had decided to stay still. The surface of the table felt smooth under their fingertips except right where the ink had settled, and Golda could smell the sweet jam that sat in its open jar only a hand’s width away. Butter waited on a little plate beside the shared pencil tin, and Golda’s breath made a quiet warm fog on the wood as they stayed close to the mark they had made.

Golda remembered how the pen had tipped while they were drawing a picture of a sleepy house next to Eshe, who sat across the same table with their own paper. The pencil tin held many colors, and the jam spoon still rested sticky and bright near Golda’s elbow. Warm air rose from the oven nearby and touched Golda’s bare arms while they stared at the spot and thought for a moment about covering it with a napkin. The light from the lamp made the butter glisten like a soft yellow moon, and Golda’s heart felt steady even though their fingers wanted to hide the dark circle before anyone else looked.

Eshe hummed a soft tune and the humming mixed with the faint sound of a cat’s collar bell coming from the hallway as the house cat wandered past the doorway. Golda glanced at Eshe’s calm face and then back at the ink spot, feeling the cool edge of the table against their wrist. The jam smelled fruity and thick, and Golda almost slid a piece of paper over the mark, yet something quiet inside them wanted the truth to stay open on the wood. The shared pencil tin clicked gently when Golda set down their pen, and the warm kitchen air carried the butter’s mild scent while Golda decided what to do next.

Ms. Okonkwo stepped into the kitchen with slow careful steps, and the light from the window fell across her kind hands as she reached for a clean cloth. Golda looked up and felt the smooth cool temperature of the tabletop under both palms. “I made this ink spot with the pen,” Golda said in a clear quiet voice. Ms. Okonkwo smiled and answered, “Thank you for telling me the plain truth, Golda.” The words settled softly between them like a gentle blanket, and Golda could feel their shoulders rest easier while the jam jar still gleamed nearby.

Together they looked at the spot again, and Golda touched the rim of the shared pencil tin which felt cool and solid. “We can clean what we can clean,” Ms. Okonkwo said, and Golda nodded. Eshe offered a small glass bead from their pocket so they could roll it gently across the clean part of the wood and watch how light caught inside it like a tiny frozen raindrop. The bead felt round and hard and wonderfully smooth against Golda’s fingers when they held it for a moment before setting it back down. Warm light from the lamp made the bead sparkle while Ms. Okonkwo dampened the cloth with careful water.

Golda helped press the cloth against the edges of the ink spot, and the cloth felt damp and cool under their hands as they worked slowly side by side. The smell of mint leaves drifted in from the little pot on the windowsill and mixed with the jam and butter in a fresh green way that made the whole table seem friendlier. “The wood will keep a soft shadow, but it will be clean enough,” Ms. Okonkwo said. Golda answered, “I like that we mended it together.” Eshe smiled across the table and the three of them stayed quiet for a breath while the oven’s gentle heat kept the kitchen cozy.

After the cloth had done its work, Golda smoothed the area with one careful finger and felt the wood return to its familiar dry texture all around the lighter mark that remained. The shared pencil tin sat open again, full of colors ready for new drawings, and the butter looked creamy and soft in the steady light. Golda and Eshe began a fresh picture of a garden path while Ms. Okonkwo set out three small cups of warm milk that steamed gently and filled the air with a mild sweet warmth. The cat’s soft steps had faded, yet the kitchen still felt full of quiet company as Golda chose a green pencil and began to color leaves.

Golda named the jam smell, the butter smell, and the warm milk smell one after another in a soft voice while their pencil moved slowly across the paper. The light above the table stayed even and golden, wrapping every face and every jar in the same calm glow. Ms. Okonkwo sat nearby and watched the new drawing grow, and Golda felt the sturdy chair under them hold their body steady and safe. The glass bead rested beside the pencil tin where Eshe had placed it, catching tiny sparks of lamp light each time someone shifted. No one hurried, and the kitchen held only the small sounds of pencils and quiet breathing.

When the drawing was finished Golda leaned back and felt the pleasant cool of the evening air drifting through the slightly open window. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Golda looked once more at the faint shadow of the ink spot that now seemed only a gentle memory on the shared table, then closed their eyes for a slow blink while the milk cups cooled and the whole room stayed peaceful and ready for sleep.
