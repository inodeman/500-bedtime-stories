The paper fan rested open in Nim’s small hands, its pale blue folds catching the early bakery light so that each crease shone like a quiet ribbon of sky. Tiny painted flowers curved along the edges, and the wooden handle felt warm already from the busy air. Nim traced one careful finger down a smooth rib of the paper and heard the soft whisper it made, a sound no louder than a moth’s wing. The queue ahead of them shifted gently, and the fan fluttered once as if it wanted to help. Nim stood close to the polished counter where loaves rested under glass, and they kept the fan steady so its breeze would stay kind.

Warmth drifted from the ovens in slow golden waves, brushing Nim’s cheeks and the backs of their hands while the paper fan waited ready. Brigid stood beside them, sneakers quiet on the tiled floor, and Ms. Okonkwo moved behind the counter with calm hands measuring flour. A faint scent of mint leaves rose from a little jar near the register, cool and green among the sweeter bakery air. Nim felt the heat settle deeper into their shoulders and opened the fan a little wider, letting the first soft push of air touch both of them. The morning felt busy yet soft, full of the low murmur of people waiting their turns without hurry.

Nim noticed how the shared space of the queue made the single paper fan seem larger than one person needed. They looked at Brigid’s slightly flushed face and then at the fan’s wide blue surface. “Brigid, the air feels heavy this morning,” Nim said in a voice no louder than the oven’s quiet hum. Brigid nodded and answered, “It does, Nim. Your fan looks cool.” Nim thought of the long warm line and knew a breeze kept only for one would leave the other waiting without comfort. They folded the outer edges inward with careful fingers until the fan became a smaller, even shape that could sweep air toward two children at once.

The smaller paper fan now fit neatly between them, its reduced wings still strong enough to stir the bakery air. Nim held one side of the handle and offered the other to Brigid so both could guide the motion together. A clear glass bead strung on a thin cord around the handle rolled under their shared grip, cool and perfectly round against their skin. “We can move it slowly so everyone nearby feels a little,” Nim said. Brigid smiled and replied, “That feels fair, Nim. Thank you for making room.” Together they waved the compact fan in gentle arcs that reached both faces and even brushed the person standing just ahead.

Ms. Okonkwo watched the quiet sharing from behind the counter and simply set two small paper bags side by side without adding any words. The choice stood as it was, light and settled. Nim kept the rhythm steady, and the smaller fan sent soft currents that smelled faintly of rising dough. Somewhere near the doorway a cat’s collar bell gave one clear silver note as the bakery cat padded past the waiting feet. Nim and Brigid both heard it and smiled without speaking, keeping their hands light on the shared handle while the warm queue inched forward under the soft morning glow.

Nim breathed in three quiet scents that drifted together—the clean mint leaves, the brown crust of cooling bread, and the faint vanilla of sugar dust left on the counter. They did not name any worry; they only noticed how the air itself felt kinder when shared. Brigid’s shoulder rested near theirs, and the paper fan’s smaller shape kept sending equal breezes across both collars. Light from the front window slid across the floor in slow rectangles, warming the tiles yet never harsh. Nim guided another slow sweep and felt the wooden handle press gently into their palm beside Brigid’s fingers.

“Ms. Okonkwo, may we keep using the fan this way until our turn?” Nim asked softly when the baker glanced their way again. Ms. Okonkwo only nodded once, her eyes kind, and turned back to the shelves. Brigid whispered, “She understands, Nim.” The smaller even share continued without fuss, the blue paper moving like a calm tide between them. Temperature stayed comfortable now, the earlier heavy warmth lifted by the joint motion of two steady hands. Nim felt proud of the simple change yet kept the feeling quiet inside, watching flour dust dance in a sunbeam above the counter.

The queue moved at last, and Nim and Brigid stepped forward together still holding the compact paper fan. Ms. Okonkwo placed warm rolls into their bags with the same calm care, and the children thanked her in soft matching voices. Outside the main rush the air felt cooler against their sleeves, yet they stayed a moment longer near the counter’s end where a small bench waited. Nim ran one thumb once more over the folded edges of the fan, noticing how the shared shape still carried the morning light inside its creases. Brigid leaned close and they waved the fan one final gentle time so the last of the bakery warmth drifted away like a sigh.

Nim sat with Brigid on the low bench while the paper fan rested open across both laps, its work complete for now. The bakery sounds softened into distant clinks of trays and the low contented talk of later customers. Light through the side window turned the remaining flour motes into slow gold sparks. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Nim felt the quiet settle around their shoulders like a light blanket, and they closed their eyes for a peaceful breath while the shared fan cooled between them and the soft busy morning kept its gentle pace.
