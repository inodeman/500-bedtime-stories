"Nana Ruth, may I lift the cloth and check the little wooden spoon now?" Paz asked in a soft voice beside the ceramic studio table where pale slip and new handles waited to set in the mild evening coolness. Nana Ruth smiled and rested one warm hand near Paz so they could feel the steady quiet of the room. The covered spoon rested under a clean square of cloth while the clay pieces dried without hurry. Paz could see the soft yellow lamp glow pooling across the table surface and hear only the slow tick of the wall clock. They wanted to look right away yet stayed still, noticing how the air felt gentle against their cheeks.

Nana Ruth shook her head kindly and said, "Not yet, Paz. The varnish needs its own quiet minutes." Paz nodded and kept both hands folded, watching the cloth stay smooth and undisturbed. Nearby a tray of clay cups cooled and a row of handles rested on wooden blocks, all of them needing stillness just like the spoon. The studio smelled faintly of clean water and drying clay, and Paz breathed it in while deciding what to do instead of peeking. They shifted from one foot to the other on the cool floorboards and listened to Nana Ruth hum a tiny tune under her breath.

Paz chose a quiet task and began sorting soft cotton rags into neat stacks at the far end of the long table. Each rag felt light and slightly fuzzy against their palms as they folded one after another. Nana Ruth stayed close and said, "That helps the studio stay ready, Paz." Paz answered, "I can wait with the rags while the spoon finishes." The light from the high window turned a deeper gold and spilled across the sorted piles, making them look like small soft hills. Paz worked slowly so the table would not shake the setting slip or the covered spoon.

After the rags were tidy Paz picked up a smooth clay tool and wiped it carefully with a dry cloth, listening to the soft whisper of fabric on wood. The tool warmed a little in their hand from the gentle rubbing. Nana Ruth watched and offered a quiet nod of approval. Outside somewhere a gate latch clicked once and then fell silent again, a small clear sound that drifted through the open window and settled into the peaceful room. Paz paused to notice it and then returned to polishing the tool until it shone under the lamp light.

Paz remembered the cinnamon stick Nana Ruth sometimes kept near her tea jar and wondered if a tiny bit of that spicy scent still lingered in the air near the drying shelves. They breathed in and found the warm cinnamon note floating gently among the clay smells. It made the studio feel like home and calm. Dahlia, who sometimes visited after school, had left a small note earlier saying she hoped the spoon would look bright when it dried, and Paz smiled at the thought of showing her later. For now Paz stayed with Nana Ruth and kept their voice soft so nothing would hurry the varnish.

Nana Ruth invited Paz to sit on the low stool and rest their elbows on a folded towel so the table remained steady. "We can hum four soft notes and then leave a rest," Nana Ruth suggested. Paz hummed four gentle notes with her, then stayed quiet for a long peaceful pause while the handles and the slip continued setting. The quiet rest felt good, like a soft blanket over the whole room. Paz could feel the cool air moving just a little near the window and see dust motes drifting slowly in the golden light.

Time passed in small slow steps while Paz counted the clay handles without touching them. There were six, each one curved and pale. Nana Ruth poured a cup of warm water for herself and set it far from the wet work so steam rose in a thin quiet ribbon. Paz watched the steam curl and fade and felt proud that they had left the cloth alone. "The spoon is still resting," Paz whispered, and Nana Ruth answered, "Yes, and you are resting with it in the best way." The words felt kind and steady in the mild air of the studio.

When the varnish had taken all the time it needed Nana Ruth lifted the cloth herself and let Paz look. There it was, the little wooden spoon, now dry and gleaming, its surface a walnut-smooth spoon that felt silky when Paz finally touched the handle with one careful finger. The light caught the clear finish and made it shine like a tiny moon on the table. Paz grinned and held the spoon near their cheek to feel how cool and finished it had become. Nana Ruth said, "You waited well, Paz," and Paz answered, "It finished in its own time." They set the spoon on a clean cloth beside the set handles so everything could share the calm table together.

Paz and Nana Ruth sat a little longer while the last of the daylight softened into evening blue outside. The clay pieces felt firm now under a gentle test of Nana Ruth's fingertip, and the studio settled into a deeper hush. Paz kept the finished spoon close on its cloth and breathed the quiet air one more time. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
