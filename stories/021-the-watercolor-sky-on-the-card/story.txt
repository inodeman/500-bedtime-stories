"Mama, may I lift the cloth and check on the watercolor sky washing across the card before it is ready?" Yuri asked in the bakery kitchen where dough rested under towels and jam cooled in open pots. Warm golden light from the window touched the flour-dusted counter and made the copper pans gleam. Mama walked over from the sink, her apron scented mildly with yeast, and stood beside the low stool where Yuri waited. Yuri's fingers hovered near the clean cloth covering the card, eager for the soft blues they had painted earlier. The leftover oven heat felt pleasant on Yuri's bare arms. "Not quite yet, Yuri," Mama said gently. "The paint needs more quiet time to dry." Yuri drew a slow breath and left the cloth in place, choosing to wait.

Yuri noticed the dough puffing softly under its towel and the jam no longer steaming. "The card will finish in its own time, just like the dough and the jam," Mama said, resting a calm hand on Yuri's shoulder. Yuri felt that steady warmth and nodded, deciding to leave the watercolor sky covered and do a quiet task instead. They climbed down and took a dry cloth from the hook. Cool smooth floorboards met Yuri's socks as they crossed to the clear counter end. A thin evening coolness drifted through the open back door. Yuri wiped the clean wood in slow even strokes, mind on the motion rather than the hidden card. Mama hummed nearby while she touched the rising dough with one light finger.

From the yard came the faint smell of rain on dust, soft drops having touched the dry path stones and released that earthy scent. Yuri paused and breathed it in, liking how it mixed with bakery sweetness. Mama opened the window wider so the freshness cooled the room just enough. Yuri noticed a circle of fog on a cool glass jar and traced it once with a dry finger, feeling the smooth glass clear under their path. The small action fitted the slow mood. Yuri smiled and kept working near the covered jam pots. Softer orange light painted the walls, and Yuri felt content letting minutes pass without lifting the cloth.

Paz came through the back after closing the outer gate, and the gate latch clicked once, clear and friendly. "I brought the satin ribbon Mama wanted for the finished packs," Paz said, holding pale blue ribbon that caught the light. Yuri touched the satin ribbon first, feeling its cool slippery smoothness before handing it to Mama. Mama tied it around a stack of dry bakery cards on the side shelf, the bow settling soft and pretty. Yuri returned to wiping near the dough, proud of the quiet task. Paz stacked clean bowls with soft clinks, and the three shared the calm kitchen without rush. Mama thanked Paz with a smile, and Yuri felt the room grow cozier.

Yuri helped Mama press the springy warm dough under the towel to check it, feeling how ready it had become. The jam had thickened to a perfect set, so Mama added clean lids. Yuri thought of the watercolor sky but kept hands from the cloth, trusting the wait. They carried a tray of cooled biscuits to a lower shelf, hearing the quiet wood sound as it settled. Cooler air touched Yuri's cheeks near the window. Mama poured warm milk into two cups for Yuri and Paz. Yuri held the cup with both hands, feeling pleasant heat soak into their palms while steam warmed their nose with mild sweetness.

"May I sit close to the card without touching the cloth?" Yuri asked. Mama said, "Yes, you may sit and wait right there." Yuri pulled the stool near and sat with folded hands, watching the white cloth stay still over the sky. Paz sorted spoons with soft metallic whispers blending into evening street sounds. Yuri felt the wooden stool grain under their legs and warm socks on the rungs. Time stretched gently through the bakery kitchen while Yuri stayed still. Mama wiped one last spot then stood beside Yuri, steady and kind. The dough rested risen, the jam finished, and the card dried quietly under its cloth as dust motes drifted in the last light.

At last Mama brushed the covering edge and smiled. "I believe the watercolor sky washing across the card is ready now, Yuri," Mama said. Yuri's face brightened while Mama lifted the cloth straight up so nothing dragged the paper. Underneath lay layered blue, a violet wash at the top, and gold glow along the lower edge blending into soft clouds. Yuri leaned close without touching and breathed the clean dried-paint scent mixed with bakery air. Paz clapped once softly in delight. Mama tilted the card so remaining light showed every color, and Yuri felt quiet pride for waiting until it finished. Dough, jam, and sky had all come ready together in the warm kitchen.

Yuri helped stand the card upright against a clean jar so the watercolor sky could be seen while final tasks ended. Mama dimmed the brighter lights and left gentler ones. Paz waved goodnight and slipped out, leaving the bakery kitchen peaceful. Yuri yawned and leaned against Mama's side, feeling the day settle like finished dough. Mama stroked Yuri's hair and guided them toward the hallway. The floor felt smooth underfoot, and air carried last sweet traces of jam and bread. Yuri held Mama's hand, thinking of the soft sky they had waited for carefully as a hush filled the space behind them.

In the quieter front room Mama paused by the window so Yuri could see the soft dusk before sleep. Cool evening air touched Yuri's face through the cracked pane and mixed pleasantly with indoor warmth. Yuri watched shadows lengthen across the sill while their eyelids grew heavy. Mama squeezed their hand with quiet love. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Yuri watched that kind light a long calm moment, then followed Mama down the short hall with sleepy steps, the finished watercolor sky safe on its jar and the evening wrapping around them like a kind blanket of home.
