A soft tick came from the high shelf, and Tamsin turned their head toward the gentle sound in the warm bakery air. Morning light poured through the tall front windows in calm golden bands that rested on the wooden floorboards. Tamsin stood near the long counter and felt the quiet heat rising from the ovens behind it. They noticed how the air smelled faintly sweet and yeasty, and they watched steam curl upward in slow silver ribbons. Tamsin shifted their weight and heard their own shoes make a tiny scuff on the smooth boards. The busy morning felt soft rather than hurried, with soft voices and the distant clink of trays. Tamsin breathed in and out and kept their eyes on the bright space where the counter met the light.

Tamsin stepped closer and saw an extra cushion resting on the low bench beside the bakery counter, wide enough for two. The cushion looked plump and inviting under the pale sunshine that warmed its cover. Ms. Okonkwo wiped the counter with a clean cloth and smiled when she noticed Tamsin. Pilar arrived a moment later, cheeks pink from the outdoor air, and stood shoulder to shoulder with Tamsin. Tamsin felt the cool edge of the counter under their fingertips and listened to the low hum of the refrigerator case. “Good morning, Tamsin,” Ms. Okonkwo said in her steady voice. Tamsin answered, “Good morning,” and then glanced at Pilar, who gave a quiet wave. The cushion waited there like an open invitation while the bakery filled with the soft shuffle of early visitors.

Tamsin ran one hand across the cushion and felt how smooth and even the fabric lay under their palm. They thought about how wide it was and how two friends could sit side by side without crowding. The morning light made the stitched seams glow a little, and Tamsin smelled a fresh green note of basil drifting from a tray of herb rolls nearby. Pilar leaned in and whispered, “It looks soft.” Tamsin nodded and kept one hand resting lightly on the cushion edge. Ms. Okonkwo set two small plates on the counter, each holding a warm square of bread still fragrant from the oven. Tamsin watched the steam rise and felt the gentle heat reach their face. The busy counter stayed calm, and Tamsin stayed close beside their friend.

Tamsin saw that the extra cushion wide enough for two would not feel fair if only one person sat first while the other waited alone. They pictured one of them settled and comfortable while the other stood, and the picture did not sit right inside their chest. The air near the ovens carried a mild warmth that brushed Tamsin’s wrists. They looked at the two plates and then at Pilar’s hopeful face. “We can share the seat and the bread,” Tamsin said. Pilar’s eyes brightened. Ms. Okonkwo simply watched without speaking, her hands resting on the counter edge. Tamsin felt sure the choice would feel better if both of them were included from the start.

Tamsin broke each warm square carefully into smaller even pieces so the share would match for two. They placed the pieces back on the plates in neat pairs while the tin clock on the wall kept its steady soft rhythm. The sound of that tin clock mixed with the quiet clink of a spoon somewhere farther down the counter. Tamsin slid one plate toward Pilar and kept the other close. “Now we both have the same,” Tamsin said. Pilar took a piece and smiled. The polished counter reflected a thin line of sunlight that stretched toward their elbows. Tamsin felt glad the pieces looked alike and that no one had to go first alone.

Tamsin and Pilar settled together onto the extra cushion wide enough for two, their shoulders almost touching. The fabric gave a little under them with a friendly hush of air. Tamsin felt a polished pebble in their pocket, smooth and cool against their fingers when they reached down for a moment. They left the pebble there and picked up a small piece of bread instead. Ms. Okonkwo nodded once and let the choice stand without a speech, turning back to arrange more rolls in tidy rows. The bakery light stayed gentle across the bench, and the cushion held them both with easy room. Tamsin chewed slowly and listened to Pilar’s quiet happy breathing beside them.

Tamsin noticed a small stack of cloth napkins near the register and decided the quiet action would fit the calm room. They stood for a moment, folded each napkin so the stitched pear faced up, and set them in a soft pile. The fabric felt thick and pleasant under their hands, and the morning light caught the tiny pear design. “These look ready now,” Tamsin said to no one in particular, though Ms. Okonkwo glanced over with a kind look. Pilar stayed on the cushion and waited. Tamsin returned and sat again, feeling the shared seat welcome them both. The air carried the lingering green scent of basil mixed with warm bread, and everything around the counter stayed peaceful.

Tamsin and Pilar finished their even pieces while the busy morning continued in its soft way. Customers came and went with quiet steps, and the ovens kept a low steady warmth that reached the bench in gentle waves. Tamsin brushed a few crumbs from their lap onto a napkin and felt the day settling into an easy rhythm. Pilar rested one hand on the cushion between them. Ms. Okonkwo moved a tray of cooling buns farther down the counter where the light fell brightest. Tamsin watched the golden crusts and felt full of quiet contentment. They stayed close to the wide cushion and to their friend, letting the soft sounds of the bakery wrap around them like a blanket.

Tamsin leaned back a little against the cushion and felt how steadily it held two. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Afternoon was still far away, yet the small light made a calm circle on the sill that matched the gentle mood inside. Tamsin heard Pilar’s soft voice say thank you, and they answered with a quiet thank you of their own. The polished floor reflected pale light near their shoes, and the air stayed comfortably warm. Tamsin closed their eyes for one peaceful breath and opened them again to the familiar bakery. They sat with Pilar on the shared cushion while Ms. Okonkwo worked nearby, and the whole soft busy morning felt complete and kind.
