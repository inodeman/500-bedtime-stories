The mild afternoon air settled over the schoolyard like a soft blanket, warm on the cheeks and quiet enough to hear a leaf slide across the dirt. Quinn stepped into the circle of stones first, feeling the smooth cool edges under their shoes, and watched the low swing drift in a slow arc as sunlight pooled gold along the chains. The light was gentle and unhurried. Quinn breathed in the calm and walked toward the heap of coats that had been draped over two chairs and a low bench to make a little tent. The fabric walls leaned close, and the space inside looked snug and inviting. Cousin Pio sat on a flat stone a short way off, elbows on knees, and simply watched without calling any instructions.

Quinn crouched and parted the coat flaps, noticing how the inside smelled faintly of playground dust and sun-warmed cloth. They patted a spot on the packed earth and sat, pulling their knees up. The light inside the coat tent was dim and rosy where it filtered through sleeves and collars. Quinn heard the low swing creak once more outside and smiled. “We can sit here a while,” Quinn said softly to the empty space beside them. A leather lace dangled from one coat cuff and brushed Quinn’s wrist with a smooth, cool slip of texture that felt steady and familiar. Quinn looped it loosely around a button so it would not drag, then listened to their own quiet breathing.

Outside the tent, near the circle of stones, Jaleh stood with hands in pockets and watched the swinging shadow move back and forth. The air around Jaleh felt a touch cooler in the open yard. Quinn peeked through a gap between two coats and saw Jaleh’s shoes scuff the dirt without coming closer. The coat tent could fit one more person if someone shifted a little. Quinn felt the idea settle like a warm hand on the shoulder. Cousin Pio glanced over once, then looked back at the stones, leaving the choice entirely to the children. Quinn did not call out a big invitation. Instead they began to move the seating with small careful hands.

Quinn slid one coat sleeve sideways and tucked another collar under the bench edge so the opening grew wider and lower. The temperature inside stayed mild and kind. “If we sit this way, the wall will not sag,” Quinn murmured, testing the new space with one palm. They patted the earth twice, smoothing a place that felt right for a friend. From somewhere beyond the fence a cat’s collar bell chimed once, light and clear, then faded into the afternoon. Quinn’s shoulders eased. They kept rearranging without hurry, changing the pace of the game before the game had even truly begun, so there would be room that simply belonged.

Jaleh took one slow step nearer the tent when Quinn left a clear open flap and an empty patch of ground that looked ready. Quinn did not announce any prize. They only shifted their own knees and made the inside quieter by speaking low. “The stones make a good path if you walk between them,” Quinn said, as if sharing a simple fact about the yard. Soft light striped Jaleh’s sleeves as they bent and entered. The coats brushed shoulders with a dry whisper. Quinn scooted half a hand-width to the left, and suddenly the tent held both of them without crowding. The earth under their sitting bones felt firm and cool.

Cousin Pio smiled a little from the stone and stayed quiet, letting the children keep solving the shape of the afternoon. Quinn reached for a third coat that had slipped and draped it so it made a softer roof. The new fold released a brief smell of soapy wool that rose gentle and clean between them. Jaleh breathed it in and settled more fully, back against the bench leg. Quinn felt glad without saying why. They tapped two pebbles into a tiny row just for the fun of lining something small, a calm motion that matched the slow swing outside. The light through the coats turned warmer as the sun eased lower.

“We can pretend the swing is a boat going past,” Jaleh whispered, and Quinn nodded. “And the circle of stones can be the quiet shore,” Quinn answered, keeping their voice even. They took turns describing the slow boat and the safe shore, never raising the volume, never rushing the turns. The air inside the tent stayed pleasant against their arms. Outside, Cousin Pio stood and stretched, then sat again, still leaving every choice to Quinn and Jaleh. Quinn felt the leather lace once more under their fingers when they adjusted a cuff, but they did not need to speak about it. The lace simply held.

After a while the three of them, Quinn and Jaleh under the coats and Pio nearby, shared a stretch of minutes that felt round and complete. Quinn listened to the low swing settle into stillness and to Jaleh’s quiet breathing beside them. The temperature began to cool by a single gentle degree as late light painted the stones pink. Quinn smoothed the last coat edge so no one would feel left at the border of the fun. “Tomorrow we can open the flaps wider if we want,” Quinn said. Jaleh’s answer came soft and sure. “I like this width,” they said. Pio only nodded, proud without taking the credit.

When the schoolyard emptied and the coats were folded carefully over arms for the walk home, Quinn carried the mild day inside them like a warm stone in a pocket. At the quiet house the evening air felt softer still, and the rooms held a peaceful hush that smelled faintly of supper already finished. Quinn washed hands, changed into night clothes that felt smooth against the skin, and climbed into bed while the last pale light left the glass. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Quinn closed their eyes and remembered the wide enough tent, the circle of stones, and the friend who had simply sat down beside them.
