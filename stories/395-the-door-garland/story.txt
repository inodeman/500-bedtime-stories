"We should hang the leaf garland before the light changes," Ines said, standing near the long tablecloth that smelled faintly of clean cotton in the cottage room.

Jorie nodded and held one end of the garland of leaves that had a definite up and down, the pointed tips always meant to face the floor while the rounded bases rested toward the ceiling, and the two friends spread the cool leaves carefully across the cloth so nothing tangled. Soft paper stars dangled from thin strings above the table, and each star caught a little golden glow from the window as Ines ran a careful finger along the row and felt a leaf's soft underside. The baker stepped in from the doorway with flour still dusting their sleeves and watched without hurry, smiling at the quiet work already begun.

"If we rush, the leaves will twist the wrong way," Ines told Jorie, and they both slowed their hands at once so every stem stayed in its proper order. The baker only offered a calm count when the middle knot needed tying, saying the numbers in a steady voice that matched the gentle pace of the room. Ines and Jorie decided to split the long garland into two slow jobs, one friend guiding the left half while the other guided the right half, never pulling hard or racing ahead. Outside the open window the wind in the fig leaves made a low continuous hush that filled the pauses between their soft movements.

Ines kept their eyes on the leaves and felt the warm air of the cottage room settle around their shoulders while Jorie mirrored each careful placement on the opposite side of the tablecloth. The baker named the simple knot they needed, calling it the quiet loop so both children could remember it without stopping to guess. Together they looped the twine once, then twice, and the leaves stayed upright in their definite order from base to tip. Paper stars brushed lightly against one another with a dry whisper whenever a small draft moved through the room, and Ines listened to that gentle sound while their fingers worked.

"Your half looks even," Jorie said, and Ines answered with a quiet smile that reached their eyes as they checked the spacing once more. The baker stood nearby and counted the remaining leaves aloud in an easy rhythm so neither child felt alone with the long stretch still ahead. Ines and Jorie moved the garland slowly toward the waiting hooks above the tablecloth, keeping every leaf turned the correct way and never letting the stems bunch or fold. The beeswax from a small polished candle on the side shelf gave the air a mild sweet scent that mixed with the open window and made the whole room feel settled and safe.

Ines paused for a moment to smooth one last leaf into place and noticed how the late light painted soft shapes across the long tablecloth and the hanging paper stars. Jorie waited patiently until Ines was ready, and then they lifted their halves together so the garland rose in one continuous gentle arc. The baker offered one more soft count for the final knot, and the three of them finished the tying without a single hurried tug. Warmth from the wooden floorboards rose through Ines's socks as they stood back a little to look, and the leaves hung exactly as they should with every tip pointing down and every base resting high.

"It stays the right way now," Ines said, and Jorie laughed a small happy sound that did not disturb the quiet of the cottage room. The baker nodded and wiped a trace of flour from the table edge with a clean cloth, then stepped aside so the children could admire the finished garland against the paper stars. Ines walked once around the long tablecloth, feeling the cool air near the window and the warmer air nearer the walls, and everything looked calm and complete. Outside a snail had begun a slow path across one whole brick on the sill, and Ines watched it for a few peaceful breaths while Jorie stood close beside them.

They returned to the table together and straightened one paper star that had tilted, their hands moving with the same slow care they had given the leaves. The baker brought two small cups of warm milk and set them on the cloth without speaking, only smiling at the neat row of green that now framed the room. Ines sipped and felt the mild heat travel down, then rested both hands on the edge of the tablecloth where the fabric felt smooth and familiar under their palms. The garland of leaves that had a definite up and down stayed perfectly ordered, and no stem had twisted during the careful work they had shared.

Evening settled a little deeper through the window, and the paper stars glowed with the last pale light while Ines and Jorie sat side by side on the low bench. The baker folded the spare twine into a neat coil and placed it on a high shelf, then dimmed the brighter lamps so only gentle light remained. Ines felt sleepy and content, the quiet of the cottage room wrapping around them like a soft blanket as Jorie leaned a shoulder lightly against theirs. The leaves hung still and correct above the long tablecloth, and the whole space smelled clean and calm after the slow shared work.

Ines closed their eyes for a moment and listened to the soft settling of the house while the baker hummed a wordless tune near the doorway. Jorie whispered that the garland looked perfect, and Ines opened their eyes again to see the green row still hanging true above the paper stars. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Ines felt the gentle warmth of the milk still lingering and the smooth cloth under their resting hands, and sleep came easily in the quiet cottage room.
