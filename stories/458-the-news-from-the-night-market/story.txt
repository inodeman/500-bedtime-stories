The soft tick of the unhurried clock floated across the cottage room first, steady and quiet as a sleepy heartbeat. Vera noticed the sound right away and turned toward the hearth, where the fire sent a mild orange light dancing over the stones and warmed their bare toes. The chair cushions felt plump under them, and the air held a gentle heat that made eyelids feel heavy in the nicest way. Auntie June sat close in her own chair, the glow painting soft shadows on her smile. Vera stayed very still, listening to that calm ticking while the fire whispered low, already holding a bigger piece of news inside but choosing for now to watch the light flicker and feel the warm stones nearby.

Auntie June lifted a thick mug, and the rich smell of cocoa rose between them like a sweet cloud. “Vera, I brought news from the night market, and I will tell it exactly in the order I saw each thing,” she said in a low voice that matched the slow clock. Vera breathed in the chocolate warmth and felt it settle on their tongue even before any sip. The hearth crackled once, a friendly pop that made the shadows jump and settle again. Vera wanted to speak up with their own bigger news right then, yet they kept their lips closed and watched the steam curl, letting the cocoa scent wrap around the quiet moment while the fire kept them both snug and the clock kept its gentle pace.

Auntie June rocked once in her chair and let it stop by itself, the wood giving a tiny creak that faded into the room. “First I saw the rows of paper lanterns swaying over the path,” she began, “each one glowing yellow and making the stones shine.” Vera listened hard, fingers resting on the arm of the chair, and felt how cool the wood stayed even near the heat. The light from the hearth brightened for a second as a log settled, painting Vera’s knees gold. They almost interrupted with the bigger news waiting on their tongue, but they stayed quiet instead, hearing every word and noticing how the cocoa smell still lingered sweetly while the unhurried clock ticked on.

“Next I saw the fruit stalls piled high with oranges and plums that caught the lantern light,” Auntie June continued, her voice smooth as warm milk. Vera leaned a little closer and brushed against the soft texture of a wool sleeve on Auntie June’s arm, feeling the cozy loops of yarn under their palm. The fire sent a steady heat across their face, and the clock kept measuring the silence between sentences. Vera’s bigger news pressed at them again, yet they only stroked the wool once more and kept listening, eyes on the flames, letting the story unfold exactly as Auntie June had seen it without rushing ahead.

A faint jingle of a cat’s collar bell sounded from the hallway as the kitchen cat wandered nearer, the tiny chime bright and clear. Vera heard it and smiled but still did not speak, turning their attention back to Auntie June. “After the fruit I saw the spice baskets, red and brown powders shining under little lamps,” Auntie June said. The hearth light flickered gently over Vera’s hair, and the air stayed warm enough that no one needed an extra blanket. Vera held the bigger news tightly inside, determined now to hear every part, while the collar bell faded and the cocoa scent drifted faintly again from the mug resting nearby.

“Then I saw the cloth booths with scarves floating like slow clouds in the night breeze,” Auntie June went on, “and the sellers folding them with careful hands.” Vera watched the fire’s orange edges and felt the solid chair under them, solid and safe. The temperature near the hearth stayed just right, never too hot, wrapping Vera in calm. They longed to jump in with bigger news of their own, yet they waited through each detail, hearing the cloth described and the way the lanterns had made everything soft, the clock ticking unhurried all the while.

“Near the end I saw the honey pots lined up like little suns, and the man who dipped a wooden stick so the gold dripped slow,” Auntie June said. Vera sat forward a little, the hearth stones warm against their side, and kept every word. The light from the flames made Auntie June’s face look kind and open. Vera’s bigger news still waited, but they let the honey story finish completely, breathing the quiet air and noticing how peaceful the cottage felt with only the clock and the fire speaking between sentences.

“Last of all I saw the musicians packing their drums under the final lanterns, and that was the end of what I saw in order,” Auntie June finished, setting the mug down with a soft clink. Vera waited until the last sentence truly ended, then asked one real question in a small clear voice. “Auntie June, did the honey smell as sweet as it looked under those lights?” The fire answered with a low friendly hiss, and the warm glow stayed steady on Vera’s hands. Auntie June nodded and answered gently that it did, the steam long gone but the memory of cocoa still kind in the room.

Vera rocked once themselves in the smaller chair and let it stop by itself, then shared something small of their own. “I found a smooth gray pebble by the garden path this afternoon, and it fits right in my pocket,” they said. Auntie June smiled and touched Vera’s shoulder lightly. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Vera felt the last of the hearth warmth on their cheeks, heard the clock tick once more, and rested easy knowing both sets of news now lived together in the quiet cottage air.
