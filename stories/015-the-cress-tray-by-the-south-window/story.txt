"I want to peek under the cloth right now," Violeta said, their voice soft as they stood in the quiet craft corner where stacks of colored paper waited beside a jar of paste and a wooden rack that held drying shapes.

Auntie Luz sat on a low stool near the south window, the light falling warm across her hands as she smoothed a fresh sheet. The tray of cress rested there under its light cover, tiny seeds still doing their quiet work in the soil, and the craft corner had been set right beside it so the afternoon sun could reach both the paper and the little green beginnings. Violeta breathed in the calm room and felt the mild temperature on their arms while they looked from the paste brush to the covered tray. "It smells ready to me," Violeta added, though they kept their feet still.

"The cress will finish in its own time," Auntie Luz answered gently, and the beeswax from the small sealing sticks on the wooden rack gave the air a clean, honey-soft scent that mixed with the paste. Violeta nodded and reached instead for a piece of soft blue paper, their fingers brushing its cool surface. They wanted so much to lift the cover and see if pale stems had risen yet, but they remembered Auntie Luz was nearby and the tray needed its quiet dark a little longer. Isla, who had come to share the craft time, sat cross-legged on a cushion and hummed while she tore paper into slow strips. The light through the south window stayed steady and kind.

Violeta pressed the paste brush onto the blue paper and felt the smooth glide of it, listening for any small sound from the tray though none came. They chose to leave the cover just as it was and began to make a simple folded boat for the wooden rack. "I can wait with you," Isla said, her voice barely louder than a breath, and Violeta smiled because the company made the waiting feel lighter. The paste smelled mild and the paper edges felt crisp under their careful fingers. Outside, past the open latch, the wind in the fig leaves made a low continuous rustle that drifted in like a friendly whisper. Violeta worked slowly, pressing each fold flat against the table so the boat would stand straight when it dried.

Auntie Luz watched with quiet eyes and offered another sheet when Violeta needed it. "Shall we set your boat on the rack when the paste has set?" she asked, and Violeta answered, "Yes, please, and then we can look together later." The temperature near the window stayed pleasantly warm without becoming hot, and the wooden rack held the afternoon glow on its smooth rungs. Violeta and Isla took turns dipping the brush, making sure no drop fell on the floor. They talked about the shapes they might cut next—stars, leaves, little houses—while the covered tray of cress stayed peaceful in its place. Every so often Violeta glanced toward it, then returned to the paper, choosing the quiet task instead of lifting the cloth too soon.

When the first boat was done, Violeta set it carefully on the wooden rack to dry and picked up a green scrap that looked almost like a real leaf. They turned it over and felt a leaf's soft underside with the pad of one finger, the texture gentle and slightly fuzzy like the real garden leaves they knew. Isla laughed a tiny happy sound and made her own green shape to match. The craft corner held only the small noises of paper and the distant fig leaves, nothing sharp or hurried. Auntie Luz brought a shallow dish of water so they could rinse the brush, and the cool water felt good against Violeta's warm hands. They stayed close to the table, content to keep making things while the cress took the time it needed.

Violeta decided the next quiet thing would be to watch the slow path of a small snail that had come in from the sill and begun to cross one whole brick of the floor near the south window. The snail moved with tiny shining pauses, and Violeta sat very still on the rug so they would not startle it. Isla scooted closer and they both breathed quietly, counting the snail's progress in soft whispers. "It is almost to the middle," Violeta said, and Isla answered, "It knows how to take its time too." The light from the window made the snail's shell look like a polished pebble, and the air remained mild and still around them. Auntie Luz smiled and kept her own work going so the children could enjoy the slow crossing without any rush.

By the time the snail reached the far edge of the brick, the paste on the paper boats had grown firm and the wooden rack held three finished shapes in a neat row. Violeta stood and stretched, feeling the soft floor mat under their bare feet, then walked with Auntie Luz and Isla the few steps to the tray. The cover stayed in place until Auntie Luz gave a small nod. Together they lifted it just enough to see the first pale green threads of cress standing upright in the damp soil, exactly as they were meant to, finished in their own time. Violeta felt a quiet gladness settle in their chest like warm tea. They lowered the cover again gently so the little plants could keep growing through the evening, then returned to the craft corner to place one more paper leaf on the rack.

Later, when the room had grown dimmer and the paste jar was closed, Violeta sat once more by the south window with Auntie Luz and Isla nearby, the finished paper shapes resting safely on the wooden rack and the tray of cress left covered and peaceful. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
