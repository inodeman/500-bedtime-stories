The gentle evening breeze touched the tall windows of the library with a soft coolness that made the panes feel lightly chilled under the fading golden light. Yelena stepped into the story circle room beside Auntie June, guiding their little cousin along the soft carpet that muffled every footfall in a pleasant way. Warm air wrapped around Yelena right away after the mild outdoor temperature, and low reading lamps cast pools of calm yellow light across the circle of cushions. Yelena noticed other children already sitting quietly, and they chose a spot near the edge where Auntie June could take the wooden chair just behind. Their little cousin clutched Yelena's fingers tightly, and Yelena gave a reassuring squeeze while breathing in the peaceful hush that filled the space before stories began. Auntie June stayed close without saying much yet, her presence like a quiet anchor as Yelena settled onto a thick cushion that sank gently under their weight.

Around the story circle the librarian stacked colorful books whose glossy covers caught the lamp light and threw tiny reflections onto nearby faces. Yelena spotted their friend Zia waving from across the cushions with a bright open smile that made the room feel friendlier at once. A warm smell of cocoa drifted from a side table where mugs waited under a cloth, and that chocolatey scent curled into Yelena's nose and eased a small knot of worry. Yelena's little cousin pointed at a picture of a bear on one cover and made a soft cooing sound that blended with the quiet. Auntie June rested her arm along the chair back so Yelena could lean if needed, and they felt ready to listen though a shy feeling still sat in their chest about the introducing soon to come. The temperature stayed even and comfortable, perfect for sitting still a long while with books and friends nearby.

Yelena knew the librarian sometimes asked if anyone had brought a guest to introduce by name, and the thought made their cheeks feel warm under the gentle overhead light as they pictured speaking up. When Auntie June shifted, the wool sleeve of her sweater brushed against Yelena's wrist with a soft fuzzy texture that felt like a familiar hug from home. Yelena decided they would not pretend the shy feeling away but would try the smallest brave version instead, just loud enough for the circle to hear without any big performance. Zia held up two thumbs in a silent cheer from her place, noticing Yelena's thoughtful face. The little cousin climbed half onto Yelena's lap and the weight felt solid and warm and good. Auntie June whispered nothing that pushed, only waited with kind eyes that said Yelena could choose the moment and the way.

The librarian smiled around the group and invited anyone who wished to share a visitor's name in a gentle voice that matched the calm room. Yelena rocked once in the low cushioned chair and let it stop by itself, using that tiny motion to gather a bit of steady breath before speaking. "This is my little cousin Milo," Yelena said clearly enough that the words reached across the story circle without needing to be loud or showy. Soft hellos came back from a few children, and Auntie June's smile was quiet and proud without any extra fuss or clapping that might feel like too much. The lamp light glowed steadily on the open book the librarian now held, painting the pages in easy brightness. Yelena felt their shoulders drop as the small brave thing settled into place, and their little cousin waved both hands at the friendly faces.

Just then a library cat padded along the border of the circle, and Yelena heard a cat's collar bell give one clear tiny jingle that sparkled through the quiet like a friendly hello of its own. Yelena stroked their cousin's soft hair and felt a glow of quiet pride for having spoken the name. "Hello there, Milo," Zia called softly with a wave of her hand. Auntie June leaned close and said, "You did that just the way that suited you, Yelena," her tone warm as fresh toast. The cushions under Yelena gave a little as they shifted, springy and kind to sit on during the longer story time ahead. Everyone turned attention to the librarian who began reading about a gentle rabbit who planted a garden, and the words floated out in a slow sweet rhythm that invited listening.

As the rabbit story unfolded with pictures of sunny rows of carrots glowing under the page lamps, Yelena kept an arm around their little cousin who watched with wide calm eyes. The air in the room held a steady mild warmth that made eyelids feel pleasantly heavy in a sleepy-good way. "Does the rabbit like cocoa too?" Yelena wondered aloud in a voice still gentle but sure, and the librarian nodded with a chuckle while turning the next page. Auntie June patted Yelena's back lightly through the sweater layers. Zia grinned at the question and the little cousin bounced once in happiness at the sound of Yelena's voice again. Pages whispered as they turned, and the whole circle leaned in together under the peaceful light that never flickered or glared.

When the story finished the children stretched their arms like the rabbit waking up, and some walked carefully toward the cocoa tray with grownups helping pour. Yelena stayed right where they were with Milo and Auntie June, enjoying how the chair had cradled them after that single rock earlier. Outside the windows the breeze still touched the glass gently though night gathered, and inside the light grew softer and more golden. Zia scooted her cushion nearer and shared a board book about stars without needing many words, just quiet company. Yelena felt the introducing of their little cousin by name had been enough in that one clear sentence, and no more was required. The group began to thin slowly as families murmured thanks to the librarian in low happy voices.

Yelena helped Milo turn the thick cardboard pages of the star book, feeling how sturdy and smooth they were under careful fingers while the room temperature stayed cozy as a blanket fort. Auntie June suggested one last quiet look at the shelves before leaving, and Yelena stood to walk a few steps with her, cousin in tow. "I am glad I said Milo's name so everyone could meet him," Yelena told Auntie June in words that carried clearly yet stayed between them. The soft sounds of zippers and whispered goodbyes filled the edges of the space as the story circle wound down. Yelena noticed their own breathing had slowed to match the peaceful end of the gathering, and the shy feeling had transformed into something lighter like a held balloon gently released. Soft shadows from the lamps stretched across the carpet in long calm shapes that invited restful thoughts of home.

As Yelena, Auntie June, and little cousin Milo gathered their things near the door of the library story circle, the last of the evening settled kindly around them. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Yelena felt the mild cool air seeping near the exit yet carried the room's warmth in their clothes and on their skin like a memory of kindness. They held both Auntie June's hand and Milo's small one while stepping out, hearing only the quietest hush behind them now. The day of the story circle and the small brave introducing rested easy in Yelena's mind as they moved toward home under the gentle sky, ready for pajamas and slow bedtime soon. Everything about the time together had stayed safe and sweet from the first breeze on the windows to this calm leaving.
