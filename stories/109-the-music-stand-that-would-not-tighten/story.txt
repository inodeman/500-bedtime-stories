"The music stand will not stay firm no matter how I turn this knob," said Neighbor Sami in a gentle voice that floated along the quiet school hallway of pegs, stools, and music stands. Paz stepped through the doorway and felt the mild afternoon warmth settle on their shoulders like a favorite sweater while soft golden light poured from the high windows and made the polished floor glow in long stripes. Coats and scarves hung from the wooden pegs in calm rows, empty stools waited in little clusters, and several music stands stood ready with their silver arms open. Paz walked forward slowly, eyes on the one crooked stand that leaned as if tired, and the whole space smelled faintly of polished wood and distant chalk dust from the rooms nearby. Distant footsteps faded, leaving only the peaceful hush of the hallway around Paz and Neighbor Sami.

Paz knelt on the cool floorboards beside Neighbor Sami and watched the silver joints slip again, the music stand that would not tighten tilting its top tray like a boat on gentle water. The sweet smell of cinnamon rose from an open paper bag resting on the nearest stool, where someone had left after-school cookies and a small cup. Paz reached without a word and placed both hands firmly on the wide base to hold it steady, taking that useful half of the job so Neighbor Sami could work the knob with both free hands. The base felt solid under Paz’s palms, and they pressed just enough to keep everything level while Neighbor Sami turned the screw carefully. Warm light continued to shift across the pegs as clouds passed outside, and Paz stayed close, shoulders relaxed, simply sharing the weight of the awkward stand.

"Try turning it a little more to the right while I keep the legs still," Paz said quietly, their voice blending with the soft scrape of metal on metal. Neighbor Sami nodded and answered, "Yes, that feels better already, Paz. Keep holding just like that." The cinnamon scent drifted nearer when Paz shifted closer to the stool, and for a moment they noticed a small spoon resting against the cookie bag, its handle worn walnut-smooth under the light touch of their fingertip as they brushed past it. Neighbor Sami twisted the knob once more with steady patience, and Paz felt the stand begin to firm up under their shared care. No one asked for thanks; Paz simply kept the base true while the hallway air stayed warm and still around them both.

A soft click of a gate latch sounded from the courtyard door at the far end of the hallway, a brief bright note that told Paz someone had closed the outer gate after afternoon practice. Dahlia peeked in for only a second, waved a quiet hello with a folded music book under one arm, then slipped away again toward the coats on the pegs, leaving Paz and Neighbor Sami alone with the music stand that would not tighten. Paz hummed four notes under their breath, a tiny rising tune, and then left a rest of silence while they adjusted their grip on the base. The floor felt smooth and cool against their knees, and the golden window light warmed the tops of their hands as they held everything steady. Neighbor Sami smiled without speaking and turned the knob another careful half circle.

"Almost there now," Neighbor Sami murmured, fingers working the silver screw with gentle pressure. Paz answered, "I can feel it locking. I’ll stay right here until it holds." The music stand that would not tighten finally settled into a straight, proud shape, its tray level and its legs firm against the floorboards. Paz released the base slowly, testing with one light touch that it no longer wobbled, then sat back on their heels while the warm hallway air brushed their cheeks. A loose coat on a peg stirred in a faint draft, and the stools nearby cast small soft shadows that moved only when the light changed. Paz brushed a bit of dust from their sleeve and stayed close, watching Neighbor Sami give the stand one final check.

Neighbor Sami lifted the now-steady music stand and carried it a few careful steps to a clear spot near the window wall, where it could wait for the next person who needed it. Paz rose and walked beside them, the mild temperature of the hallway wrapping around their legs as they moved past the rows of pegs hung with bright jackets and soft hats. They both set the stand down together so it stood tall among its neighbors, tray open and ready. The cinnamon smell still lingered faintly near the stool, mixing with the clean scent of wood, and Paz rested one hand for a moment on the smooth edge of a nearby stool. Everything felt settled and quiet, the kind of quiet that comes when a small job is finished side by side without hurry.

"Shall we put the stools back in a neat line before we go?" Paz asked, already reaching for the nearest one. Neighbor Sami replied, "Yes, let’s make the hallway ready for tomorrow," and together they slid three stools into an even row beneath the pegs. Paz hummed the same four notes once more, soft and slow, then left another rest while they straightened a hanging scarf that had slipped. The light from the windows had grown softer and more golden, painting the music stands in gentle highlights, and the floor felt pleasantly cool under their shoes. Dahlia’s footsteps had long since faded, leaving only Paz and Neighbor Sami in the peaceful space of pegs and stands.

Paz picked up the paper bag with the cookies and the walnut-smooth spoon, tucking the spoon safely inside so nothing would be left behind, then handed the bag to Neighbor Sami with a small nod. They walked together toward the far end of the hallway where the coats waited on their pegs, the air still carrying that mild warmth and the last traces of cinnamon. Paz took their own jacket down carefully, feeling the soft fabric against their wrists, and Neighbor Sami did the same. No rush filled the moment; they simply moved side by side past the now-firm music stands and the neat stools. Outside light had begun to deepen toward evening, yet inside the hallway stayed calm and kind, the silver joints of every stand catching quiet gleams.

Paz and Neighbor Sami paused one last time near the tall window before heading out, listening to the soft hush that filled the school hallway of pegs, stools, and music stands. The music stand that would not tighten now stood straight and ready among the others, its work done for the day because Paz had noticed and shared the holding without a single word of praise. Cooler evening air drifted in as the courtyard door rested shut, and Paz felt the gentle change of temperature on their face while they buttoned their jacket. They both looked once more at the tidy space they had left behind, the coats hanging still, the stools aligned, everything peaceful. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
