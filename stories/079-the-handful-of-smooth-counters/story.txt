Uncle Idris stood already in the school cloakroom, mid-task, brushing soft lint from the sleeves of the hanging coats while the late-afternoon light slanted gold across the pegs. Dori stepped inside and felt the mild chill of the tiled floor through their socks, then paused to watch Uncle Idris work with quiet care. The air held a faint freshness from the open door, and Dori breathed it in while noticing how the coats swayed just a little. “Those scarves look tidy now,” Dori said, and Uncle Idris only nodded with a gentle smile. Dori ran one finger along a smooth wooden peg and listened to the soft shuffle of fabric. Beyond the open arch the lunch mats waited in calm rows, and Dori felt ready to cross toward them.

Dori walked forward onto the first lunch mat and felt its thin cushion give under their feet like a quiet cloud. Uncle Idris followed a moment later, still holding a small cloth he had been using, and the warm glow from the high window painted soft rectangles on the floor. A handful of smooth game counters lay scattered near the edge of the mats, left from an earlier quiet game, their round edges catching the light. Dori crouched and gathered them carefully into both palms, feeling how cool and polished they were against their skin. “I found the counters right here,” Dori said softly. The space smelled clean and open, and Dori could hear the distant hush of the empty hallway while they sat back on their heels.

Milo appeared in the doorway just then, shoes in hand, and the light around him looked pale and gentle. Dori looked up and saw Milo’s hopeful face, then glanced again at the handful of smooth game counters resting in their own hands. It would not feel fair if only one person were served first with all of them. Dori turned the counters slowly, noticing how they clicked together with a tiny sound like pebbles in a stream. “Come sit with me on the mat, Milo,” Dori called. The air near the floor felt cooler than the air near the coats, and Dori shifted so there would be room for three.

Uncle Idris settled cross-legged nearby without a word, the cloth still folded in his lap, and the quiet of the cloakroom wrapped around them like a soft blanket. Dori began to divide the handful of smooth game counters into smaller, even piles so Milo would be included right away. One counter rested in Dori’s palm and felt exactly like a warm coin, smooth and comforting after the cooler ones. Dori placed three counters in front of Milo, three in front of Uncle Idris, and kept three for themself, making sure each share matched. “These are for you, and these are for you, and these are mine,” Dori said. The mats smelled faintly of soap and clean cotton, and the light made little golden dots dance across the counters.

Milo picked up one counter and turned it over, smiling at the even piles, while outside the open window the wind in the fig leaves made a low, steady rustle that reached them like a friendly whisper. Dori listened to that soft leafy sound and felt glad the shares were the same size. Uncle Idris watched the three small groups of counters and simply let the choice stand, offering no speech at all, only a calm nod that made Dori’s shoulders loosen. Dori reached out and straightened a wrinkled corner of the nearest lunch mat with one careful toe, pressing the fabric flat so it looked neat again. The temperature in the room stayed mild and pleasant, neither too warm nor too cool, and Dori breathed slowly while looking at the tidy piles.

Dori and Milo began a quiet little game of sliding the counters one by one across the mat, listening to the tiny clicks they made against one another. Uncle Idris joined by sliding his own counters in gentle arcs that stopped just short of the others, and the light from the window grew softer as the afternoon deepened. Dori felt the woven texture of the mat under their knees and liked how steady it felt. “Your turn next, Uncle Idris,” Dori said, waiting with bright eyes. The whole cloakroom seemed hushed and safe, filled only with the small sounds of the counters and the distant hush beyond the door.

After a few more slow turns, Dori gathered the counters once more and stacked them into one neat tower so they would not roll away. The stack rose like a tiny tower of polished stones, and Dori could smell the light sweet scent of pear skins drifting in from a lunch bag left open on a nearby bench. Milo leaned closer to look at the tower, and Uncle Idris rested his hands on his knees in peaceful stillness. Dori felt proud of the even shares they had made and of how everyone had a place in the game. The air near the open bag carried that gentle pear-skin fragrance, and Dori inhaled it while the soft light touched the edges of every counter.

Dori decided the counters should rest safely until another day, so they scooped them into a small cloth pouch hanging from a peg and pulled the drawstring closed with careful fingers. Milo helped by holding the pouch steady, and Uncle Idris watched with quiet approval that needed no extra words. The cloakroom grew a little dimmer as clouds drifted past the sun, yet the remaining light stayed kind and golden along the floor. Dori felt the smooth fabric of the pouch and the cool metal of its tiny ring, then hung it back on the peg where it belonged. “We can play again tomorrow with the same shares,” Dori said. Everything felt settled and calm, the lunch mats smooth once more, and the coats hanging still.

Dori sat a moment longer between Milo and Uncle Idris, listening to the soft breathing of the quiet room and feeling how the day had slowed into evening peace. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Dori watched its warm circle of light touch the lunch mats and the edge of the cloakroom floor, then leaned gently against Uncle Idris’s side while Milo rested nearby. The air held a last trace of coolness from the tiles, and Dori’s eyelids grew heavy in the gentle glow. No one spoke now; the three of them simply stayed close on the mats, the counters safe in their pouch, the coats neat on their pegs, and the whole space wrapped in stillness ready for rest.
