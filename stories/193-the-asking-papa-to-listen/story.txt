The warm smell of toasted sesame drifted from a paper bag on the low table as Zola’s hands already busied themselves smoothing the round cushions into a gentle ring on the library carpet. Late afternoon light poured through the high windows in quiet golden bars that warmed the tops of Zola’s shoes and made the book spines glow. They pressed each cushion flat with careful palms, feeling how the fabric gave a little, while Auntie June sat nearby and watched with a soft smile that asked for nothing. Greta set three picture books in the middle of the circle and whispered that the stories looked friendly today. Zola kept working, arranging one more cushion so everyone would have a place, and the calm air of the library held them like a steady hug. Outside, footsteps passed the big front doors, but inside everything stayed peaceful and ready.

Zola settled onto their cushion and felt the cool temperature of the rug against their ankles while the rest of the story circle filled in. Papa sat just behind them on a chair, close enough that Zola could see the kind lines around his eyes when they turned. Auntie June opened a book with slow pages that made a tiny whisper, and Greta leaned in with bright interest. Zola’s heart beat a little faster because they wanted Papa to listen to the special page they had practiced at home before the day ended, yet the words felt stuck behind their teeth. Soft lamp light from the corner shelf painted warm circles on the floor. Auntie June’s voice stayed gentle as she began the first tale about a mouse who planted seeds, and Zola listened with their whole body while their fingers traced the edge of their cushion.

When the first story finished, a soft hush settled and Zola heard the distant click of a gate latch from the garden path outside the library windows. The sound came clear and neat, then faded into quiet again. Zola swallowed and looked at Papa’s kind face, still feeling shy about asking him to listen before the day ended. Greta patted Zola’s knee once in a friendly way that said no hurry. Auntie June closed the book and rested her hands in her lap, the light from the windows growing a little softer as clouds drifted. Zola breathed in the calm library air and decided that pretending not to care would leave a hollow spot inside, so they would try the smallest brave version instead. Their toes curled against the rug for courage that needed no big speech.

Auntie June noticed Zola shifting and offered a kind look without pushing for any show. “Would anyone like to share a favorite page today?” she asked in a voice as warm as tea. Greta raised her hand and spoke about a blue bird in her book, then grew quiet again. Zola felt the smooth wood of the low table under one hand where it reached the circle’s edge. Papa waited patiently, his attention open like a quiet porch light. The temperature in the room stayed comfortable, neither too cool nor too warm, and Zola gathered the words the way they had gathered the cushions earlier. They sat up a little straighter on their seat and practiced the question inside their mind once more.

Zola turned toward Papa and spoke in a voice that could be heard, clear though small. “Papa, will you listen to my page before the day ends?” they said, holding the book they had brought from the middle pile. Papa’s eyes brightened at once. “I would love to listen, Zola,” he answered, leaning forward so his elbows rested on his knees. Auntie June smiled and kept her hands still, showing she would not ask for anything louder or bigger. Greta gave a tiny nod of happiness. Soft shadows stretched longer across the carpet as the light changed, and Zola felt the solid floor steady beneath them while they opened to the special page with the painted garden.

Zola read the short lines about a rabbit who shared carrots, their voice steady enough for Papa and the circle to hear every word. Papa listened with his whole face, nodding at the parts Zola liked best. “That rabbit sounds very thoughtful,” Papa said when they finished. Auntie June added, “Thank you for sharing it with us, Zola,” her tone easy and proud without any fuss. Greta clapped two quiet claps that sounded like soft rain on leaves. Zola felt warmth rise in their cheeks that came from gladness rather than worry, and the library air carried a peaceful stillness after the words. They closed the book gently and set it back in the center where the light still touched its cover.

After the sharing, Auntie June rose and smoothed the front of her cotton apron with slow hands before she gathered the remaining books into a neat stack. The fabric looked soft and well-washed under the fading window light. Zola helped by handing her one last volume, and their fingers brushed the apron’s edge for a moment. Papa stood too and waited beside Zola’s cushion. Greta stretched her arms up high and yawned a happy yawn. The room held the quiet after stories the way a shell holds the sound of the sea, gentle and complete. Zola felt the day’s edges growing softer already, and they knew the asking had fit just right inside the circle.

Before leaving, Zola noticed one chair that had drifted crooked near the low table, so they set the chair square to the table with careful pushes until its legs lined up neat. The wood felt smooth and cool under their palms. Papa watched and gave a small smile of thanks for the tidy care. Auntie June locked the story basket with a quiet click and thanked everyone for coming. Greta waved and headed toward her own grown-up by the door. Evening light now slanted low and honey-colored across the shelves, and Zola walked beside Papa with easy steps, the library carpet muffling every footfall. They carried the finished asking inside them like a smooth stone in a pocket.

On the short walk home the sky turned the color of soft blueberries while streetlamps blinked awake one by one. Zola held Papa’s hand and felt the mild evening air brush their cheeks. At their own front steps Papa unlocked the door, and inside the house smelled faintly of clean soap and supper waiting. Zola washed their hands at the sink where warm water ran over their fingers, then climbed into pajamas that felt cozy against their skin. They brushed their teeth while the bathroom light glowed mild and yellow. Papa sat on the edge of the bed and listened one more time as Zola retold the rabbit’s carrot gift in sleepy whispers. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Zola curled under the blanket, eyes heavy, and drifted toward sleep with Papa’s quiet breathing nearby and the whole calm day folded safely around them.
