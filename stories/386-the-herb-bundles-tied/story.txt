The herb bundles lay in a soft mound on the wooden table, stems pale green and leaves curled like tiny sleeping hands, each one still holding a faint dusty green that caught the pale gold light from the tall hall windows. Ilaria leaned close enough to see the fine hairs along one stem and the way the leaves overlapped in gentle folds. They touched a stem with one careful finger and felt how cool and dry it was, ready for folding yet easy to crush if someone hurried. Beside the pile sat a short length of leather lace meant for tying, its surface smooth and slightly worn from earlier handling. Ilaria breathed in the quiet air of the school hall being set for a gentle fair and smiled at the simple work waiting.

Ilaria called softly across the table where Kesi was unfolding a clean cloth. “We should fold first and then tie, one after the other,” Ilaria said, their voice calm in the wide room. Kesi nodded and stepped nearer so the two of them stood side by side under the mild ceiling lights. Grandpa Sol sat in a chair nearby with a basket of extra stems on his lap, watching without reaching forward. The hall smelled faintly of polished floors and distant baking, and the air felt pleasantly cool against Ilaria’s bare arms as they began to gather a small handful of herbs. They worked slowly so nothing tore.

Ilaria took the folding job and laid each stem across their palm, bending the leaves into a neat packet no thicker than a finger. Kesi waited with the leather lace ready, ready to wrap only after the fold was true. “If we both try to do everything at once it will tangle,” Kesi whispered, and Ilaria answered, “Yes, slow is better.” Grandpa Sol spoke once in his warm low voice. “Count to four while you fold, Ilaria, and name the knot a simple loop when you are ready.” The sound of a cat’s collar bell drifted from the far doorway as a friendly cat wandered past the stacked chairs, the tiny chime clear and soft before fading again. Ilaria kept their eyes on the green leaves and felt the coolness of the table edge under their wrists.

They finished the first fold and passed the little bundle to Kesi, who slid the leather lace around it twice and pulled gently until the herbs held without squeezing. Ilaria watched the way the lace tightened and then began another fold, taking time to tuck every leaf edge inward. The light from the windows had shifted a little, warmer now on the backs of their hands, and the hall stayed quiet except for the soft scrape of chair legs being moved by other helpers far away. Grandpa Sol only counted aloud once more when Ilaria asked, his numbers slow and even so the work never rushed. Ilaria felt proud of the growing row of finished bundles and kept their breathing steady.

Halfway through the pile Ilaria noticed a basket of clean wool cloths someone had left for the fair stalls, and when they brushed past it the smell of soapy wool rose gently into the air, clean and familiar like laundry day at home. They smiled and kept folding, making each packet the same size so Kesi could tie without adjusting. “Grandpa Sol, is this the loop knot you meant?” Kesi asked while holding up one finished bundle. “It is exactly that,” Grandpa Sol replied, still seated and offering only the name and a quiet nod. Ilaria and Kesi traded places for a few moments so each could feel both jobs, still keeping the work divided and unhurried. The temperature in the hall stayed mild and even, never too warm.

Ilaria returned to folding while Kesi returned to tying, and together they moved down the long table until only a few stems remained. They lined a row of small spoons by size at the edge of the table for the fair’s tea stall, placing the tiniest first and the largest last so everything looked ready and peaceful. The spoons clinked once against one another with a soft bright sound before settling. Ilaria ran a thumb over the last herb stem and felt its dry coolness again, then folded it with the same care as the first. Grandpa Sol smiled but stayed quiet, letting the two friends finish what they had begun. The high windows now let in a softer afternoon glow that rested on the finished bundles like a blanket of light.

When the final bundle was tied Ilaria and Kesi stepped back together and looked at the neat line they had made. “We did it without hurrying,” Ilaria said, their cheeks warm with quiet happiness. Kesi answered, “And nothing broke or came loose.” Grandpa Sol rose at last and rested one hand lightly on the table edge. “A good count and a named knot keep the work kind,” he said, then sat again so the children could place the bundles into a shallow basket lined with soft cloth. Ilaria felt the smooth wood of the basket handle and the gentle weight of the herbs inside. The air around them held the last traces of coolness from the open side doors.

Ilaria carried the basket a few steps toward the fair display table while Kesi walked beside them, both moving at the same easy pace. They set the basket down where the light fell most evenly and straightened one corner of the cloth so every bundle showed its neat tie. Grandpa Sol followed with the leftover stems and tucked them away without fuss. Ilaria stood still for a moment and listened to the distant quiet voices of other helpers arranging flowers and paper flags. The hall felt settled and ready, the temperature gentle on their skin, and the work of folding and tying now complete because they had chosen to share it slowly.

Ilaria sat on a low bench near the window with Kesi and Grandpa Sol close by, the basket of herb bundles resting safely on the floor at their feet. They watched the last of the afternoon light settle across the polished boards and felt the calm that comes after careful work done side by side. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Ilaria leaned their head against the warm wood of the bench back and closed their eyes for a breath, smelling only the clean hall air and hearing nothing louder than their own quiet heartbeat, ready now for the gentle fair and the easy rest that waited at home.
