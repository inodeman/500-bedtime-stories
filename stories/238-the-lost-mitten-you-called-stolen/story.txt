A tiny bright ring sounded near the tall bookshelves, like one soft silver note floating through still air. Lior turned their head toward the noise and watched the library cat stretch, shake its collar, and settle again on a cushion. Warm afternoon light poured through the round window and laid pale gold circles across the library table where Lior sat among open picture books. Lior rested both elbows on the cool smooth wood and followed dust motes drifting in the beam. The air felt quiet and safe. When Lior reached for a crayon that had rolled near the chair leg, their fingers brushed something soft and forgotten. It was a single blue mitten, a little dusty, with one small hole near the thumb.

Lior lifted the mitten into their lap and turned it slowly. For a short moment Lior thought about tucking it deep into their backpack and saying nothing. The mitten looked lonely, and Lior almost decided to keep it as if it had been left on purpose. Cool air drifted from the round window and brushed Lior’s cheeks while they held the knit close. Lior’s own wool sleeve brushed the table edge with a gentle scratchy feel as they shifted in the chair. The blue yarn carried a faint outdoor scent of dry leaves. Lior glanced across the quiet room and saw Miss Dalca arranging picture books on a low wooden cart.

Miss Dalca hummed a soft tune while she worked. Lior almost slid the mitten under a tall stack of books to hide it, feeling the secret idea press against their ribs. Then Lior took one slow breath and thought how steady it felt to share real things. The wooden chair creaked once when Lior rocked gently and let it stop by itself. From the staff desk drifted the warm sweet smell of cocoa in a mug Miss Dalca must have left there. Lior hugged the mitten against their sweater and stood carefully so the chair legs would not scrape the floor.

“Miss Dalca,” Lior said in a clear soft voice, “I found this blue mitten under the table by the round window.” Miss Dalca turned, and her eyes grew kind. She walked over with quiet steps on the thick carpet. Lior held out the mitten so the window light could show the little hole. “I almost hid it in my bag,” Lior added, “because it looked so empty and I wanted to keep it safe myself.” Miss Dalca took the mitten gently and turned it over in her hands. The afternoon sun warmed the backs of Lior’s hands while they waited without rushing.

“Thank you for telling me the plain truth, Lior,” Miss Dalca said. “This mitten belongs in our reading-corner basket. Someone dropped it yesterday, and I looked everywhere.” She smiled with no teasing in her voice at all. Lior felt the smooth polished edge of the table under their fingertips and watched Miss Dalca sit down beside them. The room stayed pleasantly warm from the low heater vents near the floorboards. Bram, Lior’s friend, peeked from behind a shelf with a curious face but stayed quiet so Lior could finish speaking.

“Can we mend the hole together?” Lior asked. Miss Dalca nodded and opened a small sewing tin she kept in her desk drawer. She drew out blue thread that matched almost perfectly. Lior watched the thin needle catch the light as Miss Dalca showed them how to hold the fabric steady between careful fingers. Soft carpet muffled every footstep around the table. Lior pinched the edges of the hole while Miss Dalca made tiny neat stitches that closed the gap. The mitten grew whole again under their shared work, and Lior felt a calm pride settle in their chest like a folded blanket.

“You did a careful job holding it still, Lior,” Miss Dalca said. Lior grinned and rubbed the finished spot with one fingertip. The yarn felt even and strong now. Outside the round window a small bird hopped along a bare branch, and its shadow flickered once across the tabletop. Lior and Miss Dalca set the mended mitten in the center of the table so it could rest in the light. Lior leaned closer and saw how the blue color looked richer in the slanted gold. Bram came all the way over and whispered that the mitten looked perfect again.

Lior picked up a picture book and turned the thick pages slowly. The paper felt smooth and heavy under their thumbs. Miss Dalca lifted her mug and the faint warmth of leftover steam brushed the air near Lior’s shoulder. “I am glad I told you right away,” Lior said. Miss Dalca patted Lior’s shoulder once with a gentle hand. Together they stacked the crayons back into their box with soft clicking sounds. The library felt peaceful as the light outside the glass began to soften into early evening blue.

Lior sat back and watched the last daylight fade behind the trees beyond the round window. Miss Dalca lit the small lantern that always waited on the wide sill. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Lior felt the mild heat from the lantern glass reach their face like a friendly hand. They tucked their hands into their own warm pockets and listened to the gentle hush of the room. Bram waved goodbye from the doorway, and Lior waved back with a quiet smile. Miss Dalca returned the mended mitten to the basket, and everything felt settled and right. Lior closed their eyes for one calm moment and breathed in the peace of the library table by the round window.
