"Would you like to share this spot by the rail with me?" Anil said in a gentle voice that rose just above the soft engine hum. The mild evening temperature brushed Anil's cheeks and bare arms while they stood on the ferry deck that offered a sturdy wooden rail and only two elbows of room beside the cabin wall. Neighbor Sami sat on a low crate nearby and watched with quiet interest yet said nothing so the children could arrange things themselves. Anil noticed Nils standing just outside the small circle of friends who leaned and talked together. Golden light from the low sun spilled across the planks and made the water sparkle as Anil considered how the tight space might open for one more person.

Anil slid a half step sideways along the rail and felt the solid boards press firmly under their sneakers. Right beside Nils sat the scrap basket from the earlier folding time on board and inside it rested the paper crown still unused among bright paper bits. Anil kept close to Nils so their friend would not feel separate any longer. The air held a clean river freshness while Anil quietly shifted a folded blanket and a small stool to reshape the seating. Neighbor Sami peeled fruit without comment and the children remained free to decide the new places. Soft reflections of sky danced on the moving water below the rail.

The sweet smell of pear skins drifted from the peels Neighbor Sami let fall into a little bowl on the crate. Anil breathed it in and felt ready to change the pace of their gathering so everyone belonged. "If we sit this way we all fit side by side," Anil told the group without raising any fuss. Nils edged forward into the opened space near the scrap basket and the paper crown. Anil smiled at how the light caught the edges of the crown still resting there. Cooler shadows stretched longer across the deck as the ferry glided past quiet banks and Anil kept the mood unhurried.

Anil slipped a hand into their pocket and closed fingers around a warm coin whose smooth metal felt pleasant and steady. That familiar texture helped Anil stay calm while they finished opening a true place for Nils between the rail and the cabin wall. "You can look at the crown with me if you like," Anil whispered so the words stayed private and ordinary rather than any loud prize. Nils stepped fully into the two elbows of room and rested both elbows on the rail at last. Neighbor Sami nodded once in approval yet left every choice to the children. Gentle vibrations from the ferry floor hummed upward through Anil's feet.

The friends settled into the new seating with Anil linking the circle so no one stayed at the edge. Anil lifted the paper crown from the scrap basket and passed it to Nils in the same easy manner one might share a leaf or a pebble. "It feels light like a bird feather," Nils said while turning it once in the amber light. Anil agreed and they all watched the distant shore slide by at a peaceful speed. Warm leftover day heat rose from the wooden planks under their shoes even as the air cooled. Anil kept every turn slow so Nils could speak or stay quiet as they wished.

When the ferry rounded a green bend Anil heard the sound of wind in the fig leaves carried from the trees along the bank. The soft rustle blended with water sounds and made the deck feel even calmer. Anil suggested they take turns speaking one after another at an easy pace instead of rushing. "Your story can come first today," Anil said to Nils with a small smile. Neighbor Sami finished the last pear and wiped their hands on a cloth while still letting the children lead. Faint lantern light already glowed from inside the cabin windows and mixed with the fading outdoor brightness.

Anil and Nils stood shoulder to shoulder with room enough for their elbows and the paper crown resting between them on the rail. Anil described the shapes of drifting clouds and Nils pointed out silver fish that leapt far ahead. "I like watching the water this close," Nils said and Anil nodded in shared delight. Another friend asked about the folded points of the crown and Anil explained the creases slowly so everyone could listen. Soft pink light spread across the sky and painted Anil's hands where they held the rail. The changed seating and unhurried pace held everyone inside the fun together.

Time moved gently while Anil guarded the open places and the easy rules they had chosen. Anil watched Nils laugh at a quiet joke about floating leaves and felt the whole group stay connected. Neighbor Sami packed away the pear bowl and sat back with folded hands. "Shall we tuck the crown back into the scrap basket for another day?" Anil asked. Nils and the others agreed it was a fine idea and the paper crown returned to its soft nest of scraps. Cooler evening air brushed Anil's neck as the ferry lights flickered on along the rail.

After the ferry eased to the dock Anil led the short walk into the small waiting room that still smelled of river air. Anil and Nils sat on a bench near Neighbor Sami while the paper crown rested once more in the scrap basket at their feet. Anil used one toe to straighten the corner of a woven mat that had curled upward. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Warm indoor air wrapped around Anil like a familiar blanket after the open deck and they listened to the quiet breathing of their friends. Nils leaned against Anil's shoulder in the peaceful light and the whole crossing felt complete with every person holding a true place.
