Nana Ruth sat already at the long ceramic studio table, her fingers dipping into a bowl of cool slip that smelled faintly of wet earth as she pressed a soft clay handle onto a mug that needed time to firm. Warm lamp light pooled across the wood surface and touched Sable’s bare arms with gentle heat while they leaned close enough to hear the quiet scrape of her work. Sable’s eyes kept drifting to the small stone mortar near the edge where the herb salt waited under its cloth cover, still needing its long rest before anyone could taste it. “Nana Ruth, may I lift the cloth just for a moment?” Sable asked in a low voice. Nana Ruth smiled without looking up and answered, “Not yet, Sable. It is still settling its flavors.” Outside a gate latch clicked shut with a soft metal sound that drifted through the open window and made the room feel tucked in and safe.

Sable rested both elbows on the table and felt the smooth grain of the wood under their skin while the slip on the mug handles shone wet and pale in the golden light. The covered mortar sat quiet and heavy, holding the herb salt that Sable wanted so much to check, yet the cloth stayed in place like a little blanket. Nana Ruth kept smoothing another handle with slow care, and the cool air from the window brushed Sable’s cheek. “I keep thinking about the salt,” Sable said softly. Nana Ruth nodded and replied, “I know, Sable. We leave it covered so it can finish in its own time.” Sable breathed in and noticed a warm cinnamon scent rising faintly from the mortar even through the cloth, sweet and spicy at once.

Around them the ceramic pieces stood drying in neat rows, their surfaces turning matte as the slip set and the handles grew sturdy under the steady room temperature. Sable’s fingers found a wooden spoon lying nearby and traced its walnut-smooth spoon bowl that felt silky and perfect against their thumb. They wanted to peek again, yet they remembered Nana Ruth’s words and pulled their hand back. Chioma had stepped in earlier to fetch a brush and now sat on a stool humming, but Sable stayed focused on the table. “The handles look almost ready,” Sable whispered. Nana Ruth answered, “Almost, Sable. Just like the salt. We give them quiet minutes.”

Sable decided then to leave the cloth alone and instead picked up a soft sponge to dab extra slip from the edge of a plate that needed the same calm wait. The sponge felt damp and cool in their palm, and the lamp light made tiny sparkles on the wet clay. Outside the evening air carried that same cinnamon whisper mixed with the studio’s clay smell, and Sable worked slowly so nothing would jostle the mortar. Nana Ruth passed them a clean cloth and said, “Well done choosing the quiet way, Sable.” Sable answered, “I can wait with you,” and kept dabbing in small circles.

They moved together along the table, checking each handle with the lightest touch so the clay could keep setting without hurry. Sable felt the pleasant coolness of the unfinished rims under their fingertips and heard only the soft rustle of cloth and the distant settling of the house. The herb salt stayed covered and peaceful in its mortar, and Sable no longer reached for it. Instead they hummed four gentle notes under their breath and then left a rest of silence while the sponge rested too. Nana Ruth glanced over with warm eyes and the room held that calm temperature that made waiting feel easy.

Chioma returned with a fresh bowl of water that smelled clean and cool, setting it down carefully so no splash reached the drying pieces. Sable took the walnut-smooth spoon once more and used it only to stir a bit of leftover slip in a cup, feeling again how polished and friendly the wood was. The cinnamon scent drifted past one last time as a soft reminder of the resting salt. “Does the salt need much longer?” Sable asked. Nana Ruth smiled and said, “A little more, Sable. Listen to how quiet everything has become.” Sable listened and heard nothing hurried at all.

Light from the window shifted lower and turned the clay handles a soft peach color while Sable wiped the last of the tools clean with a dry towel that felt fluffy against their wrists. They stayed close to Nana Ruth and kept their hands busy with small tidy jobs so the mortar could finish without any interruption. The air stayed mild and still, perfect for drying and resting both. Sable felt proud of the choice to leave the cloth in place and simply work beside the table until the salt was ready on its own.

Time moved gently as the slip grew firmer and the handles held their shapes with quiet strength under the warm lingering light. Sable stacked the clean sponges and hummed those same four notes again, then rested in the silence that followed, matching the peaceful wait of the herb salt. Nana Ruth rested a hand briefly on Sable’s shoulder and the touch felt steady and kind. No one lifted the cloth yet, and that was exactly right.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Sable stood with Nana Ruth as the last handles finished setting and the covered mortar waited in its own complete calm, the cinnamon note faded to memory, the gate latch long quiet, and the walnut-smooth spoon put away. Sable smiled in the gentle light and felt the mild evening air on their face while everything rested exactly as it needed to, ready whenever the right moment arrived.
