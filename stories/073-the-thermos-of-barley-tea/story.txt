Miss Dalca was already kneeling by the ferry bench, mid-task as she patted and straightened the folded blanket until every corner lay neat and even under the mild evening light. Yael walked up along the damp planks and felt the cool harbor breeze slip across their arms like a soft scarf. The wooden seat felt solid when Yael rested one hand on it, and the water made quiet little sounds against the stones below. Miss Dalca looked over with calm eyes. “I am fixing our spot so we can sit warm while we wait,” she said. Yael nodded and settled carefully beside the blanket, watching how the low sun painted silver streaks on the gentle surface of the bay.

Yael noticed the thermos of barley tea standing upright against the harbor wall only a few steps from the bench, its metal side catching a pale gleam. They slid a little closer on the blanket and breathed the cool air that smelled faintly of salt and old wood. Idris came along the path then, waving one hand, and sat on Yael’s other side so the three of them formed a quiet row. The light from the sky had turned soft blue-gray, and Yael felt the smooth fabric of the blanket under their palms. “The water looks like glass tonight,” Idris said. Yael smiled and kept their gaze on the thermos, thinking how the steam inside must still be warm.

Miss Dalca wore her familiar cotton apron over her clothes, and when Yael leaned near to help tuck one edge of the blanket, they felt the soft, slightly worn texture of that cotton apron against the back of their hand. The apron smelled clean and kind. Yael stayed close and listened to the low creak of the ferry ropes. A distant gate latch clicked shut somewhere behind the harbor sheds, a short clear sound that settled into the quiet. Yael glanced at the thermos again and felt the evening air grow a little cooler against their cheeks. Miss Dalca stayed busy with the blanket folds and said nothing more for a while.

Yael stood for a moment and walked the few steps to the harbor wall where the thermos waited. They lifted it carefully, feeling the solid weight and the faint heat still held inside. Back at the bench they set it down between them all. The smell of toasted sesame drifted from a small paper packet Miss Dalca had brought for later, warm and nutty in the cool air. Yael sat again and looked from the thermos to Idris and then to Miss Dalca. The light had softened further, wrapping the bench in gentle dusk. Yael’s fingers brushed the blanket’s edge once more while they thought.

“I think we should all have some,” Yael said, their voice steady and clear in the open air. They unscrewed the top of the thermos and the mild scent of barley tea rose up, mixing with the cooler night. Three small cups from Miss Dalca’s basket waited nearby. Yael poured a smaller even amount into each cup so the tea would stretch the same for everyone. Idris watched with bright eyes. “That looks just right,” Idris said softly. The wooden bench held steady beneath them, and Yael felt the cool metal of the thermos lid against their palm as they set it aside.

Miss Dalca watched the careful pouring without speaking a word of direction. She simply accepted the cup Yael held out and kept her hands quiet around it. Yael handed the next cup to Idris and kept the last for themself. The tea tasted warm and plain and good against the chill that had settled on the harbor. Yael sipped slowly and listened to the water’s soft slap against the wall. The folded blanket stayed smooth under their legs, and the last light of day made everything look kind and still. No one hurried. Yael felt glad that the shares matched.

They all sat together while the ferry lights began to shine farther out on the water. Yael leaned a little toward Idris so their shoulders almost touched. “We can drink it while we wait,” Yael said. Idris nodded and held the cup with both hands to keep the warmth. Miss Dalca smiled a small private smile and took another quiet sip. The air carried the faintest remaining smell of the tea and the cooler night together. Yael felt the gentle sway of the bench when a small wave bumped the pier, and they kept their cup steady. Everything around them stayed peaceful and close.

After a while the ferry horn gave one low call across the bay. Yael helped Miss Dalca gather the empty cups and screw the thermos lid back on. They stood, and Yael brushed the blanket free of any tiny crumbs before folding it once more into a neat square. The wooden planks felt firm under their shoes. Idris carried the thermos for a few steps, and Yael walked beside them. Miss Dalca moved at the same calm pace, her cotton apron shifting lightly with each step. The evening had grown fully soft now, and the harbor lamps glowed in quiet rows.

They boarded without rush and found seats inside where a small window looked back toward the wall they had left. Yael settled the blanket across their lap and Idris’s. Miss Dalca sat nearby and rested her hands. The cabin felt pleasantly warm after the open air. Yael watched the dark water slide past and felt the steady thrum of the boat. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Yael leaned against the seat back, eyelids heavy, and kept the shared quiet of the evening wrapped around them like the blanket itself.
