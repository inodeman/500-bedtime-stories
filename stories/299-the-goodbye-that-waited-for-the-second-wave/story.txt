The soft smell of pear skins rose from the open basket as Delia’s busy hands tucked the last smooth fruit beside the folded napkins and checked the wooden lid twice. Warm afternoon light lay across the ferry deck, turning the metal rail a gentle gold while Delia stood with Neighbor Sami and Ilaria in the narrow space that offered only two elbows of room. Delia’s fingers kept smoothing the cloth covering, pressing wrinkles flat, because the boat would soon pull away and the goodbye that waited so two waves could happen still needed its quiet moment. The deck boards felt steady under their shoes. Delia glanced at the water glinting beyond the rail and then back at the basket, making sure nothing would tip when the ferry gave its soft first nudge.

Ilaria stayed half a step outside the little circle, close enough to hear the water slap the hull yet not quite inside the easy space by the rail. Delia noticed how Ilaria’s shoulders angled toward the group without quite joining it, and the breeze brushed cool against Delia’s bare arms. Neighbor Sami held a small satchel and watched the children without speaking first, giving them the wide quiet they needed. Delia shifted the basket to free one hand and reached toward the rail, feeling the smooth metal already warm from the sun. “We can stand closer,” Delia said gently. Ilaria’s shoes scuffed the deck once, a tiny sound against the larger hush of the waiting boat.

Delia looked at the bench bolted near the rail and saw that the seating left Ilaria without a clear place, only the leftover corner that felt more like an afterthought than a seat. The air carried a faint salt taste, and Delia’s tongue noticed it while they thought. Neighbor Sami rested one elbow on the satchel and simply waited, letting the children find their own way. Delia slid the basket a hand-width sideways, then patted the open stretch of bench. “Sit here with me,” Delia said. “There is room if we share the middle.” Ilaria’s face brightened in the slanting light, and the space between them filled with the soft rustle of jackets as everyone adjusted.

They settled side by side, knees almost touching, while the ferry engine hummed low beneath the deck. Delia felt the warmth of Ilaria’s sleeve against their own and knew the goodbye that waited so two waves could happen would now include everyone properly. Neighbor Sami nodded once, a small approving motion that kept the pace calm. Delia slowed their own breathing to match the boat’s gentle rock. “We can wave twice,” Delia told Ilaria. “First when the ropes loosen, and second when we can still see each other clearly.” The plan felt steady, like a promise held in open hands, and the light on the water danced in quiet sparks.

Neighbor Sami opened the satchel just enough to pass Delia a warm coin that had rested in a pocket all afternoon. Delia closed their fingers around the warm coin, feeling its round edges and the faint heat that lingered from Sami’s hand, then slid it carefully into their own pocket for later. The texture stayed with them as a small comfort while the deck remained crowded yet kind. Ilaria leaned a little nearer, and Delia made sure the bench stayed shared without any rush. “Thank you for saving the second wave,” Ilaria whispered. Delia answered, “We wait together,” and the simple words settled over them like a soft blanket of sound.

The ferry lines began to ease, and Delia stood again with Ilaria so both could reach the rail at the same time. Cool air moved across their faces while Neighbor Sami stayed a step behind, still letting the children lead the goodbye. Delia raised one hand high, palm open, and Ilaria raised a hand too, the two waves beginning as the boat drifted a short distance from the dock. The first wave lasted long enough for smiles to travel across the widening gap. Delia kept their arm steady, shoulder warm from the shared motion, and the deck underfoot felt solid even as the water widened.

For a moment the second wave waited while the ferry turned slightly, giving everyone a clearer view. Delia and Ilaria held their hands ready, and Neighbor Sami’s quiet presence made the pause feel safe rather than empty. Then both children waved again, slower this time, fingers spreading wide against the bright sky. Delia felt the breeze lift a loose strand of hair across their forehead and heard, from the shore trees beyond the landing, the soft sound of wind in the fig leaves. The second wave finished just as the figures on the dock became smaller, and Delia lowered their arm with a contented sigh that matched Ilaria’s.

They returned to the bench together, the basket of pears resting safely between their feet, and the ferry settled into its easy rhythm across the water. Delia rested both palms on their knees, noticing how the late light had turned cooler and bluer along the horizon. Ilaria sat fully inside the circle now, no longer outside the fun, and Neighbor Sami offered a quiet smile that needed no extra words. Delia thought of the two waves that had finally happened because the goodbye had been willing to wait, and the shared bench still held the shape of their side-by-side seats. The metal rail behind them ticked softly as it cooled.

Later, after the ferry reached the far landing and the short walk home brought them to the familiar porch, Delia stepped inside where the evening air felt gentle on bare arms. They set the basket down, straightened a rug corner with one careful toe, and placed the warm coin on the little table. Ilaria’s laugh still echoed faintly in Delia’s memory, bright and easy. Neighbor Sami waved from the walk before turning toward home. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Delia’s hands finally rested, the pear skins’ sweetness still faint in the room, and the day folded itself into quiet like a well-kept promise.
