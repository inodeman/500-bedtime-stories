Ms. Okonkwo stood at the bakery counter already, her sleeves rolled as she brushed pale flour across a wide wooden board in the mild morning light that poured through the front glass. Soft voices of neighbors drifted in waves while trays clinked nearby, and the whole room held a gentle warmth that wrapped around Sora’s shoulders the moment they stepped inside with Kavi. Sora watched Ms. Okonkwo’s steady hands press and turn the dough, feeling the cool tile floor under their own shoes and noticing how the counter gleamed. “The dough feels ready now,” Ms. Okonkwo said in her quiet voice, and Sora smiled because the busy morning felt safe and full of ordinary kindness. Kavi stood close beside Sora, both of them breathing the calm air that smelled faintly of rising bread.

Sora and Kavi rested their elbows on the edge of a small side table near the counter where a reading lamp had been slid right to the middle so its golden pool of light covered the whole surface evenly. The lamp’s metal base felt smooth when Sora brushed a fingertip along it, and the busy morning sounds of spoons and soft talk made a steady hum around them. Sora looked at the plates of warm rolls waiting under that centered light and thought carefully about how it would not feel fair if only one person were served first while the lamp shone for everyone at once. Kavi shifted a little closer, and Sora could hear the quiet shuffle of their friend’s sneakers on the floorboards. The light from the lamp stayed bright and kind without harsh edges, inviting them both to stay.

Sora reached toward a plate of glazed rolls that sat half in the lamp’s glow and felt the faint heat rising from the fresh baking. “These look so soft,” Sora said, and Kavi nodded while watching the same plate. Ms. Okonkwo wiped her chalky hand on a clean towel and left a light dusty print that Sora noticed as the dry, powdery texture brushed near the edge of the counter. The morning stayed soft and busy with people moving gently past, yet Sora kept their attention on the table and the lamp that marked the shared middle space. A sweet ribbon of honey scent drifted up from the warm glaze, curling through the air right beside Sora’s nose and making the moment feel even gentler.

Sora decided the fair way would be to make smaller, even pieces so Kavi could share right away instead of waiting. “We can break them into little parts that match,” Sora said softly, and Kavi’s eyes brightened. Together they used clean napkins to divide two of the rolls into neat halves that fit side by side under the reading lamp’s centered light. The warm pieces felt tender between Sora’s fingers, and the soft busy morning continued around them with quiet footsteps and the distant clink of cups. Ms. Okonkwo watched from the counter without interrupting, her expression calm as she let the choice stand. Sora felt the pleasant temperature of the fresh bread and knew Kavi was included completely.

Outside, a gate latch clicked once with a clear metallic sound as someone entered the little garden path beside the bakery, and the sound reached Sora’s ears like a soft reminder that the day moved gently onward. Sora offered one half to Kavi and kept the matching half, both pieces still warmed by the lamp light that stayed fixed in the middle of the table. “Thank you for sharing the space with me,” Kavi said, and Sora answered, “It feels better this way for both of us.” The air near the counter held its mild warmth, and Sora noticed how the light made the honey glaze glisten without any rush. Ms. Okonkwo simply nodded once and returned to arranging more rolls on a tray, leaving Sora’s choice quiet and whole.

Sora and Kavi sat on the low stools beside the table and took small careful bites while the reading lamp continued to glow evenly across their shared halves. The texture of the bread was pillowy against Sora’s tongue, and the busy morning light from the windows mixed with the lamp’s softer gold. A single clock chime sounded from the wall, and Sora waited through that single chime without speaking, letting the note fade into the gentle clatter of the bakery. Kavi smiled across the table, and Sora felt the same calm settle in their chest. Everything stayed close and ordinary, with no hurry pushing them along.

Ms. Okonkwo brought over two small cups of warm milk and set them near the lamp without adding any extra words about what Sora had done. “The milk is just right,” Ms. Okonkwo said, and Sora answered, “It smells nice next to the rolls.” The cups felt smooth and solid in Sora’s hands, their heat a quiet comfort against the cooler air near the stools. Kavi lifted a cup too, and together they sipped while the reading lamp held its place in the exact middle. The soft busy morning carried on around the bakery counter with its familiar rhythms of measuring and stirring.

Sora noticed how the centered lamp made the whole table feel open for both friends at once, and they rested their free hand lightly on the wooden surface that still held a trace of morning coolness. Kavi leaned in so their shoulders almost touched, and the two of them finished the even pieces without any leftover worry. Outside light shifted a little brighter across the floor, yet the lamp’s glow stayed steady and kind. Sora breathed slowly and felt glad that the smaller shares had brought Kavi right into the circle under the light. Ms. Okonkwo moved back to her dough with quiet steps that matched the peaceful pace of the room.

Sora and Kavi stayed a little longer beside the bakery counter while the morning light softened at the edges and the last crumbs disappeared. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Sora felt the gentle hush settle around their shoulders like a warm blanket, and Kavi’s quiet breathing matched their own as they both watched the steady lamp in the middle of the table one last time before the day turned toward home.
