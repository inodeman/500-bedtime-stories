The thin wooden kite spar lay across a folded cloth on the bakery counter, and the careful line of wet glue along its edge gleamed like a narrow river under the soft golden lamps. Chioma bent close enough to see tiny air bubbles resting in the glue and to notice how the wood grain showed faintly through the clear shine. Warm air from the resting ovens drifted across their cheeks while bowls of pale dough and jars of dark jam sat unfinished nearby, filling the space with a gentle yeasty sweetness. Chioma’s fingertips hovered just above the spar, feeling the counter’s cool smoothness, and they said in a low voice, “I only want to press it once to know if the glue has set.” Mama stood a few steps away wiping a bowl, and the light made the glue look almost ready even though it still glistened wet. Chioma stayed still and watched the small reflections dance along the sticky line.

Mama glanced over and spoke softly so the quiet kitchen would not startle. “The glue needs its own slow time, Chioma,” she said, “just as the dough needs to rise and the jam needs to settle.” Chioma nodded yet kept their eyes fixed on the spar, smelling the warm flour that dusted every surface and hearing the low tick of the cooling racks. They wanted so much to check whether the line had grown firm, but the surface still looked liquid and bright. A leather lace from an old kite frame rested near the cloth, its worn texture rough and familiar under Chioma’s exploring fingers when they shifted their hand. The kitchen felt pleasantly warm around them, and Chioma pulled their hand back, deciding the glue was not yet ready no matter how still it seemed.

Chioma covered the kite spar with a light clean cloth so no flour would drift onto the wet glue, then stepped back with careful feet. Mama smiled and said, “That is a kind choice, Chioma. Shall we find a quiet task while we wait?” The air carried a faint scent of soapy wool from the soft cloths Mama had washed earlier and hung near the sink. Chioma answered, “Yes, Mama, I can line the spoons while the glue finishes.” A gentle clink sounded as they began gathering the metal spoons from the drying rack, and the lamps cast steady pools of light across the wooden floor. They felt the cool weight of each spoon and noticed how the dough bowls sat patiently under their own covers, just as the spar now rested under its cloth.

Joss, who had come to help tidy the bakery that evening, padded in with quiet steps and offered a friendly nod. Chioma held up two spoons of different sizes and said, “I am lining them from longest to shortest so everything is ready for morning.” Joss smiled without speaking much, and together they sorted the spoons along the side counter where the light fell evenly. The cat who sometimes visited the bakery wandered past the doorway, and the soft sound of a cat’s collar bell jingled once as it moved. Chioma felt the warm air curl around their ankles and smelled the sweet jam still waiting in its open jars. They kept working slowly, placing each spoon with care so the row grew neat and straight.

The spoons formed a shining line from largest to smallest, and Chioma ran one finger along the cool metal edges to make sure none sat crooked. Mama hummed a gentle tune while she checked the resting dough, and the kitchen remained calm under the steady lamps. Chioma glanced toward the covered kite spar but did not lift the cloth. Instead they whispered to themselves, “It will finish when it is ready.” The texture of the wooden counter felt smooth and solid beneath their elbows as they leaned to adjust the final spoon. Heat still drifted softly from the ovens that had been turned down, wrapping the room in a comfortable warmth that made waiting feel easy rather than hard.

Chioma wiped a few crumbs from the counter with a dry cloth and listened to the quiet creak of the floorboards under Mama’s shoes. The dough continued to rest in its bowls, rising little by little, and the jam sat glossy and still. Chioma thought about the kite spar under its cover and how the glue must be changing slowly without anyone watching. They said to Mama, “I like how the spoons look all in order now.” Mama replied, “They do look peaceful, Chioma, and so does the kitchen.” Light from the side window mixed with the lamp glow, making everything soft and golden. Chioma felt the mild temperature of the air on their bare arms and stayed near the counter, content to let minutes pass without hurry.

After the spoons were finished, Chioma helped Joss fold a few clean towels and stack them on a shelf. The towels felt soft against their hands, and the kitchen smelled gently of bread that had baked earlier in the day. Chioma kept glancing once or twice at the covered spar yet never lifted the cloth. Mama poured warm water into the sink for a final rinse of the mixing bowls, and the sound of water was low and steady. Chioma stood close enough to feel the faint steam rise and warm their face. They remembered the leather lace they had touched earlier and how patient the wood of the spar itself seemed while the glue dried. Everything in the bakery kitchen moved at a calm pace that matched the quiet task they had chosen.

When enough time had passed, Mama nodded toward the cloth. Chioma walked over slowly and lifted one corner with careful fingers. The line of glue along the kite spar no longer shone wet; it had turned clear and firm, holding the wood true. Chioma touched the edge very lightly and felt only solid wood and set glue, cool now under the kitchen air. “It finished by itself,” Chioma said with a small happy breath. Mama answered, “Just as we knew it would.” The lamps glowed evenly across the counter, and the dough bowls and jam jars still waited peacefully for their own next steps. Chioma felt proud of the neat spoon line and of the choice to leave the spar covered until the right moment arrived.

Chioma rested both hands on the counter and looked once more at the finished kite spar, the firm glue, and the tidy kitchen around them. Mama turned down the last lamp near the sink while Joss slipped quietly toward the door. The cat’s soft presence lingered only as a memory of the earlier quiet evening. Chioma breathed in the settled warmth of the room and felt ready for sleep. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
