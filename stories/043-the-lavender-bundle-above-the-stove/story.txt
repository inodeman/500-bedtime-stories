The clean scent of lemon soap rose from Kavi’s busy hands as they smoothed damp soil around the edges of the little seed pots lined up on the greenhouse bench. Warm light from the south window spilled across Kavi’s knuckles and made the clay rims look almost golden. Kavi pressed one more soft mound flat, feeling the cool grit and the slight give of the earth under their fingertips. The air inside the glass room held a gentle heat that never turned sharp, and far beyond the panes the afternoon settled quiet and bright. Kavi liked the way the pots sat in tidy rows, each one waiting for its own small green start.

Near the warm corner above the stove pipe that ran along the wall, a lavender bundle hung where the air was warm, not hot, wrapped in a thin cloth cover so the purple buds could rest. Kavi glanced up at it more than once while their hands stayed with the seed pots. The cloth looked soft and the stems peeked just a little at the edges. Kavi wanted very much to lift the cover and check whether the flowers had loosened their quiet scent yet. “Auntie June,” Kavi said, voice low and curious, “may I look under the cloth now? I think the lavender might be ready.” The south window glass felt smooth and sun-warmed when Kavi rested one palm against it for a moment.

Auntie June stepped closer, her shoes making a soft scuff on the wooden floorboards, and the faint breeze that slipped through the open vent carried the sound of wind in the fig leaves outside. She rested a calm hand on the bench edge near Kavi’s elbow. “The bundle needs a little more quiet time in that warm spot,” Auntie June said. “It finishes when it is ready, not when we peek.” Kavi nodded slowly, still watching the hanging cloth. The light through the south window shifted a shade softer as a cloud drifted past, and the seed pots glowed a gentle brown. Kavi kept their fingers on the rim of one pot and felt the cool clay circle.

Kavi drew a slow breath and decided to leave the lavender covered just as it was. “I will wait,” Kavi told Auntie June, and the words felt steady in the warm air. Auntie June smiled and handed Kavi a floured towel that smelled faintly of bread dough from the kitchen basket earlier. The towel’s dry, powdery texture brushed against Kavi’s palms as they folded it once and set it beside the seed pots. “Thank you for the towel,” Kavi said. “I can wipe the bench while the lavender rests.” The south window light rested on the towel’s white surface and made tiny flour specks sparkle.

Kavi began to wipe the table in slow circles, the floured towel gliding over the smooth wood in quiet arcs that left the bench cleaner and calmer. Each circle felt steady under Kavi’s hands, and the wood grew warmer where the sunlight touched it. Auntie June watered two of the seed pots with a tiny spout, and the water made a soft trickle sound against the soil. “You are doing careful work, Kavi,” Auntie June said. Kavi kept the circles slow and even, watching how the dust lifted and the grain of the wood appeared clearer. The lavender bundle hung still and covered above the warm air, and Kavi did not reach for it.

After a while Kavi set the towel aside and checked the seed pots again, touching the moist soil with one careful finger to feel its cool dampness. Remy the small striped caterpillar friend rested on a leaf at the far end of the bench, and Kavi spoke gently so as not to startle him. “Remy, we are waiting together,” Kavi whispered. The caterpillar stayed quiet on his leaf, and the greenhouse air felt pleasantly mild around Kavi’s bare arms. Outside the south window a bird called once and then fell silent. Kavi arranged three empty pots into a neat triangle and brushed a stray crumb of soil into the tray below.

Auntie June brought a small saucer of water and set it near the hanging bundle so the warm air could keep everything peaceful. Kavi watched the soft cloth cover move just a little when a draft passed, yet still they left it alone. “I like how the light looks on the seed pots now,” Kavi said. The late afternoon glow turned the soil a deeper brown and made the glass panes shine. Kavi traced one more slow circle on the bench with the edge of their hand, feeling the leftover smoothness from the wiping. The lavender stayed covered in its warm place, and the quiet task kept Kavi’s thoughts gentle and unhurried.

Time moved the way sunlight moves, slow and steady across the floorboards. Kavi counted the seed pots softly under their breath and then counted them again, noticing how each one held its own little promise. Auntie June sat on the low stool and mended a torn leaf label with careful stitches, the needle flashing once in the south window light. Kavi leaned their cheek against the cool edge of the bench for a moment and listened to the small greenhouse sounds—the tiny drip of leftover water, the hush of air along the glass. The covered lavender bundle rested exactly where it belonged, and Kavi felt content to let it stay that way until it finished in its own time.

When the light grew honey-colored and the seed pots cast long soft shadows, Kavi noticed a faint sweet breath beginning to slip past the edges of the cloth at last. Auntie June nodded as if she had expected it. Kavi smiled without rushing forward, hands folded loosely on the clean bench. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Outside the greenhouse the day folded itself toward evening, and inside Kavi stayed close to the quiet pots and the warm corner where the lavender had been allowed to finish, the air still mild and the wooden bench smooth under their resting palms.
