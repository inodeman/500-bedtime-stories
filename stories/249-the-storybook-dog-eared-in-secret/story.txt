The warm smell of cinnamon rose from a chipped mug at the edge of the bench while Kira’s busy hands pressed flat the soft, folded corner of a library book that had been dog-eared in secret. Wood shavings tickled their wrists, and little pots of glue sat open beside neat rows of paper labels waiting for names. Afternoon light slanted through the high window and made the varnish on the bench glow like honey. Kira’s thumbs kept sliding over the bent page as if the crease might vanish if they worked gently enough. Outside, a soft breeze stirred the curtains, and the room felt pleasantly cool against their bare arms. They almost slid the book under a stack of scrap wood, heart thumping, yet the truth sat heavy and plain in their chest.

Uncle Idris stepped closer, his boots quiet on the floorboards, and the cool air from the open door brushed Kira’s cheek. “What are you holding there, Kira?” he asked in his easy voice. Kira looked up at the kind lines around his eyes and felt the rough grain of the bench under their elbows. The book’s spine felt stiff and guilty in their grip. Labels fluttered slightly when a draft passed. Kira swallowed and kept their voice steady. “I folded the page on purpose last week so I could find my place again,” they said. “It is a library book, and I almost hid it just now.”

Uncle Idris nodded once and rested a warm hand on the bench near Kira’s. The wood felt solid and familiar under both their palms. “Thank you for telling me straight, Kira,” he said. “We can mend what can be mended.” No teasing colored his tone, only calm interest. He reached for a thin brush and a small dish of clear glue that caught the light like liquid glass. Kira watched the brush hairs shine and smelled the faint clean scent of the adhesive mixing with the earlier cinnamon. Their shoulders loosened. Together they turned the dog-eared corner with careful fingers.

Kira held the page steady while Uncle Idris dabbed the tiniest amount of glue along the crease. The workshop air felt warmer now near the lamp he switched on, a gentle yellow circle that lit the labels and the open book. Soft paper whispered under their shared touch. “Press it flat for a slow count of ten, Kira,” Uncle Idris murmured. Kira counted under their breath and felt the page grow smooth again beneath their fingertips. A distant gate latch clicked shut in the yard, a small metallic sound that settled the quiet around them. They both smiled without speaking for a moment, listening to the hush that followed.

Brisa appeared in the doorway with a tray of crackers, her footsteps light, and the cool shade of the hall clung to her sweater. “May I watch?” she asked softly. Kira nodded and scooted sideways on the bench so Brisa could see the mend taking shape. The three of them leaned in, and Kira explained in a low voice how the secret fold had happened and how the truth felt lighter once spoken. Uncle Idris handed Kira a walnut-smooth spoon to press the drying edge without leaving marks. The spoon’s polished curve felt cool and perfect against Kira’s palm as they rolled it gently along the paper. Light from the lamp painted gold edges on every tool.

They worked in easy silence for a while, the only sounds the soft scrape of the spoon and the distant birds beyond the window. Kira’s fingers learned the patient pace of mending, pressing, waiting, checking. Uncle Idris cut a fresh label and wrote the book’s title in clear letters so it could return looking cared for. “The library will be glad you brought it home honest,” he said. Kira felt a quiet pride warm their chest like the mug’s leftover heat. Brisa offered a cracker, and the salty crunch mixed with the remaining cinnamon sweetness in the air. Everything on the bench—wood, glue, labels—seemed to belong together now with the book resting open and free of its secret fold.

Kira hummed four notes, low and even, then left a rest so the workshop could breathe around the sound. The lamp’s light stayed steady and kind. Uncle Idris showed them how to wipe a drop of glue with a damp cloth until no trace remained. Kira practiced the motion twice, feeling the cloth’s soft dampness and the wood’s dry smoothness side by side. “We did it together,” Kira said, voice bright. Brisa clapped once, gently, so the glue would not be disturbed. The repaired corner lay flat and nearly invisible, and the book looked ready for its next reader. Evening coolness drifted in, pleasant against Kira’s neck.

Uncle Idris closed the cover with care and slid a protective sleeve over it while Kira watched every movement. The labels sat ready for other projects, and the glue pot received its lid with a quiet snap. Kira ran one finger along the mended edge and felt only smooth paper now. “I will return it tomorrow and tell the librarian what happened,” they promised. Uncle Idris squeezed their shoulder, warm and steady. Brisa gathered the tray, and the three of them stood in the golden circle of lamp light that made the whole bench look peaceful. Outside the last birds settled, and the room smelled clean and finished.

Kira stacked the remaining labels neatly and wiped the bench until the wood shone under their cloth. The air cooled further as dusk deepened, yet the workshop held its gentle warmth. They placed the mended library book in a clean bag beside the door. Uncle Idris turned down the main lamp so only a softer glow remained. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Kira felt the quiet settle into their bones like a blanket, hands finally still, the truth spoken and the pages smooth again, ready for sleep when the story of the day closed around them.
