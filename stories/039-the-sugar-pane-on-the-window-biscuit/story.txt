"May I peek under the cloth now, Nana Ruth?" Pema asked, standing by the ceramic studio table where warm afternoon light spilled across damp boards of slip and clay handles still soft enough to hold their shape.

Nana Ruth shook her head gently and rested one hand on the edge of the table so Pema could feel the steady cool of the wood. "Not yet, Pema. The window biscuit with the sugar pane needs more quiet time under that cloth, just like the slip and the handles. If we lift the cover too soon, the pane may not stay clear and smooth." Pema pressed their lips together and looked at the neat white cloth covering the special piece, smelling the faint sweet note of pear skins from the fruit bowl Nana Ruth kept near the drying racks. Outside the open side window the wind in the fig leaves made a soft rushing sound that filled the quiet room while Pema waited.

Pema walked once around the table, careful not to bump any boards, and felt how the air near the drying racks stayed cooler than the sunny corner. "I really want to see if the sugar pane looks like a real little window yet," Pema said. Nana Ruth smiled and handed them a smooth warm coin from her apron pocket. "Hold this for a minute, Pema. Feel how the warmth stays even when you stand still. The biscuit is doing the same kind of patient work under its cover." Pema closed their fingers around the warm coin and let the gentle heat settle into their palm while they watched dust motes drift in the slanted light.

Niko arrived with soft steps and stood beside Pema, bringing the mild scent of clean soap and outdoor air. "Is the window biscuit ready?" Niko whispered. Pema shook their head and kept the cloth undisturbed. "Nana Ruth says it has to finish in its own time, the same way the slip sets and the handles firm up," Pema answered, and Niko nodded, understanding at once. Together they listened to the quiet tick of the wall clock and the far-off hush of leaves, and Pema felt glad that the cover stayed exactly where it belonged.

Pema decided to help in a small way that would not disturb anything drying. They used the side of one foot to straighten the corner of the woven rug that had curled near the table leg, pressing the fabric flat until it lay smooth against the cool floorboards. The motion felt calm and useful. Nana Ruth watched and said, "That is a good quiet task, Pema. Thank you." Pema smiled and kept the warm coin tucked safely in their other hand, turning it slowly so the metal stayed pleasant against their skin.

After a little while Pema and Niko carried two clean water cups to the sink and rinsed them with careful hands, listening to the gentle splash and feeling the lukewarm water run over their fingers. Steam rose in thin curls that caught the light, and the studio smelled clean and settled. Pema glanced once more at the covered window biscuit but left the cloth alone, remembering how the handles needed time and how the sugar pane needed the same unbroken rest.

Nana Ruth invited them both to sit on the low bench where the cushions held the leftover warmth of the day. "Tell me what you notice while we wait," she said. Pema described the way the light had moved farther across the table and how the slip no longer looked shiny-wet. Niko added that the fig leaves outside still whispered even though the breeze had grown softer. Pema felt peaceful knowing the window biscuit stayed safe and covered, finishing exactly when it was ready rather than when anyone peeked.

When the clock showed that enough quiet minutes had passed, Nana Ruth lifted the cloth herself with slow care. Beneath it sat the window biscuit, its sugar pane clear and bright like a tiny glass square set in pale golden dough, perfectly set beside the firm clay handles. "Oh," Pema breathed. "It waited just right." Nana Ruth nodded. "Everything on this table finished in its own time, Pema, and you helped by leaving it be." Pema and Niko leaned close without touching, admiring the smooth pane and the neat edges while the last of the warm coin heat faded gently from Pema's palm.

Later, after the tools were rinsed and the boards wiped dry, Pema sat by the side window with Nana Ruth and Niko while the studio settled into evening calm. Soft shadows stretched across the floor and the air grew cooler against their arms and cheeks. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Pema held the empty space where the warm coin had been and felt proud that the window biscuit with its sugar pane had been left covered until it was truly ready, and that the quiet waiting had filled the whole room with a peaceful, finished feeling that lasted all the way until bedtime.
