The warm smell of honey drifted from the open snack basket as Remy’s small hands already smoothed the frayed corner of a bright lunch mat on the library rug under the round window. Soft golden light poured through the glass and warmed the woven circles beneath their knees. Remy tugged the mat straighter and listened to the quiet shuffle of pages turning nearby. Other mats rested in a careful row for the afternoon reading picnic, yet one lunch mat sat apart near the curve of the rug, and Kito stood just beyond it with hands tucked close. Grandma Nia rocked gently in her low chair, watching without speaking, while the air stayed mild and calm around them all.

Remy ran a finger along the mat’s smooth cloth and felt the faint coolness left by the library’s shaded floor. Kito shifted from one foot to the other, close enough to hear the story but not quite inside the circle of mats. Remy looked up at the round window where dust motes drifted like slow sparks. “The mats look like little islands,” Remy said softly. Kito’s chalky hand brushed the edge of the set-apart mat, leaving a pale dusty streak that Remy noticed at once. Grandma Nia only smiled and kept her book open on her lap, letting the children decide what came next.

A faint click of a gate latch sounded from the garden path outside the library wall, then faded into the hush of the room. Remy scooted sideways so their mat edged nearer the lonely one. The rug’s thick loops felt springy under their legs as they moved. Kito watched without stepping forward. “You can sit right here if you want,” Remy whispered, patting the open space they had made. The light through the round window turned the floor the color of warm milk. Grandma Nia turned another page with a soft rustle and said nothing at all, trusting the quiet work happening on the rug.

Remy lifted the edge of their own mat and slid it until it touched the set-apart one, making a wider shared place without any fuss. The cloth felt smooth and friendly where the two mats met. Kito’s shoulders eased a little. “Is there room for both of us now?” Kito asked in a small voice. Remy nodded and patted the joined mats again. Cool air drifted from the window and brushed Remy’s cheek while they waited. Grandma Nia’s chair creaked once as she settled deeper, still leaving the solving to the children.

Remy slowed the whole picnic pace by unfolding a napkin with careful folds instead of hurrying to the snack basket. The napkin’s thin paper made a tiny crinkle that filled the pause. Kito stepped onto the joined mats and sat cross-legged, knees almost touching Remy’s. Soft light pooled around their shared spot under the round window. “We can share the honey crackers later,” Remy said. Kito smiled and rested both hands on the rug’s warm weave. Grandma Nia watched the two mats become one place and simply closed her book for a moment.

Remy reached for the story basket and pulled out a picture book with thick pages that felt solid and safe. They held it open between them so both could see the bright drawings at the same slow speed. The air near the floor stayed pleasantly cool against their bare arms. Kito leaned closer and pointed at a drawing of a red bird. “That bird looks happy on its branch,” Kito said. Remy agreed with a quiet nod and turned the page only when Kito was ready. The round window’s light rested like a gentle hand across their shoulders while Grandma Nia hummed a low tune under her breath.

Remy and Kito took turns choosing the next picture and speaking the simple words they knew, keeping the pace easy and even. The rug held their weight with a soft give that made sitting feel steady. A thin breeze carried the last trace of honey from the basket and then moved on. Grandma Nia set her book aside completely and folded her hands, content to let the children keep the seating just as they had fixed it. Remy felt glad that no one had called the new place a prize or made a big announcement; it simply became theirs together under the round window.

Remy stacked the empty napkin scraps into a neat little pile and brushed a crumb from Kito’s sleeve with a careful sweep. The sleeve cloth felt soft and worn under their fingers. Outside, the day began to dim, and the library lamps glowed lower and kinder. Kito leaned against Remy’s shoulder for a moment and breathed out a long easy breath. Grandma Nia rose quietly and walked to the window ledge, still saying nothing about rules or seats. The children stayed side by side on the joined mats, the story book closed between them, while the room cooled into evening calm.

Remy tucked the last corner of the lunch mat flat and rested their hands in their lap as the light turned blue-gray through the round window. Kito’s chalk-dusted fingers had long since been wiped clean on the rug, and the shared place felt ordinary and right. Grandma Nia returned to her chair with a soft smile and watched the two friends sit without hurry. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Remy listened to the hush of the library settling around them and felt the quiet weight of friendship holding steady on the rug until sleep came near.
