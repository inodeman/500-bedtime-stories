The gentle smell of cinnamon floated from a little tin of warm apple pieces set on a bench as Esme’s hands already busied themselves with the dangling coats and soft scarves in the school cloakroom, smoothing each sleeve so the fabric felt cool and lightly fuzzy under their careful fingers. Afternoon light poured through the tall windows in calm golden stripes that warmed the wooden boards just enough to make them pleasant underfoot. Esme hummed four quiet notes and then left a rest while they worked, listening to the distant click of a gate latch outside as someone closed the play yard gate for the afternoon. Beyond the open cloakroom door the lunch mats waited in neat rows, and Esme glanced that way with a small smile, already thinking of settling down soon. The air stayed mild and still, carrying only the faintest spice and the soft rustle of coats.

Esme stepped farther into the cloakroom and noticed the round library cushion resting beside the low shelf where the bags usually sat, its bright cover looking out of place yet friendly among the hooks and mats. Someone must have carried it over from the reading corner earlier so the children could rest more softly after lunch on the mats just beyond. Esme ran one hand across the cushion’s top and felt how springy and yielding it was, almost like a thick cloud that held the warmth of the room. Light from the windows made tiny dust motes dance above it in slow golden circles. Esme stood still for a moment, breathing the mild air, and heard only the quiet shuffle of their own shoes on the floorboards. The cushion looked inviting, yet Esme already wondered how it might be shared when more than one person wanted to sit.

Bibi appeared in the doorway with a small cloth bag, their steps light on the threshold, and the two friends exchanged soft greetings while the cloakroom air stayed gently warm around them. “I brought the spoon from home,” Bibi said, holding up a little wooden spoon whose handle felt walnut-smooth when Esme touched it carefully. Esme nodded and answered, “We can stir the apple pieces with it after we sit.” The light shifted a little as a cloud passed outside, softening every shadow into friendly gray. Esme and Bibi walked together toward the lunch mats just beyond the cloakroom, the library cushion still waiting beside the low shelf like a quiet invitation. The mats felt cool and slightly rough under their knees when they knelt to arrange themselves.

Esme looked at the single library cushion and felt a quiet pause inside, realizing it would not feel fair if only one person settled onto it first while the other waited. The afternoon light glowed steadily on the cushion’s cover, showing every soft fold. Esme spoke gently, “If I sit first the whole way, Bibi might miss the best part.” Bibi tilted their head and listened, the mild temperature of the room wrapping around both of them like a soft blanket. Esme’s fingers brushed the cushion again, feeling its springy give, and the idea of making a smaller, even share rose up clearly. Beyond them the lunch mats stretched in calm rows, ready for quiet sitting and the cinnamon apple pieces still waiting in their tin.

Esme pressed both hands into the middle of the library cushion and carefully folded one edge inward so the sitting space became smaller yet still open on both sides, creating an even place where two could rest together without anyone being left out. The cushion fabric felt pleasantly thick and warm from the day’s light. “Now we both fit the same,” Esme said, voice low and steady. Bibi’s eyes brightened and they answered, “That feels right for both of us.” Together they eased onto the reshaped cushion beside the low shelf, their shoulders nearly touching, while the cloakroom behind them stayed quiet and the lunch mats spread softly ahead. A faint breeze through the open door carried cooler air that smelled clean and mild, brushing their cheeks like a whisper.

Uncle Idris stepped in from the hallway with quiet footsteps, his jacket still carrying the outdoor chill, and he saw how Esme had shaped the library cushion into the smaller even share so Bibi could sit right beside them on the edge of the lunch mats. The light from the windows rested gently on Uncle Idris’s kind face as he watched without speaking a single word of praise or direction. He simply nodded once, letting Esme’s choice stand exactly as it was, and then placed the tin of apple pieces nearby along with the walnut-smooth spoon. Esme felt the cushion’s springy support under them and heard Bibi’s soft breathing close by. The whole cloakroom and the mats beyond seemed to settle into a deeper quiet, temperature mild and even.

Esme picked up the walnut-smooth spoon and stirred the apple pieces slowly so the cinnamon scent rose again in a warm little cloud that made both children smile. “Taste is ready now,” Esme said, offering the spoon first toward Bibi. Bibi took a careful bite and then passed it back, the shared motion feeling easy and calm on the reshaped cushion. The light through the windows had turned a softer orange as the afternoon leaned toward evening, painting long gentle shapes across the lunch mats. Uncle Idris sat on a nearby bench without saying more, his presence steady and warm like the boards under their feet. Esme rested one hand on the cushion’s edge and felt its yielding texture once more, glad the space held both of them the same.

After the last apple piece was gone Esme and Bibi leaned back side by side on the library cushion beside the low shelf, the lunch mats stretching quietly ahead like a calm green field indoors. The air had cooled just a little, enough to make their cheeks tingle softly while the cloakroom coats hung still behind them. Esme hummed the same four notes again and left another rest, listening to the peaceful hush that filled every corner. Bibi’s shoulder pressed lightly against theirs, warm and steady. Uncle Idris remained nearby, still letting the choice stand without any speech, his quiet breathing joining the gentle sounds of the settling room. Everything felt even and safe under the soft fading light.

Esme closed their eyes for a moment and felt the cushion’s gentle hold beneath them while the school cloakroom and the lunch mats beyond grew quieter still, the last warmth of the day lingering on their hands and faces. Bibi stayed close, breathing slowly, and Uncle Idris watched over them both with the same calm acceptance. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
