The soft rustle of a turning page whispered once across the quiet room, and Lior noticed it right away while sitting in the library corner. Late-day light slid through the tall glass in thin amber stripes that warmed the tops of Lior’s bare feet. They rested a hand on the low shelf and felt the cool wood under their palm. From the open doorway Lior could see the kitchen square of the fridge, and there, still taped carefully in its place, waited the drawing Mr. Ade had chosen to keep. Lior breathed in the calm of the corner and listened to the faint hush of the house around them.

Lior reached for a thick picture book whose spine stuck out a little farther than the others. A narrow paper ribbon marked a page near the middle, its edge soft from many gentle turns. Lior opened the book and let the ribbon rest across their lap like a quiet friend. The air near the shelf felt cooler than the rest of the room, and Lior liked the way it touched their wrists. They looked again through the doorway at the fridge square where the drawing stayed, bright and safe, and Lior’s shoulders eased. Mr. Ade must have pressed the tape corners flat that morning so nothing would curl.

Lior remembered drawing the picture weeks ago and leaving it on the table without asking anyone to hang it. Now it lived on the fridge square, not replaced by new shopping lists or notes, and Lior felt a warm press of gladness in their chest. The paper ribbon in the open book smelled faintly of old glue and clean dust. Lior traced the ribbon with one finger and watched dust motes drift in the amber light above the carpet. They decided a quick thank-you would feel too thin for something so steady and kind. Lior wanted to answer the keeping of the drawing with one useful thing Mr. Ade had never asked for.

Lior stood and walked the short path from the library corner into the kitchen, still holding the book with its paper ribbon. The floor tiles felt smooth and slightly cool under their feet. On the counter sat two empty mugs that still held the sweet leftover smell of cocoa from earlier. Lior washed each mug with careful hands, rinsed them until the water ran clear, and set them upside down on the drying cloth. The small task made a soft clink of ceramic against ceramic. When both mugs shone clean, Lior dried their hands on the cloth and carried the book back to the corner.

While Lior settled again among the cushions, a cat’s collar bell jingled once from the hallway as the cat padded past and disappeared. Lior smiled at the bright little sound and pulled the book closer. They noticed Mr. Ade’s cardigan draped over the arm of the reading chair, one wool sleeve hanging free. Lior lifted the cardigan, smoothed the soft wool sleeve flat, and folded the whole sweater neatly over the chair back so it would stay tidy. The fabric felt thick and gently scratchy under Lior’s fingers. Outside the window the light had grown softer and more golden.

Mr. Ade stepped into the library corner with quiet steps and paused beside the chair. “I see the mugs are clean and the sweater is folded,” Mr. Ade said, voice low and pleased. Lior looked up from the book with its paper ribbon and felt the warm air from the kitchen still lingering on their cheeks. “I noticed you kept my drawing on the fridge square,” Lior said plainly. Mr. Ade’s eyes crinkled. “I like seeing it every morning when I open the door for milk,” Mr. Ade answered. Lior nodded once and kept their voice steady. “A quick thank-you felt too thin, so I wanted to do something useful you did not ask for.”

Mr. Ade sat on the edge of the reading chair and rested a hand on the folded cardigan. “That was thoughtful, Lior,” Mr. Ade said. The light in the corner had turned the color of warm honey and rested across both their knees. Lior turned another page and felt the paper ribbon slide like a smooth path across their lap. They could still picture the drawing safe on the fridge square, its colors bright against the white door. The house around them stayed peaceful, and Lior’s breathing matched the slow quiet of the shelves. Mr. Ade stayed nearby without rushing, simply sharing the calm corner.

Lior closed the book gently so the paper ribbon stayed in its place and set it back on the low shelf where it belonged. They climbed into the reading chair beside Mr. Ade and rocked once, then let the chair stop by itself with a soft final creak. The cushions held Lior’s back in a gentle curve, and the air near the window felt cooler now that evening had begun to arrive. Lior watched the last stripes of light thin across the carpet and thought again of the drawing kept safe. Mr. Ade’s presence felt steady as a well-placed bookmark, and Lior rested both hands in their lap without needing to speak more.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Its mild circle of light touched the edge of the shelf and the folded wool of the cardigan while Lior sat quiet in the chair. The library corner held its peaceful hush, and the paper ribbon waited inside the closed book for another day. Lior felt the last warmth of the cushions against their legs and listened to the gentle settle of the house. Everything useful had already been done, and the drawing still lived on the fridge square where Mr. Ade had chosen to keep it.
