The open bag tipped on its side, and the pale shell pasta poured in a dry rattle into the two round pots, each shell curved like a tiny boat catching the soft yellow glow from the ferry lamps overhead. Yael leaned close on the bench and watched the last shells tumble and settle into uneven heaps, some stacked high against the pot rims while others lay flat and quiet at the bottom. The wooden ferry bench felt cool under Yael’s legs, and the folded blanket beside them held a faint warmth from earlier sitting. Yael traced one shell with a careful fingertip and felt how smooth and hollow it was. Miss Dalca sat nearby with her hands folded, and Wynn waited on the other end of the bench, both watching the pots without rushing. The ferry hummed steadily through the water, and the air near the pots smelled clean and dry like uncooked grain.

Yael picked up the first pot and felt its solid weight, then set it down again beside the second one so the two sat level on the blanket’s edge. The shells inside shifted with a gentle scrape that sounded almost like tiny waves. Yael looked from one pot to the other and saw that the piles were different sizes, one clearly fuller than its twin. Cool night air drifted across the open deck and brushed Yael’s cheeks while the lantern light made the white pasta shine. “These two pots look ready,” Yael said softly, “but three of us are here.” Wynn scooted a little closer on the bench, and the folded blanket rustled under their shared movement. Miss Dalca only smiled and kept her quiet place, letting Yael decide what came next without a single extra word.

Yael reached for the bag again and poured a few more shells carefully, listening to the quiet cascade as they fell. The pasta still sat mostly in the two pots, yet Yael knew it would not feel right if only one person received a full pot first while another waited. A faint cocoa scent rose from the thermos Miss Dalca had opened earlier, warm and sweet against the cooler air of the ferry. Yael scooped a small handful from the fuller pot and let the shells slide into a third empty cup that waited nearby on the blanket. “Now we can each have some,” Yael told Wynn, voice calm and sure. The shells made a soft clink as they landed evenly, and the ferry lights threw gentle shadows across the three small containers.

Wynn held out both hands, palms open and steady, and Yael placed one modest share into them with care. The shells felt cool and dry against skin, each one light as a leaf. Yael kept another share close and left the last for Miss Dalca, making certain the amounts matched as closely as small hands could manage. A cat’s collar bell tinkled once from somewhere near the cabin door, a bright little note that faded into the ferry’s low engine murmur. “Thank you, Yael,” Wynn said, smiling at the even piles. Yael nodded and settled back against the bench, feeling the smooth wood press gently along their back while the folded blanket cushioned one side.

Miss Dalca accepted her share with a quiet nod and no long talk at all, simply holding the pot steady so nothing spilled. Yael watched the three portions sitting side by side under the soft deck lights and felt the evening air grow a touch cooler as the ferry moved farther from shore. The wool sleeve of Yael’s sweater brushed warmly against their wrist when they reached to straighten the edge of the blanket. “We all have pasta now,” Yael said, looking from Wynn to Miss Dalca. The shells gleamed faintly, and the pots rested safely where no one would knock them. Everything stayed peaceful on the wide bench while the water slipped past below in slow dark ribbons.

Yael took one shell between thumb and finger and turned it slowly, noticing how the light caught its ridges and hollow middle. The texture of the pasta stayed dry and firm, never sticky, perfect for holding without mess. Wynn did the same with a shell from their own share, and the two friends sat shoulder to shoulder on the folded blanket. Miss Dalca sipped from her thermos, and the faint cocoa scent drifted once more before settling. “It feels nice to sit like this,” Wynn whispered. Yael answered, “Yes, and everyone has the same,” then rested both hands in their lap while the ferry lamps glowed steady and kind above the pots.

The bench creaked once when Yael shifted weight and then grew still again, the wood solid and familiar under them. Yael looked out at the quiet water where distant lights blinked far away, then back at the three even shares of shell pasta waiting patiently. Cool air touched Yael’s nose and ears, carrying the clean scent of river water mixed with the last trace of cocoa. Miss Dalca stayed silent and content, her presence calm as the even night. Yael felt glad the pots no longer held uneven piles, and glad Wynn sat included right beside them on the shared blanket. The pasta shells stayed neatly in place, white and curved under the gentle light.

Yael smoothed the folded blanket with slow strokes until the fabric lay flat and soft, then leaned back and let the ferry’s steady motion rock the bench just once before it settled. The air held a mild chill now, yet the blanket and the nearness of friends kept everything comfortable. Wynn crunched one dry shell carefully, the sound tiny and pleasant, and Yael smiled at the simple shared snack. Miss Dalca watched with quiet eyes that held no need for speeches. “We did it together,” Yael said, voice low and happy. The pots sat empty of worry, only full of the fair small amounts Yael had made, and the ferry carried them onward through the peaceful dark.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Yael rested both hands on the warm wool of the blanket and felt the last cool air drift past while the even shares of shell pasta waited ready for tasting. Wynn leaned near, Miss Dalca stayed close, and the three pots held their quiet peace on the ferry bench as the night lights shone gentle and sure all around them.
