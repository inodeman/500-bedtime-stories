A gentle ticking began from the shelf in the corner of the room, and Nils turned their head to notice the small sound. The tin clock kept a calm, even beat while the last pale daylight rested warm across the floorboards. Nils felt the smooth wood under their bare feet and breathed the mild indoor air before following Cousin Pio toward the door. Outside, the schoolyard waited with its low swing hanging quiet and its circle of stones arranged like a soft meeting place in the grass. An old bus seat, brought there long ago so children could rest after games, sat near the stones with its sturdy back facing the open yard. Nils smelled the cool evening and smiled because the place felt ready for them. “The clock sounds friendly tonight,” Nils said. Pio only nodded and let Nils lead the way down the two shallow steps.

In the schoolyard the light lay soft and golden on the stones, and Nils ran a hand along the low swing rope, feeling its worn roughness. Veda stood just outside the circle, holding a folded paper map of the yard they had drawn earlier, close enough to watch but not quite inside the play. The bus seat waited empty and ordinary, the kind of seat anyone might offer without making a show. Nils saw how Veda’s shoes shifted on the grass and how their eyes followed the other children already settling near the stones. Cousin Pio sat on a nearby bench and folded their hands, watching without speaking. The air held a faint coolness that touched Nils’s neck. Nils walked over at an easy pace and sat first on the far end of the bus seat so the middle stayed open and plain.

Veda still hovered near the edge of the stones while two other children began a slow counting game. Nils patted the open middle of the bus seat once, lightly, as if simply settling their own space, then looked toward the low swing. “The swing works better if someone keeps the counting from here,” Nils said in an ordinary voice. Veda’s shoulders loosened. The paper map in Veda’s hands made a tiny crisp sound as a paper edge brushed against their thumb. Nils kept their own gaze on the stones so the invitation stayed quiet and real. Cousin Pio remained still on the bench, letting the children arrange things themselves. Warm light from the lowering sun touched the metal frame of the swing and made it glow a gentle copper.

Nils shifted a little farther toward the end of the bus seat, changing the seating without any announcement, so the middle cushion faced the circle of stones directly. Veda stepped closer and sat down there as if the place had always belonged to the group. The fabric of the seat felt smooth and a little sun-warmed under Nils’s palm. “We can count the swings together and then pass the turn around the stones,” Nils suggested. Veda smiled and rested the map on their knees. One of the other children began to push the low swing in slow arcs while everyone watched from the new arrangement. Cousin Pio’s quiet presence nearby made the whole yard feel steady. A light breeze carried the smell of wet wool from the coats hung on the fence after the earlier sprinkle of rain.

The low swing moved with a soft creak that matched the calm breathing of the children on the bus seat. Nils kept the pace easy so no one rushed and Veda’s voice could join the counting without hurry. “Three… four… five,” Veda said, and the numbers floated gently across the circle of stones. Nils nodded and added the next numbers in the same unhurried rhythm. The stones felt cool and rounded when Nils leaned down to touch one. Light slanted lower and painted long soft shadows across the grass. Cousin Pio watched the children solve the seating and the turns themselves, offering only a small encouraging tilt of the head when Nils glanced over. The bus seat held them all without fuss, ordinary and kind.

After several slow turns on the swing, Nils suggested they move the counting game into the circle of stones itself so everyone faced one another. Veda carried the paper map into the middle and smoothed it flat. Nils sat cross-legged on a smooth stone and felt its faint remaining warmth from the day. The other children settled around without needing directions. “Your map shows the best path between the swing and the seat,” Nils told Veda. Veda pointed to a little drawn line and everyone followed it with their eyes. Cousin Pio stayed on the bench, content to let the quiet changes stand. The evening air cooled another degree and brushed Nils’s bare forearms like a friendly cloth.

When the counting game wound down, Nils stood and offered a hand to help fold the map, keeping every motion ordinary. Veda handed one corner and together they creased it neatly. The children walked once more around the circle of stones, shoes whispering in the grass. Nils returned to the bus seat for a final rest and felt the solid support under their legs. “We can leave the seat ready for tomorrow,” Nils said. Veda agreed with a nod. Cousin Pio rose then and called softly that it was time to go inside. The light had turned a deeper gold, and the low swing hung perfectly still again. Nils took one last look at the friendly arrangement they had made.

Back through the door, the room received them with its familiar stillness and the lingering tick from the shelf. Nils hung their sweater carefully and felt the indoor warmth wrap around their shoulders. Cousin Pio set a cup of mild tea on the table while Nils washed hands in water that felt pleasantly cool. Veda had gone home with a wave, already part of the circle for next time. Nils sat in the low chair by the window and let their breathing slow. The day rested inside them like a smooth stone kept in a pocket. Outside, the schoolyard grew quiet under the first hints of dusk.

Nils leaned their head against the chair back and watched the soft indoor light pool on the floor. Cousin Pio moved without hurry, straightening the picture cards once more. The tin clock had long since blended into the background of the room. Nils felt their eyelids grow heavy in the kindest way. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
