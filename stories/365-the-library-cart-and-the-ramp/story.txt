"Please hold the side of the cart with me," Kei said softly as the warm lamp light settled over the cottage room.

Kei stood beside the wooden library cart at the top of the short indoor ramp that the baker had built years ago so heavy trays could roll smoothly from the cool pantry hallway into this quiet reading space. Paper stars hung from thin strings above a long tablecloth that spilled almost to the floor in gentle folds, and the air held a mild evening coolness that brushed Kei's bare arms. Ximena waited on the lower side of the ramp with both hands ready, while the baker rested near the doorway holding a coil of soft string. Kei could already smell the faint sweetness that still clung to the baker's apron, yet the room itself felt calm and ready for books. They pressed one palm against the cart handle and felt how steady it could be when no one hurried.

Kei remembered that the library cart behaved on the ramp only when a second hand guided it, and they knew that rushing would make the wheels wobble sideways toward the tablecloth fringe. So Kei and Ximena decided to split the work into two slow jobs. Kei would keep the cart steady from behind and count each careful step, while Ximena would walk backward down the ramp and guide the front corners with open palms. The baker offered only a quiet count or a simple name for the knot they would need later to tie the cart wheel lock once it reached the rug. Warm yellow light from the ceiling lamps made the paper stars glow like tiny moons, and Kei felt the smooth wood of the handle under their fingers as they prepared to begin.

"One step, then wait," Ximena said in a voice as gentle as the breeze that drifted past the damp clay wall beside the pantry door.

Kei nodded and rolled the cart forward the smallest distance, listening to the soft click of the wheels. The smell of fresh paper rose from the open books stacked on the middle shelf, bright and clean like a new notebook waiting for drawings. Kei kept both feet flat and moved only when Ximena nodded. The baker stood nearby and murmured the number "two" when it was time for the next tiny roll. Temperature in the room stayed pleasantly mild, neither warm nor cool, so Kei never felt hurried by discomfort. They watched the long tablecloth hang still without a single ripple, proof that the cart stayed true on its path.

After several slow pauses Kei rested both palms on their knees for a moment to feel the quiet strength in their legs before standing tall again. The gate latch outside the cottage clicked shut with a friendly metal sound as someone closed the garden entrance for the night, yet the noise stayed far enough away that it only added a peaceful punctuation to the room. Kei smiled at Ximena and rolled the cart another short length while the front wheels stayed perfectly centered. The baker named the knot they would use later, calling it the quiet loop so Kei and Ximena could remember it easily. Light from the paper stars shifted softly across Kei's hands and made every careful motion feel important and kind.

"Now the last stretch," Kei whispered, and Ximena answered, "I am ready with both hands."

Together they eased the library cart down the final slope of the ramp until the wheels met the soft rug with a gentle hush. Kei kept their grip steady and never rushed, while Ximena matched every pace so the cart never tipped or wandered. The baker stepped forward only long enough to show how the quiet loop knot slipped around the wheel lock and held it firm without pulling tight. A mild warmth rose from the floorboards near the table, comforting against Kei's ankles as they stepped back to admire the cart resting safely beside the long tablecloth. The paper stars overhead cast quiet patterns that seemed to celebrate the finished journey without any loud words.

Kei ran one finger along the top shelf and felt the smooth edge of a favorite picture book still waiting to be opened later. Ximena straightened a corner of the tablecloth so it hung evenly again, and the baker smiled without speaking, simply proud that the count and the knot name had been enough. Cool night air slipped once more through the window and touched Kei's cheeks like a soft goodbye to the busy part of the evening. They all three stood still for a breath and listened to the settled silence of the cottage room, knowing the books had arrived exactly where they belonged because no one had hurried alone.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Kei sat on the edge of a low cushion, Ximena settled nearby, and the baker poured warm milk into three small cups that smelled faintly of honey. Outside the damp clay wall held the day's last coolness, yet inside everything felt finished and gentle. Kei reached for a book with both hands and opened it so the fresh pages caught the lantern light, ready for a quiet story shared among friends who had moved one cart together.
