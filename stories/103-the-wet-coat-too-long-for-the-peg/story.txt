"Oh dear, this coat keeps sliding right off," Mr. Ade said, standing at the library return desk while cool rain-scented air drifted in from the open door. Noemi stood close beside the counter and felt that chill settle on their cheeks as they watched the wet coat that would not stay on its peg slip once more and land with a soft soggy flop on the mat. Amber lamps warmed the polished wood, and outside the rainy steps glistened under the porch light, each stone turned dark and shiny. Noemi kept their hands tucked in their pockets and listened to the quiet thump of returned books being set in a stack, staying near enough to see how the coat’s wide wet shoulders refused the little wooden peg again and again.

Mr. Ade bent to lift the heavy sleeves, and Noemi heard the wind in the fig leaves that rustled in the potted tree by the entrance, a gentle whisper mixed with the distant turning of pages deeper among the shelves. The coat sagged and twisted, dripping slow beads onto the mat, and Mr. Ade still held a damp armful of books that needed stamping before he could free both hands. Noemi rested their fingertips on the smooth cool edge of the return desk and noticed the small furrow of concentration on his face. Beyond the doorway the rainy steps waited empty and reflective, puddles catching the window light like quiet mirrors while the coat refused every careful try.

"May I hold the books while you manage the coat?" Noemi asked in a calm clear voice that fit the hushed room. Mr. Ade looked up and smiled, the kind of smile that seemed warmer than the lamps. "That would help very much, Noemi," he answered, and he passed the stack into their arms. The books felt solid and slightly cool along the cloth covers, and Noemi cradled them steady against their chest. Between them drifted the clean bright smell of lemon soap still fresh on Mr. Ade’s hands from washing after the walk through the rain, and Noemi breathed it in while they stood firm so nothing would tip or slide.

Even with free hands the wet coat that would not stay on its peg slid sideways once more, and Noemi saw the quiet awkwardness of the moment settle on Mr. Ade’s shoulders. Without waiting to be asked or praised, Noemi set the books carefully on the return desk and reached for the other sleeve, lifting so the weight spread evenly between them. Together they guided the soaked collar over the peg, pressing the fabric flat until at last the coat hung straight and stayed. The air near the door felt cooler than the air deeper inside, brushing Noemi’s wrists as they smoothed the lower hem with both palms and listened to the last drops fall onto the mat below.

"Thank you, Noemi. You took half the job before I even finished worrying," Mr. Ade said, and he stamped the first returned book with a friendly soft thump. Noemi only nodded and picked up the cloth left near the ink pad, wiping the return desk where raindrops had fallen, moving in slow circles across the wood until the surface shone again under the amber light. The cloth was a floured towel left from an earlier baking story hour, and its dry powdery texture felt odd and pleasant under Noemi’s fingers as they worked the circles wider and wider. Outside, the rainy steps kept their steady glistening hush, and Noemi thought the coat would dry by morning now that it could finally rest.

Sora, who had been reading in a beanbag near the tall window, padded over on quiet socks and peered up at the hanging coat. "It looks like a big gray cloud resting after the rain," Sora whispered, and Noemi smiled at the gentle picture. The three of them stood a moment in the mild indoor warmth while rain continued its soft rhythm on the steps and roof. Noemi felt the smooth spine of one more volume as Mr. Ade handed it across for the finished pile, and they lined every book so the titles faced the same neat way. Tiny sparks of lamplight caught on the coat’s buttons, and the peg held firm at last.

Mr. Ade finished the returns and closed the stamp pad with a gentle click that sounded final and kind. Noemi helped gather the stray library cards that had drifted near the desk edge, their fingers brushing the cool thin paper. "Shall we look at the steps before we close the door the rest of the way?" Mr. Ade asked, and Noemi and Sora both nodded. They stepped only to the threshold where the rainy steps began, feeling the cooler outdoor air wrap around their ankles while the porch roof kept the drip away. The wet stones smelled clean, and one shiny green fig leaf had blown near the bottom step; Noemi bent and moved it aside so the path stayed clear.

Back inside, the mild heat of the radiators met them like a soft blanket across their shoulders and knees. The wet coat that would not stay on its peg earlier now hung patiently, releasing the last of its rain in tiny dark spots on the mat. Noemi brushed their palms together and felt the final trace of flour from the towel, a fine leftover softness that reminded them of quiet kitchen mornings. Sora returned to the beanbag with a contented sigh, and Mr. Ade dimmed one row of overhead lights so the library grew even quieter and more golden. Noemi stayed near the desk a little longer, listening as the rain softened into a bare hush against the glass and stone.

When it was time to go home, Noemi zipped their own jacket and paused by the wide window where streetlamps painted pale circles on the wet sidewalk below. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Mr. Ade waved from the return desk, and Noemi waved back, feeling the calm tiredness that follows a small useful thing done without any fuss. The coat remained on its peg like a quiet friend waiting for morning, and the rainy steps shone under the night as Noemi and Sora stepped carefully down them together, cool air on their faces and the library light warm at their backs until the door closed with one gentle click.
