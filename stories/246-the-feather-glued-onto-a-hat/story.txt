The evening air drifted cool and gentle across the ceramic studio sill above the quiet plaza, brushing the rows of drying bowls with a soft hush of temperature that made their rims shine pale blue. Yuri sat with their knees tucked close, feeling the smooth cool clay dust under their palms while the last gold light rested on their hair. Beside Yuri waited a little hat whose feather had been glued fast to the band even though it was only meant to be pinned, and the sticky place tugged whenever Yuri’s sleeve touched it. From the plaza below rose the faint scent of bread ovens closing for the night, and Yuri watched the empty chairs grow dim while their own shoulders stayed warm against the sill’s edge. Grandpa Sol’s quiet footsteps sounded on the studio stairs, steady and kind.

Yuri’s fingers closed around the hat brim and almost slid the whole thing beneath a folded cloth, the feather’s stiff quill pressing awkwardly into their wrist. The cloth held a lingering smell of soapy wool that rose gently when Yuri’s hand brushed it, clean and soft like laundry dried in sun. Instead of hiding the hat, Yuri kept it in plain sight on their lap and listened to the low clink of tools settling on the shelves. A single moth tapped the window once and drifted away, leaving the studio quieter still. Yuri breathed in that soapy wool scent again and decided the plain truth felt lighter than any cover.

“Grandpa Sol, I glued the feather onto the hat when it was only supposed to be pinned,” Yuri said, holding the hat out so the evening light caught every stiff barb. Grandpa Sol sat down on the sill beside them, the wood warm from the day’s sun, and took the hat with careful hands. “Thank you for telling me straight away, Yuri,” he answered in his calm low voice. The two of them turned the hat slowly, noticing how the glue had dried in a small shiny patch that no longer flexed. Yuri felt the temperature of the sill cool a little more as night settled, yet Grandpa Sol’s sleeve against their arm stayed comfortably solid.

Leila appeared in the doorway carrying a shallow tray of clean brushes, her steps soft on the floorboards. “May I sit with you both?” she asked, and when Yuri nodded she settled on the far side of the sill where the plaza lanterns already twinkled below. Yuri explained again how the glue bottle had tipped while they tried to hold the feather steady for pinning. “We can loosen what we can and pin it properly,” Grandpa Sol said, reaching for a small jar of warm water and a soft rag. The water felt pleasantly tepid against Yuri’s fingertips as they dipped the corner of the rag and dabbed at the glue edge without haste.

Together they worked in the mild glow of the overhead lamp that cast a steady honey-colored light across their hands. Yuri held the feather gently while Grandpa Sol eased the glue free bit by bit, and Leila passed the damp rag whenever it was needed. A cat wandered along the plaza stones far below, and the clear bright note of a cat’s collar bell drifted up through the open window, cheerful and small. Yuri smiled at the sound and kept their eyes on the hat, feeling how the feather’s soft vanes brushed their cheek like a whisper. No one hurried, and the glue gave way without tearing the felt.

Once the feather lifted free, Grandpa Sol found a slender leather lace among the spare sewing bits in the drawer, its surface smooth and slightly ridged under Yuri’s exploring thumb. Yuri threaded the leather lace through the tiny holes already punched in the hat band and the feather’s stem, pulling just snug enough to hold without strain. “It feels secure now,” Yuri said, running one finger along the neat new join. Leila leaned closer and added, “The leather lace matches the band almost perfectly.” The three of them sat back for a moment, listening to the quiet plaza where a bicycle bell rang once in the distance and then faded.

Yuri noticed a jumble of measuring spoons left on the workbench after the afternoon glazing and began to line the spoons up by size, largest to smallest, the metal cool and smooth against their skin. Grandpa Sol watched with a small smile while he wiped the last trace of glue from the hat’s felt. The orderly row of spoons gleamed under the lamp, and Yuri felt the quiet satisfaction of each handle settling into place. Leila hummed a short tune without words, her shoulder touching Yuri’s for a second of easy company. Outside, the air grew cooler still, yet the studio held its gentle warmth around them like a folded blanket.

When the hat was finished, Yuri set it carefully on a clean cloth between two finished cups so the feather stood upright and free. “I like it better this way,” Yuri told Grandpa Sol, and he nodded, resting one broad hand lightly on Yuri’s shoulder. The leather lace held firm, the feather caught the lamplight in soft flashes of green and gold, and the sill beneath them felt solid and familiar. Leila gathered the damp rags into a basin while the last of the plaza sounds softened into evening hush. Yuri’s eyelids grew heavier, comforted by the steady presence of the people they trusted.

Yuri leaned their head against Grandpa Sol’s arm and watched the dark blue sky deepen beyond the sill, the quiet plaza now empty of footsteps yet still peaceful. The hat rested safe and mended, the spoons stayed lined in their neat row, and the whole studio smelled faintly of clean clay and the earlier soapy wool. Grandpa Sol’s breathing slowed to match Yuri’s own, and Leila’s quiet good-night floated across the room like a leaf settling. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
