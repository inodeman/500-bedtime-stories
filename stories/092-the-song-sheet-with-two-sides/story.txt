The song sheet printed on both sides rested right at the edge of the bakery counter, its crisp paper edges lined up with the wooden grain and its neat black notes glowing under the warm yellow light that spilled from the glass pastry case. Uma bent close enough to see every tiny mark, the paper smooth and pale as cream, and they traced one quiet line of melody with one careful finger while the busy morning hummed around them. A soft draft from the open door carried the gentle heat of the ovens across Uma’s wrists. They stayed still for a long moment, watching how both sides of the sheet held equal rows of words and music, neither side looking more important than the other.

Ms. Okonkwo moved behind the counter with steady hands, sliding fresh rolls onto a tray while the whole bakery felt softly busy with quiet footsteps and low voices. Uma stood beside Tove and smelled the bright green scent of basil rising from a little pot of leaves Ms. Okonkwo kept near the cash box for garnish. The air felt pleasantly warm against Uma’s bare arms. “Good morning, Uma and Tove,” Ms. Okonkwo said in her calm voice. Uma answered, “Good morning, Ms. Okonkwo,” and Tove added, “It smells wonderful in here today.” The three of them shared a small smile while the counter stayed neat and inviting.

Uma noticed that only one song sheet sat ready on the polished wood, and they thought about how both sides held complete songs yet the sheet itself was single. The tin clock on the high shelf ticked a soft steady beat that marked each quiet second of the morning. Uma felt the cool floorboards under their shoes and heard that gentle tick as they considered the paper. “This song sheet has two whole sides,” Uma said softly to Tove. Tove nodded and answered, “I can see the notes on the back too when the light shines through.” The clock kept its peaceful rhythm while Uma kept looking.

Uma realized that if only one person received the sheet first it would not feel fair, because both sides belonged together and both friends stood ready at the same moment. They reached into their pocket and touched a polished pebble they had found the day before, its surface cool and perfectly smooth against their fingertips. The pebble’s gentle weight helped Uma think. Warm light from the case made the pebble gleam for a second when Uma drew it halfway out. They put it back carefully and turned toward the counter again, deciding the single sheet needed a smaller even share so no one waited alone.

Uma spoke clearly across the counter. “Ms. Okonkwo, may we fold the song sheet so each of us can hold an even part at the same time?” Ms. Okonkwo paused her work and looked at the paper with kind eyes. The bakery air felt still and comfortable around them all. Uma continued, “That way Tove is included right away and both sides stay together in smaller pieces we can share.” Tove’s face brightened in the soft morning glow. Ms. Okonkwo simply nodded once and let the choice stand without any extra words, her hands resting quietly on the clean towel beside the rolls.

Uma took the song sheet gently and folded it down the middle so each half held part of the front and part of the back, creating two smaller even shares that still showed music. The paper made a tiny crisp sound as it creased under Uma’s careful fingers. Tove held out both hands and received one folded half while Uma kept the matching half. The warm light from the pastry case lit both pieces equally. They stood side by side at the counter and looked at their matching shares together, the notes still clear and friendly.

While they held the folded pieces, Uma noticed a small stack of cloth napkins nearby, each one stitched with a neat pear in green thread. They began to fold the top napkins so every stitched pear faced upward in a calm row. The fabric felt soft and slightly heavy in Uma’s hands. Tove helped by smoothing each fold, and together they made a tidy line that matched the neatness of the song sheet halves. The morning light grew a little brighter through the window and warmed the tops of their heads as they worked without hurry.

Ms. Okonkwo watched the quiet folding and the shared song sheet pieces with a peaceful expression and then returned to arranging the basil leaves on a plate of fresh bread. Uma and Tove stayed close at the counter, holding their even shares and glancing at the notes whenever they wished. The bakery kept its soft busy feeling, with the gentle clink of a tray and the low murmur of other early visitors who never rushed anyone. Uma felt glad that the smaller even shares had included Tove from the very first moment, and the polished counter stayed smooth under their elbows.

Later the two friends sat on the wooden bench near the front while the folded song sheet halves rested between them and the neatly pear-faced napkins waited on the counter. Uma listened to the quiet bakery sounds and felt the comfortable temperature of the room settle around their shoulders like a light blanket. They leaned back and watched dust motes drift in the sun. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Outside the street stayed calm, and inside everything felt ready for a slow and easy rest of the morning.
