The smell of warm bread drifted in from the hallway while Greta’s hands were already busy brushing crumbs into a neat pile on the little bedside table. Soft hall light spilled across the wooden floor and warmed the edges of the rug where Greta knelt, their palms moving in slow circles over the smooth surface. Greta liked the gentle hush of the room and the way the light made the walls look honey-colored and kind. They gathered every tiny scrap so nothing would stick to bare feet later, and the air still carried that cozy bakery sweetness that made Greta’s shoulders loosen. Outside the window the night waited quietly, and Greta’s fingers felt the cool grain of the wood as they finished. They sat back on their heels for a moment, simply breathing, and noticed how the quilt on the bed looked plump and inviting under the steady glow from the hall.

Greta stood and turned toward the pillow just as Grandma Nia stepped fully into the doorway, her slippers whispering against the floorboards. The library book rested on the nightstand, neatly set aside with a thin paper ribbon marking the exact page where the story had paused. Greta saw at once that Grandma Nia had already smoothed the cover, tucked the ribbon carefully between the pages, and placed a small bookmark card beside it so nothing would be lost. Warm light from the hall painted a soft rectangle across Grandma Nia’s hands, and Greta felt a quiet gladness rise in their chest. The book looked ready and cared for, its corners aligned with the edge of the table as if it belonged there overnight. Greta’s own fingers brushed the air near it without touching, simply noticing the tidy way everything sat.

A quick thank-you felt too thin to Greta, too light for the careful work already done. They looked around the bedroom and spotted the empty bowl that had held the warm bread earlier, still sitting near the chair. Greta decided to carry the empty bowl to the sink and come back without being asked. The hall light stretched ahead of them like a friendly path as they walked, and the air grew a little cooler near the kitchen doorway. Greta held the bowl steady, feeling the weight of it settle comfortably in both hands, and set it down gently beside the faucet so it would be ready for morning. The tiles under their feet were smooth and familiar, and Greta turned right away to return, the quiet house wrapping around them like a soft blanket. When they stepped back into the bedroom the hall light still glowed steadily behind them.

Grandma Nia smiled as Greta reappeared, and the room felt even calmer with both of them inside it. Greta sat on the edge of the bed and let their bare feet rest against the rug’s low loops. “I carried the bowl to the sink,” Greta said. The words came out plain and clear in the gentle space between them. Grandma Nia nodded once, her eyes crinkling at the corners. “That helps tomorrow start easily,” Grandma Nia answered. Greta listened to the quiet house and noticed how the temperature near the window felt just right for sleeping, neither too warm nor cool. They folded their hands in their lap and looked again at the library book with its paper ribbon resting so neatly.

Greta wanted Grandma Nia to know the noticing had happened. “I saw you took care of the library book and put the ribbon in the right place,” Greta said. The sentence felt solid and true as it left their mouth. Grandma Nia rested a hand lightly on the back of the chair. “I wanted the story ready for next time we open it together,” Grandma Nia replied. Greta’s shoulders eased further at the sound of those words, and the hall light continued to pour its mild brightness across the shared space. They both looked toward the book for a shared second, and Greta felt the quiet pride of having spoken plainly about what they had seen.

A soft breeze moved past the window screen then, carrying the sound of wind in the fig leaves that grew just beyond the sill. Greta turned their head to listen, and the leaves brushed one another with a dry, friendly rustle that rose and fell like slow breathing. The bedroom stayed peaceful while the sound continued outside, and Greta imagined the dark green leaves shifting gently under the night sky. Grandma Nia sat down on the foot of the bed so they could hear it together. Greta’s bare toes curled once into the rug’s texture and then relaxed again. The two of them stayed still long enough for the breeze to pass, and the hall light kept the corners of the room softly visible.

Greta reached for the small cup of water Grandma Nia had left earlier and felt a cool ceramic rim against their fingertips. They took a careful sip, the smooth glaze pleasant under their hands, then set the cup back down without a sound. The water tasted clean and quiet, and Greta wiped a tiny drop from the edge with their thumb. Grandma Nia watched with an easy expression, and Greta felt the day settling lower like a bird finding its nest. They pulled the quilt up over their knees and let the cotton rest loosely there. The bedroom held its gentle temperature, and Greta’s breathing slowed to match the calm around them.

“Will you stay while I listen to the leaves a little longer?” Greta asked. Grandma Nia’s answer came soft and sure. “I will stay right here,” Grandma Nia said. Greta smiled without showing teeth, just a quiet lift of the mouth, and settled deeper against the pillows. The hall light still reached across the floor in its steady band, and Greta watched dust motes drift inside that pale glow. They felt safe knowing Grandma Nia remained nearby, and the library book with its paper ribbon waited patiently on the table as if it, too, was ready for rest. Greta’s eyelids grew heavier in the kindest way, and they kept one hand resting open on the quilt.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Greta’s eyes followed its mild circle of light one last time before closing, and the bedroom filled with the slow quiet that comes just before sleep. Grandma Nia’s breathing stayed even and near, and Greta felt the quilt’s familiar weight settle over their shoulders like a promise kept. The hall light continued its gentle watch from the doorway while the whole room seemed to exhale. Greta’s thoughts drifted toward the marked page waiting in the library book, toward the fig leaves outside, and toward the simple gladness of having noticed and having helped. Everything in the bedroom rested exactly where it belonged, and Greta slept.
