The gentle evening air drifted in through the window and brushed softly across the quiet craft corner with its stacks of colored paper, open jar of paste, and sturdy wooden rack. Violeta sat down on the low padded stool there, feeling the mild coolness settle on their bare arms while the late light turned everything a quiet gold. They looked at the heavy book resting closed on the table, the one that held the pressed flower newly shut inside its thick pages. Soft shadows from the wooden rack stretched across the floor like slow-moving fingers. Violeta rested their hands in their lap and listened to the hush of the room around them. The air carried a faint warmth from the day that was just ending. They breathed in slowly and stayed close to the table without touching the book yet.

Violeta reached toward the heavy book once, then drew their hand back as Auntie Luz stepped nearer with a small bowl. “The flower needs more time inside before we open the pages,” Auntie Luz said in her calm voice. Violeta nodded and smelled the bright scent of orange peel rising from the bowl Auntie Luz set down. They watched the wooden rack hold drying sheets of paper that stirred just a little when the air moved. The paste jar caught a glint of light and looked almost like honey. Violeta rubbed their thumbs together and felt the familiar stillness of waiting begin. Auntie Luz smiled and stayed close without rushing anyone.

“I want to peek just a tiny bit,” Violeta said softly, their eyes on the heavy book. Auntie Luz answered, “We can leave it covered and choose another quiet task while it finishes in its own time.” Violeta listened to the steady ticking of a tin clock on the nearby shelf and felt the sound settle into the room like a gentle heartbeat. They ran one finger along the edge of a linen napkin folded beside the paste jar, noticing how smooth and slightly cool the cloth felt under their touch. The light from the window had grown softer now, painting pale shapes on the paper stacks. Violeta decided to trust the waiting and turned toward the wooden rack instead.

Violeta picked up a clean sheet from the wooden rack and smoothed it flat on the table, feeling the faint grain of the paper under their palms. They dipped a brush into the paste and spread a thin layer that shone wetly in the gentle light. The air near the window stayed pleasantly cool against their neck as they worked. Auntie Luz sat in the chair beside them and hummed a little tune without words. Violeta kept their eyes on the paper and away from the heavy book. The paste left a mild clean scent that mixed with everything else in the corner. They pressed another scrap of colored paper onto the paste and patted it carefully into place.

“What if we make a simple frame for later?” Auntie Luz asked. Violeta replied, “Yes, a frame that can wait too until the flower is ready.” They cut a long strip of paper with slow snips that sounded soft and even. The temperature in the craft corner felt just right, neither warm nor chilly, as Violeta lined up the strips along the edge of their work. Light from outside slipped lower and made the wooden rack look deeper brown. Violeta set the finished strips aside in a neat pile and wiped a tiny bit of paste from their fingertip onto the waiting linen napkin they had already touched. Everything stayed peaceful and unhurried.

Violeta noticed a small sink in the corner of the craft space and walked over quietly. They turned the tap just enough for slow drips to fall and began to count them without peeking at the book. One, two, three, the cool water drops landed with tiny clear sounds in the basin. Four, five, six, Violeta kept counting while the mild air brushed their wrists. Seven, eight, nine, they breathed evenly and stayed still. Ten, eleven, twelve, the last drip landed and they turned the tap off again. The tin clock continued its soft regular ticking from the shelf as if it approved of the careful counting. Violeta returned to the table feeling settled.

Auntie Luz handed Violeta a fresh piece of paper from the wooden rack and they began to fold it into a simple pocket. The folds made quiet whispering sounds against their fingers. Soft evening light pooled on the tabletop and made the paste jar look almost translucent. Violeta pressed each crease firmly and enjoyed the way the paper held the shape. They placed the little pocket near the heavy book but did not open anything. The air carried the lingering freshness of the orange peel from earlier without needing more. Violeta and Auntie Luz worked side by side in the kind of silence that felt full rather than empty.

Time moved like the slow drips Violeta had counted, and soon the light outside had faded to a gentle blue. Violeta stretched their arms and felt the comfortable warmth of the room wrapping around their shoulders. They looked at the heavy book still closed and felt no hurry rising inside them. Auntie Luz said, “It will be ready when the pages have done their quiet work.” Violeta answered, “I can wait until then.” They arranged the paper frame and the little pocket together on the wooden rack so everything could rest. The craft corner smelled clean and calm with paper and paste. Violeta rested their cheek against the cool edge of the table for a peaceful moment.

Violeta stayed beside Auntie Luz as the last of the daylight left the glass and the craft corner grew stiller. They folded the linen napkin once more and set it neatly away, then listened again to the familiar room sounds. The heavy book remained closed with the pressed flower finishing inside exactly as it needed. Violeta felt the soft chair under them and the quiet satisfaction of having chosen the slower path. Paper scraps lay ready for another day and the paste jar lid was twisted shut. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Violeta leaned against Auntie Luz and let their eyes grow heavy while the wooden rack stood watch over all the waiting things.
