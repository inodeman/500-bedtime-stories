The scarf rested right in the middle of the wooden kitchen table, its finished edges smooth and even under the pale morning light that poured through the cottage window in a wide quiet band. Chioma stepped close and looked at every stripe of color they had once pointed at with a careful finger, the deep greens next to the warm oranges and the soft lavenders all joined so neatly. Tiny bits of dust floated in the sunbeam and settled gently on the yarn without a sound. The wood of the table felt cool beneath Chioma's palms as they leaned in to see how Cousin Pio had joined each piece so the scarf looked ready for any slow walk later. Morning air still held a hush that filled the whole room and made the colors seem even brighter against the plain tabletop.

Chioma noticed at once that Cousin Pio had already taken care of the scarf finished in the colors they had pointed at once. The clean scent of soapy wool rose softly from the folds, telling Chioma that it had been washed and dried with care before being laid out so neatly. Chioma ran their eyes over the careful work and felt glad in a quiet steady way that needed no hurry. Outside a bird called once across the yard, but inside the kitchen the light stayed kind and even along the walls. Chioma stood still for a long moment, breathing in that fresh smell and watching how the colors glowed when the sun touched them again and again.

A thank-you spoken fast seemed too thin for such careful work, so Chioma looked around the ordinary kind morning kitchen for one useful thing Cousin Pio had not asked for at all. The stone floor felt smooth and cool under Chioma's bare feet as they turned slowly in place. Pots hung from hooks along the wall in a gentle row, and the kettle sat waiting on the stove with a faint warmth still rising from its side in a soft curl. Chioma's eyes landed on the drawer where the spoons lived in a jumbled heap that had waited there since the day before. The air carried a mild warmth now as the day began to open wider across the cottage.

Chioma pulled open the drawer with a soft scrape of wood on wood and began to line the spoons up by size, smallest to largest in a tidy shining row that pleased them. The metal handles clinked lightly against one another as Chioma sorted them with slow careful hands that moved without rush. Cousin Pio had not said a word about the spoons, yet the work felt right and simple in the quiet kitchen. Chioma placed the tiny teaspoon first, then the middle ones one after another, and last the big serving spoon that caught the light from the window and held it. The kitchen grew a little warmer as Chioma worked, and the wooden floorboards pressed steady and familiar under their feet the whole time.

While Chioma arranged the last spoon, their fingers brushed a leather lace that dangled from the edge of an old apron hanging nearby on its peg, and the leather felt smooth and slightly worn under the light touch. Chioma smiled at the simple texture and kept going until every spoon sat in perfect order inside the open drawer. The cottage kitchen held a faint leftover scent of bread from the day before that mixed with the morning air. Sunlight climbed higher on the plaster wall in a slow golden climb. Chioma closed the drawer carefully so it made almost no sound at all and stood back to look at the neat work they had done without being asked.

Just then a small cat padded into the room on soft paws, and the gentle ring of a cat's collar bell chimed once as it rubbed against the table leg near Chioma. Chioma looked down and whispered, "Hello there." Cousin Pio came through the doorway with a basket of fresh eggs balanced in both hands and said, "Good morning, Chioma. I see you found the scarf." Chioma nodded and kept one hand resting near the folded colors on the table. The cat's bell tinkled again softly when it settled in a warm patch of sun on the floorboards. Warmth from the stove reached Chioma's cheek in a gentle wave that felt like a quiet greeting.

Chioma turned fully toward Cousin Pio and said, "I lined up the spoons by size for you." Cousin Pio set the basket down on the counter and answered, "That was thoughtful of you. I had not even thought of them yet this morning." The eggs looked smooth and brown in the light that filled the kitchen from end to end. Chioma felt the cool edge of the table again as they rested their hands there side by side. A quiet breeze moved the curtain just a little and brought a fresher breath of air across the room toward them both. Chioma and Cousin Pio stood together without any hurry while the cat watched with half-closed eyes.

Then Chioma said plainly, "I noticed you finished the scarf in the colors I pointed at once, and you took care of it so well." Cousin Pio smiled a slow smile and replied, "I remembered exactly how you chose them that day." The scarf lay between them like a bright quiet gift that needed no wrapping. Chioma touched one soft corner and felt the yarn still holding a bit of morning coolness from the air. The whole kitchen seemed to hold its breath in a happy peaceful way that matched the ordinary kind morning. Outside leaves rustled once against the wall, but inside only the soft sounds of their voices filled the space around the table. Chioma felt settled and calm standing there with Cousin Pio near the finished scarf.

They stayed near the table a while longer while the cat purred low and the spoons waited neatly in their drawer for whenever they might be needed. Chioma helped set two cups on the counter for later tea, and the ceramic felt smooth and cool against their fingers as they placed each one just so. The ordinary kind morning stretched on with no rush at all through the cottage kitchen. Cousin Pio hummed a low tune that matched the peace of the room and the soft light. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Chioma watched its gentle light mix with the sun on the scarf and knew the day had begun in the best quiet way.
