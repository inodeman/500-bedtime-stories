A faint creak of settling wood sounded from under the rug in the early quiet. Lior heard it at once and pushed back the blanket, noticing how the pale frosty light already painted the bedroom walls in soft silver. They slid from the bed and crossed to the window where ice ferns glittered on the glass. Lior pressed one palm flat against the cold pane and watched their breath make a brief cloud. Outside the lawn sparkled white, and a clear beam of sun cut through a thawed spot, stretching across the floorboards, out the open doorway, and straight toward the little library beyond. The air near the glass stayed chilly on Lior’s cheeks while the rest of the room began to warm.

Lior followed the bright path on quiet feet, feeling the smooth wood grow warmer wherever the light lay. In the library the tall shelves made a narrow aisle carpeted with that same golden slice of sun, thick and still, filled with slow-spinning motes that looked like tiny floating stars. Lior dropped to their knees and gazed at it, the light painting warm stripes across their knees and the book spines nearby. The wish to poke it with one finger or scoop the glow into a pocket felt strong for a moment, yet Lior kept both hands folded. Dust smelled faintly of old paper and polish in the hush, and the temperature of the aisle felt gently heated compared with the bedroom.

Soft steps approached, and Bram appeared in the doorway rubbing sleep from their eyes. “What is shining there, Lior?” Bram asked in a low voice. Lior smiled and patted the rug beside them. “It is a slice of sun resting in the library aisle,” Lior answered. “Come look with me.” Bram crept forward and settled close, both children leaning in until their shoulders nearly touched. The sun slice glowed steady and calm between the shelves, never flickering. Lior felt the wool sleeve of their own sweater brush softly against Bram’s arm as they shifted, the knitted texture thick and comforting in the quiet morning light.

“I want to touch it so much,” Bram whispered, eyes wide. Lior shook their head gently and kept their own hands still in their lap. “We can look without disturbing it,” Lior said. “That way the light stays whole and bright right where it belongs.” Bram nodded and copied the careful way Lior sat, both of them breathing slow so no draft would stir the floating motes. The golden rectangle on the floor seemed almost solid, warm enough that the air above it shimmered faintly. A far clock ticked once, marking the peaceful second they shared.

Auntie June stepped into the doorway carrying two small mugs, the rich smell of cocoa drifting ahead of her like a welcome. “May I see what holds you both so still?” she asked. Lior waved her nearer without rising. Auntie June set the mugs on a low table and knelt too, her presence adding a soft rustle of fabric. The three of them formed a quiet half-circle around the sun slice while the chocolate scent filled the aisle and mixed with the dry paper smell of the books. Warm steam curled upward, and Lior felt the cozy heat from the mugs reach their bare toes.

A small silver sound rang out then as the house cat padded into the library, its collar bell giving one clear jingle with each careful step. The cat sat at the edge of the light and watched the dancing motes with round green eyes, never placing a paw inside the glow. Lior reached over and scratched gently behind the cat’s ears, feeling the soft fur and hearing the bell settle into silence again. Auntie June smiled and rested one hand on Lior’s shoulder. “It is lovely just as it is,” she said. Bram whispered agreement, and the four of them—two children, one aunt, and one quiet cat—kept perfect company with the sun.

Lior showed Bram how to tilt their head so the dust motes sparkled like secret fireworks, always careful not to lean too close or blow a breath across the beam. “See how they spin when we stay still?” Lior murmured. Bram giggled once, a tiny happy sound, then grew quiet again so the light could keep its dance. Auntie June sipped her cocoa and passed a mug to each child; the warm ceramic felt smooth against Lior’s fingers while the chocolate taste lingered sweetly. The library air stayed gentle and steady around them, no draft strong enough to move even one floating speck.

After a long shared look they rose together, leaving the slice of sun exactly where morning had placed it. Lior took Bram’s hand and led the way back along the bright path toward the bedroom, Auntie June following with the empty mugs and the cat padding last. In the bedroom Lior climbed into the wide wooden chair by the window, felt the cool seat against their legs, and rocked once slowly before letting the chair settle by itself into stillness. Frost still edged the outer glass, yet the room held the soft leftover warmth of the sun they had visited. Bram curled on the rug nearby while Auntie June drew the curtain a little wider so more gold could enter.

Lior rested their head against the chair back and watched the last thin ribbon of light stretch across the floorboards. Bram yawned and closed their eyes for a moment, and Auntie June hummed a wordless tune that matched the slow quiet of the house. The cat jumped into Lior’s lap and purred, collar silent now. Outside the frost began to loosen its hold on the grass, and inside everything felt safe and slow. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Lior breathed in the fading cocoa scent one more time and let their eyelids grow heavy while the morning kept its gentle watch over them all.
