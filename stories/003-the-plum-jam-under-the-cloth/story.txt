"Noemi, the jam needs a little more time under the cloth," Auntie June said, and her voice drifted across the warm greenhouse like a soft blanket. Noemi stood by the long wooden bench where seed pots sat in tidy rows, and the south window poured gentle gold light over everything so the glass felt almost sleepy. They could see the pot of plum jam resting there, covered by a pale cloth that did not move. The air held a mild heat against Noemi's cheeks as they leaned in, and the bench wood felt smooth under their palms. Outside the big panes, the garden waited quietly while Noemi stayed close to Auntie June and watched the cloth stay put.

Noemi wanted very much to peek, yet they kept their hands at their sides and looked instead at the little green sprouts peeping from the seed pots. Auntie June hummed a slow tune while she straightened a watering can, and the greenhouse smelled faintly of damp earth mixed with something bright and clean. Noemi noticed the scent of lemon soap on Auntie June's apron when she came nearer, and the smell made the whole bench feel like a friendly kitchen corner that had somehow wandered into the plants. They brushed a fingertip along the rim of an empty pot and felt the cool clay, then looked again at the covered jam and wondered how long sweetness needed to rest.

"May I lift the cloth just for one tiny second?" Noemi asked, their words careful and small. Auntie June shook her head with a kind smile and answered, "Not yet, Noemi. It finishes in its own time, the same way the seeds take their turns." Noemi nodded and stepped back half a step so the south light could still reach the cloth without any hurry. A soft breeze slipped through a vent high above, and Noemi heard the wind in the fig leaves tapping gently against the glass like quiet fingers. They breathed in and out and decided the sound was nicer than any rush.

Auntie June patted the floured towel that lay folded near the watering cans, and Noemi touched it too. The texture of the floured towel felt dry and powdery under their fingers, a little like soft snow that would never melt. Noemi rubbed the cloth between both hands and smiled because it left the faintest dusting on their skin. They looked once more at the pot of plum jam cooling under its own cloth and chose, with Auntie June nearby, to leave it covered just as it was. The choice settled inside Noemi like a warm stone, steady and calm, while the south window kept sending its steady light across the bench and the waiting seed pots.

"What shall we do while we wait?" Auntie June asked, and Noemi thought for a moment before they answered, "I can wipe the table in slow circles." Auntie June handed them a clean damp cloth that felt cool and a little heavy, and Noemi began at one end of the greenhouse bench. They moved the cloth in wide, slow circles, clearing tiny bits of soil and stray leaves so the wood shone softly. The south window light made each wet path gleam for a second before it dried, and Noemi liked the quiet rhythm of the work. Round and round they went, never hurrying, while Auntie June watered a tray of seedlings with the softest trickle of water.

Noemi wiped farther along the bench, careful around the seed pots so nothing tipped. The damp cloth made a faint whisper against the wood, a sound smaller than a sigh, and the temperature of the greenhouse stayed gentle enough that Noemi never felt too warm or too cool. They paused once to look at the covered jam again, still leaving it alone, then returned to their circles. Auntie June watched with quiet pride and said, "You are doing that so carefully, Noemi." Noemi felt their shoulders relax and kept wiping until the whole stretch of bench looked tidy and ready for whatever came next.

When the wiping was finished, Noemi rinsed the cloth in a shallow basin and hung it to drip. They stood beside Auntie June and listened again to the soft outdoor sounds, watching a single beam of light travel slowly across the floorboards. Time moved like honey, thick and unworried, and Noemi discovered that waiting could feel almost like floating. They counted the seed pots once, then twice, noticing how each held its own promise under the soil. Auntie June rested a hand on Noemi's shoulder, and the touch was light and warm, reminding Noemi they were not alone in the gentle pause.

At last Auntie June lifted a corner of the cloth and peeked, then nodded. "It is ready now, Noemi," she said, and Noemi stepped close so they could see together. The plum jam sat glossy and dark inside its pot, cooled to the perfect quiet sweetness. Noemi breathed in the rich fruit scent that rose when the cloth came fully away, and the smell mixed with the earlier freshness of the room. They did not rush to taste it; instead they simply looked and felt glad that the waiting had been enough. Auntie June offered a small wooden spoon, and Noemi dipped just the tip, tasting the jam that had finished in its own time while the seed pots kept growing nearby.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Noemi and Auntie June sat on the edge of the greenhouse bench with the pot of plum jam between them, and the south light had turned to a deeper rose that painted the seed pots in gentle color. Noemi held the warm spoon and felt the last of the day's mild air on their bare arms while Auntie June told a short story about the plums that had hung heavy on the tree. They listened, content, and the whole greenhouse seemed to settle around them like a quiet nest. Outside, the fig leaves rested, and inside, Noemi rested too, happy that the jam had waited and that they had waited with it.
