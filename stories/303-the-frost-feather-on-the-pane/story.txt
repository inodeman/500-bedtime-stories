"Look," Noemi whispered, pointing at the bedroom window where pale morning light turned the frost silver and blue. The glass felt cold under Noemi’s fingertips, and their breath made a small soft cloud that faded slowly. Outside, the garden waited in quiet white, and Noemi stood very still so the chill of the pane could settle against their skin like a gentle touch. From downstairs came the faint clean scent of lemon soap drifting up the stairs after Auntie June’s early wash. Noemi’s toes curled against the smooth wood floor as they leaned closer, eyes wide at the shining white shapes that covered the glass.

Noemi had woken to the soft gray light and padded across the room without a sound, drawn by the sparkle on the window. Right in the middle of the pane rested a perfect frost feather, its tiny veins and barbs etched in ice so fine they seemed almost ready to lift and float away. Noemi’s heart thumped with the wish to poke it or pocket it, yet they kept both hands flat at their sides. The air near the glass smelled crisp and clean, colder than the rest of the bedroom. Noemi tilted their head and watched the way the light caught each thin line of the frost feather, turning it into something bright and still.

“Sora, come see,” Noemi called softly toward the doorway, where their friend had spent the night on a spare mattress piled with blankets. Sora rubbed sleepy eyes and shuffled over, the floorboards cool under bare feet. Together they stood shoulder to shoulder, noses almost brushing the glass. Noemi lifted one careful finger but stopped short of touching the frost feather. “We can look without moving it,” Noemi said, voice hushed so the quiet morning would stay whole. Sora nodded, and the two children studied the delicate white plume that seemed painted by the night itself.

The frost feather stretched sideways across the pane, its edges feather-soft in appearance though made of ice. Noemi explained in a low voice how the cold had drawn the water into that shape, and Sora listened while a thin band of sunlight crept higher along the wall. Outside, the wind in the fig leaves made a dry whispering hush that rose and fell like quiet breathing. Noemi felt the coolness of the glass again and smiled because the sound of the leaves matched the calm inside the room. They both leaned in, careful not to fog the feather with too much breath.

Auntie June appeared in the doorway carrying a floured towel draped over one arm, the soft cloth still dusted white from morning baking. “What have you found, Noemi?” she asked, stepping closer so her warm presence filled the space behind the children. Noemi pointed without touching. “A frost feather,” they answered, and Auntie June bent down until her eyes were level with theirs. The three of them gazed together at the icy plume, and the kitchen warmth that clung to Auntie June mixed gently with the window’s chill. She rested a hand on Noemi’s shoulder, light and steady.

Noemi showed Sora how to tilt their head so the light revealed every tiny branch of the frost feather. “If we leave it, it stays beautiful a little longer,” Noemi said, and Sora agreed with a quiet hum. Auntie June smiled and murmured, “That is a fine way to look.” The bedroom felt peaceful, the air still cool near the glass yet comfortable where they stood. Noemi’s fingers brushed the edge of the floured towel for a moment, feeling its dry soft grain before letting go. They all remained still, simply watching the feather hold its place on the pane.

Morning brightened by degrees, and the frost patterns around the feather began to glisten more brightly. Noemi felt the old wish to reach out flicker once more, then settle into something quieter and happier. Sora pressed their cheek near the glass but not against it, matching Noemi’s careful distance. Auntie June straightened the curtains just enough to let more light fall across the window without warming the ice too soon. The room smelled faintly of the lemon soap still lingering on Auntie June’s hands, clean and bright. Noemi rocked once on their heels, content to keep the frost feather exactly where the night had left it.

They stayed by the window a little longer, speaking in soft turns about the shape of each icy line. Noemi described the barbs as tiny white roads, and Sora added that the center looked like a sleeping bird’s plume. Auntie June listened and then suggested they might draw the feather later with white crayons on blue paper. Noemi liked that idea and nodded, already picturing the page. The wooden sill under their palms felt smooth and cool, anchoring them to the calm of the moment. Nothing needed to be taken or changed; looking was enough.

Later, when the three of them stepped back, Noemi gave the frost feather one last long look and felt glad it remained untouched on the bedroom pane. Sora slipped a hand into Noemi’s, and Auntie June smoothed the blankets on the spare mattress with slow care. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Its gentle light mingled with the morning silver on the glass, and Noemi breathed in the quiet room, warm now from shared company, ready for cocoa and the slow peaceful start of the day.
