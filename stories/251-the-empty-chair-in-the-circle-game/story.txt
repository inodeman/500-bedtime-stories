The gentle smell of cedar drifted from the newly planed wooden steps at the edge of the schoolyard while Nils’s hands already busied themselves settling the last smooth stones into a tidy ring. Nils pressed each stone with careful fingers until the circle felt firm and even, and the late sun warmed the backs of their hands as they worked. Dust motes floated in the soft gold light, and Nils could hear the quiet creak of the low swing as a light breeze nudged its ropes. Cousin Pio sat on the bench with a folded cloth across their knees and simply watched, leaving the space open for whatever game might grow. Nils wiped their palms on their trousers, stood back to study the ring, and felt the pleasant cool of the stones still clinging to their skin. They smiled at the neat shape and waited for the others to arrive.

Soon the children gathered near the low swing and the circle of stones, and Nils invited everyone closer with a soft wave. Dori stood just outside the lively group, close enough to see the game yet not quite inside it, while one child waited on the wooden step as the circle game began. Nils noticed the empty space and the way Dori’s shoes scuffed lightly against the packed earth. The air held a mild warmth that brushed Nils’s cheeks whenever someone spun or stepped. Cousin Pio remained on the bench and only nodded once, letting the children find their own way. Nils felt the tug of wanting everyone included and began to think of a quieter plan.

“Let’s make the circle a little wider,” Nils said, speaking calmly so the words floated without fuss. They shifted two stones outward with the toe of a shoe and patted the new places flat. The sound of wind in the fig leaves rustled overhead like a soft continuous hush, cool and leafy. Nils glanced toward Dori and then toward the step where one child still stood, and they decided the game could move at a gentler pace. Cousin Pio rested their chin in one hand and smiled without speaking, trusting the yard to sort itself. Nils felt the afternoon light slide lower and kinder across the stones.

Nils walked over to the step and knelt so their voice stayed near the ground. “You can come walk with us now,” Nils said to the child on the step, “and we’ll keep the circle open.” The child hopped down with a small glad bounce. Nils then turned toward Dori and made a quiet space by sliding one more stone aside, creating a true seat rather than a sudden prize. The texture of a straw mat left folded under the swing bench brushed Nils’s ankle as they stepped past, dry and faintly scratchy. Dori stepped forward without ceremony and settled into the new place as if it had always belonged there. Nils felt the soft give of the earth under their own feet and kept the game moving slowly so no one rushed.

“We can go round three times before anyone sits,” Nils said, setting a gentle rule that gave every pair of feet room. The children began to walk the circle of stones at an easy pace, and the low swing swayed once as someone brushed its seat. Warm light pooled on the packed dirt and turned the stones a deeper gray. Cousin Pio watched from the bench and let the children solve each small turn without interrupting. Nils walked beside Dori for a stretch of the path so the new place felt steady and ordinary. The air smelled faintly of sun-warmed wood from the fence, and Nils’s shoulders relaxed as the game found its new shape.

When the walking slowed, Nils guided everyone to sit wherever the stones felt right, and Dori already had a real place among them. “My knees like this spot,” Dori said quietly, smoothing the ground with one palm. Nils nodded and sat across the circle so they could see every face. The temperature cooled a little as a thin cloud drifted over the sun, and the shade felt pleasant on Nils’s arms. Cousin Pio finally stood and stretched, still saying almost nothing, only offering a small pitcher of cool water from the bench. Nils took a careful sip and set the cup down after turning it so the handle faced the open yard, a tiny neat motion that matched the tidy stones.

The children stayed in the circle a while longer, trading soft stories about the low swing and how high it could go on other days. “Tomorrow we can swing after the stones,” Nils said, and several voices agreed with quiet cheerful hums. The light turned amber and long, stretching friendly shadows across the schoolyard. Nils felt the last warmth of the day resting on their hair like a light hat. Cousin Pio gathered the empty pitcher and the folded cloth, still letting the children finish the game in their own time. Nils watched Dori laugh once at a small joke someone made about a pebble shaped like a tiny boat, and the sound mixed gently with the settling yard.

As the sun slipped lower, Nils helped stack a few loose stones at the edge of the circle so the next day would find them ready again. The low swing hung still now, its ropes quiet against the posts. Cooler air touched Nils’s wrists and made the skin prickle in a pleasant way. Cousin Pio called softly that it was time to walk toward the classroom porch, and the children rose without hurry. Nils walked beside Dori and Pio along the short path, feeling the firm packed ground under each step. Inside the doorway the room held a mild indoor hush that wrapped around them like a familiar coat.

Nils washed their hands in the small basin and dried them on a soft towel while the last of the yard light faded beyond the glass. They sat on a cushion near the window and listened to Cousin Pio hum a slow tune while the other children settled nearby with quiet books. Dori shared a corner of the cushion and leaned gently against the wall. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Nils breathed in the calm indoor air, felt the cushion’s gentle give under their legs, and closed their eyes as the room grew still and kind.
