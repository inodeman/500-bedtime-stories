Soft evening air brushed the market lane with a cool, kind touch, settling lightly over the rows of woven baskets, tidy jars, and clay flower pots that lined both sides. Cosima walked beside Grandma Nia, feeling the smooth stones under their shoes and smelling the clean wood of the crate carts. The sky held a pale gold glow that made every pot shine a little. Cosima kept close, watching the way Grandma Nia balanced a wide basket against one hip while the empty jars inside clinked together. A faint warmth still rose from the sun-warmed path, and Cosima breathed it in with quiet curiosity. They liked this lane best when the day grew gentle.

Grandma Nia paused near a stall of tall jars and low baskets, and the empty jars to return to the grocer shifted with a soft rattle. Cosima saw at once that the load looked awkward for one person, the basket tilting as Grandma Nia tried to steady both the jars and a string bag of folded cloth. “These jars keep sliding when I walk,” Grandma Nia said, her voice calm and a little amused. Cosima reached out without a word of asking and took a useful half of the job, lifting four of the lighter empty jars into their own small cloth bag. The glass felt cool against Cosima’s fingers. Grandma Nia smiled but said nothing more, and Cosima did not wait for praise. They simply adjusted the bag so the jars sat snug and quiet.

They moved deeper along the lane together, past flower pots filled with green leaves and baskets stacked like soft hills. Cosima heard a cat’s collar bell somewhere near a crate of herbs, a tiny bright ring that faded as the animal slipped away. The sound made Cosima glance aside and smile. Grandma Nia carried her remaining jars with easier steps now. “Thank you for sharing the weight, Cosima,” Grandma Nia said gently. Cosima answered, “I can hold these until we reach the grocer.” The air cooled a little more, and Cosima felt the pleasant chill on their cheeks while the jars rested steady in the bag.

Near a table of small clay pots, Cosima noticed a stray glass bead lying on the wood, round and smooth. They picked it up carefully, feeling the glass bead roll once across their palm before placing it safely beside a flower pot so it would not be lost. The bead held a soft shine in the fading light. Grandma Nia waited patiently while Cosima finished. “Shall we keep walking?” Grandma Nia asked. Cosima nodded and took one more careful step forward with the empty jars. The lane smelled of earth and leaves, and Cosima stayed close enough to hear the quiet pad of Grandma Nia’s shoes.

They passed a corner where mint leaves rested in open baskets, and Cosima breathed in the fresh mint leaves smell that rose clear and cool. The scent made the whole corner feel brighter. Cosima held their bag of empty jars a little higher so nothing would bump. Grandma Nia shifted her own basket and said, “The grocer will be glad to have these back clean and ready.” Cosima replied, “We can set them on the counter together.” No one hurried. The light along the lane had turned the color of warm milk, and Cosima felt the soft weight of the jars settle kindly against their side.

A few steps later they met Elif near a row of yellow flower pots. Elif carried a small empty tin and waved. Cosima lifted their free hand in greeting while keeping the jars steady. “We are returning jars,” Cosima said. Elif smiled and walked with them for a short stretch, the three of them moving slowly past baskets of folded cloths. The air felt cooler still, and Cosima noticed how the pots held the last soft heat of the day. Elif soon turned toward another stall with a quiet goodbye, and Cosima and Grandma Nia continued alone again, the empty jars safe and shared.

At the grocer’s open window the counter waited smooth and ready. Cosima set their half of the empty jars down first, hearing each one settle with a low, friendly sound. Grandma Nia placed the rest beside them in a neat line. The grocer nodded and began to stack the returned jars on a high shelf. Cosima watched the glass catch the last of the gold light. The stones underfoot felt cooler now, and Cosima rested one hand on the edge of the counter, feeling its worn wood grain. Grandma Nia stood close, and the two of them waited without rush while the jars found their proper places again.

On the way home the market lane grew quieter. Cosima carried nothing now except the empty cloth bag folded small. They walked beside Grandma Nia past the same baskets and flower pots, the path still holding a mild warmth near the edges. Cosima listened to the soft rustle of leaves in the pots and the distant fold of evening. Grandma Nia spoke once more, her tone easy. “The walk felt lighter with two of us,” she said. Cosima only nodded and kept pace, content with the shared work already done. The sky above the lane turned a deeper blue, and Cosima felt the calm settle around their shoulders like a thin, kind blanket.

Later, inside the quiet house, Cosima sat near the window while Grandma Nia poured warm milk into two cups. The room held a gentle hush, and the floorboards felt smooth under Cosima’s bare feet. Outside the market lane had closed its baskets for the night. Cosima sipped slowly and watched the soft shapes of the pots beyond the glass. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Cosima leaned against the chair back, eyes growing heavy, and the last cool air from the open latch brushed their forehead as sleep began to gather.
