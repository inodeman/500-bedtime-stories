The soft evening breeze touched the garden row gently, cooling the earth and making the leaves sway with a quiet whispering sound. Yelena walked forward and felt the mild air brush their cheeks and arms while golden light from the low sun spread in warm patches over the soil. They noticed the coil of string lying ready and the two watering cans standing side by side with soft metallic gleams. Yelena knelt on the cool ground and rested both hands on the wooden window box that needed a holder and a hooker so it could hang safely later. The wood still held a little daytime warmth under their palms. Yelena breathed slowly and watched the light shift as they waited in the peaceful row for help to begin the careful work.

Yelena looked up as Zia came along the path with quiet steps and a bright smile. Miss Dalca followed close behind, her presence calm and steady. The garden row held the string, the watering cans, and the window box all together in one neat scene because the boxes were prepared outdoors before hanging. Yelena said, “Zia, will you help with this window box that needed a holder and a hooker?” Zia answered, “Yes, Yelena, we can manage it if we stay slow.” Miss Dalca stood nearby without taking over while the soft pink light of dusk began to settle around them. Yelena felt the cool soil under their knees and understood that one person rushing would tangle everything, so they planned to share the work from the start.

A cat wandered past the watering cans just then, and the sound of a cat's collar bell tinkled lightly through the quiet air. Yelena smiled at the gentle ring before turning back to the box. They decided with Zia to split the task into two slow jobs so nothing would slip. Yelena would hold the heavy wooden box steady with both hands while Zia shaped the string into a proper holder. Miss Dalca watched from a short distance as the air grew cooler and the light faded to soft lavender. Yelena kept their grip firm and even, feeling the solid weight of the box and knowing the shared pace would keep the holder secure.

Yelena pressed the window box firmly against the ground so it stayed still. Zia took the string and began forming careful loops. Miss Dalca spoke in a low kind voice, “The knot you need is called the garden loop, and you can count slowly to four while you pull it closed.” Yelena heard the name and the count and felt ready. “Thank you, Miss Dalca,” Yelena said. As Miss Dalca leaned near to show the place on the string, Yelena felt a wool sleeve brush their arm, the soft wool texture warm and comforting against their skin. The fading light made everything gentle while they followed the slow count together.

Zia counted softly one, two, three, four and pulled the garden loop knot tight without hurry. Yelena kept the box perfectly still so the string settled exactly right. If anyone had rushed the holder would have come loose, but the careful pace made it strong. The two watering cans stood nearby like quiet friends waiting for later flowers. Yelena felt the evening temperature drop another degree and concentrated on their steady hold. “This is going well,” Zia said to Yelena in a hushed tone. Miss Dalca only nodded and offered nothing more than the name and the count she had already given, letting the children finish their parts.

With the knot secure Yelena and Zia moved to the second slow job of fastening the holder to each side of the window box. Yelena guided one end of the string while Zia guided the other, their hands moving in the same easy rhythm. The garden loop held firm. Small sounds of careful fingers on wood and string filled the row under the last pale light. Yelena watched the holder take shape and felt the cool evening air on the back of their neck. They worked side by side until every part sat true and ready for the hooking that would come another day.

Miss Dalca checked from a few steps away and gave a quiet smile as Yelena and Zia made the last tiny adjustments together. “We went slowly and it worked,” Yelena told Zia. Zia replied, “Yes, and now the window box has its holder and is ready.” They both looked at the finished piece with calm satisfaction. The string lay neat, the watering cans waited, and the box rested solid on the ground. Yelena stood and brushed cool soil from their hands, feeling how the shared slow jobs had brought everything together without a single slip.

The three of them left the garden row as true dusk arrived and walked toward the house in easy steps. Inside the air carried the warm smell of cocoa from the kitchen, sweet and comforting after the outdoor work. Yelena washed their hands in cool water and dried them on a soft towel. They felt pleasantly tired in the quiet rooms while Zia and Miss Dalca spoke in low happy voices nearby. Yelena moved to the chair beside the window and sat down, ready to rest after the good work in the garden.

Yelena rocked once in the chair and let it stop by itself. The lantern by the window glowed softly. The gentle light reached across the room in a warm circle while the last cool air from outdoors faded away. Yelena leaned back and felt the quiet settle all around them after the careful afternoon with Zia and Miss Dalca. The finished window box waited safely outside for another day, and the whole house held a peaceful stillness that invited rest.
