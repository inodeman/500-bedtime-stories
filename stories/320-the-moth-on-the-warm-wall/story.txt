The tin clock on the shelf gave one soft tick that Tala heard before anything else in the quiet room. Tala turned their head toward the gentle sound and felt the cool evening air drifting through the open door, carrying a faint sweetness. Auntie Luz held out a small hand, and Dae stood nearby with bright eyes. Tala slipped on soft shoes and followed them outside where the grass brushed their ankles with a damp coolness. The light from the doorway stretched long and gold across the path. Tala walked carefully, listening still to that distant tick fading behind them, and the evening felt wide and kind.

Tala reached the meadow fence with Auntie Luz and Dae and smelled the green stems crushed lightly underfoot. A wooden wall of the little shed stood warm beside the fence, and a lamp glowed there so the boards held a gentle heat. Tala noticed a round bucket of water set carefully on the ground, its surface catching tiny stars of light. Dae pointed toward the wall, and Tala stepped closer until the warmth of the boards reached their cheek. The moth rested quietly on the warm wall by the lamp, not on the lamp itself, its wings folded like thin paper. Tala felt a quick wish rise to pocket it or poke it, yet they stayed still and breathed the mild air.

Tala crouched so their knees touched the cool earth and whispered to Dae, “We can look with our eyes only.” Dae nodded and knelt too, the soft dirt cool under both of them. The moth’s wings held a dusty brown pattern that caught the lamp light in tiny sparkles. Tala’s fingers hovered for a second, then rested safely on their own knees. Auntie Luz stood behind them with a calm smile, and the bucket water made a slow drip against its metal side. Tala counted the quiet drips in their mind without peeking, one through twelve, and the sound kept them steady while the moth stayed peaceful on the warm wall.

Tala spoke again in a low voice, “See how it rests? We leave it right there.” Dae answered softly, “It looks like a little leaf that learned to fly.” The air near the fence carried the light scent of orange peel from the snack Auntie Luz had peeled earlier and left on a plate by the path. Tala breathed it in and felt their shoulders loosen. The moth shifted one wing the smallest bit, then settled again against the warm boards. Tala watched the way the lamp painted a soft circle of gold, and the fence rails felt smooth under one resting hand. Everything stayed quiet enough that Tala could hear their own calm breathing.

Auntie Luz leaned closer and said, “You two are looking with such care.” Tala felt proud without needing any big words and simply nodded. The linen napkin Auntie Luz had brought for wiping sticky fingers brushed Tala’s wrist with its dry, woven softness when she passed it over. Tala held the napkin for a moment, feeling each tiny ridge, then set it aside on the grass. The moth remained on the warm wall by the lamp, still and safe. Dae whispered one more time, “I like seeing it this way,” and Tala smiled at the shared quiet. The bucket water mirrored the sky in slow ripples whenever another drop fell.

Tala stood slowly so their shadow would not startle the moth and guided Dae a half step back with a gentle touch on the sleeve. The temperature near the shed wall stayed pleasantly warm while the meadow beyond cooled under the first stars. Tala described the moth’s tiny antennae to Dae in a careful hush, naming the curves without reaching. Auntie Luz watched them both with soft eyes and kept her hands folded. A breeze moved the tall grass with a whispery hush that sounded almost like far-away talking. Tala felt the evening settle around their shoulders like a light blanket and kept their gaze steady on the resting wings.

Tala invited Auntie Luz to look from the same safe distance and pointed with a slow finger in the air. The three of them stood together while the lamp light made the moth’s edges glow like faint honey. Tala’s voice stayed small as they said, “It chose this warm spot all by itself.” No one touched the wall near the moth. The bucket sat nearby with its clear water smelling faintly of rain, and Tala dipped one fingertip only to feel the cool wetness before wiping it dry. Dae copied the careful looking, eyes wide and quiet. The fence posts held the last of the day’s heat, and Tala pressed a palm there for a second of comfort.

Tala decided with their whole calm heart to leave the moth exactly where it rested, and the wish to pocket or poke it drifted away like a cloud. Auntie Luz rested a warm hand on Tala’s shoulder, and Dae gave a tiny nod of agreement. They turned together toward the path that led back to the house, grass cool now under their steps. The meadow fence and the bucket of water stayed behind them in the gentle dark. Tala glanced once more over their shoulder and saw the moth still peaceful on the warm wall by the lamp. The night air tasted clean and mild, and Tala walked between Auntie Luz and Dae with slow, happy feet.

Inside again, Tala washed their hands in the basin and felt the water cool against their skin while the house held its familiar quiet. Auntie Luz folded the linen napkin neatly on the table, and Dae sat on the rug humming a wordless tune. Tala climbed into the soft bed and pulled the covers up to their chin, noticing how the pillow smelled clean and how the sheets felt smooth. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Tala closed their eyes and pictured the moth still resting safely, the fence standing quiet, and the bucket holding its still water under the stars, all of it kept gentle until morning.
