The soft evening air touched the ferry bench gently with a cool caress that drifted in from the quiet river. Yelena climbed onto the wooden seat and settled beside the neatly folded blanket, feeling its calm weight against their legs while the mild temperature brushed their cheeks. Late daylight slanted across the water and made the bench boards look pale and friendly. Yelena noticed the pot of tomato soup sitting steady on a flat board, steam rising in slow curls, and the second bowl resting empty nearby. Miss Dalca sat on one side with hands folded, and Zia sat on the other, both watching the river glide past. Yelena smoothed the blanket once more and breathed the fresh air that carried a faint water scent.

Yelena leaned closer to the pot and heard a soft clink when the ferry shifted, then looked up as a small ferry cat walked by with a tiny collar bell that jingled once and faded. The sound felt friendly in the open air. Yelena said, “The soup looks warm and ready.” Miss Dalca answered quietly, “I brought it so we could share on this ride.” Zia smiled and said, “It smells nice from here.” Yelena reached out and touched the folded blanket again, feeling how the fabric held the last of the day’s mild warmth against their knees while the cat’s collar bell stayed silent after that single clear note.

Yelena studied the pot and the second bowl and felt that it would not feel fair if only one person received soup first while the others waited. The light from the low sun made the orange surface of the soup shine gently. Yelena said, “We can make smaller even shares so everyone has some together.” Miss Dalca simply nodded and kept her hands still. Zia watched with bright eyes. Yelena picked up the ladle that rested beside the pot and began to portion the tomato soup carefully into three small amounts, using the second bowl and two extra cups Miss Dalca had tucked under the blanket edge. The steam felt warm on Yelena’s wrists as they worked.

The air near the pot carried a sudden sweet smell of cocoa from a thermos Miss Dalca opened and set beside the board. Yelena breathed it in while finishing the three equal shares, each one smaller yet matching the others. Zia held out both hands for a cup and said, “Thank you, Yelena.” Yelena passed the first share to Zia, the second to Miss Dalca, and kept the third for themself. The cocoa scent mixed softly with the tomato steam and made the whole bench feel cozier. Yelena sat back against the folded blanket and felt the ferry move in a slow steady rhythm beneath them.

Yelena lifted their own cup and felt the rim smooth against their lips while the warm soup filled the space with gentle heat. Miss Dalca sipped without speaking and simply let Yelena’s choice stand. Zia tasted theirs and smiled wider. The light on the water turned softer and more golden as the ferry drifted forward. Yelena noticed how the three shares looked even and friendly side by side on the board. The blanket under Yelena stayed smooth and ready if anyone grew cooler. Yelena took another careful sip and listened to the quiet hush of the river against the hull.

Yelena brushed against Miss Dalca’s arm and felt the soft texture of a wool sleeve that covered her wrist. The wool felt thick and kind under Yelena’s fingertips for a moment before they drew back. Yelena said, “It tastes just right like this.” Zia answered, “I like that we all started at the same time.” Miss Dalca only smiled and kept sipping. The wool sleeve stayed near enough that Yelena could see its quiet pattern in the fading light. Yelena rested their free hand on the folded blanket again and felt how the evening air cooled just a little more around their shoulders.

Yelena watched the steam rise slower now from each small share while the ferry glided past green banks that looked peaceful in the dimming glow. The temperature of the air stayed mild enough that no one needed to pull the blanket higher yet. Yelena finished half the cup and set it down carefully so nothing spilled. Zia did the same. Miss Dalca placed her cup beside the pot with a soft sound of ceramic on wood. Yelena felt glad the shares had been even from the first moment. The river light shimmered in tiny waves that made the pot lid shine.

Yelena reached for the blanket edge and drew it a little higher across their lap, feeling the calm weight settle. The ferry cabin window nearby caught the last pale light, and the air smelled clean and open. Yelena said nothing more and simply sat with the empty cups now resting together. Zia leaned gently against Yelena’s shoulder for a moment. Miss Dalca gathered the pot and bowls into a neat basket without a single word of instruction. Yelena watched the water darken slowly and felt the bench hold them steady as the ferry continued its quiet path.

Yelena rocked once against the back of the bench and let the motion stop by itself while the folded blanket kept their legs warm. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Yelena closed their eyes for a slow breath and felt the mild night air settle around them, the cocoa scent fading gently, the wool sleeve still near if needed, and the whole ferry calm under the early stars.
