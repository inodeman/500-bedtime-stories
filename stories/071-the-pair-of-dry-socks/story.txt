"Niko, come sit before the bread cools," Grandpa Sol said, his voice gentle under the early evening light that spilled gold across the courtyard stones. Niko hurried to the long table beneath the fig tree and felt the leftover warmth of the day rising faintly from the ground. Anil waved from a nearby chair, already settled with a napkin across their lap. Niko slid onto the bench and noticed how the lanterns cast soft circles on the cloth. The evening air brushed cool against Niko's bare arms as they reached for a cup. Grandpa Sol set a basket of rolls near them and smiled. Niko looked up through the branches where tiny moths drifted, and the whole courtyard felt calm and ready.

Niko noticed Anil shifting uncomfortably and saw that Anil's socks were dark with water from an earlier spill near the watering can. A small wooden chest sat open on a side bench, and inside lay a clean pair of dry socks smelling faintly of cedar. Grandpa Sol lifted them out and held them toward the table as if offering first choice. Niko's fingers rested on the table edge while they watched Anil rub one damp foot against the other. The light from the nearest lantern made the dry socks look soft and inviting. Niko felt the mild night air on their neck and thought carefully about the seat and the socks together.

"Those socks look warm," Anil said quietly, glancing at their own soaked pair. Niko nodded and touched the edge of the chest. Grandpa Sol waited without rushing anyone. Niko felt the texture of a straw mat under their sandals when they shifted closer to the basket. The dry socks waited in Grandpa Sol's hands like a small gift for whoever sat nearest. Niko looked from Anil's wet socks to the clean ones and back again. A moth brushed past the lantern glass with a tiny whisper of wings. Niko decided the first serving of anything, even socks, should not leave a friend left out.

Niko took the pair of dry socks and gently pulled them apart so each child could have one. "We can each wear one dry sock and keep the other foot on this soft cloth," Niko said, folding a spare napkin into two even squares. Anil's eyes brightened as they accepted one sock and one square of cloth. Grandpa Sol watched the careful dividing and simply nodded once. Niko helped Anil slip the dry sock over the wet foot while the courtyard stones cooled beneath them. The cloth felt smooth against Niko's own bare toes when they wrapped the second foot the same way. Soft clinks rose as Grandpa Sol set bowls of soup along the long table.

Wind in the fig leaves stirred a slow, leafy hush above their heads while Niko and Anil finished tying the cloth snug. Grandpa Sol ladled warm soup into three bowls without saying a single speech about what had just happened. Niko turned the cup so the handle faced the room and then passed the bread basket down to Anil first. The soup steamed gently, and the rising warmth touched Niko's cheeks. Anil wiggled the dry socked foot happily under the table. Niko felt proud of the smaller even shares that let both of them feel dry and included. Lantern light flickered on the soup spoons as everyone began to eat.

"Thank you for splitting them," Anil whispered across the table. Niko smiled and took a slow bite of bread that tasted mild and good. Grandpa Sol buttered another roll and set it halfway between the two children so neither had to stretch farther than the other. The night air grew a little cooler against Niko's shoulders, yet the soup kept their hands warm around the bowl. Fig leaves overhead shifted again with a soft rustle that matched the quiet clink of spoons. Niko glanced at the single dry sock on their own foot and the matching one on Anil's and felt the evening settle into something steady and kind.

Grandpa Sol poured more water and asked whether anyone wanted another slice of cheese. Niko answered, "Yes, please, and one for Anil too," making sure the pieces came out the same size. The cheese felt cool and firm when Niko picked it up. Anil laughed a small happy laugh that mixed with the low murmur of the courtyard fountain nearby. Niko rested both elbows lightly on the tablecloth and listened to the peaceful sounds around them. Grandpa Sol refilled the basket without fuss. Every plate stayed full enough, and no one reached ahead of anyone else.

After the bowls were empty, Niko and Anil leaned back while Grandpa Sol cleared the spoons. Niko felt the straw mat again when they stretched their legs under the bench. The dry sock on one foot and the soft cloth on the other kept both children comfortable as the last light faded from the walls. Anil told a short silly story about a beetle that had climbed the fig trunk earlier, and Niko listened with a quiet grin. Grandpa Sol brought out three small cups of warm milk that smelled sweet. Niko held theirs carefully so the heat stayed against their palms. The courtyard grew quieter except for the gentle settling of chairs.

Niko helped fold the spare napkins while Anil stacked the empty bowls. Grandpa Sol smiled at the neat pile and left the choice of the shared socks exactly as Niko had made it. The air turned cooler still, yet the milk had left a lasting warmth inside. Niko yawned once and leaned against Grandpa Sol's arm for a moment. Anil did the same on the other side of the bench. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Niko watched the quiet light and felt the courtyard supper close around them like a gentle blanket ready for sleep.
