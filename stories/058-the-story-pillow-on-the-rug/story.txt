A tiny jingle of a cat's collar bell drifted across the quiet cabin floor. Vera heard it at once and tilted their head, watching the soft yellow lamp light slide along the wooden boards as the ferry rocked with the slowest possible sway. The air still held a gentle warmth from the day, and Vera felt it settle against their cheeks like a careful hand. They sat on the ferry bench with a folded blanket tucked beside them and waited for the jingle to come again, smiling when it did, soft and near.

Vera shifted a little so their shoulder brushed the smooth wood of the bench back. The ferry windows showed only a calm dark water and a few distant lights that looked like sleepy fireflies. Beside Vera rested a story pillow big enough to share, round and plump and covered in cloth that caught the lamp glow in small silver threads. Miss Dalca sat at the other end of the same bench, and Arun sat cross-legged on the folded blanket at Vera’s feet, both of them quiet in the easy way of people who liked evenings on the water. Vera breathed in and noticed how the cabin felt cozy rather than cold.

“The pillow is for stories,” Miss Dalca said in a voice no louder than the water against the hull. Vera nodded and reached out one hand to pat the pillow’s top. The fabric felt cool at first, then warmed under their palm. Arun looked up with bright eyes and waited. Vera could hear the ferry engines humming far below like a contented bee, a low sound that never hurried. The three of them sat in that gentle space while the lamps made little pools of gold on the bench.

Vera thought about the pillow and how large it was. They pictured one person leaning on it first and another waiting, and the picture did not sit right inside their chest. “If only one of us leans on it first, it will not feel fair,” Vera said softly. Arun’s face stayed open and kind. Miss Dalca only watched without speaking. The cabin air carried the mild sweetness of cocoa from the thermos still resting near the door, and Vera felt that smell settle around them like an extra blanket. They wanted every person on the bench to feel the same easy welcome.

Vera pressed both hands into the middle of the story pillow and made a smaller, even place right in the center where two shoulders could rest at once. They smoothed the cloth so the dip was the same on both sides. “There,” Vera said. “Now we can share it the same way.” Arun scooted closer on the folded blanket until their arm touched Vera’s. The wool sleeve of Arun’s sweater brushed Vera’s wrist with a soft scratchy warmth that felt friendly and real. Miss Dalca saw the new shape of the pillow and simply nodded once, letting the choice stand without any long talk.

“I like this better,” Arun whispered. Vera smiled and leaned their shoulder into one side of the even dip while Arun leaned into the other. The pillow held them both without tipping. Outside the window the water made a quiet lapping sound against the ferry’s side, steady and kind. Vera felt the bench under them stay solid and cool, and the folded blanket under Arun stayed neat. The lamps painted soft circles on the ceiling, and everything inside the cabin felt slow and safe.

Miss Dalca poured a little more warm drink into two small cups and set them within easy reach. Vera took a careful sip and tasted the last gentle cocoa notes while the cup warmed their fingers. “We can take turns telling one small piece of a story,” Vera said. Arun nodded and began with a quiet sentence about a boat that carried moonlight in its pockets. Vera added a sentence about stars that liked to rest on blankets. Miss Dalca listened and added nothing more than a smile that stayed in her eyes. The ferry kept its slow rock, never sharp, never sudden.

Vera rested their cheek against the shared pillow and felt how evenly it held them both. The cabin temperature stayed mild, neither chilly nor heavy, and the light from the lamps grew softer as the evening deepened. Arun’s breathing matched Vera’s for a few calm breaths. Somewhere near the door the cat’s collar had long gone quiet, yet the memory of its friendly jingle still floated in Vera’s mind like a small good thing already known. Miss Dalca folded her hands and watched the water slide past in dark smooth ribbons. Vera felt their own eyelids grow pleasantly heavy while the pillow stayed steady under both of them.

Vera rocked once on the bench in a tiny motion and then let the motion stop by itself, settling deeper into the even share of the pillow. Arun did the same. The ferry carried them onward under a sky full of quiet stars, and the folded blanket kept its place like a soft shore. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Vera closed their eyes all the way and felt only warmth, only the shared pillow, only the gentle boat, while sleep arrived without hurry and without a single sharp edge.
