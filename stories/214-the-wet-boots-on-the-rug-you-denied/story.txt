A soft drip-drip whispered from the corner of the workshop, and Pema paused to notice it right away. The warm yellow light from the overhead lamp painted the wooden bench in gentle stripes, and the air felt pleasantly cool against Pema’s cheeks. Wet boots sat on a folded rug right beside the stacks of wood, glue pots, and neat rows of paper labels, and Pema’s fingers brushed the damp leather as they leaned closer. The quiet drip came from a little puddle that had seeped out onto the rug’s edge. Pema’s shoulders felt heavy for a moment while they stood still and listened.

Pema almost slid the whole soggy bundle behind a pile of scrap wood so no one would see. The rug smelled faintly of wool and water, and the boots left dark prints that looked messy next to the tidy glue bottles. A distant kitchen scent of tomato soup drifted through the open doorway and made the workshop feel cozy instead of worried. Pema’s hands hovered, ready to tuck everything away, but they stopped and took a slow breath of that warm soup smell. The lamp hummed softly overhead while Pema decided what to do next.

Just then a cat’s collar bell gave one clear little jingle as the cat padded across the floorboards near Pema’s feet. The sound was bright and friendly, and Pema glanced down to see the cat sniff the edge of the wet rug without any blame in its green eyes. Soft afternoon light filtered through the window and rested on the labels that waited to be stuck onto finished wooden boxes. Pema whispered to the cat, “I almost said you did it,” and the cat only blinked and sat down. The bench felt solid and familiar under Pema’s palm.

Uncle Idris stepped into the workshop with quiet footsteps and a kind smile already waiting. The wood of the bench still held the faint warmth of the day’s work, and glue bottles stood ready like quiet helpers. Pema looked up and said, “Uncle Idris, my boots got wet outside and I set them on this rug and made a mess. I almost hid them.” Uncle Idris nodded slowly and answered, “Thank you for telling me the plain truth, Pema. We can mend what can be mended together.” The air between them felt easy and calm, filled only with the soft tick of a wall clock.

Pema pointed to the damp circle on the rug and added, “I thought maybe the cat had spilled something, but it was only me.” Uncle Idris crouched beside the bench so his eyes met Pema’s and said, “That was brave to say, Pema. No teasing here—just us fixing it.” Together they lifted the wet boots carefully onto a dry wooden stool near the glue pots. The labels nearby stayed crisp and clean in the steady lamp light, and Pema felt their chest loosen with every shared movement. The workshop smelled of wood shavings and the lingering tomato soup from earlier.

Uncle Idris reached for a low basket whose wicker handle felt smooth and slightly springy under Pema’s curious fingers when they helped carry it closer. Inside lay soft cloths perfect for soaking up water. Pema took one cloth and pressed it gently against the rug while Uncle Idris dabbed the boots. “The rug will dry fine by the warm lamp,” Uncle Idris said, and Pema replied, “I can help wipe every spot.” The texture of the wicker handle stayed in Pema’s mind as a friendly reminder of working side by side. Cool air from the doorway mixed with the gentle heat rising from the lamp.

They smoothed the rug flat across a clear stretch of the bench, and Pema carefully smoothed one wrinkle out of the cloth before folding it neatly. Glue caps clicked shut with tiny sounds as Uncle Idris set them aside to make more room. Pema held a dry cloth and patted the boots until no more droplets fell, feeling the leather grow less cold under their hands. The cat watched from a safe distance, its quiet presence adding to the peaceful room. Soft shadows stretched across the labels but never hid the honest work happening right there.

Uncle Idris and Pema placed the boots on a rack of wooden pegs to finish airing and draped the rug over a low rail where warm air could reach every fiber. Pema said, “It feels better telling the truth,” and Uncle Idris answered with a gentle voice, “It always does, Pema. Look how neat the bench looks again.” They wiped the last damp marks from the wood surface until it gleamed under the lamp. The whole workshop seemed to breathe quietly around them, full of the mild scent of drying wool and clean leather. Pema rested both hands on the solid edge of the bench and felt settled.

Later Pema sat on a stool beside Uncle Idris while the last of the daylight faded into evening calm. The lantern by the window glowed softly. A light draft carried the faintest remaining warmth from the kitchen, and the cat’s soft fur brushed Pema’s ankle once more. Everything that needed care had been tended with steady hands and quiet words, and the workshop bench of wood, glue, and labels looked ready for whatever gentle project tomorrow might bring. Pema leaned their head against Uncle Idris’s shoulder and listened to the slow, peaceful ticking that filled the peaceful space.
