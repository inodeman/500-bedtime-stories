The warm smell of cinnamon drifted down the quiet school hallway as Kira’s hands stayed busy, carefully straightening the soft scarves that hung from the wooden pegs and brushing a bit of dust from the seat of a low stool. Afternoon light slanted through the high windows in gentle gold stripes, and the polished floor felt cool under Kira’s shoes while they worked. Music stands stood in a neat row like patient silver trees, and the air still held a little of the day’s leftover hum from singing practice. Kira liked this calm stretch of corridor best when everything was almost ready for the evening gathering of families and neighbors. They pressed one last scarf into place, feeling the soft knit against their fingers, and listened to their own quiet breathing.

Neighbor Sami appeared at the far end of the hallway, arms full in a way that made walking slow. In one hand Sami balanced a round pie tin wrapped in a clean cloth, and in the other they tried to keep a small lantern from tipping. The lantern’s metal handle dug into Sami’s palm, and the pie tin wobbled each time a step landed. Kira watched the careful shuffle and the way the lantern light trembled across the stools and music stands. The hallway smelled faintly cleaner now that the cinnamon scent had settled lower near the floorboards. Kira stood still by the pegs, noticing how awkward the two things looked together for one person.

“That pie looks heavy,” Kira said softly, already stepping closer so their voice would not bounce too loud off the walls.  
Sami looked up with a small tired smile and answered, “It is warm still, and the lantern keeps slipping when I try to hold both.”  
Kira nodded and reached without waiting for more words. “I can carry the lantern. Your hands can keep the pie steady.”  
“Thank you, Kira,” Sami said, and the relief in the tone was clear as a gentle breeze through an open door.

Kira took the lantern by its smooth handle and felt the mild heat that rose from the glass sides, a cozy temperature that warmed their fingers without burning. The light inside glowed the color of soft honey and painted slow circles on the floor as they walked. Music stands caught tiny sparks of that glow and sent them sliding along the metal edges. Kira stayed close beside Sami so the path stayed even between the stools. They could hear the quiet brush of their own sleeves and the soft clink of the pie tin against Sami’s coat button. Nothing hurried them. The hallway seemed wider and kinder with two people sharing the load.

They passed the long row of pegs where coats waited like sleeping friends, and Kira kept the lantern lifted just high enough that the light would not swing into anyone’s eyes if someone else arrived. Sami shifted the pie to both hands now and walked with easier steps. “I baked it for the music circle tonight,” Sami explained. “I wanted the lantern so the path from the gate would stay bright, but my arms decided otherwise.” Kira smiled and answered, “Now the lantern has its own walker, and the pie has both of yours.” The words floated gently between them. Somewhere far at the schoolyard edge a gate latch clicked shut with a neat metal sound, quick and finished, then silence returned. Kira held the lantern steadier and felt proud of the quiet help without needing anyone to clap.

Near the middle of the hallway a small table held a few extra spoons for the gathering, and one of them caught Kira’s eye because it felt different when they brushed it by accident. The spoon was walnut-smooth against Kira’s fingertip, polished so carefully that no ridge remained, cool and pleasant to touch. Kira set it back in its place and kept walking. The lantern light made the spoon gleam for a second like a tiny moon. Sami chuckled under their breath at the unexpected shine. “Everything looks friendlier with your light,” Sami said. Kira only shrugged and kept the lantern level, happy that the pie tin no longer tilted. The air near the windows felt cooler than the middle of the hall, a soft difference on their cheeks.

They reached the spot where the music stands curved into a half circle, ready for the evening songs. Kira lowered the lantern onto a safe ledge beside a stool so its glow could reach the open space without being in the way. The light spread across the floor in a calm pool. Sami set the pie on the same ledge, and the cloth cover still held a whisper of heat. Kira stood for a moment and listened to the quiet. Then they hummed four notes, soft as a moth’s wing, and left a rest of silence after them so the notes could settle. The hallway accepted the little tune and the empty pause alike. No one else needed to hear it. Kira simply wanted the room to feel ready and peaceful.

Sami adjusted the cloth over the pie one last time and turned toward Kira with warm eyes. “You saw what I needed before I could ask,” Sami said. Kira looked at the lantern and the pie side by side and answered, “Two hands for the pie and one pair for the light made better sense.” The words came plain and easy. Outside light faded a little more, and the lantern’s glow grew gentler against the darkening corners near the pegs. Kira brushed a speck of lint from a nearby stool so the seat would feel clean later. The temperature in the hallway stayed mild, neither warm nor cold, perfect for waiting. They both stood without hurry, sharing the comfortable quiet that comes after a small job done together.

Later, when families began to arrive in soft pairs and the first low voices filled the hall, Kira stayed near the music stands and watched the lantern do its simple work. The pie waited under its cloth, and the pegs held coats that smelled faintly of outdoor air. Kira’s hands rested open at their sides now, no longer busy, and the cool floor still steadied their feet. They thought of the walk from the far end of the hallway and how light the lantern had felt once it had a proper carrier. Neighbor Sami waved once from across the circle of stands, and Kira waved back. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
