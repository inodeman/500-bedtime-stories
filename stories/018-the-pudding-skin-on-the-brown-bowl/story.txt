The brown bowl rested in the center of the greenhouse bench, its sides smooth and plain, while the rice pudding inside waited under a pale cloth for the soft skin to settle across the top. Fine warm steam still curled in tiny threads from the places where the cloth did not quite seal the rim, and the south window sent a steady golden light that turned the seed pots nearby into little circles of brightness. The air close to the bowl felt gently heated, as if the pudding held onto the afternoon. Kavi leaned in so near that their breath almost touched the cloth, studying every fold without lifting it, because Auntie June had carried the bowl out here so the pudding could rest quietly among the plants while the two of them tended the soil.

Kavi’s fingers hovered above the edge of the cloth, full of the wish to check whether the soft skin had begun to form, yet the pudding was still busy with its own slow work. The greenhouse held a mild warmth that wrapped around Kavi’s bare arms like a light blanket, and the wooden bench felt solid under their palms. Auntie June sat on a low stool only an arm’s reach away, sorting seed packets with calm hands. “Auntie June, may I lift the cloth just a little to see?” Kavi asked in a voice no louder than a leaf rustle. The question hung in the bright space while Kavi waited for her answer, eyes still fixed on the covered bowl.

Auntie June turned and gave a small kind smile. “Not yet, Kavi,” she said. “The rice pudding needs to stay covered until the skin grows soft all on its own.” As she spoke she reached across, and the thick wool sleeve of her cardigan brushed lightly against Kavi’s wrist, its cozy texture soft and familiar. Kavi felt that gentle touch and nodded. A faint scent of cocoa drifted from the mug Auntie June had placed at the far end of the bench, mixing with the green smell of leaves. Soft light from the south window shifted across the seed pots when a cloud passed high above, painting brief shadows that moved like quiet friends.

Kavi chose to leave the cloth exactly where it lay and look for a quiet task instead. They drew a small wooden chair close to the bench and sat down with care. The chair gave one soft creak as Kavi rocked it backward once and then let it drift forward and stop all by itself. In the stillness that followed, a cat slipped through the open greenhouse door, and the tiny bell on its collar gave a single clear chime that faded into the warm air. The temperature stayed even and comfortable, never sharp, while the south window kept pouring its steady brightness over everything. Kavi watched the cat settle on a mat and then turned their attention to the neat row of seed pots waiting on the bench.

With slow fingers Kavi began pressing small seeds into the cool damp earth of each pot, feeling the soft grit under their nails and the slight chill of the moist soil. Auntie June observed for a moment and said, “You are placing them so gently, Kavi.” Kavi answered, “I like helping the seeds have their own time too.” The light from the window laid delicate patterns across the bench where the shadow of a hanging fern swayed ever so slightly. A mild heat rose from the dark earth as if it remembered the sun, and Kavi covered every seed with a thin blanket of dirt, patting the surface smooth without any hurry at all.

Long easy minutes stretched while Auntie June tipped a watering can in thin shining streams. Kavi listened to the quiet patter of water soaking into the pots and kept their glance on the brown bowl yet never touched the cloth. The greenhouse air carried the fresh green scent of watered leaves, and Kavi noticed how the afternoon light grew deeper gold as time moved. Their hands rested on the warm smooth wood of the bench, tracing the grain with one fingertip. “Shall we give these last pots a drink together?” Auntie June asked. Kavi replied, “Yes, when you are ready,” and the two of them worked side by side in the calm brightness.

The water made soft musical sounds as it met the soil, and Kavi stood close enough to feel a cooler breath of air rising from the wet pots, a pleasant contrast to the warmer pocket of space near the pudding bowl. They sat again in the chair without rocking and simply watched the cloth remain perfectly still now that the steam had gone. The south window showed the garden path outside washed in the same gentle light that filled the greenhouse. Kavi’s shoulders loosened, and they breathed in the quiet, knowing the rice pudding was finishing its soft skin in its own unhurried way while the seeds rested under their new earth covers.

After a good stretch of peaceful time Auntie June rested the back of her hand against the side of the brown bowl. “I believe it is ready now, Kavi,” she said in a low happy tone. Together they lifted the cloth away, and underneath the rice pudding wore a perfect smooth skin that looked creamy and inviting. The sweet pudding scent mingled with the earlier cocoa, and the bowl felt just cool enough for careful hands to hold. Auntie June handed Kavi a small spoon, and Kavi tasted a tiny edge portion, feeling the gentle silky texture on their tongue while soft light danced across the surface like a quiet welcome.

As the day eased toward evening the greenhouse light softened, yet The lantern by the window glowed softly. Kavi and Auntie June shared the rest of the rice pudding in slow spoonfuls while the seed pots stood ready in their neat line. The air cooled to a mild night temperature that felt kind against Kavi’s cheeks, and they leaned for a moment against Auntie June’s side, listening to the greenhouse settle into deeper quiet. The cat’s bell stayed silent now, the wool sleeve nearby still cozy, and everything had arrived at its right moment under the gentle lantern light.
