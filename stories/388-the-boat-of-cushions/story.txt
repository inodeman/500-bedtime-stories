A tiny ticking sound drifted through the quiet bedroom from the dresser. Bodhi turned their head on the pillow and noticed at once that it was the tin clock, its hands advancing with a soft friendly click in the mild evening air. Warm lamp light pooled across the floorboards near Bodhi’s bed and made the walls look buttery while a gentle draft cooled their bare ankles. Bodhi sat up slowly, rubbing sleep from their eyes, and listened again to that steady tick before the open window carried Miss Dalca’s calm invitation from the garden. They swung their legs down, felt the rug underfoot, slipped into slippers, and padded downstairs with quiet steps, already curious about the special thing waiting among the plants before true night arrived.

Bodhi stepped outside into the garden where cool dusk air brushed their cheeks and arms. Miss Dalca stood beside a long garden row marked by simple string stretched between posts, and two watering cans rested at either end like quiet markers. Lale waved from the middle, hands resting on a pile of soft cushions arranged into the shape of a little boat right there between the young plants. Miss Dalca had brought the cushions outdoors so Bodhi and Lale could pretend a gentle voyage along the soil path before bedtime, using the string as a guide rope and the watering cans as start and finish posts. The late sunlight cast long soft shadows across the dirt, and Bodhi reached out to touch a cushion that felt pleasantly cool. “We made a cushion boat for you, Bodhi,” Lale said with a bright smile.

Bodhi knelt beside the cushion boat and ran one finger along an edge, feeling a quilt stitch that ran in neat little raised lines across the fabric. The stitches felt bumpy and comforting under their fingertip, like tiny paths sewn by careful hands. Lale helped slide two more cushions into place while Miss Dalca held the string that would serve as a pretend rope for the short journey down the garden row. The watering cans gleamed dully in the fading light, one at each end. Bodhi heard a distant bee humming among the flowers, and the soil under their knees stayed cool after the warm day. “If we rush the tucking, the sides will slip and it will sink,” Miss Dalca said calmly. “We take our time together.”

At first Bodhi and Lale both reached for the same side of the cushion boat in a small hurry to begin the game. The sides slipped apart because they moved too quickly, and the middle cushion dipped down into the soft dirt with a quiet plop that made the whole shape sag. Bodhi felt a little warmth rise in their cheeks from the sudden motion, and the light on the nearby leaves flickered as a mild breeze passed. “Oh, it sank,” Lale said, giggling without any worry at all. Bodhi laughed too and patted the cushion gently back into place while Miss Dalca simply smiled and waited. The string between the posts swayed, the two watering cans stood steady, and the garden row kept its peaceful feeling even while the boat needed fixing again.

Bodhi thought for a moment and looked over at Lale with a clear idea. “We can split it into two slow jobs,” Bodhi said. “I will tuck this long side while you tuck the other side, and we go as slow as turtles walking.” Lale nodded at once, their hair catching the soft orange light of the setting sun. The air had cooled another gentle degree, and Bodhi felt the change clearly on their bare arms as they began the careful work. Miss Dalca only watched at first, then prepared to offer a quiet count when the moment was right. The cushions pressed together under patient hands, and no one rushed anything along the garden row with its string and two watering cans waiting at the ends.

While Bodhi tucked the left side fold by careful fold, Lale mirrored the same slow care on the right side so the boat would hold. Miss Dalca spoke softly and said, “This joining can be called the gentle loop knot when you finish the last tuck.” Bodhi repeated the name under their breath and kept their fingers moving with steady patience. A light scent of lavender drifted from the purple flowers planted at the far end of the row and mixed kindly with the evening air. Bodhi noticed their own soft breathing filling the quiet spaces between each tuck. The shared pace with Lale felt easy and sure, and the cushion boat began to hold its round shape better with every slow motion they made together.

Miss Dalca began a soft even count to help them stay matched. “One for the corner, two for the middle, three for the end,” she said, and Bodhi and Lale timed their tucks to the gentle numbers. The string above them cast a thin shadow that Bodhi watched for a second as it moved the width of a thumb when one leaf shifted in the breeze. Cool air brushed Bodhi’s cheeks again, and the last cushion edge felt smooth now that all the parts lined up. They worked without any hurry beside the garden row, the two watering cans reflecting small bits of pale sky. Bodhi glanced at Lale and saw the same calm focus in their friend’s face, and together the sides stayed tucked tight and true.

At last the cushion boat sat firm and ready between the lines of string. Bodhi and Lale climbed in carefully, settling among the soft shapes while Miss Dalca poured a little pretend water from one watering can into the other with a quiet chuckle of delight. The light had turned to a deep blue dusk, and Bodhi felt the cushions support them like a real gentle craft that would not sink. “We did it together,” Lale whispered happily. Bodhi nodded and held the string like a tiller, steering them slowly from one end of the garden row to the other without any dipping. The temperature dropped just enough that Miss Dalca brought a light wrap for their shoulders, and everything felt complete and peaceful among the plants and the two watering cans.

Later Bodhi returned inside with Lale and Miss Dalca after the garden play finished in the kindest way. They sat together by the window where the last outdoor light faded into night, and Bodhi rested their hands on the sill. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Bodhi breathed the calm of the room and felt the smooth cool wood under their palms while Miss Dalca tucked a blanket around their knees and Lale leaned close with a sleepy smile. Bodhi closed their eyes for a moment, remembering the tucked sides and the two slow jobs shared side by side, while the warm steady glow stayed and the whole house seemed to rest with them in quiet comfort.
