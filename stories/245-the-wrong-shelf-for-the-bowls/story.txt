"Please help me set these bowls on the shelf before supper," the baker said to Tala in a calm voice that matched the soft light filling the cottage hallway of hooks, rugs, and boots. Cool evening air drifted in and brushed Tala's face while coats hung still on the wooden hooks. Tala smiled at the baker and took a careful step forward, ready to help with the evening task of putting every bowl where it belonged after a long day of mixing.

Tala reached toward the shelves built into the wall near the boots and began to place the bowls one by one with slow care. The baker had asked for six bowls to rest in a neat line so the count looked right for tomorrow's baking plans. Tala wanted everything to appear perfect and stacked three bowls on a higher shelf that was meant only for plates instead. The bowls fit the narrow space, and from below the number still seemed correct if no one looked too closely at the arrangement. Warm light from a lamp touched the edges of the bowls and made them shine like pale moons against the wood. Tala's fingers lingered on the last bowl and felt its glazed surface, smooth and familiar from many days of holding dough.

Tala almost stepped back and said nothing at all about the mistake. Then Tala heard a gate latch click softly outside the cottage, a small metal sound that made Tala pause and listen with wide eyes. The sound reminded Tala that the baker trusted them with this simple evening job. Tala rested both palms on their knees for a quiet second and breathed in the calm of the hallway. Boots and coats waited without any hurry while Tala decided what felt right to do next.

Tala walked over to where the baker stood near a low shelf and spoke the plain truth without waiting. "I put the bowls on the wrong shelf so the count looked right, but they do not belong there," Tala said in a steady voice. The baker turned and looked at Tala with kind eyes that held no cloud of worry. The air near that part of the wall carried a faint coolness from a damp clay wall that held the shelves steady through many seasons of weather. Tala could feel the cool dampness when they brushed the wall lightly with one hand. Nothing about the moment felt heavy. The baker simply nodded and set down a folded cloth on a nearby hook.

"Thank you for telling me, Tala," the baker replied in a warm tone that filled the whole hallway like gentle steam. Coats swayed a little when Tala brushed past one wooden hook. Soft yellow light rested on the floorboards and made the wood grain easy to see under their feet. Tala and the baker worked without any hurry, stacking the bowls on the proper lower shelf where mixing bowls always lived in a happy row.

Tala watched as the baker wrote the correct shelf numbers with a slow pencil so tomorrow would be simple for everyone. The fresh paper smell mixed gently with the leftover scent of bread drifting from the kitchen doorway. Tala held one bowl steady with both hands while the baker finished the neat note. Outside, evening settled deeper over the yard, and the hallway grew quieter still around them. Tala liked standing close to the baker and hearing the soft scratch of the pencil across the page.

"The bowls look happy in their right place now," Tala said with a small laugh that bounced lightly off the walls and ceiling. The baker smiled and answered, "Yes, Tala, and the count is true without any extra stacking." They both checked the straight line of six bowls once more and nodded together. Hooks held their coats in peaceful rows, rugs warmed the floor under tired feet, and boots waited for morning walks through the garden. Tala ran one hand along the edge of a thick rug and felt its worn wool threads soft against their palm. The baker simply patted Tala's shoulder with a flour-dusted hand that felt soft and kind against the sweater. Tala looked at the hallway one more time, at the hooks, the rugs, and the boots all resting in their places just like the bowls now. "Time for bed soon, dear Tala," the baker said. Tala nodded and stretched both arms toward the ceiling beams.

Later Tala sat on the edge of their bed while the baker tucked a blanket around their shoulders with slow care. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Tala remembered the bowls resting on the proper shelf and the honest words they had spoken without fear. Everything that needed mending had been mended side by side, and Tala drifted toward sleep feeling calm and safe in the gentle steady light.
