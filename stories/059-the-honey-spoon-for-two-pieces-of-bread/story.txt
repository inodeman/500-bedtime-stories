"Uncle Idris, the coats smell like rain and the lunch mats are waiting," Anil said, their voice soft in the quiet school cloakroom where hooks held damp jackets and little shoes lined the wall. Afternoon light slanted through the high window and warmed the wooden benches. Anil stood near the open doorway that led just beyond to the lunch mats, where the air felt still and kind. From somewhere outside came the gentle hush of wind in the fig leaves, brushing the panes like a slow breath. Uncle Idris set down a small cloth-wrapped bundle on the bench, and Anil watched the folds settle. The cloakroom felt cool at the edges yet cozy near the doorway, and Anil rested one hand on a sleeve that felt soft as a blanket.

Anil unwrapped the cloth and found two pieces of bread with a honey spoon resting carefully between them, the golden honey gleaming where it clung to the spoon’s curve. The bread looked plain and sturdy, and the honey smelled faintly sweet beside the fresh scent of pear skins that drifted from a fruit box left open on a low shelf. Anil touched the spoon handle and felt how smooth it was. Uncle Idris smiled without hurrying them. Nils had just stepped in from the hallway, cheeks pink from the walk, and sat cross-legged on the edge of the first lunch mat just beyond the cloakroom threshold. Anil glanced from the honey spoon to Nils and back again, holding the bread so the spoon stayed snug.

Anil thought about how the honey spoon between two pieces of bread would not feel fair if only one person was served first. The mats beyond the doorway were smooth and clean, and a thin strip of sunlight lay across them like a quiet path. Anil’s fingers brushed a warm coin in their pocket, the metal smooth and comforting against their palm, and that small warmth helped them think. “Nils is here too,” Anil said gently, looking at Uncle Idris. Nils looked up with bright eyes and said nothing yet, only waited. The cloakroom air carried a mild coolness from the open door, and Anil could hear the soft shuffle of Nils settling more comfortably on the mat.

Uncle Idris stood nearby with calm hands and did not reach for the bread. Anil turned the honey spoon a little so the light caught the honey and made it shine like a tiny river. The bread felt firm under Anil’s careful fingers. Outside the window the day stayed mild, and inside the cloakroom the coats hung still. Anil decided to make a smaller, even share so that Nils would be included right away. They pressed the spoon gently so a little honey stayed on each piece, then broke each slice into smaller halves that matched as closely as small hands could manage. The honey stretched in thin threads that smelled sweet and clean.

“Here, Nils, this part is for you,” Anil said, carrying two small even pieces across the short step onto the lunch mats just beyond. Nils took them with both hands and smiled. “Thank you, Anil. It looks lovely,” Nils answered, the words soft and glad. Anil sat down on the mat beside Nils and kept two matching pieces for themself, the honey spoon now resting empty and clean on the cloth. Uncle Idris watched from the cloakroom doorway and let the choice stand without a speech, only a quiet nod that felt like permission and peace. The mats were smooth under Anil’s legs, and the room’s light stayed gentle and golden.

Anil and Nils ate slowly, tasting the honey that was still warm from the spoon’s earlier rest between the bread. The sweetness spread gently on Anil’s tongue. Uncle Idris folded the cloth neatly and set it aside on the bench. A faint draft from the hallway brushed Anil’s ankles, cool and soft. Anil noticed a rug corner that had curled up near the edge of the lunch mats and straightened the rug corner with a toe until it lay flat again. The simple motion felt good and tidy. Nils hummed a tiny tune while chewing, and Anil listened and felt settled.

“The honey tastes like sunshine,” Anil said after a quiet moment, and Nils nodded in agreement. Uncle Idris remained near the hooks, checking that every coat hung straight, his footsteps barely sounding on the floor. Anil looked at the empty honey spoon shining faintly on the cloth and felt glad that the share had been even from the first bite. The lunch mats held their two small shadows side by side. Pear light from the high window softened the walls, and the whole space smelled clean and friendly. Anil rested their back against the low wall between cloakroom and mats and breathed slowly.

When the last crumbs were gone, Anil wiped their fingers on a napkin Uncle Idris offered without words. Nils did the same. The cloakroom grew quieter still as the afternoon deepened. Anil felt the leftover warmth of the coin in their pocket once more and smiled to themself. Outside, the fig leaves had gone still, and only a distant bird called once. Uncle Idris gathered the cloth and the spoon and tucked them away. Anil and Nils sat a little longer on the mats, swinging their feet gently, content and full of quiet honey sweetness. No one needed to hurry.

Later, when the hallway lights dimmed and the school day folded into evening calm, Anil walked with Uncle Idris toward the door that led home. Nils waved from the corner and went the other way with soft steps. Anil’s hand rested in Uncle Idris’s larger one, warm and steady. The air outside smelled of evening gardens. Back near the lunch mats the room waited empty and peaceful. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Anil glanced once more toward that gentle light before the door closed, carrying the memory of even shares and quiet mats into the kind night.
