The warm smell of toasted sesame drifted up the stairs while Lior stood at the bedroom window, their busy hands already rubbing slow circles through the thin mist that fogged the cold glass. Pale frosty light spilled across Lior’s cheeks and made the sill feel sharp and clean under their fingertips. Outside, every twig wore a coat of silver, and the quiet of the early morning pressed gently against the pane. Lior breathed out a soft cloud, watched it bloom, then wiped it away again with careful palms. The room behind them held leftover kitchen warmth, yet the glass stayed icy and bright. Lior leaned closer until their nose almost touched the frost feathers. “Hello,” they whispered, soft as falling dust.

From the glittered corner of the window came an answer, an echo that answered one soft hello with the same gentle word a heartbeat later, round and clear as a pebble dropped in still water. Lior froze in the thin morning light, eyes wide, listening hard. The cool draft that slipped under the frame brushed their wrists and made the little hairs there stand up. They waited, then tried the word again, even softer. The echo returned it once more, patient and exact, living somehow among the ice crystals. Lior’s heart felt full and light at the same time. The wooden sill pressed smooth and cold beneath their leaning elbows while the rest of the house stayed hushed.

A real wish rose in Lior to pocket the echo or poke it just to see what might happen, and their fingers twitched near the sparkling frost. The temperature by the glass nipped at their knuckles, yet the wish stayed curious rather than rough. Lior imagined scooping the sound into a mitten or tapping it with one careful nail. Instead they flattened both hands on the sill and took a long quiet breath. “I could keep you,” Lior said to the shimmer, “but maybe you like the window best.” Sunlight shifted across the floorboards in soft gold stripes and warmed the backs of their legs while the pane stayed chilly. Lior decided the echo deserved the same gentleness they would give a sleeping kitten.

Soft footsteps padded down the hall, and Femi appeared in the doorway rubbing sleep from their eyes as the floorboards gave a tiny friendly creak. Cool air from the open window reached Femi’s face and made them blink. Lior turned with a bright quiet smile. “Come look, Femi, but quiet,” Lior said. Femi crossed the rug on careful socked feet and stood shoulder to shoulder with Lior, both framed by the frosty glow. Lior showed Femi how to look without disturbing it, pointing with a slow finger that never touched the glass where the echo seemed to rest. “Just watch,” Lior whispered. “Say hello very soft and wait.” The shared draft smelled clean and felt like winter’s first polite visit.

Femi leaned in and offered one soft hello of their own, and the echo answered again, gentle and clear. Lior nodded, calm and proud, while together they watched the faint shimmer living among the ice feathers on the pane. Morning light grew a little brighter as clouds drifted outside, painting soft gold across the white sheets and the wooden toys on the shelf. Lior kept every word low so nothing would startle the quiet wonder. “See how it stays if we are gentle?” Lior said. Femi’s warm shoulder pressed against Lior’s cooler one, and the stillness between them felt like holding a secret made of air and frost. Neither child reached forward; both simply looked and listened.

Auntie June stepped into the room soon after, wiping her hands on a cotton apron that felt soft and worn from many mornings, the fabric brushing the doorframe with a hush. She brought the cozy temperature of the hallway with her. “What are you two watching so carefully?” Auntie June asked in a warm voice. Outside, a gate latch clicked once in the quiet street, a small metal sound that settled into the morning like a polite knock. Lior gestured for her to come close without rushing and explained the echo that answered one soft hello and how they were leaving it be. Auntie June crouched so her eyes met the same height as Lior’s and Femi’s, and the three of them gazed together at the frosted glass.

They stayed side by side while Lior showed again how to look without poking or pocketing, hands open and still in the cool air near the pane. “It belongs to the window and the frost,” Lior said. Auntie June smiled and answered with her own soft hello, and the echo returned it once more, tying their three voices into a quiet circle. The cold from the glass made their noses tingle in a friendly way, and the pale sun turned the frost edges into tiny rainbows that danced without melting. No one spoke loudly. The bedroom held a peaceful hush broken only by their careful breaths and the distant settling of the house.

After a while Lior felt the wish to keep the echo fade into something kinder, a gladness that it could stay right where it was. Femi set the chair square to the table by the wall, sliding the wooden legs with a soft scrape so it sat neat and ready for later drawing or shared tea. The room grew gently warmer with three people close together, though the window glass remained fresh and bright. Lior patted the sill once, lightly, as if saying thank you without leaving yet. The frost began to thin in places where the sun touched hardest, yet the echo remained patient in its corner. They had all seen it, heard it, and chosen not to disturb it, and that choice felt as soft and right as a well-loved blanket.

As the morning stretched longer, Lior, Femi, and Auntie June stepped back from the window together, leaving the echo where it belonged among the last sparkles of frost. Lior’s hands, no longer busy with wiping, rested calmly at their sides while a hush settled over the bedroom like favorite flannel. Soft light pooled on the rug in slow golden shapes, and the air felt still and kind against their skin. They would remember the answered hello without needing to carry anything away. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Outside the world woke slowly under the thinning frost, and inside Lior felt full of quiet happiness that needed no extra words at all.
