Auntie Luz sat cross-legged on the woven mat in the music corner and turned the small wooden drum slowly in her lap, her palms gliding a thin layer of polish across its curved side while the air filled with the gentle smell of beeswax. Golden late-day light poured through the open window and warmed the smooth recorder that rested on a folded blue cloth beside her knee. Isla stood at the edge of the mat and felt the cool boards under their bare toes, watching the careful circles Auntie Luz made without any rush. A soft breeze drifted in and brushed Isla’s cheek, and the room held only the quiet creak of the drum’s rim and the faint hush of cloth. Auntie Luz glanced up once, smiled, and kept polishing as if there was all the time in the world for Isla to step closer when they were ready.

Isla lowered themself onto the cushion nearest the drum and noticed how the polished wood caught the light like a little moon. The music corner felt both familiar and new because Kei sat on the far side of the mat, a quiet friend who had only just arrived that afternoon. Isla’s own name sat shyly behind their teeth, small and hesitant in this slightly different circle of faces. They rested one hand on the mat and felt the rough weave press against their palm while Auntie Luz set the drum down with a soft wooden tap. Outside the window the fig tree moved, and Isla could hear the wind in the fig leaves making a steady, whispering rustle that seemed to keep time with their own careful breathing. Kei offered a small wave, and Isla waved back without speaking yet.

Auntie Luz picked up the recorder and wiped its mouthpiece with the cloth, the wood cool and pale under her fingers. “I have been waiting for both of you,” she said in a voice as soft as the afternoon light. “The drum is ready if anyone wants a turn, and the recorder can wait its turn too.” Isla listened to the calm words and felt the warmth of the sun patch that had crept across their ankle. The shy feeling around their name did not vanish, yet the music corner stayed peaceful, with no hurry and no demand for a loud performance. Kei looked at the drum with quiet interest, and Isla watched the way the light moved across the instrument’s surface like slow honey.

Isla reached out and let their fingertips rest on the side of the drum, feeling the polished wood that still held a faint warmth from Auntie Luz’s hands. They thought about pretending they did not mind if no one heard their name, about staying silent and letting the moment pass. Instead Isla drew a slow breath that tasted of the clean air coming through the window. Auntie Luz simply waited, her hands folded loosely in her lap, offering no push and no big stage. The temperature in the corner felt just right, neither too warm nor cool, and Isla’s shoulders eased a little as they sat with the choice resting gently between them and the drum.

“Would you like to try one soft beat first?” Auntie Luz asked, her tone kind and even. Isla nodded once and tapped the drum with two fingers, producing a low, round sound that rolled briefly through the corner and faded. Kei smiled without speaking, and Isla felt the mat’s texture again under their knees. The smallest brave version rose inside them then, not a shout, only a clear enough voice that could be heard by the people right there. Isla looked at Kei and then at Auntie Luz and said, “My name is Isla.” The words came out steady and plain, filling the quiet space without needing to be large.

Auntie Luz’s eyes crinkled at the corners. “Thank you, Isla. That was clear,” she answered. Kei gave a tiny nod and whispered, “Hello, Isla.” The three of them sat together while a single fig leaf drifted in through the open window and landed near Isla’s hand. Isla turned it over and touched a leaf’s soft underside, which felt like the finest velvet against their fingertip. The music corner held the leaf’s green scent for a moment, and Isla placed it carefully beside the recorder so it could stay part of the afternoon. No one asked for more words; the name had been said and received, and that was enough.

Isla lifted the recorder next and felt its light weight, then blew one long, gentle note that floated out like a thin silver thread. Auntie Luz kept time with two fingertips on the drum, soft and slow, while Kei watched the snail that had begun to cross one whole brick on the windowsill outside. The light had shifted lower now, turning the walls a calm peach color, and Isla played another quiet note that matched the easy pace of the drum. Their name no longer felt tucked away; it simply belonged in the corner along with the instruments and the friendly faces. The temperature stayed mild as the sun eased downward, and Isla’s breath moved easily in and out with each soft sound.

They set the recorder down and rested both hands on their lap, listening to the last echo fade into the room. Auntie Luz began to gather the cloth and the small jar of polish, her movements unhurried. “We can leave everything here for another day,” she said. Isla watched Kei still following the snail’s slow path across the brick, and the three of them shared the peaceful pause without needing any more talk. The music corner felt settled now, the drum silent yet ready, the recorder quiet on its cloth, and Isla’s name resting easily in the air between them all. A faint coolness arrived with the changing light, brushing Isla’s arms like a friendly goodbye to the brighter hours.

Isla leaned back against the cushion and felt the soft fabric support their shoulders while the room grew quieter still. Kei yawned behind one hand, and Auntie Luz dimmed the brighter lamp so only the gentler glow remained. Outside the fig leaves had gone almost still, and the snail had finished its brick. Isla closed their eyes for a moment and heard only the far-off hush of evening settling over the garden. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Auntie Luz smoothed the mat one last time, and Isla breathed in the calm of the music corner, ready for rest with their name spoken and held.
