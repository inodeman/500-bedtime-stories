Grandpa Sol sat already on the wide ceramic studio sill above the quiet plaza, his fingers working a soft gray sponge in slow circles over a half-dried bowl. Warm evening light poured across the sill boards and made the wet clay shine like river stones. Brisa climbed up beside him and felt the leftover heat of the day still rising from the wood. Grandpa Sol hummed a low tune while he rinsed the sponge in a cool basin, the water lapping gently against the sides. Brisa watched the sponge leave smooth tracks and listened to the distant shuffle of feet far below in the plaza. The air held a quiet stillness that wrapped around them both like a soft blanket.

Brisa reached for the metal bucket that held clean water for the brushes and began to drag it closer across the long wooden bench that ran under the sill. The bucket scrap scraped suddenly and left a fresh pale scratch right in the middle of the worn bench top. Brisa froze and stared at the thin white line that had not been there a moment before. Cool air drifted in from the open shutters and brushed Brisa’s cheeks while the scratch caught the light and looked brighter than everything else. Brisa’s hands stayed tight on the bucket handle, and they could feel the metal still vibrating a little from the drag. Grandpa Sol kept his eyes on the bowl for another breath, giving Brisa a small private space of quiet.

Brisa almost slid a folded cloth over the scratch so no one would see it. The cloth felt dry and thin under their fingers, and for a second they thought hiding it might be easier. Golden light from the low sun painted long stripes across the bench and made the scratch seem even clearer. Brisa’s heart beat a little faster, and they smelled the faint dust that always floated near the drying shelves. They held the cloth ready, then slowly set it down again without covering anything. Grandpa Sol dipped his sponge once more and waited without turning his head.

Brisa took a slow breath and said, “Grandpa Sol, I dragged the bucket and made this scratch on the bench.” Their voice stayed soft but clear in the quiet studio. Grandpa Sol turned at last and looked at the pale line with calm eyes. He set the sponge down on a small plate and rested one warm hand on Brisa’s shoulder. Brisa felt the steady weight of that hand and heard the quiet drip of water still falling back into the basin. Outside the window the first evening shadows stretched longer across the plaza stones.

Grandpa Sol smiled the gentle way he always did and said, “Thank you for telling me the plain truth, Brisa. We can mend what can be mended.” Brisa nodded and felt the tightness in their chest ease like a knot coming loose. They both leaned closer to the scratch while the last warm light of day slid lower across the sill. Brisa touched the rough edge of the scratch with one fingertip and noticed how it caught differently than the smooth old wood around it. Grandpa Sol reached for a small wooden box of tools that sat nearby.

Together they opened the box and took out a tiny piece of fine sandpaper and a soft beeswax stick. Brisa rubbed the sandpaper in careful little circles over the scratch while Grandpa Sol held the bench steady. The paper made a whispery sound against the wood, and Brisa felt the grain growing smoother under their hand. A light breeze carried the smell of cedar from the tool handles themselves into the air around them. Grandpa Sol said, “Easy circles, Brisa, just like smoothing clay,” and Brisa followed the gentle rhythm until the pale line grew softer and less sharp.

They wiped away the fine dust with a clean rag, then Brisa pressed the warm beeswax into the remaining mark and rubbed it with the side of their thumb. The wax felt smooth and slightly sticky at first, then melted into the wood under the heat of their skin. Grandpa Sol watched and said, “You are doing fine work, Brisa.” Brisa smiled and kept rubbing until the scratch nearly disappeared into the rest of the bench. Cooler air began to drift higher from the plaza, and the light turned a softer blue along the edges of the sill. They both sat back for a moment and looked at the mended place together.

Brisa noticed a finished cup resting near the edge of the sill and carefully turned the cup so the handle faced the room the way Grandpa Sol liked. The ceramic felt cool and smooth under their palms. They settled onto a straw mat that lay folded beside the bench and ran their fingers over its dry woven surface while Grandpa Sol packed the tools away. Outside they could hear the wind in the fig leaves moving in slow soft rushes that rose and fell like quiet breathing. Brisa leaned their head against Grandpa Sol’s arm and felt how steady and warm he stayed. The studio grew quieter still as the last bits of daylight slipped away.

Grandpa Sol poured a little fresh water into two small cups and handed one to Brisa. They sipped the cool water and watched the first lantern light begin to glow in windows across the plaza below. Brisa said, “I like mending things with you.” Grandpa Sol answered, “I like it too, Brisa.” They stayed side by side on the sill while the air cooled gently around their bare arms and the wooden boards under them held the last of the day’s warmth. Brisa’s eyelids grew heavier as they listened to the distant quiet of the plaza settling for night. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
