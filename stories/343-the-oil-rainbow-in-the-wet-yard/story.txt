The bright smell of lemon soap rose from Kavi’s busy hands as they wiped the kitchen table in slow circles with a damp cloth that left the wood cool and slick. Soft morning light poured through the frosty glass and made tiny rainbows dance across the wet surface while Kavi’s fingers pressed and turned, pressing and turning. The air against their bare arms felt crisp yet kind, and the cloth warmed a little with every pass. Kavi kept working until every crumb was gone and the table gleamed like still water under winter sun. They paused only to breathe in the clean citrus scent that clung to their skin and to smile at how quiet the house still felt so early.

Kavi carried the cloth to the sink, rinsed it under lukewarm water that trickled with a gentle hush, then walked down the short hall toward their bedroom where the big window waited. The floorboards felt smooth and cool under their socks. Outside, the yard lay silver with frost that sparkled like sugar on every blade of grass. Kavi pressed their nose close to the cold pane and felt the glass chill their forehead. Pale light filled the room and painted long soft bars across the bed. They stood very still, listening to the house settle, and watched their own breath make a small cloud on the glass before it faded.

Down below, leaning against the fence, sat the blue bicycle with its shiny spokes catching the first real sun. Right beside the front tire a thin oil rainbow shimmered in a shallow wet patch left by the melting frost, bands of pink, green, and gold lying flat and perfect on the dark ground. Kavi’s eyes widened at the quiet colors. The air outside looked still and sharp, and the rainbow stayed motionless as if it had always belonged there. They rested both palms on the chilly windowsill and leaned closer, feeling the wood grain under their skin. The sight made their chest feel light and warm even though the morning was cold.

For a moment Kavi wished they could run downstairs, scoop the pretty rainbow into a jar, or poke it gently with one finger to see the colors swirl. The desire felt real and bright inside them, like wanting a piece of sky. Yet they stayed at the window and simply looked, noticing how the rainbow curved around a fallen leaf without moving it. Light from the rising sun made the colors glow brighter, and the frost nearby crackled softly as it melted. Kavi breathed out slowly and decided the rainbow looked happiest right where it was, safe and whole on the ground by the bike.

They turned and called softly toward the hallway, “Remy, come see something beautiful by the bike, but we have to look only with our eyes.” Remy hurried in on quiet feet, hair still messy from sleep, and stood shoulder to shoulder with Kavi at the frosty window. “See the thin rainbow next to the tire?” Kavi whispered, pointing carefully so their finger never touched the glass. “It is pretty and not to touch. We can watch it stay just like that.” Remy nodded and pressed close, and together they studied the shiny bands without rushing. Warmth from Remy’s arm felt comforting against Kavi’s side while the cold window cooled their other cheek.

Outside the fig tree stirred and the sound of wind in the fig leaves drifted up like a faraway whisper of paper pages turning. Kavi and Remy stayed quiet so they could hear it while the oil rainbow held its gentle colors. “If we leave it alone the light will keep painting it all morning,” Kavi said. Remy answered, “It looks like someone spilled a tiny sunset on the ground.” They both smiled and kept their hands safely on the sill. The frost on the grass glittered harder for a moment, then softened under the growing sun, and the bedroom filled with a peaceful silver glow that made everything feel slow and kind.

Auntie June appeared in the doorway holding a floured towel that felt soft and powdery when Kavi reached out to touch its edge. “What are my two explorers watching so carefully?” she asked in a low warm voice. Kavi explained about the thin oil rainbow by the bike and how they had chosen to look without disturbing it. Auntie June stepped closer, rested one hand on Kavi’s shoulder, and peered out with them. “It is lovely just where it is,” she said. “Thank you for showing me how to see it that way.” The three of them stood together breathing the cool air that slipped through the window crack, and the floured towel left a faint dusty comfort on Kavi’s fingertips.

They watched a little longer until the colors stayed steady and the bike’s shadow stretched longer across the yard. Kavi felt glad they had kept their hands still and glad Remy and Auntie June had shared the quiet moment. The bedroom air warmed slowly as the sun climbed higher, and the last bits of frost on the sill melted into clear beads that caught the light. No one spoke for a whole minute; they simply let the rainbow be. Then Kavi gave a small happy sigh and stepped back from the glass, knowing the pretty thing would remain safe by the bicycle all day.

Later Kavi climbed into the big chair by the same window with a soft blanket that smelled faintly of sunshine, and Remy curled nearby while Auntie June hummed in the kitchen. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Outside the oil rainbow still rested undisturbed beside the bike, and inside the room felt gentle and full of quiet light as Kavi’s eyelids grew heavy and the morning continued its slow calm walk toward noon.
