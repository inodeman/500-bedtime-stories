Grandma Nia sat already on the low stool beside the bed in the bedroom with the hall light still on, her careful hands spreading a wide paper map across the quilt so the green lines caught the gentle glow. The soft light from the hall mixed with the cooler shadows near the curtains and made the paper look almost golden at the edges. Golda stood very still and watched Grandma Nia press a fingertip along one special route while the quilt felt warm under the map. Outside the open window a faint breeze carried the far-off click of a gate latch as someone closed it for the night. Golda took one quiet step nearer and smelled the clean cotton of the sheets mixed with something sweet.

Golda noticed right away that Grandma Nia had already taken care of the map marked with the route that had more benches. The thick green path curved past little drawings of trees and resting spots, and Grandma Nia had even circled three benches in soft pencil. Golda felt the cool air on their cheeks while they looked closer and heard the quiet tick of the small clock on the shelf. “I chose the slower way so our legs can rest whenever we need,” Grandma Nia said in her low calm voice. Golda nodded and touched the edge of the paper, which felt smooth and slightly warm from Grandma Nia’s hands.

A quick thank-you felt too thin inside Golda’s chest, so they looked around the bedroom for one useful thing Grandma Nia had not asked for. The hall light still shone steadily across the floorboards and made a pale rectangle near Golda’s feet. Golda walked to the low table where a glass sat empty and poured fresh water from the blue pitcher until the glass was full and clear. The water made a soft splashing sound that filled the quiet room for a moment. Golda set the glass carefully beside the map so Grandma Nia could reach it without stretching.

While Golda did that small helpful task, Grandma Nia kept smoothing the corners of the map so it would not curl overnight. Golda’s own hand still felt a bit chalky from the drawing they had finished earlier on the big pad by the window, and the dry powdery texture brushed lightly against the wooden table edge. “The benches face the garden,” Grandma Nia added softly, pointing to one circled spot. Golda smiled and straightened the two pillows at the head of the bed so they sat neat and even against the headboard. The room smelled faintly of honey from the cup of warm tea Grandma Nia had placed on the far nightstand earlier.

Golda climbed onto the soft rug beside the bed and rested both hands on the quilt near the map. The fabric felt thick and cozy under their palms while the hall light continued to glow like a friendly night-light through the open door. Grandma Nia folded one corner of the map with slow care so the green route stayed on top. “We can sit and watch the birds if we get tired,” Grandma Nia said. Golda answered in a plain clear voice, “I noticed you marked the route that has more benches.” The words settled gently between them like another soft blanket.

Grandma Nia looked up and her eyes crinkled at the corners in the warm spill of hall light. Golda reached over and slid the honey-sweet cup a little closer so it would not tip if anyone moved. The quiet room held the low hum of the house settling for sleep and the far-away whisper of leaves. Golda waited through a single chime from the small clock on the shelf, letting the pure note fade completely before they spoke again. “You already took care of the map for us,” Golda said simply, their voice steady and kind.

Grandma Nia rested one hand lightly on Golda’s shoulder for a moment and the touch felt steady and familiar. Golda leaned a little closer so they could see the tiny drawings of benches once more under the gentle light. The air near the window carried a cooler breath while the rest of the room stayed comfortably warm. “Tomorrow we will walk slow and stop often,” Grandma Nia promised. Golda nodded and tucked the loose edge of the quilt so no corner of the map could slip off during the night.

Golda then lifted the folded spare blanket from the chair and draped it neatly at the foot of the bed where Grandma Nia could pull it close if the night grew cooler. The wool felt soft and slightly springy under Golda’s fingers as they smoothed it flat. Outside the window the garden stayed peaceful and dark while the hall light kept its steady friendly shine across the floor. Grandma Nia whispered, “That was a thoughtful help, Golda.” Golda felt a quiet gladness settle in their chest like a warm stone and stayed close without rushing.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Golda climbed into bed at last and pulled the covers up to their chin while Grandma Nia gave the map one last gentle pat and turned toward the door. The hall light still spilled a calm path across the room and the air smelled clean and restful. Golda closed their eyes and listened to Grandma Nia’s quiet footsteps fade down the hall, knowing the map with its kind route full of benches waited safely for morning.
