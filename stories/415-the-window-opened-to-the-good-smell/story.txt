"Papa, the workshop smells so quiet tonight," Violeta said as they stepped across the threshold and felt the last warm light of day brush their bare arms like a gentle hand.

Inside the workshop the tools rested side by side on a low peg, their wooden handles smooth from years of careful use, and Violeta noticed at once that Papa had already closed the window that had been opened earlier because bread was baking somewhere in the lane. A faint trace of that distant loaf still lingered near the glass, yet the sash sat snug now, and cool evening air no longer spilled across the workbench. Violeta ran one fingertip along the edge of a plane that hung nearest the door and listened to the soft creak of the floorboards under their own feet. Papa stood nearby wiping a chisel, and the quiet clink of metal against cloth filled the space between them. Violeta watched the way the last sunlight made tiny dust motes dance above the pegs and felt glad simply to be there with him.

Violeta walked farther in and breathed deeply, catching the clean scent of beeswax that rose from an open tin on the shelf where Papa had been polishing the tool handles earlier. The wax had left everything gleaming without shine, just a soft readiness that made each hammer and saw look ready for morning. Violeta touched the rounded end of a mallet and found it cool and solid under their palm. Outside the closed window the leaves of the old fig tree stirred, and Violeta heard the wind in the fig leaves moving like a slow whisper through the yard. Papa hummed a little tune under his breath while he set the chisel back on its peg, and the sound blended with the gentle leaf rustle. Violeta stayed close, noticing every careful motion Papa made, and the warm wood smell of the benches mixed with the beeswax until the whole room felt safe and settled.

"I saw you closed it before the air grew too cool," Violeta said, standing beside the workbench and looking up at Papa’s kind face. Papa smiled and rested a hand lightly on Violeta’s shoulder so they could feel the steady warmth of his palm through their shirt. The workshop held a mild temperature now that the window was shut, neither too warm nor chill, just right for lingering. Violeta glanced toward the low peg again and saw how every tool hung within easy reach, each one clean and waiting. A small lantern already sat on the sill though it was not yet lit, and its glass sides caught a faint reflection of the fading sky. Violeta leaned closer to the window glass and felt its smooth coolness against their cheek for a moment before stepping back.

Violeta decided that a plain thank-you would not be enough, so they looked around for one useful thing Papa had not asked them to do. Near the door a short broom rested against the wall, and a thin scatter of pale wood shavings still dotted the floorboards in a loose path from the bench to the peg. Violeta took the broom and began to sweep with long slow strokes, listening to the soft hush of bristles across wood. The shavings gathered into a neat little pile that smelled faintly of fresh pine. Papa watched without speaking, and Violeta kept working until every stray curl had been collected and tipped into the scrap box by the door. When the floor felt smooth under their bare feet again, Violeta set the broom back exactly where it belonged and wiped their hands on the soft cloth hanging from a nail.

"I swept the shavings because I saw you had already taken care of the window," Violeta said plainly, standing still so Papa could hear every word. Papa’s eyes crinkled at the corners and he answered, “That was thoughtful of you, Violeta. The floor looks ready for tomorrow.” The words settled between them like another layer of calm. Violeta felt the mild air against their neck and noticed how the last pink light outside had turned the closed window into a quiet mirror. They reached toward a fig leaf that had drifted in earlier and rested on the sill; turning it over, Violeta stroked a leaf's soft underside and found it velvety and cool. The texture stayed on their fingertips even after they set the leaf gently aside near the scrap box.

Outside, just beyond the doorstep, a small snail had begun to travel across one whole brick that formed the edge of the path. Violeta stepped to the open doorway and watched the snail move with patient silver trails, its shell catching the dimming light. The brick felt faintly warm still from the day when Violeta crouched and rested one hand on it. Papa came to stand behind them, and together they stayed quiet while the snail finished its slow crossing and disappeared into the grass. The evening temperature had dropped only a little, enough to make the air fresh without biting. Violeta stood again and returned inside, feeling the change from outdoor coolness to the workshop’s gentle shelter.

Violeta walked once more along the row of tools on the low peg, straightening a small square that had tilted and brushing a last fleck of dust from a folding ruler. Each movement was unhurried, and the soft scrape of wood against wood made a tiny comfortable sound. Papa lit a match and touched it to the lantern wick so a steady glow began to grow. Violeta breathed in the mingled scents of wood and wax and felt their shoulders loosen. “The tools look happy hanging there,” Violeta said, and Papa replied, “They do, especially after you helped.” The simple exchange filled the room like another kind of light. Violeta stayed near Papa while he checked that every peg held its tool securely, and the quiet companionship wrapped around them both.

Later Violeta sat on the low stool by the workbench and rested their hands in their lap, feeling the smooth grain of the seat under their legs. The workshop had grown dim except for the lantern, and the temperature stayed kindly, as if the walls themselves held the day’s warmth a little longer. Papa closed the lid of the beeswax tin with a soft click and hung his polishing cloth on its hook. Outside the fig leaves had stilled, yet the memory of their earlier sound lingered in Violeta’s mind like a lullaby. Violeta looked at the closed window and remembered the bread smell that had first invited it open, then remembered how Papa had known when to shut it again. They felt glad all the way through, the kind of glad that needed no big words.

Violeta rose and walked with Papa toward the door, pausing once more to touch the cool metal latch. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
