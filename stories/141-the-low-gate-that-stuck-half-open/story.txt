Cousin Pio knelt already on the cottage porch in the soft blue evening light, both palms pressed against the low wooden gate that had stopped halfway open with a small gray pebble jammed under its bottom edge. The boards felt cool and slightly damp under Pio’s knees, and a thin breeze carried the smell of rain on dust from the path where the first drops had touched the dry earth. Pio pushed once, then pulled, and the gate creaked but refused to swing free or shut. From the open doorway Bibi watched for a quiet moment, noticing how Pio’s shoulders tensed with the awkward angle, and Bibi stepped forward across the worn planks that still held the day’s leftover warmth.

Bibi crouched beside Cousin Pio without a word at first, feeling the smooth grain of the porch rail under one steadying hand. “The pebble is caught right under the middle,” Bibi said softly, pointing with a careful finger so Pio could see the exact spot. Pio looked up and offered a small tired smile. “I keep trying to lift and swing it at the same time, but it won’t move for me alone,” Cousin Pio answered. Bibi nodded and slid closer, the air around them mild and gentle, and together they studied the stuck gate while a moth brushed past the lantern glass with a faint papery whisper.

Bibi set one hand on the top rail of the low gate and the other near the hinge, sharing the weight so Pio no longer had to stretch alone. “If you lift the free side just a little, I can nudge the pebble out with my shoe,” Bibi said. Cousin Pio lifted carefully, and Bibi’s toe eased the gray stone free; it rolled once across the porch with a tiny hollow sound and came to rest near the flower box. The gate swung smoothly then, and the evening air felt cooler against their cheeks as the opening widened toward the hall. Bibi held the gate steady while Pio stepped through first, and neither of them hurried.

Inside the hall the floorboards smelled faintly of old wood polish, and the light from the porch lantern spilled in a long golden rectangle across the rug. Bibi noticed a satin ribbon that had slipped from a coat hook and lay in a soft loop on the bench; its cool glossy texture caught the glow as Bibi picked it up and set it safely back in place. Cousin Pio was already trying to ease the low gate the rest of the way so it would latch properly from inside, but the angle was still awkward for one person. Bibi simply took hold of the inner edge and guided it closed the last few inches while Pio managed the latch itself.

When the gate latch clicked shut with a clean bright sound, both of them stood still for a moment in the quiet hall and listened to the soft settling of the wood. “That feels much better,” Cousin Pio said, voice warm with relief. Bibi only smiled and brushed a bit of porch dust from their own sleeve, the fabric rough under their fingertips. Outside, the last light of day faded into deeper blue, and the air inside the cottage stayed pleasantly cool against their skin. They hung their light jackets on the pegs side by side, and the jackets brushed together with a gentle swish.

Bibi wandered a few steps farther into the hall where a small window looked out over the porch, and the glass had fogged a little from the temperature change. With one dry finger Bibi traced a slow circle on the cool pane, watching the clear line appear in the pale mist. Cousin Pio came to stand nearby and watched the circle take shape without interrupting. “The porch looks peaceful now,” Bibi murmured, still facing the glass. Pio rested a hand lightly on the windowsill, feeling its smooth painted edge, and the two of them shared the calm view of the empty gate standing properly closed in the dusk.

They moved together toward the little bench beneath the coats, where a folded blanket waited, soft and thick. Bibi sat first and smoothed the blanket across both their laps so the woolly warmth spread evenly. Cousin Pio leaned back and let out a quiet breath that smelled faintly of the mint leaves they had chewed earlier on the path. “Thank you for helping with the gate,” Pio said after a comfortable pause. Bibi shrugged one shoulder in the dim light and answered, “It was easier with two,” then tucked the blanket edge more neatly so no cold draft could slip underneath.

Outside the moth returned and tapped once more against the lantern, a tiny rhythmic sound that drifted through the closed gate. Inside, Bibi and Cousin Pio stayed side by side on the bench, listening to the cottage settle around them—the soft creak of a beam, the distant hush of leaves beyond the porch. Bibi’s hands rested open on the blanket, feeling its looped texture, while Pio’s breathing slowed into the same easy rhythm. The hall grew dimmer by degrees, yet the remaining light stayed kind and steady, wrapping the quiet space like another layer of the blanket itself.

Bibi rose only long enough to straighten the satin ribbon one last time on its hook, then returned to the bench and leaned gently against Cousin Pio’s shoulder. The air smelled clean and settled now, no longer carrying the earlier porch dust. They sat without speaking for a stretch of slow breaths, simply glad the low gate no longer stood half open and awkward. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
