The evening mist drifted gently across the ceramic studio sill above the quiet plaza, cooling every surface with a soft damp touch that made the clay pieces gleam under the low lamp light. Bibi sat very still on a low stool beside that sill, their knees drawn up and their fingers resting on the cool edge of a pale cup that should have been perfectly smooth. The quiet plaza below held only a few soft footsteps and the faint shimmer of wet cobblestones catching the last daylight. Bibi felt the mild chill of the air settle on their cheeks and smelled the clean clay all around them while they turned the cup slowly in both hands. Somewhere deeper in the studio a kettle waited on the stove, and Bibi breathed while the kettle began its quiet tick, matching the rise and fall of their own chest. The cup had been meant for Grandpa Sol, smooth as river stone, yet a small thumbprint sat right near the rim like a quiet secret.

Bibi pressed their lips together and almost slid the cup behind a taller vase so no one would see the mark. The soft light from the high window made the thumbprint look even clearer, a tiny oval dent that caught the shadows. Bibi’s shoulders felt warm under their sweater, yet a small worry sat in their middle like a pebble. They could hear the gentle hum of the building and the far-off murmur of the plaza as evening grew quieter. Bibi’s fingertips traced the dent again and again, feeling how the clay had given way under their thumb earlier that afternoon when they had tried to help glaze the rim. The cup still smelled faintly of wet clay and drying glaze, a friendly smell that usually made Bibi smile, but tonight it only made them hold the cup closer.

Grandpa Sol stepped into the studio with slow careful steps, his soft wool cardigan still damp from the short walk across the plaza. Bibi caught the smell of wet wool as he came near the sill and set a small bag of tools on the worktable. “Bibi, I thought I might find you up here with the cups,” Grandpa Sol said in his warm low voice. Bibi’s hands tightened around the cup, the glaze surface cool and slightly rough where the thumbprint sat. The tin clock on the far shelf ticked once, clear and bright in the quiet room, marking the moment Bibi almost kept the cup hidden. Grandpa Sol’s kind eyes rested on Bibi without hurry, and the mild temperature of the studio wrapped around them both like a gentle blanket.

Bibi looked down at the cup and then up at Grandpa Sol’s calm face. “Grandpa Sol, I put my thumb on the cup when it was still soft, and now it has a mark that was not supposed to be there,” Bibi said, the words coming out plain and steady. The air felt cooler against Bibi’s neck as they spoke, and the soft light from the window painted a pale stripe across the sill. Grandpa Sol did not frown or laugh; he simply nodded and reached out one large hand. “May I see it, Bibi?” he asked. Bibi placed the cup carefully into his palm, feeling the small relief of telling the truth settle into their chest like a warm sip of tea.

Grandpa Sol turned the cup under the lamp light so the thumbprint showed clearly, a neat little hollow near the rim. “We can smooth the rim a bit more and add a soft glaze wash that will make the mark look like a gentle cloud,” he said. Bibi watched his careful fingers and felt the paper edge of a glaze recipe card that lay on the table brush against their wrist as they leaned closer. The texture was thin and slightly crisp, reminding Bibi of turning pages in a picture book. Together they moved to the workbench where soft cloths and fine sponges waited, and the quiet plaza below sent up only the faintest sounds of evening. Bibi’s own hands stayed busy holding the cup steady while Grandpa Sol worked, and the mild warmth of the studio lamps kept the clay from feeling cold.

“I almost hid it behind the tall vase,” Bibi admitted while they watched the sponge glide gently over the rim. Grandpa Sol smiled a small smile that reached his eyes. “I am glad you told me instead, Bibi,” he answered. The sponge made a soft whispering sound against the clay, and Bibi felt the temperature of the room stay pleasantly mild as they worked side by side. A little tray of water sat nearby, its surface still, and Bibi dipped a clean cloth into it to wipe away tiny crumbs of dried clay. The cup slowly grew smoother around the mark, the thumbprint becoming part of a soft swirl rather than a mistake, and Bibi’s breathing stayed even and calm.

Ugo the studio cat wandered in on quiet paws and settled near Bibi’s feet, his fur brushing softly against their ankle like a living blanket. Bibi reached down once to stroke the warm fur, then returned both hands to the cup so Grandpa Sol could guide the final light sanding. “It feels better already,” Bibi said, listening to the tiny scratch of the fine sandpaper. Grandpa Sol nodded and held the cup up to the light once more. The plaza outside had grown fully dark, and only the soft glow of the studio lamps filled the sill. Bibi noticed how the thumbprint now looked almost like a small leaf pressed into the clay, friendly rather than wrong, and the air carried the clean scent of finished glaze work.

They set the mended cup carefully back on the sill to dry a little longer, side by side with two other smooth pieces that waited for morning. Bibi rested their chin on their folded arms and watched the cup, feeling the cool air from the open window touch their forehead. Grandpa Sol poured warm water from the kettle into two mugs and handed one to Bibi, the steam rising in gentle curls that smelled faintly of mint. “Tomorrow we can paint a tiny blue edge around the rim if you like,” Grandpa Sol said. Bibi nodded and sipped, the warm liquid chasing away the last of the day’s small worry, while the tin clock continued its steady soft ticks in the background like a quiet friend keeping time.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Bibi leaned against Grandpa Sol’s side on the wide sill bench, their sweater warm against the mild night air, and watched the quiet plaza lights twinkle far below like scattered stars. The mended cup sat safely nearby, its thumbprint now part of a gentle design, and Bibi felt the steady calm of the studio settle deep into their bones as the last of the mist drifted past the open window.
