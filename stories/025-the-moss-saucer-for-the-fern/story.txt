The warm craft corner smelled of fresh paper as Pilar’s busy hands smoothed a pale sheet flat against the wooden table, fingertips tracing the clean edges while paste waited in a little jar nearby. Soft afternoon light fell across the wooden rack that held more paper rolls and scissors, and Pilar could feel the cool air drifting from the open window. Auntie Luz sat close on a low stool, her own hands folding a tiny paper leaf. “The saucer of moss is resting under its cloth cover, Pilar,” Auntie Luz said gently. “It still needs quiet time before the tiny fern can settle there.” Pilar nodded but kept glancing toward the covered saucer that sat on the far end of the table, wanting very much to lift the cloth and see the green softness underneath.

Pilar reached toward the cloth once, then stopped when the cool light shifted across the wooden rack and made the paste jar gleam like a small moon. They pulled their hand back and pressed a new strip of paper instead, listening to the soft scrape of scissors on the table. Auntie Luz smiled and held out a square of thicker paper. “Would you like to cut a little roof for the fern’s future home while we wait?” she asked. Pilar took the square and began to snip carefully, feeling the smooth resistance of the paper under the blades. The room stayed quiet except for those small snips and the distant hush of leaves outside. Pilar’s shoulders loosened a little as they worked, though their eyes still wandered now and then to the covered saucer.

Warm air brushed Pilar’s cheek when a soft breeze came through the window, carrying the faint far sound of a gate latch clicking shut in the garden beyond. Pilar paused mid-cut and looked toward the sound, then back at the saucer still hidden under its cloth. “I could peek just once, Auntie Luz,” Pilar whispered. Auntie Luz shook her head kindly and pointed to the paper shapes already waiting on the rack. “The moss is still gathering its own calm, Pilar. Let’s finish the little paper fence instead.” Pilar breathed out and chose a longer strip, folding it into a gentle zigzag that would stand around the saucer later. The paper felt dry and friendly between their fingers, and the sound of the gate latch faded into ordinary garden quiet.

Pilar stood for a moment and let their palm rest against the damp clay wall beside the craft corner, feeling its cool, grainy texture steady them while they decided what to do next. The wall smelled faintly of earth after rain, though no rain fell now, and it helped Pilar remember that some things simply take their own gentle time. They returned to the table and chose a round piece of paper meant to become a tiny path leading to the saucer. Auntie Luz watched with warm eyes and said, “You are choosing patience with your hands, Pilar. That is a quiet kind of kindness.” Pilar’s cheeks felt pleasantly warm from the work, and they kept cutting slow curves so the path would look soft and welcoming once everything was ready.

Light from the window turned golden and slid across the wooden rack, making the hanging paper rolls glow like rolled sunshine while Pilar arranged the finished pieces in a neat row. They wanted again to lift the cloth from the saucer of moss, yet they stopped themselves and instead sorted the paper leaves by size, largest to smallest, feeling each one whisper against the next. Auntie Luz hummed a short tune with no words, and Pilar joined the humming under their breath. “When the cover comes off, the moss will look greener for having rested,” Auntie Luz said. Pilar believed her and kept sorting until every leaf sat ready, the paste jar still waiting with its clean brush resting across the rim.

Pilar sat back on the little stool and chose to rest both palms on their knees, feeling the warm fabric of their trousers and the steady rise of their own breathing. The craft corner stayed peaceful; the wooden rack cast long soft shadows, and the covered saucer waited like a secret gift. “I can wait a little longer,” Pilar said aloud, more to themselves than anyone else. Auntie Luz nodded and answered, “Yes, Pilar, and while we wait the paper roof can receive a drop of paste so it will hold firm.” Pilar dipped the brush once, hearing the tiny wet sound it made, then carefully set the roof piece on a clean scrap to dry. The temperature in the room felt just right, neither too warm nor too cool, and Pilar’s hands stayed calm.

Outside, birds settled into evening calls that drifted through the window like soft bells, and Pilar listened while they pressed the paper path pieces together with the lightest touch of paste. They still felt the small wish to check the moss, yet each time the wish rose they simply returned to the next paper piece waiting on the rack. Auntie Luz reached over and tucked a stray curl behind Pilar’s ear. “Look how steady your work has become, Pilar,” she said. Pilar smiled and answered, “The saucer is finishing in its own time, just like you told me.” The paste smelled mild and clean as it dried, and the golden light deepened into a quieter glow that made every finished paper shape look ready and kind.

At last Auntie Luz lifted the cloth from the saucer of moss with both hands, and Pilar leaned close to see the tiny green cushions that now looked perfect and settled. Cool evening air touched Pilar’s nose as they studied the moss, soft and living, waiting for the tiny fern that would arrive tomorrow. Together they placed the paper path, the zigzag fence, and the little roof around the saucer so the whole scene felt complete. “It waited, and now it is ready,” Pilar said happily. Auntie Luz answered, “And you waited with it, Pilar, which made the waiting gentle.” Pilar’s fingers brushed the edge of the wooden table, feeling its smooth worn grain, while the last of the daylight rested like a blanket over the craft corner.

Pilar helped tidy the leftover paper scraps into a neat pile on the rack, listening to the quiet rustle they made, then sat once more with both palms resting lightly on their knees while Auntie Luz wiped the paste brush clean. The room had grown softer, filled with the hush of finished work and the faint scent of dried paper edges. Pilar looked at the finished moss saucer surrounded by its paper home and felt a warm, sleepy gladness settle in their chest. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Auntie Luz drew the curtain just a little, and Pilar yawned a small calm yawn, ready to leave the craft corner for bed, knowing the moss and the tiny fern would be waiting safely in the morning.
