"Would anyone like to share a quiet feeling before we open our picture books?" Auntie June asked, her voice warm as the afternoon light that spilled across the library carpet in soft golden stripes.

Vera sat cross-legged in the story circle with the other children, their knees touching the thick blue rug that felt cool and a little springy under them. The tall windows stood half open, and outside Vera could hear the wind in the fig leaves making a low, leafy hush that mixed with the turning of pages. Auntie June smiled at each face without hurrying anyone. Uma sat two spots away and gave Vera a small nod. Vera pressed their hands together and noticed the faint smell of lemon soap still clinging to their fingers from washing up at the sink before the circle began. They wanted to speak, yet the words felt heavy and shy inside their chest.

Auntie June held up a floured towel she had brought from home, the soft cloth still dusted white from baking morning biscuits for the group. She laid it across the low wooden table at the edge of the circle and wiped the table in slow circles, clearing a space for the stack of books. Vera watched the gentle motion and felt the cool air from the window brush their cheek. Uma whispered something kind to the child beside her. Vera's shoulders stayed tight. They had practiced the sentence at home, but here in the bright library the thought of saying it straight out, without a silly joke to hide behind, made their stomach flutter like a moth.

"I brought lemon cookies too," Auntie June said, setting a small plate near the floured towel so the citrus scent drifted lightly through the room. Vera breathed in the clean lemon soap on their own hands again and then the sweeter cookie smell. The wind in the fig leaves rose for a moment and settled. Auntie June looked around the circle once more, her eyes kind and steady. She did not call on anyone by name. She simply waited, giving every child room to choose. Vera felt the warm patch of sunlight on their lap and the cooler shadow on their back. They decided the smallest brave version might be enough.

Vera cleared their throat the tiniest bit. The rug under them felt soft and safe. "I feel nervous," Vera said in a voice that could be heard, plain and clear without any joke attached. The words left their mouth and floated into the quiet circle. Auntie June nodded once, slow and gentle, as if Vera had simply handed her a smooth stone. Uma smiled across the space. No one laughed or pressed for more. The light through the windows stayed golden and calm. Vera's hands unclenched a little on their knees.

"Thank you for telling us, Vera," Auntie June answered softly. She smoothed the floured towel one last time with her palm, the texture dry and powdery under her fingers, then picked up the first picture book. Vera felt a quiet warmth spread through their chest that had nothing to do with the sun patch. The wind in the fig leaves whispered again outside. Vera listened to Auntie June begin the story about a small boat on a quiet pond. Uma shifted closer so their elbows nearly touched. Vera kept hearing their own words in their mind, simple and true, and the library air smelled faintly of lemon soap and old paper.

The story continued with soft page turns and Auntie June's steady reading voice. Vera watched the pictures of blue water and green banks while the cool breeze from the open window touched their hair. They noticed how the floured towel still rested on the table like a quiet helper. When Auntie June paused to show a wide illustration, Vera felt the solid floor beneath the rug and the easy rise and fall of their own breathing. Uma pointed at a duck in the picture and Vera nodded. No one asked Vera to say more. The nervous feeling had room to sit beside them without needing to be fixed or joked away.

After the last page Auntie June closed the book with a gentle thump. "We can sit a little longer if anyone wants," she said. Vera stayed on the rug, tracing a small circle on the carpet with one finger while the afternoon light grew softer and more amber. The smell of lemon soap lingered on their skin. Outside the wind in the fig leaves kept its calm rhythm. Vera glanced at the floured towel and the cookie plate and felt glad they had spoken the plain sentence. Auntie June began stacking the books without any hurry, her movements unhurried and kind. Uma leaned over and whispered, "I liked how you said it," and Vera whispered back, "Me too."

The children rose one by one to choose quiet corners for looking at books on their own. Vera walked to the low table, touched the edge of the floured towel once more, and helped Auntie June set the remaining cookies aside. The library grew quieter still. Vera felt the cool air on their arms and the solid wooden chair under them when they sat near the window with a new book. Auntie June passed by and rested a light hand on Vera's shoulder for half a second, nothing more. Vera opened the pages and let the pictures fill their eyes while the last of the sunlight slipped lower.

Vera stayed by the window as the room settled into evening quiet, the book warm in their lap and the soft chair holding them steady. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
