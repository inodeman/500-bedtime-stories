The soft dawn breeze drifted through the bakery kitchen doorway and brushed the hanging pans with a cool touch that made them sway just a little. Sora stepped inside and felt that same gentle coolness settle on their wrists while the warm glow from the proofing lamps painted pale yellow circles across the clean floor tiles. Sora walked slowly past the long counter that would become the early table, listening to their own quiet footsteps, and they noticed how the air already held a faint sweetness from the dough resting under cloths. Sora paused near the far corner where something new had been set up overnight, a small wooden frame with folded fabric hanging from it, and the cool morning still clung to the metal legs of that frame.

Sora reached out and touched the heavy red cloth of the puppet curtain, finding it thick and a little stiff under their fingers in the mild kitchen warmth. The fabric smelled faintly of the clean soap used to wash it, and Sora wondered why a puppet stage stood here among the mixing bowls and rolling pins. Lumen appeared from the pantry carrying a small basket, their sneakers making soft scuffs on the tile, and together they examined the curtain that would not rise properly when Sora tried alone. “It sticks at the top if I pull too fast,” Sora said, feeling the cloth bunch unevenly. Lumen nodded and rested a hand on the other side of the frame where a polished pebble had been left as a simple weight to keep papers from flying, its smooth cool surface pleasant against their palm.

Ms. Okonkwo came in from the storeroom wiping her hands on her apron, and the soft light from the side window caught the silver threads in her hair. She smiled at Sora and Lumen standing by the little stage and explained that the curtain needed two people to lift it evenly so the rods would not jam. “If one person rushes the lift, the whole thing twists,” she said gently, her voice calm in the quiet room. Sora felt the warm air from a nearby oven brush their ankles and looked at the stubborn knot holding one side of the curtain cord. The tin clock on the shelf above the spice rack ticked steadily, marking the early minutes before the bakery opened.

Sora and Lumen decided right then to split the work into two slow jobs instead of hurrying. Sora would loosen and guide the left cord while Lumen steadied the right rod, both moving at the same careful pace. “We can each take one side and go slow,” Sora said, already placing their hands on the left edge where the fabric felt denser. Lumen agreed with a quiet “Yes, slow is better,” and they both felt the mild heat rising from the floor where the ovens had begun their morning work. Ms. Okonkwo did not take over; she only watched and offered a simple name for the knot that had tightened overnight. “That is a sailor’s loop,” she said, “and counting three breaths before you pull will free it.”

Sora breathed once, twice, three times while their fingers worked the sailor’s loop free, feeling the cord slide smoothly once the name and the count settled in their mind. The basil sitting in a little pot on the windowsill released its fresh green smell when a draft from the door stirred the leaves, mixing lightly with the dough scent already in the air. Lumen held the opposite side steady so nothing snagged, and together they raised the curtain a short distance, the cloth whispering as it unfolded. The light through the rising fabric turned softer and pinker for a moment, and Sora felt proud of how the two slow jobs were working side by side without any rush.

They lowered the curtain again to practice once more, still splitting the tasks so each person stayed calm and matched the other’s pace. Sora guided the left cord with even pressure while Lumen kept the right rod level, and the polished pebble rolled a tiny bit when the frame shifted, its smooth cool texture reminding Sora to stay gentle. “Ready on three again,” Sora whispered, and Lumen answered “Ready,” so they lifted together after the quiet count. Ms. Okonkwo folded a few napkins at the side counter so the stitched pear on each one faced neatly upward, the cloth soft under her hands, and she hummed a low tune that blended with the distant oven hum.

Sora noticed how the curtain now moved freely when both of them worked at the same unhurried speed, the rods sliding in their grooves without sticking. They raised it all the way this time, and the fabric opened like a quiet door onto the empty stage space, letting more of the kitchen’s warm light spill through. Lumen smiled across at Sora, and Sora smiled back, both of them feeling the gentle heat from the nearby racks where trays waited for the early breads. The two slow jobs had become one easy motion, and nothing twisted or jammed because neither of them had rushed ahead of the other.

After the curtain hung open and ready, Sora helped Lumen check that every cord lay straight, still moving with the same careful patience they had chosen at the start. Ms. Okonkwo set the folded napkins with their stitched pears facing up on the edge of the early table, the cotton cool and crisp against the wood. Sora ran one last hand along the bottom hem of the puppet curtain, feeling how smoothly it now hung, and they listened to the quiet kitchen settle around them. “We did it together,” Sora said softly, and Lumen replied “We really did,” while the warm lamps continued to glow overhead and the cool morning air drifted in one last time through the open door.

Sora sat for a moment on the low stool near the stage and felt the leftover coolness of the stone floor through their socks while Lumen put the polished pebble back in its place as a paperweight. The bakery kitchen felt peaceful and ready, the early table almost set, and Sora knew the puppet curtain would welcome the first visitors later with its easy open motion. Ms. Okonkwo dimmed one of the brighter lights so the space grew softer, and Sora watched the shadows lengthen gently across the clean counters. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
