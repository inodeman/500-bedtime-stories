A soft creak rose from the wooden floorboards inside the ferry cabin as the boat swayed, and Farah noticed the small sound at once while the pale evening light filtered through the curtained window. Farah rubbed their palms together in the mild air and breathed in the faint warmth still clinging to the benches. Farah crossed the room with quiet steps, trailing one hand along the smooth wall, then paused beside the doorway where Neighbor Sami stood holding a thermos. Farah said, “The deck looks open now, Sami.” Neighbor Sami nodded and followed without hurry as Farah led the way along the short passage, the cabin lamp glowing behind them like a steady friend.

Out on the ferry deck the rail felt cool and solid under Farah’s hands, and only two elbows of room remained between the benches and the open water that lapped with a gentle hush. Farah saw Juno standing just outside the little circle where two other children had begun humming a familiar tune, the empty harmony part hanging in the air like an open seat nobody had yet offered to the quiet friend. Farah felt the evening breeze brush their neck and smelled the soft chamomile rising from Neighbor Sami’s thermos as Sami poured a careful cup nearby. Farah watched Juno’s shoes shift on the deck planks and decided the space needed rearranging so every voice could fit without any announcement.

Farah stepped closer to the rail and shifted one of the small folding stools sideways with a soft scrape, making a clear spot right beside them. “Juno, will you stand here by me?” Farah asked in a low voice that blended with the water sounds. Juno moved forward at once and rested both elbows on the rail next to Farah, the wood grain rough yet friendly under their shared hands. Neighbor Sami stayed a few steps back, smiling but saying nothing, letting the children settle the places themselves while the ferry engines thrummed low and steady beneath the deck.

The children began the song again, and Farah kept the pace slower so the empty harmony part could open naturally for Juno’s voice without anyone pointing or calling it a special prize. Farah felt the cool metal of the rail and the warmer press of Juno’s shoulder against theirs as the notes rose together. “This part fits you perfectly,” Farah whispered once between phrases, and Juno answered, “I like singing it with you.” The light from the ferry windows painted soft gold stripes across the deck boards while Neighbor Sami sipped the warm drink and watched the three voices weave without interference.

A tin clock somewhere inside the cabin marked the passing minutes with its clear steady ticks that drifted out through an open porthole, and Farah heard it clearly between the lines of the song. Farah guided the group to lean a little left so Juno had full room at the rail and never felt crowded out of the fun. The air carried a faint salt freshness mixed with the lingering warmth of the day, and Farah rested one hand lightly on Juno’s back for a moment of quiet company. Neighbor Sami set the thermos down carefully, still leaving every choice to Farah and Juno and the simple circle they had made.

Farah noticed a small round tin resting on the bench where someone had packed crackers earlier, and they brushed their fingertips across the smooth tin lid that felt cool and slightly ridged under the touch. Farah lifted the lid once to share a plain biscuit with Juno, then set it back without fuss so the singing could continue at the gentle pace Farah had chosen. “We can keep going as long as the ferry moves,” Farah said, and Juno replied softly, “I am glad you made space.” The deck lights glowed in warm pools around their feet while Neighbor Sami remained nearby yet silent, trusting the children to keep the harmony full and kind.

They sang two more verses with Farah adjusting the seating once again by sliding another stool an inch closer to the rail so Juno’s elbows had equal room and the quiet friend stood fully inside the group. Farah felt the mild night temperature settle across their bare arms and listened to the water slipping past the hull in a calm rhythm that matched their slower tempo. Farah glanced at Neighbor Sami, who simply nodded once in approval and stayed out of the way, allowing every small change to belong to the children alone. The ferry deck held them safely between the rail and the benches as the song rose and fell like easy breathing.

When the last note faded, Farah and Juno stayed side by side at the rail for a quiet stretch of time, watching the distant shore lights twinkle without hurry. Farah reached into a warm heap of laundry someone had left on the bench and matched two soft socks together just for the calm of it, then tucked them neatly aside. Farah said, “The song feels complete now,” and Juno smiled without needing more words. Neighbor Sami gathered the thermos and the tin, still letting the children lead the way back toward the cabin door as the ferry rocked gently onward under the wide sky.

Inside the cabin once more Farah settled onto the wide bench near the window while Juno sat close and Neighbor Sami dimmed the brighter lamps. Farah felt the familiar blanket texture against their legs and listened to the soft engine hum that had become a lullaby. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Farah closed their eyes with Juno nearby and the memory of the shared harmony still warm around them like a held hand.
