The soft evening breeze brushed across the open ferry deck and cooled the wooden boards until they felt pleasant under shoes. Dori stood near the rail where only two elbows of room remained between the benches and the water, and Dori watched the pale gold light slide over the painted metal. The air smelled faintly of salt and distant kitchens. Dori settled a music folder on their lap and hummed the first notes of a song that had once been meant for two voices. Neighbor Sami sat a little apart on a folding stool, a thermos open nearby, and simply watched the children with quiet eyes while the ferry engines hummed low and steady beneath them all.

Dori began the melody alone, turning what should have been a duet into a careful solo that floated out over the rail. Oren stood just outside the small open space, close enough to hear every note yet not quite inside the circle of practice. Dori noticed the way Oren’s shoes shifted on the deck and the way Oren’s hands stayed tucked in pockets. The late light turned softer and warmer across Dori’s shoulders. Dori kept singing the single line, yet something about the empty harmony made the song feel thinner than it should. Neighbor Sami poured a little more from the thermos and the rich smell of tomato soup drifted across the boards without a word of direction.

Dori paused after the second verse and looked straight at Oren. “Would you like to stand here by the rail with me?” Dori asked in a calm voice that carried easily over the quiet water. Oren took one step closer but still hovered at the edge. The deck boards held the leftover warmth of the day against Dori’s soles. Neighbor Sami only smiled and stayed silent, letting the children work out the space themselves. Dori felt the music folder’s edge against their fingers and thought about the empty second part that waited for another voice.

Dori shifted the two small stools that faced the rail so one sat squarely beside the other instead of one in front. “We can share this spot,” Dori said. “There is room for both of us if we sit side by side.” Oren’s face brightened in the gentle glow. Dori smoothed one wrinkle out of the cloth that covered the nearer stool so the seat felt even and welcoming. The cloth held a faint scent of soap from earlier washing. Neighbor Sami watched without speaking, content that the children were solving the small puzzle of places on their own.

Oren sat down carefully and the two elbows of room suddenly felt open enough for both. Dori handed over the second sheet and pointed to the harmony line with a patient finger. “You take the lower notes and I will keep the higher ones,” Dori explained. “We can go slowly at first.” A soft tinkling reached them then, the bright sound of a cat’s collar bell as a ferry cat strolled past the benches and settled near a coil of rope. Dori smiled at the tiny music the bell made and waited while Oren studied the page. The breeze touched Dori’s hair and cooled the back of their neck in a friendly way.

Dori began again at a gentler pace so every note had time to settle. Oren joined on the second phrase and the two voices met in the open air above the rail. Dori felt the song grow fuller and warmer with each shared measure. Neighbor Sami nodded once but offered no prize or announcement, only a quiet presence that left the children free. Dori’s hands rested on the wicker handle of the small basket that held extra pencils and a spare folder, and the woven texture felt dry and familiar under their palms. The ferry moved steadily through the calm water while the duet that had been a solo became two voices again.

Dori and Oren practiced the chorus three more times, slowing whenever a note needed extra care and smiling when the lines locked together. “That sounds right now,” Oren said softly after the third try. Dori answered, “It does. We can keep this pace whenever we want.” The light along the deck turned a deeper gold and the air cooled another degree against Dori’s arms. Neighbor Sami lifted the thermos once more yet still stayed outside the decision, trusting the children to hold the new arrangement themselves. Dori felt the music settle into something steady and kind between them.

As the ferry rounded a quiet bend, Dori and Oren sang the whole song once through without stopping. Their voices rose and fell together near the rail, and the two elbows of room no longer felt crowded. Dori kept one hand near the music so the pages would not lift in the breeze. The deck held a soft residual warmth that rose through the soles of Dori’s shoes. Neighbor Sami packed the thermos away with unhurried movements, the children having found their own way to share the practice. Dori glanced at Oren and saw the same calm satisfaction reflected there.

Later the ferry lights came on and Dori led the way a few steps toward the small cabin door where a window looked out over the water. Oren carried the folder while Dori held the basket by its familiar grip. Inside the air felt still and gentle after the open deck. Dori set the music down on a low table and sat where they could still see the darkening water. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Dori rested their hands in their lap and listened to the quiet hum of the boat while the last notes of the shared song lingered in memory like a warm blanket.
