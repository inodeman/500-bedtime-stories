The soft smell of wet clay rose from the ceramic studio table as Kira’s hands already pressed and turned a cool gray lump, patting it into a gentle oval under the warm stripes of afternoon light. Their palms felt the damp chill of the clay while little pitcher handles rested nearby under clear plastic so the slip could set without hurry. Nana Ruth sat close with quiet tools, and the floorboards felt smooth under Kira’s bare feet. A mild breeze slipped through the open doorway and touched Kira’s hair. They kept smoothing the oval, glancing once toward the top step where a bean jar wrapped in a damp cloth waited in the shade. Kira’s fingers made soft whispering sounds on the clay, and the bright room felt steady and kind around their busy hands.

“May I go look at the bean jar now, Nana Ruth?” Kira asked, eyes bright toward the doorway. Nana Ruth set her tool down with a tiny click and smiled. The air near the mugs held a sweet trace of cinnamon from the tea she had poured earlier. “The cloth is still damp for a reason, Kira,” Nana Ruth said. “The beans need their own quiet time under there before we lift it.” Outside, a gate latch sounded once, short and friendly, as someone closed a garden gate far along the path. Kira listened and stayed seated. They picked up a walnut-smooth spoon kept for stirring slip and turned it slowly, feeling the polished wood glide under their thumb before setting it aside again with care.

Kira wanted very much to unwrap the cloth on the top step, yet Nana Ruth’s calm face helped the wish settle. The solid table edge pressed cool against Kira’s elbows as they leaned to watch a covered handle. Light made the plastic shine like quiet water. “I can wait with you, Nana Ruth,” Kira said. “That is a fine choice, Kira,” Nana Ruth answered. Kira reached for a soft sponge instead and began dabbing the table rim. Cool beads of water sat on the wood for a breath before vanishing under the slow strokes. The sponge squished gently in their palm, and Kira’s shoulders eased while the bean jar stayed covered just as it should.

Kira wiped the rim in careful circles, listening to the faint damp sound of the sponge. Nana Ruth shaped a small bowl without rush, and Kira matched that peace. Now and then they glanced at the doorway and the shaded top step where the damp cloth held its secret nap. “What are the beans doing under there?” Kira whispered. Nana Ruth tilted her head. “They are taking the time they need, Kira, just as our slip and handles take theirs,” she said. Kira pressed the sponge again and felt its soft give. A far bee buzzed past the window, a lazy note that made them smile while their hands kept the quiet task going.

Tiny soft cracks rose from the plastic covers as they settled, almost too gentle to catch, and Kira listened with care. Their toes curled once against the cool floor for comfort. Kira hummed four low notes and then left a rest of silence that felt like a held peaceful breath. After the rest they hummed the same four notes softer still, and Nana Ruth’s smile grew warmer. The clay tools made faint friendly clinks. Afternoon light turned more golden across the table. Kira filled long stretches with wiping and soft song, leaving the bean jar untouched so it could finish in its own time, and pride settled quiet and light inside their chest.

Brisa appeared at the doorway with a basket of folded towels, her steps light, and cooler air drifted in with her. “Hello, Kira. Hello, Nana Ruth,” Brisa said, setting the basket down with a soft cloth thump. Kira waved a clay-dusted hand. “We are letting things set,” Kira told her. Brisa nodded as if she understood waiting completely, left the towels, and waved goodbye. When she went, the doorway framed the top step and the wrapped jar once more. Kira returned to the sponge and cleaned every edge where clay likes to gather. The fresh towels had brought a light cotton scent that soon joined the studio’s own calm smells, and Kira’s arms felt pleasantly tired in a good way.

Nana Ruth poured a little warm water into the slip cup, and a thin curl of steam rose. The mild heat brushed Kira’s nose before it faded. Kira checked the handles with their eyes only and saw the clay looking firmer. “They are doing well,” Nana Ruth murmured. Kira nodded and rested both hands quiet in their lap for a stretch of stillness. In that stillness they heard their own calm breathing and the soft rustle of leaves outside. The top step stayed peaceful, cloth undisturbed. Kira felt the smooth chair seat under them and wiggled once for comfort while the light shifted in slow shapes across the floorboards.

Kira stood at last and walked only as far as the doorway, stopping where the indoor boards met the outdoor step, and looked without touching. The damp cloth still wrapped the jar snugly and looked cool in the shade. A mild breeze moved its edge a hair’s width. Kira smelled the garden mixed with studio clay and curled their fingers at their sides. “I left it covered, Nana Ruth,” Kira called softly. “I know you did, Kira,” Nana Ruth called back, her voice warm. Kira counted five slow breaths, watched a small beetle walk the step edge, then turned and came back. Their feet made soft sounds on the floor, and the short walk felt complete. At the table they polished a clean spot with a dry cloth until the wood glowed dull and nice.

When the light grew gentler and the handles earned Nana Ruth’s pleased nod, she and Kira walked together to the top step. Nana Ruth lifted the damp cloth with both hands, and the bean jar sat revealed, cool glass and quiet beans settled just as they should be. Kira touched the jar’s side with one fingertip, felt the smooth chill, and drew back with a happy sigh. Inside the studio the air felt soft and dimmer, and everything that needed time had received it. Kira leaned against Nana Ruth, cheek against the cotton sleeve that smelled faintly of soap and clay. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Together they carried the jar to a low shelf where the last warm light could find it, and Kira’s eyes grew heavy with the good tiredness of a day spent kindly working and waiting with calm hands.
