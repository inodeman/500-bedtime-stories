The baker stood midway through hanging the last of the paper stars above the long tablecloth in the cottage room, twisting a thin string around a nail so the star would catch the soft lamp glow without spinning. Warm light pooled across the cloth and made every crease shine like a quiet river. Eko stepped inside and felt the cool evening air brush their bare arms. The baker smiled without turning fully and said, “Eko, the wall clock is ready for its gentle clean now, if you and Una would like to help.” Eko looked up at the round face of the clock resting on a chair and heard the distant click of a gate latch closing in the garden path outside. Una appeared at Eko’s side, her socks whispering on the floorboards, and together they breathed in the calm of the room.

Eko reached out and touched the smooth wooden edge of the wall clock while Una held the cloth ready. The clock needed one steady person to hold it firmly against the wall and one careful person to wipe the dust from its face and hands. Eko remembered that rushing would make the clock slip or the cloth catch, so they spoke softly. “Una, let’s split it into two slow jobs,” Eko said. Una answered, “I can wipe if you hold, Eko, and we go as slow as honey dripping.” The baker stayed near the table, folding the long tablecloth corners neatly, and the air near the window felt cooler than the rest of the room. Eko rested both palms on their knees for a moment to settle their breath before lifting the clock.

Eko lifted the wall clock with both hands while Una waited with the soft cloth. The back of the clock pressed lightly against the damp clay wall, and Eko felt the cool, slightly rough texture under their fingertips where the plaster met the wood. “Hold it just there, Eko,” Una whispered. Eko kept the clock steady and watched Una begin the first slow circle across the glass. The baker glanced over and offered a quiet count so they would not hurry. “One for the top, two for the sides,” the baker said. Eko matched their breathing to the count and felt the solid weight of the clock settle safely in their arms. Soft light from the lamps made tiny rainbows on the cleaned glass.

Una wiped in small careful arcs while Eko held the wall clock level against the cool surface. The smell of fresh paper drifted from the open stack of stars still waiting on the long tablecloth, mixing gently with the warm bread scent already in the air. Eko said, “This side is done, Una. Now the numbers.” Una nodded and moved the cloth even more slowly so no dust fell onto Eko’s sleeves. The baker named the little loop of string that secured the clock’s back and said, “We call that knot the evening loop, Eko, so you know it stays gentle.” Eko smiled at the simple name and kept their hold firm yet soft. The room stayed still except for the quiet swish of the cloth.

Eko shifted their feet a little closer to the damp clay wall so the clock would not tilt while Una reached the lower edge. Cool air from the open window touched the back of Eko’s neck and made the paper stars above the tablecloth flutter once. “Almost finished with the face, Eko,” Una said. Eko answered, “I am ready for the frame next.” Together they moved without hurry, Eko holding the clock steady and Una wiping every wooden curve. The baker counted again from the table, soft and even, “Three for the rim, four for the edges.” Eko felt proud of the slow pace they had chosen and kept listening to the baker’s calm voice.

The wall clock grew clean and bright under Una’s cloth while Eko’s arms stayed sure. Warm light from the lamps reflected in the polished glass and threw gentle shapes across the long tablecloth below. Eko felt the slight vibration of the clock’s quiet ticking return as the dust left its hands. The baker folded one more paper star and said, “The evening loop will hold it well once you hang it, Eko.” Eko and Una both nodded. They worked side by side, never rushing, until every speck was gone. The texture of the damp clay wall under Eko’s knuckles reminded them how steady the cottage felt.

Eko and Una lifted the clean wall clock together toward its nail. The baker stepped closer only long enough to steady the chair they stood on, then returned to the table. Cooler air moved through the room and made the hanging paper stars turn slowly like sleepy dancers. “On three we set it, Eko,” Una said. Eko counted with the baker’s earlier rhythm in mind and answered, “One, two, three.” The clock settled perfectly against the wall. Eko stepped back and felt the solid floor under their feet while Una folded the cloth. The long tablecloth glowed softly under the lamps, and the whole cottage room seemed to breathe with them.

Eko stood beside Una and looked at the shining wall clock while the baker finished the last paper star. Warm light filled every corner and made the cleaned glass sparkle without glare. Eko rested both palms on their knees again for a quiet moment and felt the day’s gentle work settle into calm. Una sat on the edge of the chair and smiled at Eko. The baker spoke one last soft line, “You two made the slow jobs fit together beautifully.” Eko felt the pleasant tiredness in their arms and knew the clock would keep steady time through the night. Outside, the garden had grown fully quiet after the earlier gate sound.

Eko climbed onto the wide bench beneath the window and leaned against the cool cushion while Una and the baker smoothed the long tablecloth one final time. The paper stars hung motionless now, and the wall clock ticked its soft new clean song. Eko watched the lamps lower their glow and felt the temperature of the room become perfectly mild for sleep. The baker set a small cup of warm milk near Eko’s hand without a word. Una curled up on the bench too, close enough that Eko could hear her quiet breathing. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
