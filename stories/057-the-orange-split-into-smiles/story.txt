The sweet smell of warm rolls curled through the air while Remy’s hands already stayed busy along the bakery counter, fingertips tracing the cool grain of the wood and the soft flour dust that clung there like fine snow. Morning light poured in pale and golden through the wide front glass, and the soft busy hush of early customers filled the room with quiet scrapes of baskets and the low clink of spoons. Remy rocked gently on their toes, feeling the smooth counter under their palms, and watched Ms. Okonkwo move between trays with calm care. Steam rose in thin ribbons from a tray of fresh buns, carrying a gentle heat that warmed Remy’s cheeks. Remy breathed slowly and kept their hands busy, patting the wood in a soft rhythm that matched the peaceful morning.

Remy saw the bright orange resting in a shallow blue bowl near the register, its skin glossy under the soft counter lamp. The fruit sat alone like a small sun, and Remy leaned closer until the citrus scent mixed with the bakery air. Ms. Okonkwo wiped her apron and smiled across the wood. “Good morning, Remy,” she said in a voice as steady as rising dough. Remy answered, “Good morning, Ms. Okonkwo,” and kept their hands flat on the counter so the cool surface soothed their palms. The lamp glow made the orange look almost ready to open, and Remy felt the quiet bustle settle around their shoulders like a light shawl of warmth.

Kito stepped up beside Remy, and the two friends stood shoulder to shoulder at the counter while the bakery hummed with soft footsteps and paper bags rustling. Remy glanced at Kito and then back at the orange, noticing how the peel held two natural curves that looked like quiet smiles waiting to appear. The air near the ovens stayed pleasantly warm against Remy’s arms. “Look how round it is,” Kito whispered. Remy nodded and answered, “It could become two smiles.” Their voices stayed low so they would not disturb the gentle morning, and Remy felt the wooden edge under their fingers, smooth and familiar.

Remy thought carefully while the orange shone under the lamp light, realizing that if only one person received the whole fruit first it would not feel fair to the friend standing so close. The idea settled inside Remy like a soft blanket of calm. Ms. Okonkwo turned a tray and the metal made a tiny bright sound that faded at once. Remy kept their eyes on the orange and felt the warm air from the ovens brush their neck. They wanted both of them to share the smiling curves, and the thought made Remy’s chest feel light and steady. The bakery counter stayed busy yet soft, full of quiet hands packing bread and the low murmur of kind voices.

Remy reached a decision and lifted their chin toward Ms. Okonkwo, ready to ask for a smaller even share so Kito would be included right from the start. The smell of honey drifted from a glazed pastry cooling on a nearby rack, sweet and gentle. Remy spoke clearly, “May we share the orange in two small smiles, please?” Ms. Okonkwo’s eyes softened as she listened. Remy felt the counter’s cool wood again and waited with quiet patience while the morning light brightened one more degree across the floorboards. Kito stood very still, and Remy could sense their friend’s hopeful stillness like a shared breath.

Ms. Okonkwo took the orange in her chalky hand, the flour making her palm feel dry and soft as she set the fruit on a little cutting board. She divided it with care into two even half-moons that truly looked like gentle smiles resting side by side. Remy watched every slow motion and felt the pleasant coolness of the counter against their wrists. “Here you are,” Ms. Okonkwo said simply, and she let the choice stand without any extra words. Remy accepted one smiling half and passed the other to Kito, the citrus oil bright on their fingertips. The two friends held their pieces carefully, and the bakery around them stayed full of soft warmth and quiet clinks.

Remy and Kito tasted the bright segments together, the juice cool and sweet on their tongues while a single chime sounded from the little clock near the shelves. They waited through that single chime, standing still until the soft tone faded, and then they smiled at each other over the counter. Remy felt the smooth orange membrane against their lips and heard the distant click of a gate latch as someone entered the side yard with a quiet care that matched the morning. The light through the window stayed pale and kind, and Remy’s shoulders loosened in the gentle heat rising from the ovens. Everything felt even and peaceful as they finished the last sweet bites.

Remy wiped their fingers on a clean napkin Ms. Okonkwo offered, and the cloth felt soft and dry against their skin. Outside the light grew a little stronger yet still gentle, and inside the bakery the air held the fading sweetness of fruit and bread. Kito rested an elbow lightly on the counter edge and Remy did the same, both of them quiet now and content. Ms. Okonkwo returned to her trays without fuss, and Remy watched her move with the same calm rhythm as before. The wooden floor felt solid under Remy’s shoes, and the whole soft busy morning wrapped around them like a familiar hug of sound and light.

Remy leaned once more against the counter and felt the last coolness of the wood under their palms while the bakery settled into an even quieter hush. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Remy breathed in the remaining warm air and listened to the low, peaceful sounds of trays being set down for the day, and they stood with Kito in that gentle light until it was time to walk home with full hearts and sticky-sweet fingers.
