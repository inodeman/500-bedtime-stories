The smell of rain on dust drifted up from the quiet plaza as Dahlia’s hands already moved busily across the wide ceramic studio sill. Soft afternoon light poured through the high window and warmed the wooden ledge under their palms. Dahlia straightened a row of unfinished cups, fingers tracing the cool smooth rims, then smoothed a folded cloth napkin that rested near the edge. The breeze carried that earthy damp scent higher while the plaza stones below stayed empty and still. Dahlia felt the mild air on their cheeks and kept working, humming a little tune only they could hear. Grandpa Sol’s quiet footsteps sounded farther back in the studio, yet Dahlia stayed focused on the sill and the simple tasks that filled their afternoon.

Dahlia’s fingertips brushed something firm beneath the napkin and paused. They lifted one corner and saw a pale saucer with a thin blue ring painted around its center. A hairline crack ran across the glaze like a faint silver thread, almost hidden until the light caught it. The porcelain felt cool and satiny under Dahlia’s touch, and for a moment they thought of covering it again so no one would notice. Warmth from the sill seeped into their wrists while they held the napkin still. Outside, a distant bird called once and then fell silent. Dahlia drew a slow breath and watched dust motes float in the golden beam that crossed the ledge.

Grandpa Sol stepped closer, the boards under his shoes giving a soft low creak. “Dahlia, you have been busy up here,” he said in his gentle voice. Dahlia looked up and felt the cooler air near the glass against their forehead. They kept one hand on the napkin edge and answered quietly, “I found this saucer, Grandpa Sol. It has a little crack.” The light shifted as a cloud passed, turning the studio walls a softer gray for a moment. Dahlia’s shoulders stayed relaxed, and the mild temperature of the room wrapped around them both like a familiar blanket.

Grandpa Sol knelt so his eyes met Dahlia’s. “Will you show me?” he asked. Dahlia nodded and lifted the napkin all the way free. The hairline crack caught a thin stripe of light and gleamed. “I almost left it hidden,” Dahlia said, voice steady. “But I wanted to tell you the plain truth.” A faint click of a gate latch rose from the plaza far below, clear and brief in the quiet. Grandpa Sol smiled without hurry and rested one broad hand on the sill beside theirs. The wood felt smooth and dry under both their palms.

Together they examined the saucer while the light grew warmer again. “We can mend what can be mended,” Grandpa Sol said. Dahlia fetched the small jar of clear adhesive he kept on the low shelf, its lid cool against their fingers. They worked side by side, Dahlia holding the saucer steady while Grandpa Sol guided a thin line of glue along the crack. The air smelled faintly of the clay dust still settled on nearby shelves. Dahlia watched the crack grow less sharp as the glue settled, and the gentle pressure of their own careful hands felt reassuring.

When the mend was set enough to leave alone, Dahlia noticed a length of soft fabric coiled near a stack of finished plates. They picked it up and felt the smooth slide of a satin ribbon between their fingers, cool and slippery like water. “May I tie this around the napkin so it stays neat?” Dahlia asked. Grandpa Sol nodded. “That would keep everything tidy,” he replied. Dahlia looped the ribbon loosely and set the napkin aside, the texture lingering pleasantly on their skin. Outside the window the plaza remained hushed, only a soft shuffle of leaves drifting past.

Dahlia turned toward the cool glass and saw a faint fog of their own breath already gathered there. With one dry finger they traced a slow careful circle, watching the clear line appear in the mist. The glass felt chilly against their fingertip, a pleasant contrast to the warmer sill. Grandpa Sol watched quietly and then said, “Your circle looks like the rim of that saucer.” Dahlia smiled and traced a second smaller ring inside the first. The studio stayed peaceful, filled with the low hum of the afternoon settling toward evening.

They rinsed their hands in the little basin where the water ran lukewarm and soft. Dahlia dried them on a clean towel that smelled faintly of sunshine still held in the cloth. Grandpa Sol placed the mended saucer on a high safe shelf where it could rest undisturbed. “Thank you for telling me right away, Dahlia,” he said once more. Dahlia leaned against his side for a moment and felt the steady warmth of his sweater. Below the sill the quiet plaza began to gather longer shadows, yet the light inside the studio remained gentle and golden.

Dahlia and Grandpa Sol sat together on the wide sill as the day softened, the mended saucer safe nearby and the satin ribbon coiled neatly beside the napkin. A last breeze brought cool air through the open pane and made the edges of the drawn fog circle fade. Dahlia rested their hands in their lap and listened to the slow peaceful sounds of the studio settling. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
