The parcel sat right on the flat harbor step, its brown paper soft and wrinkled from travel, and the twine that had given up hung in loose loops that no longer pulled the corners snug. Ximena bent near enough to see every frayed fiber where the string had slipped free, the paper edges lifting a little in the mild air. Warm evening light settled across the package like a slow blanket while the water made a quiet lapping sound against the wood below. Papa held two other parcels already, and Ximena saw at once that this one with its open twine would be hard for him to manage alone without the sides falling open. They stayed close and kept their eyes on the dangling string as another soft wave brushed the step.

Papa tried to scoop the loose parcel under one arm while balancing the others, but the paper sides wanted to flop and the free twine tangled against his sleeve. Ximena stepped nearer on the cool stone and felt the evening chill rising from the harbor boards under their shoes. “I can hold the sides closed while you lift,” Ximena said in a low voice that matched the calm water. Papa glanced down and nodded once, the corners of his mouth easing. Ximena pressed both hands gently against the brown paper, feeling its dry fold under their palms, and the parcel stayed neat as Papa raised it. Together they made the first careful step away from the edge where parcels changed hands each evening.

A nearby crate of garden goods released the green smell of tomato vines into the salty air, mixing with the harbor scent in a friendly way that made Ximena breathe deeper. They kept one hand steady on the package while Papa shifted his grip, and the twine ends brushed lightly against Ximena’s wrist like soft threads. “The string gave up on the way,” Papa said, “but we can carry it just fine now.” Ximena answered, “We can walk slow and keep it closed,” their words quiet so they would not startle any waiting boats. The pair moved along the step with even paces, and Ximena felt the solid weight of the parcel shared between them without any rush.

Halfway along the worn boards Ximena noticed a wooden bench set back from the water, its surface ready for a short rest if needed. They guided Papa toward it with a small tilt of their shoulder, still cradling their half of the awkward parcel. When they paused, Ximena ran one finger along the seat and felt a grain of the bench rise smooth and narrow under the pad of their fingertip. The wood held the leftover warmth of the day even as cooler air drifted in from the open water. Papa set his other packages down carefully and smiled at Ximena without saying much, simply glad for the steady help. Ximena stayed beside the open parcel and kept the paper sides from shifting while they both caught their breath.

A small cat slipped between the crates then, and the bright sound of a cat’s collar bell rang once, twice, clear and tiny against the hush of the harbor. Ximena turned their head just enough to watch the cat settle nearby without letting go of the parcel. “Hello, little walker,” Ximena whispered, and the cat blinked slow eyes in reply. Papa chuckled softly and said, “Even the cat comes to see good teamwork.” Ximena felt the paper still firm under their hands and the mild breeze lift a loose end of twine like a friendly flag. They waited a moment longer on the step, sharing the quiet company of the cat and the steady hold on the package whose twine had given up.

After the cat wandered off toward a coil of rope, Ximena and Papa lifted the parcels again and continued past the place where hands traded packages at dusk. The light had turned softer now, the gold fading to gentle blue that rested easy on the water and the boards. Ximena kept their side of the loose parcel tucked close so nothing spilled, matching Papa’s longer steps with careful shorter ones. “Almost to the cart,” Papa murmured, and Ximena replied, “I have this edge still.” Their shared load felt lighter somehow because two sets of arms carried what one person alone would have found clumsy. No one needed extra words; the work itself stayed simple and kind.

They reached the low cart waiting near the far end of the harbor step and settled every package onto its flat bed with slow care. Ximena smoothed the brown paper one last time and coiled the free twine into a loose circle so it would not catch later. The air grew cooler against their bare arms, a pleasant night coolness that made the skin tingle just a little. Papa checked the cart wheels and said, “You saw what was needed and simply did it.” Ximena only nodded and rested a hand on the cart rail, listening now for the far clock in the town tower rather than the nearer splash of water against the pilings. The distant chime came faint and even, counting the calm minutes.

On the short walk home from the harbor the cart rolled smooth behind them, and Ximena walked close enough to touch the side of the parcel whenever a bump threatened the paper. Street lamps began to glow in soft pools of yellow that lit the path without glare. They passed a row of quiet houses where windows already showed warm inside light, and the wheels made a steady wooden hum on the stones. Ximena breathed the cooler night air and kept pace with Papa, still ready to steady anything that shifted. At their own door Papa lifted the special parcel once more while Ximena held the gate, and together they carried it inside to the table by the sitting-room window.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Ximena stood near the parcel on the table and watched the gentle light settle over the brown paper and the coiled twine, the same quiet light that filled the room with a sleepy orange hush. Papa set a small plate of sliced apple nearby, and the sweet fruit scent drifted up while Ximena washed their hands in warm water that felt kind on the skin. They sat together on the low couch, shoulders touching, and Ximena listened again to the far clock marking the peaceful hour. Outside the harbor sounds had faded to almost nothing, and inside only the soft tick of the house and the lantern’s steady shine remained. Ximena rested their head against the cushion and felt the day close around them like a familiar blanket, the helped parcel safe, the evening complete, and sleep arriving on quiet feet.
