The soft clink of a pencil rolling off a low table reached Remy’s ears first. Remy sat up a little straighter on the thick library rug under the round window and watched the pencil come to rest near a stack of picture books. Late-day light filtered through the glass in gentle gold bands that warmed Remy’s bare arms. The rug felt springy under their knees, and the whole corner smelled faintly of old paper and polished wood. Grandma Nia sat in the big chair nearby with her knitting resting quiet in her lap, smiling as if she already knew the afternoon would turn out kind. Remy glanced toward the doorway where Gio hovered, one sneaker still on the smooth floorboards instead of the rug.

Remy patted the empty space beside them and felt the nap of the rug give under their palm. “Gio, come sit here with me,” Remy said. The air near the window carried a cooler draft that brushed Remy’s cheek like a soft feather. Gio took one step closer but stayed half in and half out, fingers twisting the strap of a small backpack. Remy remembered the joke they had kept folded up inside like a secret note, waiting for the exact right friend to hear it. The round window above them painted a perfect circle of brightness on the floor, and dust motes drifted slowly through it. Grandma Nia only watched, her needles silent now, letting the children find their own way.

Remy scooted sideways so the open spot grew wider and more inviting. The movement made the rug fibers whisper against Remy’s socks. “We can make a bigger circle,” Remy said. Gio’s shoulders loosened a little at the sound of Remy’s voice. From somewhere down the hall a cat’s collar bell jingled once, clear and tiny, then faded. Remy smiled at the unexpected chime and patted the rug again. Warmth from the sunlit glass still lingered on the fabric, and Remy could feel it through their jeans. Gio finally set the backpack down and lowered themself onto the edge of the rug, close enough now that their knees almost touched Remy’s.

Remy reached into their pocket and drew out a smooth glass bead they had found earlier on the craft table. The bead felt cool and perfectly round between Remy’s thumb and finger. Remy rolled it once across the rug toward Gio as a quiet hello. Gio caught it gently and held it up so the window light caught inside it like a tiny captured star. “I saved something funny,” Remy said, voice low and happy. “I didn’t tell it yet because I wanted you right here when I did.” The bead warmed quickly in Gio’s hand, and both children watched the small sparkle it made. Grandma Nia’s chair creaked once as she leaned back, still saying nothing, still trusting them.

Remy shifted again so that Gio sat fully inside the circle of light cast by the round window. The change put Gio right in the middle of the soft rug instead of on its border. Cool air from the window mixed with the warmer air of the room and settled over Remy’s shoulders like a light scarf. Remy cleared their throat and began the joke they had practiced in whispers all morning. “Why did the book go to sleep early?” Remy asked. Gio tilted their head, waiting, eyes bright. The answer waited on Remy’s tongue like a bubble ready to pop. Somewhere nearby the pages of an open atlas fluttered once in the draft, adding a papery hush to the moment.

Gio’s quiet laugh arrived even before the punch line finished. Remy delivered it anyway, and both of them giggled into their hands so the sound stayed soft enough for the library. The mint leaves Grandma Nia had tucked into a small jar on the windowsill released a clean green smell when Remy leaned closer to the glass. Remy breathed it in and felt the day grow even calmer. “That was a good one,” Gio said, still holding the glass bead. Remy nodded and leaned back on both hands, feeling the solid floor underneath the rug. The joke now belonged to both of them instead of waiting alone inside Remy’s pocket of thoughts.

Remy suggested they take turns choosing the next book from the low shelf. The rule changed the pace so no one sat outside the choosing. Gio’s turn came first, and Remy watched their friend run careful fingers along the spines until one book with a blue cover slid free. The cover felt slightly rough under Gio’s touch, and Remy noticed the same texture when the book passed into their own hands. Soft library quiet wrapped around them again except for the gentle turn of pages. Grandma Nia’s knitting needles began a slow soft click, the only other sound, while the children made a nest of pillows and settled deeper into the rug.

Remy named the clean scent of the mint leaves, then the warm smell of the sun on the wooden sill, then the faint sweet smell of the glue that held the book’s pages. The three smells layered gently without any hurry. Gio smiled and added the dry-paper smell of the open pages themselves. Remy felt the afternoon stretch long and easy around them. The joke still glowed between them like a shared secret that had found its proper home. Light from the round window shifted lower and turned the rug a deeper shade of gold. Remy rested their cheek against a pillow and listened to Gio read the first few lines in a careful whisper.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Remy watched its steady circle of light join the last of the daylight on the rug and felt sleep begin to tug at their eyelids. Gio’s breathing slowed beside them, the glass bead now resting safe between their palms. Grandma Nia rose without a word and draped a light blanket that smelled faintly of clean cotton over both children. The library held its quiet like a cupped hand, and Remy closed their eyes knowing the joke, the friend, and the round window all belonged together in one peaceful place.
