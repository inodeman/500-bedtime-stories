The warm bread smell already filled the bakery kitchen as Nim’s small hands worked the bright paper strips into careful loops. Soft yellow light from the high wall lamps settled on the flour-dusted counters, and Nim felt the smooth paper slide between their fingers while they linked one ring to the next. The long paper chain lay half-finished across the wide wooden table like a colorful river waiting to reach the big front door. Nim hummed a little tune under their breath and kept their shoulders loose, knowing the early table would soon need setting but the chain had to hang first. Cool air drifted near the floor tiles, and Nim wiggled their toes inside their soft socks to stay steady.

Yelena stood close beside Nim and smoothed another strip with gentle palms while the kitchen stayed quiet except for the soft tick of the big wall clock. Pale morning light slipped through the side window and made the paper colors glow like tiny flags. “We should take our time with every single loop,” Nim said in a calm voice. Yelena nodded and answered, “If we rush the joining, the whole chain might twist before it reaches the door.” Ms. Okonkwo moved near the ovens with slow steps, her apron smelling faintly of butter, and she watched the children with kind eyes. Nim kept looping, feeling the paper’s light stiffness give way under their careful press, and the room stayed peaceful as the first loaves rested on cooling racks.

The paper chain needed to stretch all the way around the bakery door frame so every morning guest would see the cheerful colors when they arrived. Nim measured the growing length against their own outstretched arms and noticed how the far end still waited for more rings. Warm air rose from the nearby proofing shelves and brushed Nim’s cheeks while they worked. Yelena held the finished section steady so it would not tangle on the table edge. Outside the open window the wind in the fig leaves made a soft rustling sound that mixed with the quiet kitchen. Nim smiled at the gentle noise and kept their hands moving at an easy pace, never pulling too hard on any loop.

Ms. Okonkwo stepped closer and rested one hand lightly on the table rim while she looked at the place where two long sections of chain would meet. “That joining spot needs a special knot we can call the morning loop,” she said softly. Nim repeated the name under their breath and felt glad for the clear word that would help them remember. The light from the lamps stayed steady and golden across the flour sacks stacked in the corner. Yelena and Nim decided together to split the remaining work into two slow jobs so nothing would go wrong. Nim took the left half of the unfinished strips, and Yelena took the right half, each of them settling into a calm rhythm of folding and linking.

Nim’s fingers moved like quiet birds as they shaped each new ring and slid it through the last one with care. The paper felt dry and crisp yet never tore under their gentle pressure. Across the table Yelena matched the same slow pace, and the two halves of the chain grew longer side by side. A soft warmth still rose from the bread racks and kept the air comfortable around their shoulders. Nim glanced up once and said, “My half is almost ready to meet yours.” Yelena answered with a quiet smile, “Mine is ready too, and we can join them without any hurry.” The kitchen felt safe and bright, and the children kept their breathing even while the clock ticked kindly onward.

When the two long sections finally met in the middle, Nim held the ends steady while Yelena prepared the special morning loop. Ms. Okonkwo simply counted aloud in a low voice, “One slow tuck, two soft pulls, three gentle closes,” so the children could follow without rushing. Nim felt the paper tighten just enough under their thumbs and knew the knot would hold. Cool light from the window mixed with the warmer lamp glow and painted soft patterns across the finished chain. The children lifted the whole length together and carried it toward the tall bakery door, their steps light on the smooth floor. Everything stayed peaceful as they raised the colorful rings higher.

Nim stood on the low wooden stool and guided one end of the paper chain onto the first small hook above the door while Yelena held the middle steady from the floor. The chain draped in gentle waves that caught the morning light like floating ribbons. Ms. Okonkwo handed Nim the next hook and only smiled without adding extra words. Nim felt the soft wood of the door frame under their free hand and kept their balance easy. Outside, the fig leaves continued their quiet movement in the breeze, and inside the kitchen the warm air stayed gentle against everyone’s arms. The children worked in the same slow spirit, linking each section to the next hook until the door wore its full bright circle of paper.

After the last loop settled into place, Nim noticed an empty mixing bowl still resting on the counter near the ovens. They carried the empty bowl to the sink with both hands, rinsed it under the quiet stream of water, and came back to the doorway where Yelena waited. The ceramic felt smooth when Nim set the clean bowl aside, and a cool ceramic rim brushed their palm for a moment before they let go. Together Nim and Yelena stepped back to look at the finished paper chain hanging proud and even around the whole door. Ms. Okonkwo nodded once and said, “The morning loop holds perfectly because you both stayed slow.” Nim felt a quiet gladness settle in their chest as the first soft light of day grew stronger through the glass.

Nim and Yelena stood side by side in the calm bakery kitchen and watched the paper chain sway just a little in the gentle indoor air. The early table waited nearby with its clean cloth still folded, ready for the quiet work that would come next. Nim breathed in the peaceful room and felt their hands rest easy at their sides after all the careful linking. Yelena’s shoulder touched Nim’s lightly in a friendly way while Ms. Okonkwo turned toward the cooling racks with her usual unhurried steps. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
