A tiny chime rang near the bakery door, and Remy paused with one hand on the warm wooden edge of the counter. Remy listened again and smiled when the soft tinkle of a cat's collar bell floated from the sunny corner where a sleepy tabby stretched on a cushion. Morning light poured through the big front window in pale gold stripes, and the air felt gentle against Remy's cheeks. Remy watched flour dust drift in those stripes like quiet snow. Outside, a bicycle wheeled past with a low hush of tires, and Remy felt the smooth counter under their fingertips. Remy rocked once on their heels and breathed the cozy room.

Remy stood on the little step stool so they could see the whole glass case, and the cool air from inside the case brushed Remy's nose. Trays of golden buns and freckled muffins sat in neat rows, and a single slice of seed cake rested alone on a green plate near the front. The cake looked speckled and soft, with tiny seeds glinting like sand after rain. Remy smelled butter and toasted grain rising with the heat from the ovens behind the wall. Ms. Okonkwo wiped her hands on a clean towel and came closer, her apron still warm from the baking room. Remy glanced toward the door again, hoping Gio would arrive before the morning rush grew louder.

"Good morning, Remy," Ms. Okonkwo said, her voice calm as steam. Remy answered, "Good morning, Ms. Okonkwo. The seed cake looks ready." The green plate sat so bright under the glass that Remy could almost feel its smooth glaze without touching it. A customer farther down the counter laughed softly, and the sound rolled like a small wave through the busy but kind morning. Remy kept their eyes on the slice and on the empty space beside it where another plate might fit. The bakery smelled rich and steady, and Remy's shoulders loosened in the familiar warmth.

Gio pushed open the door with a careful hand, and the little bell above it gave one gentle note. Gio hurried to the step stool and stood close enough that Remy felt the soft brush of Gio's sweater sleeve. "I came as fast as I could," Gio whispered, still catching their breath. Remy whispered back, "I saved us a spot by the cake." Together they looked at the single slice on the green plate, and the seeds caught the light like tiny lanterns. Remy felt a quiet tug inside, the kind that comes when something good seems meant for more than one pair of hands. The floorboards under the stool held a mild heat from the ovens, and Remy shifted so Gio had room.

Remy studied the slice again and decided it would not feel fair if only one person was served first. Remy turned to Ms. Okonkwo and said, "May we share it into two smaller pieces, please, so Gio and I can both have some?" Ms. Okonkwo nodded once, eyes kind, and reached for a clean knife without making a speech. She cut the seed cake straight down the middle on the green plate, and the two halves looked even and friendly. A faint scent of mint leaves drifted from a little pot of fresh sprigs Ms. Okonkwo kept beside the tea jars, cool and bright in the warm room. Remy watched the knife glide and heard only the soft press of the blade through crumb.

Ms. Okonkwo slid each half onto its own small napkin and set them side by side on the same green plate, still letting Remy's choice stand. Remy picked up one napkin carefully, and Gio took the other, and the cake felt springy and light through the paper. Remy noticed a glass bead on a thin string tied around the napkin stack, smooth and cool against Remy's thumb for a second before they set it back. The bead caught a spark of window light and then rested quiet again. Remy and Gio carried their shares to the low bench by the side counter where the morning sun made a soft rectangle on the wood. The bench was smooth under their palms, and the bakery hummed with low voices and the distant clink of cups.

Remy took a small bite and tasted the gentle crunch of seeds, while Gio did the same, and both of them smiled with cheeks full. Remy said, "It tastes like sunshine and toast." Gio nodded and answered, "And the green plate makes it feel special." Between bites they listened to the easy shuffle of shoes and the mild clatter of trays being set down. Remy named three smells without hurrying, breathing in butter, warm bread, and the faint sweetness of sugar left on the cutting board. The air stayed kind around them, neither hot nor chilly, just right for sitting still. Remy leaned a little toward Gio so their shoulders touched, and the seed cake crumbs stayed tidy on the napkins.

Ms. Okonkwo passed by with a basket of rolls and paused only long enough to check that the green plate was empty in a happy way. She offered a quiet smile and moved on, still without a speech, and Remy felt glad the choice had been enough on its own. Remy brushed a stray seed from their sleeve and listened to the low murmur of the morning continuing around the counter. The light on the floor had shifted a finger's width, softer now, and the tabby in the corner stretched again with a tiny sound of fabric. Remy and Gio finished the last slow bites and folded their napkins into neat squares. The bakery counter glowed in the gentle bustle, and Remy felt the day settling like a blanket.

Remy and Gio returned the green plate to the counter edge, and Ms. Okonkwo accepted it with both hands as if it were something carefully kept. Remy thanked her in a soft voice, and Gio did too, and then they walked toward the door where the morning still felt open and kind. Outside light waited in a mild wash, and Remy held the door so Gio could step through first. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Remy glanced back once at the warm room, smelling the last drift of baked grain, and felt the cool door handle under their palm before following Gio into the quiet street.
