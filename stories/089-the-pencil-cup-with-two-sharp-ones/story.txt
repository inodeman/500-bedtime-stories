"Farah, the coats are ready and the mats are waiting just beyond the door," Uncle Idris said, and his calm words floated through the cloakroom like the warm afternoon light that poured from the high windows and rested on Farah’s hair.
Farah walked along the row of hooks where damp sleeves still held a little outdoor coolness, and they touched a soft wool scarf that brushed their fingers with a quiet tickle. Beyond the open doorway the lunch mats waited in a neat circle of pale green cloth, and Farah could already see the low tables set with cups and trays. The cloakroom smelled ordinary and friendly, full of soap and clean cotton, while a soft draft moved past Farah’s ankles and made the hanging coats sway once. Farah liked how the stone floor felt steady under their shoes, and they paused to listen to the far hush of the school settling into rest time.

On a low shelf near the lunch mats Farah noticed a small pencil cup with two pencils still sharp, their clean points bright in the gentle light that came through the cloakroom door. Farah lifted the cup carefully so the wood felt smooth against their palms, and they counted the two pencils again while the air stayed pleasantly mild around their shoulders. Uncle Idris stood nearby without rushing, and Farah glanced toward Yuri who had just slipped off a backpack and was settling onto a mat with quiet interest. The pencils looked ready for drawing after the simple meal, yet Farah felt the cup would not feel fair if only one person received a sharp point first while the other waited empty-handed.

Farah sat cross-legged on the soft lunch mat and set the pencil cup between themselves and Yuri so both could see the twin points catching the same soft glow from the windows. “These two are still sharp,” Farah said, and the words came out clear and friendly in the quiet room. Yuri nodded and reached a careful hand toward the edge of the mat, while Uncle Idris only watched with a small smile and let the air stay peaceful. Farah remembered that sharing the whole cup at once might leave someone without, so they began to think of a smaller, even way that would include Yuri right from the start.

Farah found a short scrap of paper already waiting on the tray and tore it gently into two matching pieces that felt dry and light between their fingers. “We can each keep one sharp pencil and draw side by side,” Farah told Yuri, and the invitation hung gently in the mild air. Yuri’s eyes brightened, and they answered, “That feels right to me,” while the faint scrape of paper settled into silence again. Uncle Idris remained quiet on his stool, the light resting evenly on his hands, and he offered no speech at all as Farah’s choice stood open and kind.

Farah placed one sharp pencil on Yuri’s piece of paper and kept the second for themselves so the pencil cup now held only empty space that looked balanced and calm. The lunch mats felt springy under Farah’s knees, and a walnut-smooth spoon rested on the nearest tray where someone had left it after stirring warm milk earlier. Farah picked up their pencil and drew a simple house with a round door, listening to the soft scratch of graphite while Yuri drew a tree with wide branches on the other scrap. The temperature in the room stayed comfortable, never too warm, and Farah breathed easily as the two pictures grew together without hurry.

A soft click sounded when someone outside closed a gate latch in the courtyard, and the brief metallic note drifted in and then faded, leaving the cloakroom and mats even quieter than before. Farah looked up and smiled at Yuri, who was adding leaves to the tree, and they both kept their pencils moving in the same gentle rhythm. Uncle Idris poured a little more milk into two cups and set them down without a word, trusting the even share Farah had made. Farah felt the mat’s woven texture under their free hand and noticed how the light had grown softer as the afternoon leaned toward evening rest.

Farah hummed four quiet notes and then left a rest so the silence could sit peacefully between them, and the small melody matched the slow breathing of the room. Yuri finished a last branch and set the pencil down carefully so it would stay sharp for another day, while Farah added a tiny cloud above the house. “We both have what we need,” Farah said, and Yuri answered, “Yes, and the pictures can stay together on the mat.” Uncle Idris nodded once, still without any speech, and the air carried a faint leftover cinnamon that drifted from a satchel near the coats, sweet and familiar and soon gone again into the ordinary school smell.

Farah gathered the two drawings and slipped them into a shared folder on the shelf so neither picture would feel left out, and the folder’s cardboard edges felt firm and dry under their thumbs. The pencil cup stood empty yet content on the tray, its simple shape catching the last mild light that crossed the cloakroom threshold. Farah stretched their legs on the mat and felt the cloth cool slightly where shadow had begun to fall, and they listened to the soft settling of coats on their hooks. Yuri rested back on both hands and watched the doorway with quiet happiness while Uncle Idris rose and checked that every hook was free of forgotten scarves.

Farah walked once more through the cloakroom so their shoes made only the softest sound on the stone, and they touched a hanging sleeve that still held a trace of outdoor freshness against their palm. The lunch mats behind them looked neat and finished, the two pencils returned side by side to the cup for morning, and the whole space felt settled and kind. Uncle Idris waited by the outer door with his usual calm face, letting Farah’s even choice remain exactly as it was. Farah took one last breath of the mild indoor air and felt ready for the quiet walk home. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
