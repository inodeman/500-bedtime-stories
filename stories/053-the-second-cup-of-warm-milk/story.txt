"The second cup is still warm, Yelena," Miss Dalca said as the ferry slid away from the dock and the evening air brushed cool against their cheeks. Yelena sat close on the wooden bench with Cosima tucked under the same folded blanket that smelled faintly of clean soap and river water. Soft lantern light from the cabin window painted gold stripes across the floorboards while the boat hummed low and steady. Yelena watched the dark water ripple past and felt the gentle sway rock their shoulders. Cosima leaned nearer so their elbows touched. Miss Dalca held a small tray with two steaming cups and rested it carefully on her knees. Yelena noticed how the steam rose in thin curls and how the blanket's edge brushed their wrists with every quiet breath of the wind.

Yelena ran one hand along the folded blanket and felt its soft weight settle over their lap like a slow hug. Cosima whispered something about the far lights on the opposite shore, and Yelena nodded without looking away from the two cups. The first cup waited nearer Miss Dalca's hand while the second cup sat a little apart, both filled nearly to the brim. Yelena thought about how odd it would feel if only one person sipped first and the other waited empty-handed. The boat lights glowed warmer now against the growing dusk. Miss Dalca's cotton apron lay smooth across her lap where the tray balanced, and Yelena touched the edge of it once by accident when the ferry gave a tiny roll. The fabric felt worn and kind under their fingertips.

A faint scent of toasted sesame drifted from the small paper bag tucked beside Miss Dalca's elbow, the same bag that had held the crisp little crackers she shared earlier. Yelena breathed it in and felt their stomach settle into quiet comfort. Cosima's shoe bumped gently against Yelena's under the blanket. Miss Dalca lifted the tray a fraction higher so the cups would not tip. Yelena kept their eyes on the second cup of warm milk and pictured pouring half of each into something smaller so three people could taste it together. The cabin window glass felt cool when Yelena rested one palm near it for a moment. Outside, the river moved like dark silk under the fading sky.

Far back on the dock a gate latch clicked shut with a soft metal sound that carried across the water and then faded. Yelena listened until the click was gone and only the ferry engines remained. Cosima pulled the blanket higher so it covered both their arms. Miss Dalca smiled but said nothing yet. Yelena reached toward the tray with careful fingers and asked, "May we make the cups smaller so Cosima can have some too?" The steam still rose in gentle clouds that warmed the space between them. Miss Dalca simply nodded and shifted so Yelena could reach both cups without spilling. The second cup of warm milk felt solid and safe in Yelena's hands.

Yelena poured a little from the first cup into the clean empty lid Miss Dalca offered, then poured the same careful amount from the second cup so all three portions sat even and small. Cosima watched with bright eyes and said, "Thank you, Yelena, that looks perfect." The milk stayed warm enough that a thin mist still curled above each share. Yelena handed one portion to Cosima, kept one, and left the last for Miss Dalca. The blanket slipped a little and Yelena smoothed it flat again across their knees so no cold air could sneak underneath. Soft golden light from the cabin lanterns made the milk look like pale moonlight in the cups.

Miss Dalca accepted her share without any long words and simply lifted it in quiet thanks. Yelena took a slow sip and felt the warmth travel down their throat while the ferry rocked them all in the same gentle rhythm. Cosima blew once across the surface of their milk and then drank too. The second cup of warm milk had become three even tastes, and no one waited empty. Yelena heard the water lapping against the hull in a steady hush. Miss Dalca's free hand rested lightly on the folded blanket near Yelena's shoulder. Everything felt balanced and calm under the soft cabin glow.

Yelena leaned back against the bench and let their head rest near Cosima's for a moment while the milk cooled just enough to sip without hurry. "It tastes like the kitchen at home," Cosima said, and Yelena answered, "I wanted us all to try it together." Miss Dalca kept her silence and only smiled once more, letting the choice stand exactly as Yelena had made it. The air inside the cabin stayed pleasantly warm against the cooler draft near the door. Yelena tucked the blanket edge under Cosima's leg so it would not slide. Outside the wide window the opposite shore lights grew larger and clearer with every quiet minute.

Yelena finished the last of their share and set the empty lid carefully on the tray beside the others. Cosima yawned behind one small hand and leaned heavier against Yelena's side. Miss Dalca gathered the cups with slow movements that made almost no sound. Yelena felt the ferry slow as it neared the far landing, the engines dropping to a lower note that vibrated gently through the bench. The folded blanket still held the day's leftover warmth against their legs. Yelena watched the approaching dock lights and felt sleepy peace settle over their eyelids. Miss Dalca's presence stayed steady and kind without any need for extra talk.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Yelena pulled the blanket higher one last time so Cosima and they could share its weight while the ferry bumped gently against the pilings. Miss Dalca rose and waited without rushing them. Cool night air drifted through the open cabin door and mixed with the last traces of warmth from the milk. Yelena stood slowly, still holding one corner of the blanket, and followed Miss Dalca toward the quiet ramp. Cosima stayed close enough that their sleeves brushed. The whole ride had become a gentle circle of shared cups and steady light, and Yelena carried that calm feeling with them as they stepped onto solid ground under the soft glow that followed them home.
