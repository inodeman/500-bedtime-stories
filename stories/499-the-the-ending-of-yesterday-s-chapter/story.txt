The sweet smell of pear skins rose from the bowl as Delia's hands already turned and peeled the soft fruit with slow care on the covered porch. Rain ticked gently against the roof boards in a steady quiet pattern while the evening stayed mild and nobody was late for supper or stories. Nana Ruth settled into the big chair beside Delia, her shawl brushing Delia's arm with its worn softness. Cool damp air touched Delia's cheeks and wrists as they kept working the pears, and the porch light threw a gentle yellow pool across the floorboards. Delia pressed their bare toes against the wood and listened to the rain without rushing. Nana Ruth watched Delia's busy fingers and waited until the last peel curled free before she spoke at all.

Nana Ruth said, "I need to retell the ending of yesterday's chapter because I left out the part where the little boat came home under the moon." Delia felt a quick urge to share bigger news about the drawing they had made earlier, yet they kept still and let the words settle. The rain kept ticking overhead in its calm rhythm while a faint warmth rose from the porch boards under Delia's feet. Delia set the finished pears aside and folded their sticky hands in their lap so they could listen without hurry. Soft light from the kitchen window made the rain look like silver threads beyond the railing. Nana Ruth's voice stayed low and even as she began again, and Delia breathed in the leftover pear sweetness that still hung near the bowl.

Nana Ruth continued, "In the ending of yesterday's chapter the boat drifted past the tall reeds and the lantern on its bow glowed just enough to show the way home." Delia wanted for a moment to jump in with their own news, yet they pressed their lips together and watched Nana Ruth's hands move in the air. The air carried a gentle coolness that made Delia pull their sweater sleeves down over their wrists. Delia heard the rain tick and tick while Nana Ruth described the calm water and the quiet return. Delia rested their cheek against the chair arm and felt the smooth wood under their skin. Everything stayed peaceful on the porch as the story unfolded once more for both of them.

Delia listened all the way through the retelling while Nana Ruth spoke of how the chapter ended with the boat tying up at the old dock and everyone safe inside the cottage. The sound of wind in the fig leaves drifted across the yard between the rain ticks, soft and rustling like quiet pages turning. Delia kept their eyes on Nana Ruth's face and did not interrupt even once. Warm light from inside the house reached Delia's knees and made small shadows dance when Nana Ruth gestured. Delia felt the leftover stickiness of pear juice on their fingers and rubbed them gently together. Nana Ruth's voice never rose, and the porch remained a calm circle of rain and story for as long as it took.

When Nana Ruth reached the final sentence of the ending she said, "And that is how yesterday's chapter truly closed, with the lantern still shining and the water still." Delia stayed quiet a moment longer, feeling the cool evening air slip under the porch railing and touch their ankles. Delia wanted to speak their bigger news yet held it back until the last word faded. The rain ticked on without hurry and the fig leaves whispered again somewhere beyond the steps. Delia shifted slightly in the chair and let the whole retelling settle inside them like a soft blanket. Nana Ruth folded her hands and waited with a patient smile while Delia thought.

Delia asked one real question then, saying, "Nana Ruth, did the boat captain feel happy when the lantern showed the dock at last?" Nana Ruth nodded and answered, "Yes, Delia, the captain felt quiet happy and glad the journey had ended safely under that light." Delia felt a small warm coin that Nana Ruth pressed into their palm as a keepsake from the story pocket, its metal smooth and comforting against their skin. The porch air stayed mild and the rain continued its gentle ticking above them. Delia closed their fingers around the coin and smiled at the simple answer. Soft kitchen light still spilled across the boards and made everything feel close and safe.

Only after the question did Delia share something small of their own, saying, "I drew a little boat today with a yellow light on the front." Nana Ruth leaned nearer and said, "That sounds lovely, Delia, and I would like to see it when we go inside." Delia felt the warm coin still resting in their hand and the mild dampness of the evening on their face. Rain ticked steadily while Delia described the simple lines of the drawing without needing to make it bigger than it was. Delia stretched one bare foot and gently straightened the rug corner with a toe so it lay flat again near the chair legs. The porch felt peaceful and unhurried as they sat together after the retelling.

Delia leaned against Nana Ruth's side and listened to the rain once more while the warm coin stayed tucked in their fist. Nana Ruth stroked Delia's hair with slow strokes that felt soft and familiar in the cool air. Delia smelled the last faint trace of pear skins near the bowl and felt glad they had waited to speak. The light from the house doorway stayed steady and golden across the porch floor. Delia yawned a little and pressed closer, hearing only the rain and the quiet creak of the rocking chair. Nothing pressed them to hurry, and the evening held them both in its calm circle.

Nana Ruth rose slowly and held out her hand so Delia could stand too, the warm coin still safe in Delia's other palm. Delia followed her toward the doorway where the air felt a touch warmer already. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Rain still ticked on the porch roof behind them as they stepped inside together, and Delia kept the small shared quiet of the retelling close while the night settled gently around the house.
