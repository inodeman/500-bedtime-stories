"Look how far the tide has slipped away," Idris said to Bodhi as they stood side by side on the broad harbor wall. The evening light poured gently over the exposed mudflats and made every wet pebble gleam like a tiny mirror, while the air against Idris's face felt pleasantly cool after the warm day. Bodhi shuffled closer so their shoulders almost touched, and Idris pointed down at the quiet stretch of sand where small pools held the sky's reflection. They both watched a pair of gulls walk carefully across the wet ground far below, their feet leaving little marks that filled slowly with water. Idris shifted their weight and felt the solid stones steady underfoot, grateful for the calm stretch of time before supper.

Idris and Bodhi walked a little farther along the harbor wall until Idris spotted something unexpected resting against the rough stones near a gentle curve. It was a quilt folded into a neat square, its edges just touching the damp moss that clung to the wall at low tide. Idris reached out carefully and felt the soft cloth under their fingertips, thick and smooth like a well-loved blanket someone had left after watching the water. The light from the sky had turned a deeper blue now, casting long soft shadows from the wall down onto the empty beach. Bodhi crouched beside Idris and they both leaned in without saying much at first, simply taking in the surprise of finding such a cozy thing out here by the quiet harbor.

When Idris looked more closely at the center of the quilt they saw that the white stitches had formed little groups of stars connected by faint lines of thread, making an accidental constellation right there on the deep blue fabric. The shapes shone gently in the remaining light, and Idris felt the wish rise up to pocket a small corner of the quilt or to poke at one of the raised stitches with a curious finger. Instead they pulled their hand back and let the cool temperature of the coming night settle around their shoulders like a quiet friend. Bodhi waited patiently while Idris decided what to do next, and the stones of the wall still held a leftover warmth beneath the quilt that Idris could sense even without touching again.

"See these stitches that look like stars, Bodhi?" Idris said in a calm voice. "Someone sewed them without meaning to make a constellation, but here it is. We can look as long as we want if we keep our hands away." Bodhi nodded and settled on their heels so they could study the pattern from the same angle as Idris. A distant boat creaked faintly across the water, soft and steady, while the two friends traced the shapes with their eyes alone. Idris pointed gently in the air above the quilt, showing the outlines of what might be a little bear or a curve of bright points, never letting a finger land on the cloth. They spoke Bodhi's name again while describing each cluster, keeping the space open and kind.

"It looks just like the sky on a clear night," Bodhi replied softly, smiling at the discovery. Idris showed Bodhi how to tip their head sideways a bit so the accidental constellation seemed even clearer against the blue background of the quilt. Together they stayed very still, and the air around them felt fresh and light as more of the day slipped away. Idris explained which stitch might be the brightest star, always careful to keep hands far from the fabric so nothing would shift or loosen. No rush pushed them; they simply enjoyed the quiet finding right there on the harbor wall at low tide, shoulders nearly touching as they shared the view.

The ferryman appeared then, climbing the worn steps that led up from his small boat moored below the wall. His footsteps made a low scraping sound on the stone, and he stopped when he noticed Idris and Bodhi gathered around the quilt. The fading light touched his kind face and the sleeves of his jacket as he came nearer with an easy step. Idris looked up and felt glad the ferryman was there to share the moment. A thin mist of cooler air rose from the low tide flats and brushed past them all like a soft scarf, while the stones underfoot stayed firm and familiar.

"What have you found there?" the ferryman asked in a warm tone. Idris explained about the accidental constellation on the quilt and how they were only looking, never poking or pocketing any part of it. The ferryman crouched down too so his eyes were level with the stitched stars, and he nodded slowly as he took in the neat white thread against the deep blue. "Best to leave such a surprise right where it is," the ferryman said with a gentle smile. Idris agreed at once, and all three of them looked once more at the quiet pattern without moving a single thread or fold.

The wind stirred in the fig leaves of the old tree that grew beside the harbor wall, making a dry rustling sound as Idris and Bodhi and the ferryman stood quiet for a moment longer. Then they turned away together, leaving the quilt exactly as they found it on the cool stones. Idris glanced back once to make certain the constellation still rested undisturbed, and Bodhi did the same before they followed the ferryman toward the path that led home. The last of the evening light softened everything to gray-blue, and the air cooled a little more against Idris's wrists and neck as they walked side by side.

Back at home Idris carried the empty bowl to the sink and came back to the table where the smell of warm bread filled the kitchen in a comforting wave. They touched a cool ceramic rim of a mug while Bodhi sat nearby, and the kitchen felt snug after the open harbor. Soon it was time to rest, so Idris climbed the stairs with slow steps and settled under their own covers, still thinking of the stitched stars left safe on the wall. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
