A soft clink rose from the counter as a jam jar settled, and Dahlia noticed it at once in the bakery kitchen where dough and jam were left to finish. Warm air drifted from the cooling ovens and brushed Dahlia’s cheeks like a gentle breath. Dahlia stood on the low stool and listened again, toes curling against the smooth floorboards that still held a little heat. Flour dust floated in the low golden light that spilled from the wall lamps. Mama moved nearby with quiet steps, and Dahlia felt the steady calm of the room wrap around them like a soft blanket.

Dahlia’s eyes found the small beeswax candle cooling in its tin near the back of the counter, the pale wax still soft under its thin cloth cover. The tin felt cool when Dahlia rested one careful fingertip on its rim for half a second. “Mama, may I lift the cloth and check the candle now?” Dahlia asked in a small voice that barely rose above the hush. Mama smiled and shook her head gently. “Not yet, Dahlia. It needs its own quiet time,” Mama said. The sweet scent of rising dough mixed with the faint fruit smell of the resting jam, and Dahlia breathed it in while watching the covered tin.

Dahlia wanted so much to peek, yet they kept their hands folded and looked instead at the wooden spoon jar. A leather lace hung from Mama’s apron pocket, smooth and slightly warm from being near the ovens, and Dahlia touched it once with curious fingers before letting it swing free again. “I can wait,” Dahlia said softly. Mama nodded. “That is a kind choice, Dahlia.” The kitchen lights glowed steady and low, painting soft circles on the scrubbed table where jars of jam waited under their lids.

Dahlia decided the quiet task of lining the spoons up by size would fill the waiting minutes well. One by one they lifted the spoons from the crock, feeling each handle’s solid weight, and set them in a neat row from longest to shortest along the cool marble edge. The metal made the tiniest whisper as it touched stone. Dahlia worked slowly so the candle would not be hurried. Warmth still rose from the nearby bread boards, and Dahlia’s bare arms felt comfortable in that mild heat while the spoons gleamed under the gentle lamps.

A cat’s collar bell gave one light jingle from the doorway as the kitchen cat padded past and settled on a folded towel. Dahlia glanced over, smiled, then returned to the last two spoons, placing them carefully so the row looked even and peaceful. Mama wiped a bowl nearby and spoke again. “You are doing that so neatly, Dahlia.” Dahlia answered, “Thank you, Mama. The spoons like being in order while we wait.” The air held a mild temperature that neither chilled nor overheated, perfect for the dough that rested under its cloth and the jam that thickened slowly in its pots.

While arranging the final spoon, Dahlia caught the clean smell of soapy wool drifting from the laundry basket Mama had set by the back wall, where washed cloths waited to dry. The scent was fresh and gentle, different from the bakery sweetness yet still friendly. Dahlia breathed it once and felt settled. The beeswax candle stayed covered in its tin, and Dahlia did not lift the cloth even though curiosity tugged. Soft light from the overhead fixtures made the tin’s edge shine like a small moon resting on the counter.

Dahlia stepped back to the stool and watched the covered tin while resting both palms on the warm wooden edge of the table. Mama poured a little milk into two cups and the liquid made a soft pouring sound. “The candle will tell us when it is ready by growing firm,” Mama said. Dahlia nodded and answered, “I can keep waiting with you.” The texture of the table grain felt familiar under their hands, and the whole kitchen seemed to breathe slowly together—dough rising, jam settling, candle cooling—while the lamps kept their steady glow.

Time stretched in a peaceful way as Dahlia and Mama sat side by side listening to the quiet kitchen sounds. The covered tin waited without hurry. Dahlia counted the lined spoons again with their eyes and felt proud of the neat row. Cool night air slipped under the door for a moment and brushed Dahlia’s ankles before the warmth of the room returned. Mama rested a hand lightly on Dahlia’s shoulder, and the simple touch felt steady and kind. Everything finished in its own time, just as the dough would, just as the jam would, and just as the small beeswax candle would.

At last Mama lifted the cloth with careful fingers and showed Dahlia the candle standing firm and smooth inside its tin, ready at last. Dahlia clapped once very softly so the cat would not startle. The kitchen smelled of finished baking and clean counters. Dahlia leaned close and saw the pale wax catch the light. Mama said nothing more, only smiled, and Dahlia felt the day’s quiet work settle into rest. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
