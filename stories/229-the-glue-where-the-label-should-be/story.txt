Uncle Idris stood already at the workshop bench of wood, glue, and labels, pressing a fresh paper edge with steady fingers while the late sun laid a honey-colored stripe across the boards. The air near his tea cup carried a gentle smell of cinnamon that drifted past Sable’s nose as they climbed onto the low stool. Uncle Idris hummed four notes and then left a rest, the quiet hanging soft as cloth. Sable watched the careful work and felt the warm wood of the stool under their legs. “Sable, will you help me choose the next label for this little drawer?” Uncle Idris asked without looking up, and his voice sounded kind in the still room.

Sable slid closer along the workshop bench of wood, glue, and labels until their palms rested on the grain. Outside, somewhere down the path, a gate latch clicked once and then went still. A neat row of blank labels waited in a shallow tray, and the open glue pot sat with a soft brush leaning on the rim. Sable took a label and the brush and tried to dab a thin strip of glue, just as Uncle Idris had shown them. The wet shine caught the window light and looked almost like a tiny puddle of clear water on the wood.

Yet the glue left Sable’s brush too heavily, and a thick clear blob sat right where a careful label should be on the small wooden lid they had chosen. The blob felt cool and stretchy against Sable’s fingertips, and a little stickiness stayed behind. Chioma, visiting on a cushion near the bench, watched with quiet eyes and said nothing at all. Sable’s cheeks grew warm in the mild air of the workshop. For one long breath Sable thought about sliding a spare label over the mess so no one would notice the lump.

Sable’s hand hovered with that spare label while dust motes floated in the slanted light above the bench. They almost hid the glue blob where a careful label should be. Then Sable looked at the neat rows Uncle Idris had already finished and remembered the calm way he always spoke. Sable set the spare label down on the tray and took another slow breath. The blob still glistened, stubborn and plain, under the high window’s glow.

“Uncle Idris,” Sable said, their voice soft but clear, “I put too much glue, and now there is a blob where the careful label should be.” Uncle Idris turned at once, and the light caught the silver threads in his hair as he stepped nearer the bench. He wiped his hands on a clean cloth and studied the wooden lid without any frown. “Thank you for telling me the plain truth, Sable,” Uncle Idris said. The room felt gentle and still, and a moth brushed the warm glass of the lamp with a tiny whisper of wings.

Together they looked at what could be mended on the workshop bench of wood, glue, and labels. Uncle Idris opened the tool drawer and handed Sable a walnut-smooth spoon, and Sable felt how polished and cool the spoon was against their fingers as they eased the extra glue away in slow, careful scrapes. “We can lift the thickest part first,” Uncle Idris said, “and then we will wait a little while for the rest to settle.” Chioma brought a soft cloth and laid it beside them without fuss. Sable worked the spoon gently, and the quiet sliding sound on the wood mixed with the low creak of the old stool whenever they shifted.

After a short wait the glue that remained was thin enough for a proper label. Sable pressed the new label down with both hands, and the paper felt dry and crisp under their palms in the cool shade of the bench. Uncle Idris held the edges steady so nothing wrinkled. “You did well to speak up, Sable,” he said, and his voice was warm. Sable smiled and pressed once more until the label lay flat and true across the mended spot. A mild draft from the open door brushed Sable’s arms like a soft sleeve, and the wooden lid looked ready again, tidy and proud on the bench.

Chioma clapped once, softly, then folded her hands in her lap. Sable rinsed their fingers in a shallow bowl of warm water that steamed faintly beside the labels, and the water felt silky as it loosened the last sticky spots. “Can we put the box on the shelf now?” Sable asked. Uncle Idris nodded, and together they carried the mended piece across the room. The shelf wood felt sturdy under Sable’s free hand, slightly rough yet welcoming. Outside the light was turning gold and slow. Sable felt quiet and glad inside, the way a full cup sits still on a table after pouring.

Later Sable sat on the stool again while Uncle Idris tidied the remaining labels into their tray, and the workshop grew dim and peaceful around them. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Sable listened to the slow drip of leftover glue returning to the pot and felt the evening coolness settle on their shoulders like a light blanket. Uncle Idris hummed four notes once more and then left a rest, and Sable rested too, calm and ready whenever sleep arrived. Chioma waved goodbye from the doorway with a soft swish of her scarf, and Sable waved back, already thinking of the neat label they had saved together on the workshop bench.
