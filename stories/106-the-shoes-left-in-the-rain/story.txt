The pair of canvas shoes rested side by side on the cottage porch boards, their damp blue fabric darkened by the gentle rain that whispered down in soft silver threads. Water beaded along the seams and slid toward the toes before falling with tiny ticks onto the wood. The leather lace threaded through the eyelets of the left shoe lay cool and slightly stiff where the rain had touched it, and Chioma leaned close enough to see every droplet catch the porch light like small clear beads. Cool mist brushed Chioma’s bare ankles while the quiet glow from the open doorway painted a pale gold strip across the wet canvas.

Chioma rose and brushed damp fingers against their own sleeve, hearing the hush of the rain on the roof shingles. Inside the hall just beyond the threshold, Cousin Pio stood with a towel bunched awkwardly in both hands, trying to reach the shoes without letting the dripping water trail across the clean floorboards. Pio’s shoulders hunched as they stretched and nearly tipped a small basket of folded cloths. Chioma watched the struggle for a moment, noticing how the warm indoor light made Pio’s face look pink and determined, and how the air drifting out smelled faintly of soapy wool from a freshly washed sweater draped over a chair.

“I can take one shoe if you take the other,” Chioma said in a low voice that barely rose above the rain’s soft drumming. Pio glanced up, eyes bright with relief, and nodded once. Chioma stepped carefully onto the porch again, lifted the right canvas shoe by its heel so the leftover drops fell outside, and carried it across the threshold without a single splash on the hall rug. The shoe felt heavy and cool in Chioma’s palms, and the hall’s warmer air wrapped around their wrists like a gentle blanket.

Pio followed with the second shoe and the towel, setting both on a wide mat near the wall. “The laces are still dripping a little,” Pio murmured, pressing the towel along the sides. Chioma knelt beside them and blotted the other shoe with a second dry corner of the same towel, working slowly so the fabric would not bunch. A soft jingle drifted from the next room as a cat’s collar bell rang once when the household cat padded past the doorway, the sound clear and small in the quiet hall. Warm yellow light from a wall sconce fell across Chioma’s hands and turned the drying canvas a softer blue.

Together they turned each shoe so the open tops faced the gentle indoor breeze that came from a cracked window farther down the hall. Chioma held one shoe steady while Pio smoothed the damp tongue flat, and neither of them spoke for a long calm minute. The wooden floor felt smooth and slightly cool under Chioma’s bare feet, and the smell of rain still clung faintly to the canvas even as the hall’s dryness began to drink the water away. Chioma noticed a stray drop on the mat and wiped it with the edge of the towel before it could spread.

“Shall we set them where the air moves best?” Pio asked, glancing toward a low bench beneath the window. Chioma nodded and lifted their shoe again, walking the few steps with careful balance so nothing dripped. They placed both shoes neatly side by side on the bench, toes pointing outward, and Chioma reached over to straighten the tongue of the left one until it looked comfortable. The bench wood felt dry and solid under Chioma’s fingers, and the rain outside softened to an even quieter hush against the cottage walls.

Joss appeared briefly in the doorway with a small stack of clean dish towels and offered one without a word. Chioma took it and folded it once before tucking it under the heels of the shoes to catch any last moisture. “Thank you, Joss,” Chioma said softly, and Joss smiled and slipped away again toward the kitchen. The hall air felt pleasantly warm now against Chioma’s cheeks, and the faint golden light made the drying canvas look almost ready for morning walks. Pio exhaled a long, easy breath and rested both hands on their knees.

Chioma and Pio sat together on the edge of the hall rug for a quiet stretch of time, watching the last beads of rainwater fade from the shoe tips. Chioma ran one finger lightly along the outer edge of a shoe, feeling the canvas grow less cool by degrees, and Pio hummed a tiny wordless tune. Outside, the gentle rain continued its soft pattern on the porch roof, and inside the cottage the air stayed still and kind. Chioma felt the steady warmth of the room settle around their shoulders like a familiar shawl, and neither cousin needed to say anything more about the work they had shared.

Later, when the shoes stood dry enough on their bench and the hall had grown quieter still, Chioma wandered to the window seat and rested their chin on folded arms. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Outside the rain had thinned to a mist that left the porch boards shining under the porch lamp, and inside Chioma’s eyelids grew heavy while the last cool breath of evening drifted through the cracked pane and brushed their forehead with gentle temperature.
