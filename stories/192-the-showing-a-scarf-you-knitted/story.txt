Uma peered very close at the short scarf with one bumpy row resting across their lap behind the bakery counter facing the street. Soft golden light from the wide front window spilled over every wool loop and made the bumpy row shine like a tiny raised path of cloudy yarn. Uma moved one careful finger along that higher ridge of stitches and felt how it rose gently above the smoother rows on either side. The wool held the mild warmth of Uma’s own hands from carrying it all the way to the bakery that afternoon. Uma sat very still on the low wooden stool and kept looking at the scarf they had made, turning it slowly so the light could touch the bumpy place again and again.

Tove moved nearby among the bread baskets and the little clay pot of green leaves that released a fresh smell of basil into the warm bakery air. Uma glanced up once at Tove’s friendly face and then looked back down at the short scarf with one bumpy row. Outside the glass the street stayed busy with quiet footsteps and slow carts, yet inside the light stayed gentle and even. Uma felt the wooden counter under their elbows, smooth from years of polishing, and shifted the scarf so it lay flat. The air around Uma stayed pleasantly heated from the big ovens at the back, wrapping the whole counter in a cozy blanket of warmth that never grew too strong.

A tin clock on the high shelf near the oven ticked with a clear steady sound that marked each slow second while Uma waited. The door opened with a soft chime and the ferryman stepped inside, brushing river mist from his coat before he smiled across the counter. “Good afternoon, Uma,” the ferryman said in his low kind voice that never hurried anyone. Uma’s cheeks felt a little warmer under the bakery light, yet they kept the short scarf with one bumpy row resting openly on their knees instead of tucking it away. Tove handed the ferryman a fresh roll wrapped in paper and then returned to the loaves without saying more. Uma listened to the tin clock keep its calm rhythm and breathed the familiar heated air.

Uma felt shy about the bumpy row that stood higher than the rest of the short scarf, yet Uma chose not to pretend the scarf did not matter. Instead Uma took the smallest brave step and lifted the scarf just high enough for the ferryman to see it clearly across the bakery counter facing the street. Soft afternoon light still poured through the window and painted pale shapes on the floorboards near Uma’s shoes. The air stayed warm and steady around Uma’s shoulders. Tove gave one quiet nod from the far end of the counter while arranging more rolls, and that small sign helped Uma keep the scarf in view.

“I knitted this short scarf,” Uma said in a voice that could be heard all the way across the counter, holding the bumpy row toward the light so it showed without hiding. The ferryman looked with gentle eyes and did not ask Uma to stand up or wave the scarf about or make any bigger show. “What careful work you have done,” the ferryman replied softly, his words as steady as the river he crossed each day. Uma felt the wool stay warm against their palms and kept the scarf at that same small height. No one pushed for anything more, and the kindness settled around Uma like another layer of bakery heat.

The ferryman leaned his elbows on the counter facing the street and watched the quiet passersby while he ate his roll. Uma noticed the smooth round paperweight Tove kept beside the order slips and touched it once; the polished pebble felt cool and hard under Uma’s fingertip before they drew their hand back. “You can wear it on cool mornings on the boat if you like,” Uma added in the same clear small voice that still reached the ferryman. “I would be glad to keep it with me,” the ferryman said, smiling without reaching out or asking Uma to place it on him right then. The light from the street window had grown softer and more golden as the afternoon moved along.

Uma set the short scarf with one bumpy row safely beside the napkin basket and began to help Tove. Together they folded the cloth napkins so the stitched pear on each one faced straight up in neat stacks on the counter. The napkin cloth felt crisp and clean under Uma’s fingers while the late light made the pear stitches gleam faintly like little fruits. “These look ready for the morning customers,” Tove said with a quiet smile. Uma nodded and kept folding in the same calm rhythm, placing each finished napkin just so. The bakery air stayed warm near the ovens yet cooler near the door whenever it opened for another visitor.

Before he left, the ferryman spoke once more in his kind unhurried way. “Thank you for sharing your knitting with me, Uma. The bumpy row makes the scarf feel true and full of care.” Uma answered right away, “I like how it turned out after all the evenings I spent with the needles.” Warm air from the ovens mixed with cooler drafts each time the door swung, yet the mixture never felt sharp. Uma tucked the short scarf carefully into a clean basket the ferryman could collect later when his ferry work finished. Outside the street sounds stayed distant and gentle while inside the bakery held its peaceful even light.

As the day turned toward evening Uma and Tove wiped the last crumbs from the counter facing the street and set the napkin stacks in their proper place. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Uma watched its gentle circle of light settle over the empty stool and the basket that held the short scarf with one bumpy row, feeling the bakery grow still and safe while the first street lamps began to twinkle beyond the glass and the warm air lingered like a quiet goodnight around their shoulders.
