In the library room beside the return desk, a tin clock made a small steady tick that reached Kito first. Kito noticed the sound at once and held still with their fingers resting on an open picture book. Cool air drifted from the doorway and touched Kito’s cheeks while the soft tick continued. Pale light from the high windows lay in quiet bars across the wooden floor near Kito’s shoes. Kito listened for several moments and felt the peaceful rhythm settle into their chest. They closed the book with care and placed it on the smooth counter, still hearing that gentle metallic sound. Outside a light rain whispered against the glass, yet the room itself stayed warm and still. Kito breathed in the clean scent of paper and wood and decided to look toward the open door where the rainy steps began.

Kito walked closer to the return desk and felt the cooler air growing stronger near the entrance. Through the open doorway they saw Mr. Ade standing on the rainy steps with two market bags whose thin handles cut into his wrist. The bags sagged heavily with vegetables and folded cloths, and Mr. Ade shifted from one foot to the other while raindrops tapped the stone. Kito watched the way the handles pressed white lines into Mr. Ade’s skin and how he tried to keep the bags from slipping. Soft gray light washed over the wet steps and made the stone shine. Kito felt the temperature drop a little more as they stepped nearer the threshold. They noticed Mr. Ade’s careful balance and the way he glanced at the library door as if hoping to come inside soon.

Kito stepped onto the rainy steps and felt the cool damp stone under their shoes. Mr. Ade looked up and offered a tired smile while the rain made tiny sounds on the bags. “These market bags are heavier than I thought today,” Mr. Ade said in a low voice. Kito nodded and reached out without waiting. “I can carry one of them for you,” Kito said. The lavender smell drifted from a small cloth sachet tucked among the greens inside the nearest bag. Kito took hold of that bag’s handle and felt the weight lift from Mr. Ade’s wrist at once. Rain tapped softly on Kito’s sleeves while they stood together on the steps. Mr. Ade adjusted the remaining bag and let out a quiet breath of relief.

Together they moved carefully up the rainy steps toward the return desk. Kito kept the market bag close and felt the damp coolness of the air on their bare wrists. Mr. Ade said, “The steps are slick when it rains like this.” Kito answered, “We can go slowly and set them just inside.” The light from the open doorway glowed warmer than the gray outdoors and guided their feet. Kito noticed how the bag no longer cut into anyone’s skin now that the load was shared. They crossed the last step and felt the solid dry floor of the library underfoot again. Inside the return desk waited with its lamp still shining on the counter. Kito set their bag down gently beside the desk and waited while Mr. Ade did the same.

Mr. Ade rubbed his wrist and smiled at Kito near the return desk. “Thank you for taking half without me even asking,” he said. Kito simply nodded and felt the warmer indoor air settle around their shoulders. They helped slide the bags a little farther from the doorway so no one would trip. Soft lamp light touched the edges of the market bags and made the cloth look almost golden. Kito noticed a loose thread on one bag and smoothed it with one finger. The quiet of the library wrapped around them both while the rain continued its soft pattern outside. Kito stayed close and watched Mr. Ade begin to unpack a few library books that had been tucked carefully on top of the vegetables.

Kito stood beside the return desk and watched Mr. Ade place the books into the return slot one by one. The wooden counter felt smooth and cool under Kito’s palm when they rested their hand there. Mr. Ade said, “These books were due today, so I carried them along with the market things.” Kito listened and felt glad the books were safe and dry. A faint warmth rose from the lamp and touched the side of Kito’s face. They helped straighten the remaining bag so it sat neatly against the desk leg. Outside the rainy steps glistened under the gray sky, yet inside everything felt steady. Kito’s breathing slowed to match the peaceful room once more.

After the books were returned, Kito and Mr. Ade rested for a moment near the desk. Kito’s fingers found a small quilt stitch along the edge of a folded cloth that had slipped from one market bag. The raised stitch felt firm and even under their fingertip, like a tiny path sewn by careful hands. Warm lamp light pooled on the cloth and made the stitch easy to see. Kito traced it once more and then tucked the cloth back inside the bag. Mr. Ade hummed a short quiet tune while he checked that nothing else needed returning. The air smelled clean and calm again. Kito stayed nearby and felt the simple comfort of having shared the weight.

Kito glanced toward the tall window and watched a thin shadow from the window frame move the width of a thumb across the floor as a cloud passed. The movement was slow and gentle, and Kito tracked it with quiet eyes. Soft light shifted with the shadow and warmed the spot where Kito stood. They could still hear the rain on the steps, now softer than before. Mr. Ade finished arranging the market bags so they would be easy to lift again later. Kito felt the pleasant temperature of the room hold steady around them. Nothing hurried them. They simply stood together in the peaceful space beside the return desk while the afternoon grew quieter.

Kito walked a few steps closer to the window and felt the last cool air from the rainy steps fade behind them. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Kito watched the gentle light settle over the floorboards and the edge of the return desk. They breathed in the calm of the room and knew the market bags now rested safely without cutting into anyone’s wrist. Outside the rain had slowed to a whisper on the stone. Kito stayed near the warm glow and felt ready for the quiet walk home whenever Mr. Ade was ready too.
