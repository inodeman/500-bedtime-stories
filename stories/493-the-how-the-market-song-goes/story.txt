The warm smell of toasted sesame drifted from the iron pan as Zola's small hands already turned the wooden spoon in slow circles beside the cottage hearth. Soft orange firelight washed over Zola's face and made the floorboards shine in gentle patches while the clock on the mantel ticked in its unhurried way that never pressed anyone forward. Auntie June stood near enough for her calm breathing to mix with the quiet crackle of the wood, and Zola felt the steady heat rising against their bare wrists each time the spoon moved. Evening air cooled the window glass yet left the hearth stones pleasantly warm under Zola's knees as they kept the seeds from sticking. Zola watched the tiny seeds shift from pale to golden and listened only to the fire and the clock for those first peaceful minutes.

Auntie June smoothed the front of her cotton apron and sat on the low stool facing Zola, her voice gentle when she began. "Zola, let me tell you about the market song that has a silly middle you must not skip," she said, the words settling into the warm air like leaves. The fire gave a low steady murmur as a log settled deeper into the coals, and Zola kept their eyes on the pan while the last seeds made soft little pops. Auntie June leaned closer so the story stayed just between them under the unhurried clock. Zola already felt bigger news about Greta rising in their chest, yet they stayed quiet and watched the kind light move across Auntie June's face. The apron felt familiar and soft where it brushed the stool edge in the glow.

"It begins with the farmers calling out their bright fruits under the morning sky," Auntie June continued in the same even tone that matched the clock. Zola felt the smooth worn handle of the spoon rest easy in their palm and noticed how the hearth stones still sent gentle heat wrapping around their legs. The market voices in the story rose cheerful and full, filling an imaginary square with melody that seemed almost real in the cottage. Zola's news pressed forward again, a picture of Greta holding something yellow, but they folded it away and only stirred once more. Firelight softened every corner of the room into calm rounded shapes while Zola listened without rushing the next part.

Auntie June reached the middle and her eyes brightened as she said, "Now comes the silly part that goes like this: the turnips dance with the potatoes while the onions giggle at the moon, and you must never skip it or the whole song loses its joy." Zola almost spoke their bigger news right then, yet they pressed their lips closed and felt the cool smooth edge of the table under their free hand. Outside a gate latch clicked once softly in the distance and then fell quiet again. The single sound drifted through the open window just long enough for Zola to notice before the evening swallowed it. Auntie June moved straight through every silly word without missing one, and Zola stayed right beside her all the way. Warm air from the hearth brushed lightly across Zola's hair like a friendly hand.

"After the silly middle the song returns to the sellers packing their baskets and wishing everyone a peaceful walk home," Auntie June said, never hurrying past a single line. Zola watched the finished seeds rest quiet in the pan and felt the temperature ease as the iron cooled on its hook above the stones. The unhurried clock kept its steady pace overhead, matching the calm of each word Auntie June offered. Zola's bigger news waited patiently now, tucked small and safe inside. Fire shadows drifted slowly over the walls in the kind low light, and Zola breathed the remaining warmth of the room. They followed every last phrase as carefully as they had followed the first.

Auntie June spoke the final sentence of the market song and then rested her hands quietly in her lap. The hearth fire had settled into a steady orange glow that threw long soft shadows, and Zola felt leftover heat still comforting their toes inside soft slippers. For a short moment only the clock ticked on without any rush. Zola had heard the entire song from the first calling voices all the way through the dancing turnips and giggling onions to the last peaceful wish. The air near the hearth felt full of that finished melody rather than empty. Zola set the spoon down on the warm stone and turned completely toward Auntie June with both hands open.

Zola looked up and asked one real question in a clear soft voice. "Auntie June, why must no one skip the silly middle of the market song?" The question rested gently in the firelit space between them. Auntie June smiled and the light caught the edges of her hair like a quiet halo. Zola waited with hands still on their knees, feeling the slight weave of their sleeves against their skin. The cottage stayed peaceful around the unhurried clock while the answer came. "Because the silly middle is where the joy lives, Zola, and joy needs every note to stay whole," Auntie June replied in the same warm tone. Zola nodded once and felt the answer settle around them like an extra layer of comfort from the hearth.

Only then did Zola share something small of their own. "I saw Greta wave from her window today and she held up a yellow leaf she found," Zola said, the words quiet as the settling coals. They came after the full listening, simple and bright without needing to grow larger. Auntie June listened in return while the hearth stones kept giving their gentle leftover heat into the room. Zola felt glad inside that they had waited all the way to the last sentence before speaking. The firelight had grown lower and kinder, wrapping the whole cottage in evening calm. Zola's hands rested open and quiet now on their lap, no longer busy with stirring.

Later Zola helped set the chair square to the table so the room felt orderly and ready. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Auntie June and Zola sat a little longer in the last warmth of the cottage hearth while the unhurried clock marked the slow minutes. Zola's eyelids grew heavy under the kind light and the air held only the clean scent of wood and cooling stone. They leaned lightly against Auntie June's side and felt safe in the quiet that followed a song heard all the way through. No further words were needed as the evening deepened gently around them both.
