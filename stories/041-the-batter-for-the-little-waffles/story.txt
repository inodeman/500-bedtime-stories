Mama stood at the long wooden counter in the bakery kitchen, her hands already dusted white as she pressed the last soft mounds of dough under clean cloths so they could rest and rise. Warm air carried the sweet scent of berries simmering gently in a pot of jam, and the low oven hummed a steady golden heat that made the whole room feel snug and safe. Outside the high window the afternoon light fell in soft rectangles across the floorboards, and Bibi stepped inside on quiet socks, drawn by the familiar comfort of the place where everything waited in its own gentle way.

Bibi spotted the yellow jug right away, sitting on the cooler marble slab near the mixing bowls, its lid snug and the waffle batter inside looking thick and still. They leaned close enough to feel the cool stone under their palms and to see the faint steam that had fogged a small circle on the jug’s side. “Mama, may I peek at the batter now?” Bibi asked, their voice soft so it would not wake the dough. The kitchen smelled faintly of rain on dust from the open side door where the last of a passing shower had settled on the path, and Bibi’s fingers itched to lift the lid just a little.

Mama turned with a calm smile, wiping her hands on her apron, and the light from the hanging lamp caught the edges of her hair. “Not yet, Bibi,” she said. “The batter needs more quiet time before it is ready for the iron. We leave the dough and the jam to finish, and the batter too.” Bibi’s shoulders rose and fell once. They wanted so much to stir or taste or check, yet they kept their hands at their sides and nodded. The yellow jug stayed covered, its smooth surface cool against the marble.

Instead of lifting the lid, Bibi found a quiet task that fit the peaceful kitchen. They picked up a clean satin ribbon that had slipped from a stack of gift boxes waiting for the morning loaves, and the ribbon felt cool and slippery between their fingers as they wound it slowly around a wooden spool. Mama hummed a little tune while she stirred the jam one last time, and the spoon made a soft clink against the pot. Bibi breathed in the warm kitchen air and kept winding, letting the batter rest exactly as it was.

A gate latch clicked once from the courtyard beyond the bakery wall, a small clear sound that drifted in with the cooler breeze and then faded. Bibi looked toward the window where tiny beads of moisture still clung to the glass, then returned to the ribbon. They traced a slow circle through a patch of fog that had gathered on the lower pane, their dry finger gliding in a perfect round shape until the glass cleared in that spot. The yellow jug sat unchanged, patient and still, and Bibi felt the soft heat of the room settling around their shoulders like a blanket.

“Would you help me fold these napkins, Bibi?” Mama asked, her voice low and kind. Bibi carried the satin ribbon over and set it gently on the counter so it would not tangle, then took the square cloths one by one. The fabric felt smooth and slightly warm from sitting near the oven. Together they made neat triangles, stacking them in a quiet pile while the jam cooled and the dough continued its slow rise under the cloths. Bibi glanced at the yellow jug twice but left the lid alone, choosing instead to smooth each napkin edge with careful thumbs.

Time moved the way soft light moves across a floor. Bibi fetched two clean spoons from the drawer and laid them beside the waffle iron so everything would be ready later. “I can wait,” Bibi said quietly, almost to themselves, and Mama answered, “Yes, you can, and the batter will thank you by being just right.” The kitchen held the gentle ticking of the wall clock and the far-off drip of leftover rain from the eaves. Bibi rested their cheek against the cool edge of the counter for a moment, listening and smelling the sweet jam and the clean dough, feeling how good it was to stay close without hurrying.

At last Mama lifted the lid of the yellow jug and peered inside. The batter had settled into a perfect smooth thickness, ready for the iron. “Now, Bibi,” she said with a warm smile. Bibi climbed onto the sturdy stool and watched as Mama poured careful circles onto the hot plates. The first waffle came out golden and fragrant, and they shared the warm pieces at the little table by the window, the jam spooned on top in glossy red swirls. Outside the light had grown softer and bluer, and the bakery kitchen felt full of quiet pride that needed no rushing at all.

Bibi leaned against Mama’s side while the last crumbs disappeared and the yellow jug stood empty and clean on the counter once more. The dough under its cloths had risen high and proud, the jam had set into a lovely shine, and everything had finished in its own good time. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
