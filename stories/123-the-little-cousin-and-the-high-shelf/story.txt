Mr. Ade stood already at the library return desk, mid-task with a tall stack of picture books he had gathered from the rainy steps outside the entrance. The cool dampness from those steps made the book covers feel slightly cold and slippery as he tried to hold them all at once against his sweater. Soft golden light streamed through the tall windows and warmed the polished surface of the desk where his elbow rested. He stretched his free hand high toward the shelf just above, where one cheerful picture book sat out of reach, its cover showing smiling animals in a garden. Gita watched from a few steps away and noticed the careful way Mr. Ade balanced everything without dropping a single volume. The quiet library air carried a gentle hush broken only by distant page turns.

Gita moved closer across the soft carpet that cushioned each of their footsteps with a thick plush texture. They saw Mr. Ade try again to lift the stack high enough but the picture book remained just beyond his fingers. "I can take some of those books for you," Gita said in a calm voice. Mr. Ade looked down and his face brightened with a warm smile. The fresh scent of lemon soap rose from the recently cleaned return desk and filled the space around them like a bright clean note. Outside through the glass, the rainy steps glistened under the pale sky, and a fig tree stood nearby. Gita held out their arms ready to help with half the load without waiting for any special thanks.

Mr. Ade passed several books carefully into Gita's waiting hands, and the pages felt smooth and slightly cool from the earlier rain. "That is perfect, Gita. Now I can reach properly," he replied. Together they stood side by side at the desk while the indoor warmth wrapped around their shoulders after the chill near the doors. Gita sorted the books by size on the desk surface, lining them up in a neat row under the gentle lamp light that cast a cozy yellow glow. They could hear the soft wind in the fig leaves outside making a peaceful rustling sound that came through the cracked window. It reminded Gita of quiet evenings at home. Their fingers traced the colorful spines as they worked.

With the stack lightened, Mr. Ade climbed the small wooden stool beside the return desk and stretched up easily now. Gita lifted the special picture book from where it had been left too high earlier and passed it up to him with both hands steady. The cover felt glossy and smooth against their palms in the warm room air. "Here you go," Gita said quietly. Mr. Ade slid the book into its proper place on the high shelf with a soft satisfying sound of paper against wood. He climbed down again and they both looked at the neat row of stories now complete. A floured towel rested on the far end of the desk, and Gita brushed it lightly by chance, feeling its dry powdery softness.

Gita and Mr. Ade turned next to the remaining books still damp from the rainy steps. They carried them inside one by one, feeling the cooler temperature near the open doorway each time they approached it. The light rain had left shiny puddles that reflected the library windows like little mirrors. Mr. Ade took a dry cloth and Gita helped by holding the books open slightly so air could reach the pages. "We make a good team with this," Mr. Ade said with a gentle chuckle. Gita nodded and kept their focus on the simple useful work. The library smelled of old paper and fresh rain, mixing into something familiar and nice. Soft shadows stretched across the floor as clouds moved outside.

After placing the last of the lower books onto open shelves nearby, Gita noticed a few water spots on the return desk itself. They picked up a clean cloth and began to wipe the table in slow circles, moving the fabric carefully over the wood. The surface felt cool and even under their slow strokes, and the motion felt peaceful in the quiet room. Afternoon light slanted lower now and made dust motes dance slowly in the air like tiny stars. Tamsin, who had been reading in a big armchair, looked over and smiled at the calm scene without interrupting. Gita continued the slow circles until every damp mark disappeared and the desk shone softly again under their hands.

Mr. Ade finished shelving a final adventure story and came to stand beside Gita at the clean desk. The rainy steps outside were emptier now as the light rain eased into a soft drip from the eaves. Gita felt the comfortable warmth of the library settle deep into their sweater sleeves. They heard the distant soft chime of the library's old clock marking the hour. "Shall we put everything back in order before closing time comes closer?" Mr. Ade asked. Gita agreed with a small nod and helped slide the step stool underneath the desk where it belonged, the legs making a muted sound on the floor. Everything felt settled and right after their shared effort.

As more quiet filled the library corners, Gita walked over to the window seat near the return area and rested for a short while on the cushioned bench. The fabric of the seat felt worn and friendly under them. Outside the fig tree still stood guard by the rainy steps, its leaves quieter now. Mr. Ade tidied a few last papers on the desk with unhurried motions. Gita watched the way the light changed as evening approached slowly. They thought of how simple it had been to notice and take half the job so things could go smoothly for everyone. The whole space seemed to breathe calmly around them both.

In the growing soft dusk of the library, Gita stood up again and prepared to leave with a peaceful feeling inside. Mr. Ade waved from behind the return desk where the picture book now rested safely high up on its shelf. The air had a clean freshness still, and the temperature felt just right for winding down. Gita took one last look at the neat shelves and the clean wood. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Its gentle light made a warm pool on the sill and reflected a little on the glass beside the rainy steps view. Gita smiled once more and walked toward the door with slow easy steps, ready for home and rest.
