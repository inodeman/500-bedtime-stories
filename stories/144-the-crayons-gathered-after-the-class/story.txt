A tiny click sounded from somewhere near the music stands, soft as a pebble tapping wood, and Opal paused in the quiet school hallway to listen. Warm afternoon light spilled through the tall windows and laid long gold bars across the floorboards, making the wooden pegs along the wall shine like polished honey. Opal felt the mild air against their cheeks and noticed how the stools stood in a neat crooked row beside the empty music stands. The hallway smelled faintly of chalk dust and clean soap, and Opal’s sneakers made almost no noise as they stepped closer to the soft click. They wondered what small thing had made that gentle sound and looked around with calm curiosity.

Opal saw bright crayons scattered like short colorful sticks across the floor near one of the stools, red and blue and sunny yellow lying every which way. Neighbor Sami knelt among them, reaching with both hands yet still missing half the pile because the crayons kept rolling under the legs of the music stands. The air felt cooler down low where Sami worked, and Opal caught the faint distant smell of chamomile drifting from an open door farther along the hall. Opal watched Sami stretch and gather three crayons only for two more to tumble free again. The pegs above held empty coats and a single soft scarf, and the whole scene felt peaceful even while the crayons stayed awkward for one person alone.

“Those crayons look slippery today,” Opal said in a gentle voice that matched the hush of the hallway. Neighbor Sami glanced up and offered a small smile while the light from the windows painted soft stripes across their sweater. Opal crouched without hurry and felt the smooth cool floor under their palms. A tin clock ticked once from a high shelf above the pegs, a clear bright sound that settled into the quiet like a friendly reminder of slow time. Opal began picking up the nearest crayons, the wax sticks warm from the sunbeams, and set them carefully into the open box that waited beside Sami’s knee.

“I can take this side if you like,” Opal said, already scooping a handful of green and purple sticks before any answer came. Neighbor Sami nodded and answered, “That would feel so much easier.” Together they worked in easy silence for a while, the crayons making tiny soft taps as they dropped into the box. Opal noticed the texture of a tin lid resting on the nearest stool, cool and faintly ridged under their fingers when they steadied themselves. The lid had been used to cover extra art supplies earlier, and now it served as a calm resting place while the two of them cleared the bright sticks from under the music stands. Warm light touched Opal’s hair and made the scattered colors glow like little garden paths on the wood.

Opal slid one music stand a few inches aside so the last orange crayon could roll free into their open hand. The stand felt sturdy and cool along its metal edges, and the hallway stayed still except for the quiet scrape of wood on wood. Neighbor Sami collected the blues and browns while Opal handled the reds and yellows, splitting the job right down the middle without any fuss. “You found the tricky ones under there,” Sami said softly, voice warm as the afternoon air. Opal simply smiled and kept placing crayons into neat rows inside the box so none would roll again. The pegs cast thin shadows that stretched and shortened as a cloud drifted past the window, and Opal felt content just sharing the simple work.

When the floor finally looked clear, Opal sat for a moment on a low stool and ran one finger along the smooth seat. The wood held a gentle leftover warmth from the sun, and Opal breathed in the calm of the empty hallway. Neighbor Sami closed the crayon box with a soft click and rested it on another stool. “Shall we set the music stands straight again?” Sami asked. Opal stood and answered, “Yes, let’s make them tidy too.” Side by side they eased each stand back into a gentle curve against the wall, the metal legs whispering faintly on the floorboards. Opal liked the way the light now fell evenly across the clean space where the bright sticks had been.

Opal noticed a small heap of clean cloths near the pegs, left from some earlier class, and quietly matched the corners of two soft squares so they lay flat and even. The cloth felt fleecy and kind under their hands, and the simple motion fitted the peaceful mood of the hallway. Neighbor Sami watched with quiet thanks already plain in their eyes and said nothing more because nothing more was needed. Opal hung one matched cloth on an empty peg where it draped like a pale curtain. The stools now stood ready for tomorrow, and the music stands gleamed softly in the lowering light. A comfortable hush filled the space between the walls, broken only by the distant muffled sounds of someone sweeping far away.

Opal and Neighbor Sami walked a few steps toward the window end of the hallway, their footsteps light on the warm boards. Outside the glass the sky had turned the color of soft peaches, and inside the air held a mild evening coolness that brushed Opal’s arms. They paused together and looked back at the neat row of pegs, the quiet stools, and the upright music stands. The crayon box sat closed and safe on its stool, no bright sticks left behind. Opal felt the satisfaction of shared work settle around them like a light blanket. Neighbor Sami gave one last grateful nod, and Opal returned it with the same easy calm.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Opal rested one hand on the cool sill and watched the gentle light pool across the floor where the crayons had once lain. Neighbor Sami stood nearby in the quiet glow, and the whole hallway felt settled and kind. Opal breathed the mild air one more time, then turned toward home with slow happy steps, the memory of bright sticks and shared hands staying warm inside them like a small steady lantern of their own.
