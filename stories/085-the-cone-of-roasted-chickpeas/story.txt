The baker stood already at the edge of the picnic cloth in the calm meadow, twisting fresh sheets into tall paper cones and tucking warm roasted chickpeas inside each one while the late sun lay golden and gentle across the grass. Eko stepped nearer on quiet feet and watched the careful work, feeling the mild heat of the day settle on their bare arms like a soft blanket. Tiny seeds of light danced through the leaves overhead and speckled the cloth with moving dots. Eko breathed in the meadow air and stayed close enough to see every fold the baker made. The cloth itself felt smooth under Eko’s toes when they shifted weight, and a slow bee hummed past without hurry. Eko’s shoulders dropped as they rested both palms on their knees for a moment, simply watching.

Eko reached out when the baker held one finished cone toward the cloth and caught the dry, clean smell of fresh paper rising from its crisp sides. “Thank you for bringing these,” Eko said softly, turning the cone in both hands so the roasted chickpeas made a tiny dry rattle. The paper felt cool and new against Eko’s fingertips while the meadow grass whispered around the edges of the cloth. Una appeared then, walking from the far side of the open space with a bright face, and Eko smiled at once. The cone sat upright between them all, filled nearly to the top with the golden chickpeas that still held a little oven warmth. Eko kept the cone steady and looked from the baker to Una, noticing how the paper edges caught the light like tiny sails.

A sudden clear click of a gate latch sounded from the low fence that bordered the meadow, and Eko turned their head toward the noise before looking back at the cone. Una settled onto the cloth opposite Eko and smoothed a corner of the fabric with one hand. “The chickpeas smell wonderful,” Una said, leaning forward so the soft breeze lifted a strand of hair. Eko nodded and held the cone a little higher so both could see the even layer of roasted beans inside. The baker continued packing two more cones without speaking, the sound of paper folding mixing with distant birdsong. Eko felt the solid ground under the cloth and kept their gaze on the single cone that now seemed large between the three of them.

Eko shifted sideways until one hand brushed the low border wall that ran beside the picnic cloth, finding its surface cool and grainy like a damp clay wall still holding last night’s dew. The texture grounded Eko while they considered the full cone resting in their lap. “It will not feel fair if only one person is served first,” Eko said, voice calm and clear in the open air. Una tilted their head and waited, and the baker paused with a half-folded sheet. Sunlight warmed the top of Eko’s hair as they set the cone down carefully on the cloth. The clay wall stayed cool under Eko’s palm for another moment before they drew their hand back and rested both palms on their knees again.

Eko picked up the cone once more and began to tip small equal handfuls of the roasted chickpeas onto three clean corners of the picnic cloth, making tidy little piles that matched one another in size. “We can each have the same amount this way,” Eko said, glancing first at Una and then at the baker. The chickpeas made soft clicking sounds as they landed, and a light scent of roasted earth rose from them into the mild afternoon. Una watched the careful sorting with quiet eyes while a dragonfly skimmed the nearby grass tips. The baker simply nodded once and kept silent, allowing the new arrangement to remain exactly as Eko had shaped it. Eko felt the cloth’s weave under their shins and finished the last even share with steady fingers.

Una reached for the nearest small pile and cupped it gently so the chickpeas rested warm against their skin. “These are just right,” Una said, tasting one and smiling at the gentle crunch. Eko took a chickpea from their own share and let the quiet flavor settle on their tongue while the meadow held its peaceful stillness. Far off, leaves rustled in a slow rhythm that matched Eko’s breathing. The baker finished the last empty cone and set it aside, never adding a word about the choice Eko had made. Eko looked at the three equal piles and felt the afternoon light grow softer across their shoulders, turning the whole cloth the color of pale honey.

Eko and Una sat cross-legged facing each other, taking turns with one chickpea at a time so the little piles lasted longer in the calm air. The grass beyond the cloth smelled green and sweet whenever a breeze stirred it, and Eko listened to the faint rustle with contented ears. The baker moved a basket closer and then simply sat nearby, hands resting open on their own knees. Eko noticed how the shadows of the tall flowers had grown longer and cooler along the cloth edges. Another quiet moment passed while everyone chewed slowly, and Eko felt the solid comfort of the earth holding them all in place.

As the light shifted toward evening, Eko brushed a few stray crumbs from the cloth and watched Una do the same with careful strokes. The temperature of the air had cooled just enough that Eko’s arms felt pleasantly fresh. “We can fold the empty paper later,” Eko said, gathering the used cone into a neat roll. The baker gave a small smile of agreement and rose to stretch without speaking. Eko stood too, feeling the soft give of the grass underfoot once more, and helped Una shake the picnic cloth free of every last seed. Together they folded the cloth into a smooth square while the meadow grew quieter around them.

Eko carried the folded cloth toward the little house that stood beyond the gate, walking slowly so the last warmth of the day stayed with them a little longer. Una kept pace beside Eko, and the baker followed with the remaining cones. Inside the open doorway the rooms already held a gentle dimness that felt restful after the bright meadow. Eko stepped across the threshold and looked toward the familiar sill where evening had begun. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Eko set the cloth down, rested both palms on their knees one final time, and let the quiet house settle around them like a kind blanket.
