The evening air touched the harbor step as softly as a folded blanket, and the water beside it shone in pale gold. Una stood on the smooth stones and felt the mild warmth of the day still held in the rock under their shoes. Gulls called far out over the quiet boats, and the light slid along the ropes in long gentle stripes. Una breathed in the calm of the place and watched the last of the sun rest on the posts. Papa moved among the stacked parcels with careful hands, and Una stayed close so they could see every small job. The air cooled just enough to make Una pull their sweater sleeves over their wrists.

On the harbor step where parcels changed hands each evening, wooden crates sat in tidy rows waiting for their next keepers. Una ran a finger along the edge of one box and felt how cool the wood had become. A cat walked past with a tiny collar bell that rang once and then fell silent again. Una smiled at the soft sound and looked toward Papa, who was balancing a tray that already looked a little unsteady. The teacups chimed against one another whenever the tray tipped even a little. Papa’s shoulders rose with the effort of holding everything level while he tried to pass a parcel with his free hand. Una watched the bright cups catch the fading light and decided the job was too much for one person alone.

Papa set one foot on the higher part of the step and tried again to keep the tray steady. The teacups chimed a thin bright music that made Una’s ears prick with attention. “These cups want to dance more than they want to rest,” Papa said, and his voice stayed kind even though the tray wobbled. Una stepped nearer and smelled tomato vines growing in a row of pots someone had placed along the wall for the summer. The green scent mixed with the salt air in a friendly way. Una saw that Papa needed one hand for the parcels and one hand for the tray, and the two needs did not fit together. Una’s own hands felt ready and quiet.

Without waiting for anyone to ask, Una reached out and took the lighter end of the tray so that the weight settled evenly between them. The teacups chimed once more and then grew still. “I can hold this half,” Una said, and their voice stayed soft so it would not startle the cups. Papa’s face eased into a smile that showed he understood at once. The grain of the bench beside them felt smooth under Una’s free palm when they steadied themselves for a moment. Together they moved the tray toward the flat place where the parcels waited to be claimed. The light turned the cups the color of milk and butter.

Rafi appeared from the path above the step carrying a small cloth bag and waved with quiet cheer. “I brought the spoons,” Rafi said, and set the bag down carefully so nothing would clink. Una kept both hands on their half of the tray and felt the cool porcelain edges against their fingers. Papa passed the last two parcels to a waiting boat hand who nodded thanks and stepped away. The water made a low lapping sound against the stones, slower than before. Una and Papa lowered the tray together onto the bench, and the teacups rested without another chime. The air held a gentle coolness that made everything feel finished and peaceful.

Una sat on the bench for a short rest and pressed their hand again to the wood so they could notice its quiet texture. Papa sat beside them and rested one hand lightly on Una’s shoulder. “You saw what was needed,” Papa said, and nothing more was required. Rafi opened the cloth bag and laid the spoons in a neat row beside the cups. Una listened past the near splash of water and tried instead to hear the far clock that lived in the town square; its soft distant mark of the hour came faintly across the roofs. The tomato vines nearby released one last green breath into the cooling air. Everything around the harbor step seemed to settle into a slower rhythm.

Papa poured a little warm tea into three of the cups so they could share a quiet sip before the walk home. The steam rose in thin silver curls that smelled mild and sweet. Una held their cup with both hands and felt the pleasant heat travel into their palms. “The tray is much happier now,” Una said, and Rafi laughed a small quiet laugh that did not disturb the evening. The boats along the dock rocked so gently that their ropes made almost no sound. Una watched the last light leave the water and turn the surface the soft color of a dove’s wing. Papa gathered the empty parcels tags and tucked them into his pocket with care.

They rose together when the tea was gone and carried the tray of quiet cups up the short path toward home. Una walked between Papa and Rafi and matched their slower steps to the easy pace of the evening. The stones underfoot still held a little daytime warmth, and Una enjoyed the way it pressed through the soles of their shoes. A single moth drifted past a lamppost and vanished into the darker blue above. Una kept one hand lightly on the edge of the tray even though it no longer needed help, just to stay close to the work they had shared. The harbor fell behind them in a hush of water and wood.

At home the rooms waited in kind low light, and Una helped set the cups on the kitchen shelf where they belonged. Papa opened the window a little so the night air could drift in, cool and clean. Una washed their hands in water that felt soft against their skin and dried them on the familiar towel. They sat on the low bed while Papa drew the curtain halfway. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Una closed their eyes and heard only the quiet house and the memory of the far clock, and sleep came as gently as the evening air had touched the harbor step.
