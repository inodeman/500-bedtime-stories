"Noemi, come sit with me at the library table by the round window," Miss Dalca said in her quiet voice. Noemi settled onto the chair and felt the cool smooth wood press gently against their elbows while soft afternoon light spilled through the round glass and made pale circles on the open picture books. An extra biscuit that had not been offered rested alone on a little blue plate near the stack of fairy-tale volumes, its golden top still faintly warm from the kitchen. Noemi's eyes lingered on it as dust motes drifted slowly in the bright beam. The air smelled clean and still, and Noemi rested their chin on one hand, listening to the faint rustle of pages somewhere deeper in the room. Miss Dalca smiled across the table and slid a fresh story closer so Noemi could see the painted foxes on the cover.

Noemi reached toward the plate almost without thinking, then pulled their hand back as a tiny shiver of cool air from the open window brushed their wrist. The extra biscuit that had not been offered seemed to wait there, plain and round, and Noemi felt a small tangle of wanting inside their chest. They glanced toward the shelves where tall books stood like quiet friends, then down at their own lap. For one heartbeat Noemi thought of sliding the biscuit into their pocket where no one would notice. The wood of the chair felt solid and steady under them, holding them still while the light shifted a little on the tabletop. Noemi breathed out slowly and kept both hands folded instead, watching how the biscuit's edges caught the glow.

Outside the round window a soft breeze moved, and Noemi heard the wind in the fig leaves whispering like a faraway lullaby. The sound made the room feel even calmer, as if the whole library were rocking gently. Noemi looked again at the extra biscuit that had not been offered and remembered how Miss Dalca always shared snacks with careful fairness so every child felt included. Their fingers brushed the edge of a book cover that felt slightly rough from many happy readings. Noemi decided the biscuit belonged to the shared plate and not to a hidden pocket. Warmth from the sunlight on their shoulder helped the tangled feeling loosen like a knot coming undone.

Just then Sora appeared at the end of the table carrying two crayons and a wide sheet of paper that smelled faintly of lemon soap from the sink where they had washed their hands. "May I draw the fig tree with you, Noemi?" Sora asked in a hushed library voice. Noemi nodded and scooted sideways so Sora could settle close, the paper whispering as it spread flat. The extra biscuit that had not been offered still sat untouched, and Noemi felt glad Sora was there because sharing the quiet made everything simpler. Light from the round window fell across both their hands while they sketched green leaves together. The crayons left soft waxy trails that smelled a little sweet, and Noemi's shoulders dropped lower with each calm stroke.

After a few quiet minutes Noemi set the crayon down and turned toward Miss Dalca, who was arranging bookmarks in a neat fan. "Miss Dalca, there is an extra biscuit that had not been offered on the plate, and I almost hid it but I did not," Noemi said clearly. Miss Dalca looked up with kind eyes that held no surprise, only gentle attention. The air near the table felt pleasantly warm now, as if the whole room approved. Noemi waited, hands resting open on the wood, while Sora kept coloring a single leaf so the moment stayed peaceful. Miss Dalca reached across and rested her palm lightly near Noemi's for a second of quiet thanks.

"Thank you for telling me the plain truth, Noemi," Miss Dalca answered softly. "We can mend this together with no fuss at all." She lifted a floured towel from the basket under the table, its soft dusty texture brushing Noemi's fingers as they helped unfold it. Together they draped the towel over the little plate so the extra biscuit that had not been offered stayed fresh for the next snack time when it could be offered properly to everyone. Noemi felt the fine flour dust tickle their skin like a friendly powder. Sora smiled without a single teasing word and kept drawing while the three of them sat in easy silence. The round window framed the soft green outside like a picture made just for them.

Noemi picked up a clean cloth and began to wipe the table in slow circles, clearing a few stray crumbs that had fallen near the books. The cloth felt damp and cool against the wood, leaving faint shiny paths that caught the light. Miss Dalca hummed a tiny tune under her breath while she set out tomorrow's story baskets. "You chose well, Noemi," she said once more, her voice low as a turning page. Noemi kept wiping in wider and wider circles until the whole surface gleamed. The temperature in the room stayed perfectly mild, neither hot nor chilly, wrapping around them like a favorite blanket. Sora added a yellow sun to the drawing and held it up for Noemi to admire.

Later the three of them shared a single cup of warm apple water that steamed gently in the cooling air, the liquid smooth against Noemi's tongue. Noemi told Sora how the biscuit had tempted them for only a moment before the truth felt better, and Sora simply nodded as if that were the most natural thing. Miss Dalca placed the covered plate on a high shelf where it would wait safely. Outside the fig leaves kept their soft conversation while inside the library the last of the daylight turned rose-gold across the spines of the books. Noemi leaned back in the chair and felt every muscle rest, proud of the plain words they had spoken and the quiet fixing that followed. The wooden table now smelled faintly of clean cloth and old paper, a scent that meant home inside these walls.

When the sky beyond the round window deepened to soft blue, Miss Dalca lit the small lantern that lived on the sill. Noemi and Sora packed away the crayons and the drawing of the fig tree, their movements slow and sleepy. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Noemi rested their head on folded arms for a final peaceful minute, listening to the hush of the library and feeling the last warmth of the day fade kindly around them as Miss Dalca closed the story baskets with careful hands.
