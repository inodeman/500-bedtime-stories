"Miss Dalca, I brought a little tune I want to share," Kito said in a quiet voice at the library table by the round window, where afternoon light lay warm and golden across the smooth wood.

Kito rested both hands on the open notebook and felt the cool air drifting from the glass while the smell of toasted sesame drifted faintly from a snack basket near the door. Miss Dalca, wearing a soft cotton apron over her cardigan, pulled out the chair opposite and smiled without hurry. Kito almost slid the notebook under a stack of picture books, heart thumping, because the melody had first floated through the wall from next door the evening before, and saying it was brand new no longer felt right. Golda sat two seats away humming under her breath and turning pages, giving Kito gentle space. Outside a gate latch clicked shut with a tidy metal sound that made the quiet room feel even safer. Kito straightened the pencil, listened to the soft shuffle of feet on the carpet, and decided the plain truth would sit better than any hiding.

Miss Dalca waited, her calm eyes reflecting the round window’s pale glow, while Kito’s fingers traced the pencil lines that climbed and dipped like tiny hills. “I heard this tune next door last night,” Kito told her, voice steady though a little soft. “I liked it so much I wrote it down and said I made it, but that was not true.” The air stayed warm around their shoulders, and no one laughed or frowned. Miss Dalca nodded once, the fabric of her apron brushing the table edge with a dry whisper of cloth. Golda looked up only long enough to offer a small friendly nod before returning to her own book. Kito felt the tightness in their chest loosen like a knot coming free in good light.

“Thank you for telling me straight,” Miss Dalca said. “We can mend what needs mending together.” She reached for a clean sheet of staff paper that smelled faintly of fresh ink and laid it between them so the round window light fell squarely across the lines. Kito watched the pencil tip make new marks while their own notebook stayed open and honest beside it. The two of them hummed the first few notes very low, testing how the heard melody could sit beside a brand-new ending Kito invented on the spot. Outside the window a leaf brushed the glass with a dry rustle, and inside the library the soft tick of the wall clock kept gentle time. Kito set the chair square to the table so their elbows rested evenly and the work felt steady.

They practiced the joined tune once, twice, three times, letting each note land clear and kind. Kito’s voice stayed light as they sang the part that had come from next door and then the fresh part that belonged only to them. Miss Dalca tapped the table gently with one fingertip to keep the beat, and the sound was no louder than a moth’s wing. Warm light from the round window slid across Kito’s hands and made the pencil lead shine. Golda closed her book with a quiet thump and listened without interrupting. Kito felt the wood of the table smooth under their palms and knew the tangled feeling had almost left.

“Shall we write a tiny thank-you note to the neighbor who played it first?” Miss Dalca asked. Kito nodded and dipped the pencil again, shaping careful letters that said the tune had been beautiful and that a new ending now kept it company. The paper felt slightly rough under the moving lead, a pleasant scrape that matched the honest work. Miss Dalca folded the note once and set it aside for later, her apron pocket holding a spare eraser that smelled clean of rubber. Kito watched dust motes drift in the window light and felt pride settle soft and ordinary inside them. No one teased, and the room stayed kind.

They played the finished melody one last time on the quiet library xylophone that lived on a low shelf, the bars cool and smooth under Kito’s mallets. Each note rang clear and gentle, never sharp, filling the corner by the round window with soft silver sound. Miss Dalca kept time with a slow nod, and Golda smiled without a word. Kito’s shoulders dropped as the last note faded into the warm air. The whole table felt settled now, notebook closed with the truth inside it and the mended tune written cleanly beside the thank-you note.

Kito stacked the papers neatly and slid them into a folder whose cloth cover felt soft against their wrists. Miss Dalca stood, smoothed her cotton apron once more, and thanked Kito for the careful work. Outside the light had turned the color of honey, and inside the library lamps began to glow one by one. Golda waved a quiet good-bye and headed toward the picture-book corner. Kito breathed in the last of the sesame scent still lingering near the door and felt the day fold itself into evening like a well-read page.

Kito lingered a moment longer at the round window, watching the sky deepen while the table behind them stayed tidy and true. Miss Dalca dimmed the overhead lights so only the gentlest glow remained. The library grew hushed except for the distant hush of turning pages far down the aisle. Kito felt the cool evening air kiss their cheeks through a cracked pane and knew sleep would come easily tonight. Everything that needed mending had been mended with plain words and shared pencils, and the tune now belonged to kindness as much as to melody.

Kito walked slowly toward the door, notebook hugged close, while Miss Dalca locked the side cabinet with a soft click. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
