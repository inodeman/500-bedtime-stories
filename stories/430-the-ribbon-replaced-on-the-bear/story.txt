The fresh ribbon on the bear lay smooth and neat across its soft chest, catching the low lamp light in a gentle rose sheen that made Ximena bend near enough to feel the cool satin against their fingertip. Tiny even stitches kept it snug where the old ribbon had slipped away, and the bear sat upright on the wide wooden bench as if it had never known a missing bow. Warmth from the late day still clung to the air near the open side door, and Ximena breathed it in while studying every careful loop Papa had made. The tools hung quiet on their low peg, handles turned the same way, and the whole room felt settled and kind.

Ximena stood in the middle of the workshop floor and let their eyes travel from the mended bear to the peg board and back again. Papa had already finished the small repair before Ximena even asked, and the sight of that tidy ribbon filled their chest with a quiet gladness that needed more than two quick words. The grain of the bench showed under the bear’s paws in fine pale lines that Ximena traced once with one careful finger. A soft draft carried the green smell of tomato vines from the pots just outside the door, mixing with the clean scent of wood shavings. Ximena smiled at the bear and decided a plain thank-you would feel too thin for such thoughtful work.

Papa was sorting a few spare buttons into a shallow tin at the far end of the bench, the metal lids making tiny clicks as he set them down. Ximena watched him for a moment, then turned toward the low peg where the hammers and awls rested. One small brush had slipped from its place and lay on the floor; Ximena picked it up, felt the smooth handle, and hung it back beside the others so every tool lined up straight again. The lantern light stretched long mild shadows across the floorboards, and the room stayed pleasantly cool after the warmer day. Ximena moved slowly so the quiet would not break.

“Papa, the brush is home again,” Ximena said in a clear soft voice. Papa looked up and gave a small nod that reached his eyes. “I see that, Ximena. Thank you for catching it,” he answered. Ximena felt the gladness grow a little bigger and kept looking for one more helpful thing that no one had requested. They noticed a short length of leftover ribbon coiled near the scissors and wound it neatly onto a wooden spool, tucking the spool into the open drawer where the other trims lived. The drawer slid shut with a gentle wooden sigh.

Outside the door a cat’s collar bell gave one light faraway chime as the animal wandered past the tomato pots, and Ximena paused to listen to that single clear note before returning to the bench. The sound drifted away and left the workshop even calmer. Ximena smoothed the bear’s new ribbon once more, feeling how secure the bow sat, then wiped a light dusting of wood dust from the bench edge with a clean cloth. Papa had not asked for the wiping or the spool or the brush, yet each small act felt right and steady. The lamp glow stayed steady and golden on the walls.

“I noticed you fixed the bear’s ribbon already,” Ximena said plainly while standing close to Papa. Papa set the button tin aside and rested one hand on the bench. “I did it this morning while the light was good,” he replied. Ximena nodded and kept their voice even. “It looks just right on him now. I wanted you to know I saw.” Papa’s smile was quiet and warm. “That means a lot, Ximena,” he said. The four short sentences settled between them like soft folded cloth.

Orla, who had been reading on the low stool near the door, looked up with a sleepy grin and offered no more than a friendly wave before returning to her page. Ximena waved back once and then stayed near Papa, content that the useful tasks were finished. The air near the bench held a mild coolness that felt pleasant against bare arms, and the last of the day’s brightness slipped lower across the windowsill. Ximena rested both hands on the edge of the bench and simply stood for a slow breath, letting the finished work rest in the quiet. Nothing hurried them, and the bear sat patiently in its place of honor.

Papa closed the button tin with a soft final click and lifted the bear so Ximena could give its ears one last gentle stroke. The fur felt thick and familiar under their palm. Together they set the bear on a higher shelf where it could watch the room without being in the way of tomorrow’s projects. Ximena took one more look at the straight row of tools on the low peg and felt the same calm settle deeper. The workshop smelled faintly of clean wood and the green plants outside, and every corner seemed ready for rest.

Ximena and Papa stepped toward the doorway side by side while Orla closed her book and followed. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Its mild circle of light touched the bear’s new ribbon and the neat tools alike, and the whole room stayed warm enough for slow good-nights. Ximena leaned once against Papa’s side, breathed the quiet air, and felt the day close gently around the three of them like a well-mended blanket.
