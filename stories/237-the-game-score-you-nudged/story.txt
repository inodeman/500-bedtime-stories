The sweet smell of honey drifted from the open jar as Femi’s hands already sorted colored pencils inside the shared tin on the kitchen table, where a sticky spoon of jam sat beside a butter dish and a crumpled score sheet from the afternoon board game. Warm yellow light from the overhead lamp made the jam sparkle like tiny rubies while Femi’s fingers brushed the cool metal rim of the tin and felt the faint grit of eraser crumbs left from earlier drawings. Outside, a soft evening hush settled over the yard, and Femi kept their eyes on the paper where one little mark had been nudged higher than it should have been, a single extra point that did not truly belong. The butter looked smooth and pale next to the jam, and Femi’s thumbs pressed lightly against the pencil tin’s lid as if the small pressure could quiet the flutter in their chest.

Femi smoothed the score sheet flat with careful palms and felt the dry paper rasp under their skin while the kitchen air stayed pleasantly warm from the oven that had finished baking bread. The nudged mark stood out like a tiny dark comma that Femi had pushed a little farther with the tip of a blue pencil when Noemi had looked away for just a moment during their friendly game. Noemi had gone home already, leaving only the memory of laughter and the shared tin of pencils that still smelled faintly of cedar. Femi’s shoulders felt the gentle draft from the window as they stared at that one mark and wondered how easy it would be to rub it away before anyone else noticed. The jam jar’s lid clicked softly when Femi set it farther aside so nothing would stain the paper.

Almost without deciding, Femi reached for an eraser and held it ready above the score, feeling the soft rubbery give between their fingers while the room’s light glowed steadily on the butter dish. The idea of hiding the nudged mark fluttered through Femi’s mind like a quick moth, and for one long breath they nearly pressed the eraser down. Then Femi’s free hand brushed the pencil tin again and heard the faint rattle of crayons inside, a small sound that somehow made the almost-secret feel heavier. Femi set the eraser down unused and rested both hands flat on the cool tabletop, watching a thin bead of jam catch the light. The kitchen stayed quiet except for the low hum of the refrigerator, and Femi’s bare feet felt the smooth floorboards under the chair.

Ms. Okonkwo stepped into the kitchen doorway with a gentle smile, and Femi smelled the clean soap on her sleeves mixed with the lingering honey from the jar. “Femi, you look thoughtful tonight,” Ms. Okonkwo said in a voice as calm as warm milk. Femi looked up and felt the chalky hand of their own left palm, still dusted from the earlier hopscotch squares they had drawn on the patio stones before coming inside. That dry, powdery texture reminded Femi of marks that could be changed and marks that should stay true. Femi took a slow breath of the honey-sweet air and decided the plain truth belonged on the table right beside the jam and butter.

“I nudged the game score by one mark when Noemi and I played,” Femi said, voice steady though soft. “I almost hid it with the eraser, but I want to tell you the plain truth instead.” Ms. Okonkwo pulled out the chair across from Femi and sat so the wooden legs made a quiet scrape against the floor. The overhead light cast a soft circle over both of them while the pencil tin sat between the jam and the butter like a tiny silver boat. Femi’s fingers traced the edge of the score sheet and felt its slightly rough surface once more.

“Thank you for telling me straightaway, Femi,” Ms. Okonkwo answered, and her words carried no teasing at all, only a quiet warmth that matched the temperature of the bread still resting on the counter. Together they leaned over the paper, and Ms. Okonkwo handed Femi a clean pencil from the shared tin so the extra mark could be gently corrected with a light line and a careful note beside it. Femi wrote the true total in neat numbers while the butter dish reflected a small gleam of light and the jam jar stayed still. Outside the open window a gate latch clicked once with a clear metallic note, then fell silent again as if the evening itself approved of the mended page.

Femi and Ms. Okonkwo waited through a single soft chime from the old clock on the wall, letting the sound fade completely before they spoke again, and in that pause the kitchen felt cooler near the window yet cozy near the lamp. “We fixed what we could fix,” Ms. Okonkwo said, “and the game still counts as fun because you chose to set it right.” Femi nodded and felt the smooth wood of the table under both elbows while they tucked the corrected score sheet safely into the pencil tin lid. The jam spoon made a tiny clink when Femi moved it, and the butter stayed cool and firm in its dish. No scolding came, only the shared quiet of two people making something better than it had been a moment before.

Later Femi washed their chalky hand under the warm faucet until the powdery feel rinsed clean, listening to the water patter into the sink like gentle rain on leaves. Ms. Okonkwo spread a thin ribbon of honey across a slice of bread for each of them, and the sweet golden scent rose once more while Femi chewed slowly and felt the soft crumb against their tongue. The score sheet rested inside the closed tin now, honest and settled, and Femi’s shoulders dropped into an easy looseness. Through the window the last blue light of dusk touched the jam jar’s glass, and Femi smiled at how simple the mending had felt once the words were spoken aloud.

Femi carried the empty plates to the counter with careful steps, hearing only the soft pad of bare feet on the floor, then returned to the table where the pencil tin, the jam, and the butter still waited in friendly stillness. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Femi rested both hands on the warm wood one last time, breathing the quiet kitchen air, and let sleepiness settle like a light blanket while Ms. Okonkwo dimmed the overhead lamp so only the lantern’s calm circle remained.
