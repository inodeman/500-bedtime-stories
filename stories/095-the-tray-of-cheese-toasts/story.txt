"Would you like the soft corner of the picnic cloth nearest the tall grass, Ines?" asked the baker in a gentle voice that floated on the warm afternoon air. Ines stepped onto the cloth and felt the mild heat of the sun resting on their shoulders like a light blanket. The meadow stretched out in quiet greens, and far off a few white clouds drifted without hurry. Ines sat down carefully, smoothing a wrinkle in the cloth with one hand while the baker set a woven basket nearby. The air held a mild sweetness, and Ines listened for a moment to the distant hush of the open space. Jorie arrived a moment later and settled opposite, smiling at the calm scene. Ines looked around at the gentle light that touched every blade and felt glad to be exactly there.

The baker lifted a wooden tray from the basket and placed it in the middle of the cloth where everyone could see. On the tray rested golden cheese toasts cut cleanly on the diagonal, their edges crisp and their tops faintly glossy. Ines leaned closer and noticed how the triangles caught the sunlight and threw tiny warm shadows onto the cloth. The baker had wrapped the tray earlier in a cloth rubbed with beeswax, and that quiet scent rose now in the still air around the picnic. Ines breathed it in once and smiled at the familiar comfort. Jorie’s eyes grew wide at the neat rows of toast. Ines counted the pieces slowly in their mind and felt the soft breeze brush across their bare arms.

Ines saw at once that the tray of cheese toasts cut on the diagonal would not feel fair if only one person was served first. The pieces looked generous, yet if the baker handed the first toast only to Ines, Jorie would wait with empty hands while the smell of melted cheese drifted past. Ines rested both palms on the cloth and thought about the even space between friends. The temperature of the air stayed pleasantly mild, never hot and never cool. Ines glanced at Jorie, who waited quietly without reaching. The baker stood ready with a small plate but did not move yet. Ines decided the toasts needed a different arrangement so that no one sat left out.

“I can make smaller pieces so we all begin together,” Ines said clearly. Jorie brightened and answered, “That sounds kind, Ines.” The baker simply watched and gave a small nod that carried no extra words. Ines took the tray carefully and began to break each diagonal toast into neat halves, lining the smaller triangles in two equal rows. While working, Ines reached out and touched a fallen leaf that rested near the cloth edge, feeling a leaf’s soft underside against their fingertips. The texture was cool and velvety, and Ines smiled at the gentle surprise of it. The light stayed golden and steady across the meadow as Ines finished the even shares.

A soft sound rose then, the wind in the fig leaves that grew along the meadow’s edge, whispering in long easy sighs. Ines paused to listen and felt the sound settle everything into place. “Now the tray holds the same number for each of us,” Ines said, arranging the last piece. Jorie replied, “Thank you for thinking of me, Ines.” The baker set two small plates down without speaking further, simply allowing the choice Ines had made to stand. Ines handed one plate across the cloth and kept the matching plate. The cheese toasts smelled warm and inviting, and the sun warmed the tops of Ines’s hands as they held the plate steady.

They began to eat in unhurried bites, the cheese soft and the toast lightly crunchy under careful teeth. Ines chewed slowly and watched a pale butterfly drift past without landing. The temperature of the meadow remained kind, wrapping the three of them in easy comfort. Ines glanced at the baker, who sat with quiet satisfaction and offered no lecture or long talk, only a peaceful presence. Jorie nibbled and looked content. Ines felt the cloth under their legs stay smooth and dry. Between bites Ines said, “The meadow feels friendly today.” The words hung lightly, and everyone seemed to agree without needing more.

After the last toast crumbs were gone, Ines noticed a small snail making its way across a single flat brick that lay half hidden in the grass beside the cloth. Ines pointed softly so Jorie could see, and together they watched the snail cross one whole brick at its own patient speed. The shell carried soft browns and creams that caught the lowering light. Ines kept perfectly still so the snail would not feel rushed. The air cooled only a little, remaining gentle on their skin. When the snail reached the far side and slipped into the green, Ines let out a quiet breath of wonder. The baker smiled across the cloth, still saying nothing, simply sharing the calm.

The afternoon light turned honey-colored as the sun slipped lower, painting long soft stripes across the picnic cloth. Ines helped fold the cloth into neat squares while Jorie gathered the empty plates. The baker packed the tray away with the same quiet care. Ines stood and stretched, feeling the mild evening air move across their cheeks. They walked together toward the small cottage that stood at the meadow’s border, its door already open and welcoming. Inside the first room the air felt still and safe. Ines paused by the wide sill and looked back once at the darkening grass. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Ines watched the warm circle of light settle over the wooden floor and felt the whole day fold gently into rest.
