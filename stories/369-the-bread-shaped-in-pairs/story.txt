The warm smell of cinnamon rose from the soft dough as Esme’s small hands already pressed and turned the long pale strands on the wide wooden board set near the harbor dock. Cool salt air drifted over their wrists and the low sun threw soft orange light across the planks. Esme felt the dough give gently under their palms while water lapped the pilings in a steady hush. Tall baskets lined the edge ready to be passed up to the carts, and the braided loaf they were shaping would ride inside one of them once it was ready. Uncle Idris stood a little behind them with flour on his sleeves, and Bibi waited close by holding a clean cloth. Esme kept their eyes on the three thick ropes of dough and breathed in the spicy sweetness again.

Esme glanced up at the gently rocking boats and felt the board’s smooth grain under their fingertips. Bibi stepped nearer so their shoulders almost touched, and the two of them looked at the unbraided strands together. “We have to start the cross just right,” Esme said softly. The air stayed mild and the light stayed golden on the water. Uncle Idris only nodded and kept his voice quiet so he would not hurry anyone. Baskets shifted a little as a dock helper lifted the first one, yet Esme stayed focused on the loaf that still needed careful hands.

Esme and Bibi decided the work would go wrong if either of them rushed the crossing of the strands. “You hold the left one steady and I will lift the middle,” Bibi whispered. Esme answered, “Then I will tuck the right one under, nice and slow.” The dock boards felt solid and slightly cool beneath their bare feet. Uncle Idris offered only a calm count and a name for the knot they needed. “Call it the quiet cross,” he said, “and count one when you are ready.” Soft waves kept whispering against the pilings while Esme and Bibi shared the two slow jobs without bumping elbows.

Esme began the first lift of the middle strand while Bibi guided the left one into place, both moving as if they had all the time in the harbor. The dough felt warm and springy between Esme’s fingers. A gate latch clicked once somewhere up the path, a small clear sound that faded quickly into the evening. Uncle Idris watched but did not reach in. “Two,” he counted when they paused. Esme hummed four gentle notes and then left a rest so the quiet could settle around them before they moved again. The baskets waited patiently in a line, their woven sides catching the last of the daylight.

Esme felt the next fold settle neatly because neither of them hurried. Bibi’s hands stayed light on the outer strand while Esme finished the tuck that locked the quiet cross. “It looks even already,” Esme said with a small smile. The air carried a faint coolness now that the sun had slipped lower, yet the dough still held its soft heat. Uncle Idris spoke the next number only when both children were still. “Three,” he said, and nothing more. Farther along the dock another basket was passed upward with a soft scrape of rope, but Esme kept their attention on the loaf taking shape between them.

Esme and Bibi worked the second set of folds with the same unhurried care, splitting every motion so one child steadied while the other crossed. The walnut-smooth spoon that Uncle Idris had set beside the board felt cool and polished when Esme’s wrist brushed it by accident. They did not need the spoon yet, but the pleasant texture reminded them to stay gentle. “Now the last tuck,” Bibi murmured. Esme nodded and answered, “I am ready when you are.” The light on the water had turned to soft lavender, and the harbor smelled clean and wide. Uncle Idris offered the name of the finishing knot once more so they could hear it clearly.

Esme finished the final cross while Bibi held the ends in place, and the braided loaf lay complete and even on the board. Their fingers brushed flour dust that felt dry and fine. No one spoke for a long moment; they simply looked at the tidy pattern they had made together. Uncle Idris smiled and lifted the clean cloth. “You two made the quiet cross hold,” he said. Esme felt the evening breeze lift a few loose hairs from their forehead while the boats rocked like slow cradles. The baskets stood ready, and soon the loaf would rest inside one of them to be passed up with the others.

Esme helped ease the finished braid onto the waiting cloth, still moving slowly so nothing shifted. Bibi folded the corners with equal care. The planks underfoot held the leftover warmth of the day even as the air cooled. Esme listened to the soft creak of a nearby rope and felt proud of the steady work they had shared. Uncle Idris only touched the edge of the cloth to keep it tidy. Together they carried the loaf the short distance to the nearest basket, and Esme watched it settle among the other wrapped bundles like a small treasure ready for its ride.

Esme stood close to the basket while it was lifted and passed upward, the motion smooth and sure in the quiet dusk. They brushed a last bit of flour from their hands and felt the mild night air on their skin. Bibi stayed beside them, and Uncle Idris rested a calm hand on the rail. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Esme watched the warm circle of light hold steady against the darkening harbor and knew the braided loaf was safely on its way, shaped by two slow pairs of hands and one quiet count.
