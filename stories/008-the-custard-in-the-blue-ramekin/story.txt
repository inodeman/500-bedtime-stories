The tin clock on the wooden shelf ticked with a gentle steady beat that filled the quiet greenhouse air. Kito heard the small sound first and turned their head toward it, noticing how the warm afternoon light from the south window painted soft gold stripes across the seed pots lined up on the bench. The glass panes held a mild heat that felt pleasant on Kito’s bare arms, and the green leaves nearby brushed against one another with a faint dry rustle. Kito sat cross-legged on the wide bench, close enough to touch the smooth rims of the little pots filled with dark soil. Auntie June hummed nearby while she watered a tray of sprouts, and the whole room smelled faintly of damp earth mixed with something sweeter later on.

Kito’s eyes drifted to the blue ramekin resting in a shallow basket at the far end of the bench, right where the south window’s brightest patch fell. The custard inside sat covered with a neat white cloth that Auntie June had tucked around the edges. Kito wanted so much to lift the cloth and check whether the custard had set into its creamy shape yet. The blue dish looked cool and smooth under the cloth, and Kito’s fingers twitched toward it. “Auntie June, may I peek just once?” Kito asked in a soft voice. The light through the glass felt warmer now on Kito’s cheeks, and a tiny bead of moisture gathered on one of the seed-pot rims.

Auntie June set down her watering can and came closer, the soles of her shoes making a quiet shuffle on the stone floor. “The custard needs its own quiet time, Kito,” she said with a smile that crinkled the corners of her eyes. “If we lift the cloth too soon, it will not finish the way it likes.” Kito nodded slowly and pulled their hand back. The air near the south window carried a gentle warmth that made the blue ramekin seem almost glowing under its cover. Kito rested both palms flat on the bench boards, feeling the grain of the wood, and listened again to the distant ticking while the seed pots sat patient in their neat rows.

“I can wait,” Kito said after a moment, voice calm. Auntie June patted the space beside the basket. “Good. Shall we do something quiet together until it is ready?” Kito agreed and shifted a little so their knees brushed the edge of a folded quilt that someone had left on the bench for sitting. One careful fingertip traced a single quilt stitch that rose like a tiny ridge under the fabric, smooth cotton threads forming a neat line. The texture felt steady and kind under Kito’s skin. Sunlight from the south window stretched longer across the seed pots, and the greenhouse held its mild temperature like a soft blanket of air.

Kito decided to watch the shadow of a thin bamboo stake that stood among the seed pots. The dark line on the bench moved so slowly that Kito could measure it against their own thumb. They held still and breathed the warm air while the shadow crept the exact width of that thumb, no faster. Auntie June settled on the other side of the bench and whispered, “See how the light takes its time? The custard is doing the same.” Kito kept their eyes on the slow dark edge and felt the quiet stretch around them. Somewhere near a cluster of potted herbs the sweet smell of lavender drifted up, light and clean, mixing with the earth scent already in the room.

Hadi appeared at the greenhouse door with a small basket of fresh mint leaves and stepped inside carefully so the hinges made almost no sound. “I brought these for later,” Hadi said, setting the basket down without jostling anything. Kito smiled and patted the quilt so Hadi could sit too. The three of them watched the seed pots together while the tin clock kept its soft rhythm far behind them. Kito’s shoulder brushed the warm glass of the south window for a second, and the heat there felt gentle against their shirt. No one hurried. The blue ramekin stayed covered, and the custard finished itself in the quiet light.

Minutes passed like slow breaths. Kito counted the seed pots once, then twice, noticing how some already showed tiny green curls pushing upward. Auntie June poured a little more water into a saucer under one pot, and the liquid made a soft plink that faded quickly. “It must be nearly ready now,” Auntie June said. Kito felt the air cool just a fraction as a cloud drifted across the sun outside, dimming the gold stripes for a moment before they returned brighter than before. The quilt under Kito’s legs held its comfortable weight, and the whole bench seemed to wait with them.

At last Auntie June lifted the white cloth with careful fingers. The custard in the blue ramekin had set into a smooth pale surface that caught the south-window light like quiet cream. Kito leaned close and breathed in the mild vanilla scent rising from it. “It looks perfect,” Kito said, eyes wide with happy calm. Hadi clapped once, softly, so the sound stayed gentle. Auntie June handed Kito a small wooden spoon, and together they shared the first cool spoonfuls right there on the greenhouse bench among the seed pots. The custard tasted silky and sweet, and the blue dish felt solid and friendly in Kito’s hands.

Later the three of them sat side by side while the afternoon light softened toward evening. Kito rested against Auntie June’s arm and watched the last thin shadow slide across one more seed pot. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Outside the glass the garden rested under a sky turning the color of pale lavender tea, and inside the greenhouse the air stayed pleasantly warm around Kito’s shoulders. Hadi folded the quilt edge neater, and Auntie June collected the empty blue ramekin. Kito yawned a quiet yawn, feeling full of the slow good afternoon, ready for bedtime stories and soft pillows whenever they walked back to the house.
