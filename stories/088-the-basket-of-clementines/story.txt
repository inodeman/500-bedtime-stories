The quiet tick of a tin clock came softly from the shelf by the ferry window. Bodhi noticed that small steady sound right away and sat very still on the ferry bench with the folded blanket resting under them. Cool evening air drifted through a cracked pane and brushed Bodhi’s cheeks while the wooden seat held steady beneath their legs. Golden lamp light pooled across the cabin floor and lit the toes of Bodhi’s shoes. Outside, water whispered against the hull in slow gentle waves. Bodhi listened to the tin clock and watched Miss Dalca stand near the low choir step at the front where passengers sometimes sang. A basket of clementines sat right on that choir step, its woven rim bright in the glow. Lale sat two benches over and smiled across at Bodhi. Bodhi felt the calm of the moving ferry settle into their shoulders and kept listening to every quiet tick.

Bodhi smoothed the edge of the folded blanket and felt how the fabric lay neat and cool in the cabin warmth. Miss Dalca turned toward the benches and said, “Bodhi, after we hum one short song, would you help share these clementines?” Bodhi answered, “Yes, Miss Dalca, I would like to help.” Soft yellow light from the hanging lamps shone on Miss Dalca’s kind face and made the clementines look round and sunny. The air near the vents felt pleasantly warm against Bodhi’s ankles. Lale watched with bright eyes while the ferry engines hummed low and steady underneath everything. Bodhi looked at the basket again and already began to wonder how the fruit might be given out so no one waited too long. The cabin stayed peaceful and the blanket stayed smooth under Bodhi’s hands.

Bodhi rose carefully and walked the short way to the choir step where the basket waited. The floorboards felt solid and slightly cool under their socks. Miss Dalca began a soft hum and Lale joined with a quiet voice that floated like a feather. Bodhi stood close enough to see the orange skins gleaming under the lamp light and smelled the clean fresh scent of the open water mixing with something else soon. They counted the clementines slowly with their eyes and thought about everyone who might want one. The temperature near the step stayed gentle and even. Bodhi’s fingers hovered above the basket rim while the humming continued in calm round tones. Lale’s feet swung lightly and the whole cabin seemed wrapped in that easy sound.

Bodhi lifted the basket a little and caught the soft smell of lavender rising from a small cloth tucked among the fruit. The lavender scent floated warm and calm around Bodhi’s face as they carried the basket back toward the ferry bench. Soft light followed them across the floor in a pale path. Bodhi set the basket down on the bench beside the folded blanket and felt the wooden seat still steady. Lale came closer and said, “Those look so bright, Bodhi.” Bodhi replied, “They do, Lale. We can share them.” The air held that gentle lavender note while the ferry rocked just enough to feel like a slow cradle. Bodhi kept one hand on the basket handle and watched the clementines settle.

Bodhi saw that the basket of clementines at the choir step would not feel fair if only one person was served first. They decided to make a smaller even share so Lale was included right away along with everyone near. Cool air from the window touched Bodhi’s wrist as they began peeling one clementine with careful fingers. The peel came away in a smooth curl and the fruit broke into neat little sections. Lamp light made each section shine like a tiny lantern. Bodhi placed equal small piles on the folded blanket so no share looked bigger than another. Lale waited quietly and the cabin temperature stayed mild and comfortable around them both.

Bodhi held out the first even share and said, “Here, Lale, this part is for you.” Lale took it gently and answered, “Thank you, Bodhi, it smells sweet.” The sections felt cool and smooth in Bodhi’s palm before they passed them. Miss Dalca watched from the choir step but said nothing long or heavy; she simply nodded once and let the choice stand. Warm light from the lamps rested on Bodhi’s hair while they kept handing out the same small fair amounts. The ferry moved on with its soft engine hum and Bodhi felt the quiet gladness of everyone having a taste at the same time. The blanket stayed folded neat beneath the little piles of fruit.

Bodhi sat back down on the ferry bench and ran one finger along the folded blanket until they found a single quilt stitch raised under the cloth. The quilt stitch felt tiny and firm against Bodhi’s fingertip like a secret knot of thread. Soft cabin light made the stitch cast a miniature shadow of its own. Bodhi breathed in the last trace of lavender still lingering near the empty basket. Lale chewed slowly and smiled with orange sweetness on their tongue. Miss Dalca hummed a finishing note that faded into the gentle slap of water outside. Bodhi kept touching that quilt stitch and felt how safe and finished the sharing had become.

Bodhi and Lale watched a thin shadow from the window frame move the width of a thumb across the bench as the ferry turned a little. The shadow slipped slow and quiet over the folded blanket and over Bodhi’s hand. Cool air brushed their arms once more while the lamps glowed steady overhead. Bodhi stacked the empty peels into a small tidy heap so nothing rolled away. Lale leaned against the bench back and the wood felt smooth under both of them. Miss Dalca gathered the basket and set it aside without another word. Bodhi listened to the far soft water and felt the whole cabin grow even quieter and ready for rest.

Bodhi curled a little closer to the folded blanket and let their eyes grow heavy in the calm cabin air. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Warm light from it touched the bench and the last orange peels and Bodhi’s resting hands. The air stayed mild and the ferry rocked like a slow cradle under the stars outside. Bodhi heard only the faintest water sounds now and felt the quilt stitch one last time beneath their palm. Lale’s breathing slowed nearby and Miss Dalca sat quiet in her own seat. Bodhi closed their eyes all the way and drifted into sleep while the gentle glow held the cabin safe and still.
