The soft evening breeze touched the calm meadow gently with a mild warmth that ruffled the tall grasses in slow waves and carried a hint of wildflowers across the open space. Eko stepped onto the picnic cloth spread there in the middle of the green and felt the last golden rays of sunlight rest warmly on their arms and hair as they settled down cross-legged. The baker was already kneeling beside the basket unpacking careful packages while Casper waited nearby with bright eyes. Eko smiled at both of them and ran their fingers over the smooth cloth surface noticing how the light made tiny shadows dance. Everything felt peaceful under that gentle weather and Eko breathed out a happy sigh ready for the time together in the calm meadow.

Eko reached for a linen napkin from the pile and explored its fine smooth texture with slow careful strokes of their thumb before placing it open on their lap. "I am so glad we came out here today," Eko said to Casper who sat close enough that their shoulders almost touched. The baker wound a small tin clock and set it on the edge of the cloth where it began to mark time with a steady soft ticking sound that blended into the quiet. Eko listened to that gentle tick and watched a butterfly drift past in the fading light while Casper pointed at a cloud shape that looked like a boat. The air stayed comfortably warm around Eko as they arranged cups and waited for the meal to begin in the calm meadow.

Soon the baker brought out a bowl of fruit and the fresh smell of orange peel rose sweetly into the air around the picnic cloth. Eko leaned closer to enjoy it fully and saw the small lantern with room for two hands on the handle sitting ready in the center to light their gathering later. "Shall I serve the warm bread now?" the baker asked in a low friendly voice. Eko nodded but already thought carefully about the order of things while the soft purple light of coming dusk began to color the distant trees. Casper clapped once quietly in anticipation and Eko felt the cool evening temperature start to ease the earlier warmth from their skin in a pleasant way.

Eko saw that the small lantern with room for two hands on the handle would not feel fair if only one person was served first so they decided to make a smaller even share so Casper was included. "Let us make the pieces the same size for everyone," Eko said clearly and kindly. With steady hands Eko took the three soft rolls the baker offered and broke each one into matching halves then quarters until every portion looked alike on the plates. The baker simply watched with a quiet smile and let the choice stand without a speech as Eko finished the careful work. A soft glow from the low sun still touched Eko's face with gentle heat while they passed the even shares around the picnic cloth in the calm meadow.

Casper took their even share and held it carefully. "These pieces taste better because we all have the same," Casper said with a happy laugh that sounded like little bells. Eko chewed slowly and savored the mild flavor while the baker took a bite too and nodded approval in silence. The picnic cloth felt firm yet soft under Eko's legs and they shifted slightly to get more comfortable as night birds began a distant call. Eko looked at the small lantern again and imagined how its light would soon join them all equally. The temperature dropped another degree into a cozy coolness that made the shared food even more welcome in the calm meadow.

After the bread Eko and Casper both reached for the small lantern at the same moment and their hands fit side by side on the wide handle just as it was made for two. "The light is nicer when we hold it this way," Eko said softly to Casper. Together they lifted it a little so the warm yellow beam fell evenly across the plates and the baker's kind face. Eko felt the metal of the handle cool under their palm yet the light itself brought a gentle brightness that pushed back the gathering shadows. Casper's fingers stayed steady next to Eko's and they sat that way for several quiet minutes listening to the meadow settle into evening around the picnic cloth.

Eko suggested they count the soft fireflies that began to appear one by one over the grass while still holding the lantern. They reached twelve without rushing and then rested again. The baker packed leftover fruit and the tin clock ticked on unnoticed now. Eko rubbed their free hand over the picnic cloth feeling its weave and thought how nice it was that everyone had the same amount from the start. A light breeze returned cooler than before and brushed Eko's neck with a refreshing touch while Casper told a short story about a friendly rabbit. The lantern light stayed steady and kind over all of them in the calm meadow as the sky turned deep blue.

When it was time to leave Eko helped fold the picnic cloth into a neat square while still near the small lantern that now lit their packing. The baker lifted the basket and Casper carried the napkins. "I will remember this evening for a long time," Eko said as they began the slow walk home along the path. The air grew cooler still and smelled faintly of damp earth underfoot but stayed calm without any hurry. Eko and Casper took turns with the lantern handle so neither walked in shadow and the baker walked just behind humming. Soft moths fluttered near the light and Eko watched them with sleepy eyes already thinking of bed.

At home Eko placed the small lantern carefully on the table near the window after saying goodnight to the baker and to Casper. They washed their hands and changed into soft pajamas feeling the smooth fabric against their skin. Eko climbed into bed and pulled the covers up to their chin while the room stayed quiet and safe. Outside the meadow was dark now but the memory of even shares and shared light stayed warm inside Eko. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Eko closed their eyes to the gentle hush and cool night air from the open window and drifted off with a peaceful smile.
