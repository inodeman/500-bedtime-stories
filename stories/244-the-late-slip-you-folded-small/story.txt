In the quiet workshop a small ticking sound came from the high shelf, the steady gentle beat of a tin clock. Opal noticed the sound right away and stood still with one hand resting inside their jacket pocket. The wooden bench stretched wide in front of them under a hanging bulb that cast warm yellow light across every jar and scrap. Bits of wood waited in neat piles beside pots of glue and rows of paper labels ready for sticking. Opal felt the late note you folded small pressed flat against their palm, its paper edges already soft from the long walk. They took a slow breath that filled their chest and looked along the tidy row of brushes and clamps while the clock kept its calm rhythm.

Opal almost slid the late note you folded small behind a stack of wooden blocks so it would stay hidden, yet their fingers stayed curled around the tiny square instead. Cool air drifted near the bench and brushed softly against Opal’s cheeks after the warmer hallway. They ran their free hand over the smooth grain of the wood and listened to a quiet drip of leftover glue inside a cup. Uncle Idris stood a few steps away sorting thin strips of pine, his shadow long and gentle across the floorboards. Opal watched him for a long moment and decided the pocket was no place for keeping things tucked away.

A gentle smell of chamomile rose from the mug Uncle Idris had set on the far corner of the bench, curling warm and sleepy through the whole room. Opal stepped closer until their shoes made a soft scuffing sound on the worn floorboards. They drew the late note you folded small into the open and held it carefully in both hands, the creases still tight from being folded so many times. Bright labels lay scattered under the lamp light, some already printed with neat words and some left blank for new writing. Opal cleared their throat once and waited until Uncle Idris turned around with a kind, unhurried smile.

Uncle Idris lifted a round tin of wood glue and twisted off its tin lid, the cool metal texture solid and smooth under his palm before he set the lid aside on a clean cloth. “Hello, Opal,” he said in a low voice that fitted the peaceful room. Opal looked at the open tin and the clear sticky shine of the glue waiting inside. “I brought this,” Opal said, holding out the late note you folded small so it rested in the middle of the big wooden bench. The paper looked even smaller there among the tools. Uncle Idris wiped his hands carefully and waited without any rush or questions.

“It is a late note from this morning,” Opal said, unfolding the paper slowly so each crease opened one by one under the steady light. “I almost hid it because I felt all tangled up about being late, but I want to tell you the plain truth now.” The hanging bulb glowed kind and even across the open note and the waiting glue pot. Uncle Idris read the short lines and gave one quiet nod. “Thank you for showing me, Opal,” he said. “We can mend what can be mended together right here.” Opal felt their shoulders loosen as the words settled like a soft covering over the whole bench.

Together they smoothed the late note you folded small flat on a clean corner of the wood and placed a little wooden weight on one edge so it would stay neat and open. Opal dipped a thin brush into the glue and painted a careful line along the cracked side of a small wooden box they had started the week before. The glue spread mild and clear while Uncle Idris held the pieces steady with patient hands. Soft clicks sounded when the wood edges met just right and stayed in place. They worked side by side without hurry, pressing gently until the joint felt firm again under their shared fingers.

Opal chose two matching labels from the bright pile and pressed them onto the mended box, one centered on the lid and one along the side. The dry paper labels felt smooth and slightly rough under their thumbs as they rubbed each one flat against the wood. A faint draft from the open window carried cooler evening air across the bench and made the hanging light sway only a little. “These labels make it look finished,” Opal said. Uncle Idris smiled and answered, “They do, and the box will hold small treasures safely now.” Opal stacked the leftover labels into a tidy pile and brushed a few pale wood shavings into the waiting dustpan.

After the glue began to set firm, Opal found a warm heap of clean rags near the low heater and matched two soft cloths that looked alike, folding them neatly for later wiping of any sticky spots. The rags held a gentle warmth from the nearby radiator that felt pleasant against their hands. Uncle Idris checked the box once more and added one tiny drop of glue where a thin gap still showed. “I am glad we fixed it,” Opal whispered while the workshop grew quieter as blue evening settled outside the glass panes. They wiped the brush clean together and set every tool back in its proper place along the bench.

Opal sat on the tall wooden stool beside the bench and rested both hands on the cool smooth surface while the mended box finished drying under its new labels. The late note you folded small lay open and honest beneath its little weight, no longer crumpled or hidden. Outside the sky deepened to soft blue and the whole room felt safe and still around them. Uncle Idris put away the glue pot, wiped the last faint sticky spots, and hung the clean cloths on their peg. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Opal yawned a small quiet yawn and leaned their cheek against one folded arm, ready for the short calm walk home under the first early stars.
