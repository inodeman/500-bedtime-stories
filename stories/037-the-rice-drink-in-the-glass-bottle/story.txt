The warm smell of honey drifted across the cottage as Femi’s small hands already moved along the wide windowsill, brushing soft dust from the rims of quiet jars and straightening a wooden tray that held pale drying wool. Afternoon light poured through the glass in gentle gold stripes, and Femi felt the cool smoothness of each jar under their fingertips while the wool gave off a faint clean scent of soap and open air. Outside, the river whispered against the bank, and inside the room stayed still and kind. Femi hummed a little tune under their breath and reached again for another jar, careful not to tip the trays that sat in neat rows beside the soft white fleece.

Femi paused when their eyes found the tall glass bottle standing near the middle of the sill, its sides cloudy and its cloth cover tied with a thin string. Inside, the rice drink was culturing slowly, turning from thin liquid into something thicker and milder, though Femi could not yet see the finished quiet change. The bottle caught a soft bar of sunlight and threw a pale circle onto the wood. Femi leaned closer and felt the cool glass against one cheek, then drew back again. Behind them the floorboards gave a small warm creak, and the ferryman stepped into the room with the easy hush of someone who spent long hours listening to water.

“I want to lift the cloth and look,” Femi said softly, their voice almost as quiet as the dust motes floating in the light. The ferryman set a chalky hand on the edge of the sill, the dry powdery feel of river chalk still on his skin from the landing stones outside, and he smiled without hurry. “It is still working in its own time,” he answered, “and the cloth keeps it peaceful until it is ready.” Femi looked from the bottle to the ferryman’s calm face and then back again, fingers resting lightly on the string that held the cover closed.

A soft click sounded from the garden path as a gate latch settled into place, and Femi turned their head toward the open door for a moment before returning to the sill. Noemi appeared at the doorway with a basket of clean cloths and a smile, and Femi waved once. “Will you wait with me?” Femi asked, and Noemi nodded and set the basket down near the drying wool so the cloths could rest in the same gentle air. The three of them stood together in the quiet gold light while the rice drink continued its slow work under the cloth, unseen and unhurried.

Femi drew a slow breath and decided to leave the bottle covered just as it was. Instead of peeking, Femi chose a quiet task that fit the room: they began to smooth the drying wool across its trays, spreading each soft fleece so the air could move evenly between the strands. The wool felt springy and warm from the sun, and Femi’s hands moved slowly, pressing and lifting, arranging the white locks until they lay like little clouds. The ferryman watched with kind eyes and said, “That is good work, Femi. The wool likes patience the same way the bottle does.” Femi felt a small proud warmth in their chest and kept smoothing.

Noemi brought a low stool so Femi could sit while they worked, and the wood of the stool felt smooth and steady under Femi’s legs. Outside the window the river light flickered on the water in soft silver shapes, and inside the cottage the air stayed mild and sweet. Femi counted the jars one by one—seven tall ones and four short ones—then returned to the wool and fluffed a corner that had curled. The rice drink bottle stood quiet and still, its cloth cover never moving, and Femi glanced at it only now and then, choosing each time to leave the string tied.

“I can hear the river talking to the stones,” Femi said after a long gentle stretch of quiet. The ferryman nodded and replied, “It always does, slow and steady, just like the drink inside that glass.” Femi smiled and kept their hands busy with the last tray of wool, feeling the fibers slide between their fingers like soft snow that never melted. The light on the sill shifted a little lower, turning from bright gold to a warmer honey-colored glow that touched the edges of every jar and the curve of the bottle alike. Noemi hummed a low tune that matched the river’s hush, and Femi hummed along without needing words.

Time eased forward the way a boat eases from a bank, and Femi waited through the single clear chime of the little brass clock on the shelf, letting the sound fade completely before they stood again. When the chime had gone, Femi and the ferryman and Noemi all looked toward the glass bottle together. The cloth cover still sat neatly in place, yet something about the liquid beneath it looked thicker and more settled, as if the quiet work had finished while they tended the wool and listened to the room. Femi’s heart felt calm and full. They did not rush. They simply stood close and breathed the mild air.

Femi untied the string at last with careful fingers and lifted the cloth just enough to see that the rice drink had become smooth and ready in its own good time. The ferryman rested a steady hand on Femi’s shoulder, and Noemi smiled beside the trays of soft wool. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Femi replaced the cloth for the night, patted the warm wood of the sill once more, and felt the whole cottage settle around them like a gentle blanket as the last light of day rested on the jars, the trays, and the quiet glass bottle that had waited so well.
