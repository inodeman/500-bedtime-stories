Papa was already kneeling on the bedroom rug of cushions, puppets, and paper, pressing a thin wooden brace against a low cardboard stage while the lamp threw warm golden light across his sleeves. Eko settled onto a thick blue cushion beside him and felt the gentle heat of the room on their cheeks. Soft yarn hair on a sock puppet brushed Eko’s wrist as they reached to steady a corner. The smell of fresh paper rose from the open stack near Papa’s knee. “This stage wants one more brace before the puppets begin,” Papa said in a calm voice. Eko held the edge steady and watched Papa’s careful fingers finish the work without hurry.

Una stood just outside the circle of cushions, close to a smooth round ball that rested against the wall where anyone might roll it toward the child if they chose. Eko noticed Una’s quiet face and the way Una’s bare toes curled on the cooler floor beyond the soft edge of the rug. A faint draft slipped along the baseboard and touched Eko’s ankle with a mild chill. Papa kept his eyes on the puppets and did not name the gap out loud. Eko shifted one cushion an inch and listened to the quiet rustle of fabric. “The puppets are almost ready,” Eko said gently, yet Una only took a half step and remained near the waiting ball.

Eko looked again at the closed ring of seats and at Una’s still hands. Paper scraps whispered dryly when Eko moved a puppet aside to clear space. Papa sat back on his heels and rested both palms on his knees, waiting without a single push. Eko decided the circle felt too tight. They pulled two cushions outward toward the rim of the rug until a soft open doorway appeared between the pillows. Cooler air gathered in that new space near the floor. Eko rolled the ball a short distance farther along the wall so it would not block the path. “You can sit right here with us,” Eko said. Una’s shoulders eased as the invitation settled.

Una stepped onto the rug and sank onto the fresh cushion Eko had made. Eko placed a paper butterfly puppet into Una’s open hands with careful fingers. Lamp light made the butterfly wings glow a pale soft yellow against Una’s palms. Papa only nodded once and stayed quiet, leaving the children free to sort the places themselves. Eko felt a steady warmth spread through their chest. They slowed the whole pace of the game so Una could learn each puppet’s name without rush. “This one is the moon rabbit,” Eko explained, holding up a gray sock with long soft ears. Una cradled the butterfly and answered with a small real smile that reached their eyes.

Behind the cushions stood a short play wall they had shaped earlier from cool clay so the puppets could have a castle side. Eko reached back and brushed the damp clay wall with one fingertip, feeling its cool grainy texture while thinking of the next scene. The ball waited nearby against the real bedroom wall, ready for gentle rolls. Eko decided the rules could widen too. Instead of one child keeping the ball, everyone would take slow turns sending it under the cardboard stage. Papa watched with kind patient eyes and offered no directions. Eko set the ball in the open space between the three of them so the path stayed clear and fair.

Outside in the garden a gate latch clicked once and then fell silent, a small far sound that drifted through the cracked window and left the bedroom peaceful. Eko heard it and felt the quiet hold. They invited Una to roll first. Una pushed the ball softly so it traveled toward Eko against the wall and bumped the baseboard with a tiny friendly thud. Eko caught it in both hands and rolled it back toward Una beneath the paper edge of the stage. The cushions held their weight like gentle nests. “Your turn again whenever you are ready,” Eko whispered. Una laughed a quiet laugh that filled the calm space without startling anyone.

Papa lifted two sock puppets and made them bow to each other while the children kept their own rhythm. Eko changed the pace once more, slowing every motion so the ball rolled only after each puppet had spoken a soft line. The fuzzy texture of the rug pressed warmly under Eko’s bare feet. Evening air cooled a little more around their shoulders yet stayed comfortable. Una made the moon rabbit hop twice across a cushion. Eko let the butterfly float above the stage. “We can share the ball every single round,” Eko said. Papa smiled and kept his hands quiet again, trusting the choice Eko had made.

They played until the puppets rested in a neat row on one wide cushion. Eko gathered the stray paper scraps into a small tidy pile and felt the smooth cool surface of the ball one last time before setting it beside the clay wall for morning. Una leaned lightly against Eko’s shoulder in a friendly easy way. The lamp light grew softer as the sky outside deepened to blue. Papa folded the cardboard stage with slow careful movements and stacked the wooden braces. No one needed to announce a prize or a rule change. The three of them simply sat together on the widened rug, breathing the settled quiet of the room while Eko rested both palms lightly on their own knees and felt deep calm.

Soon Eko’s eyelids grew heavy in the gentle warmth of the blanket Papa draped over their legs. Una nestled on the neighboring cushion with the butterfly still loosely in hand and yawned a quiet yawn. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Eko heard Papa’s slow footsteps on the floorboards and felt the clean cool night air drift from the cracked window across their forehead. They closed their eyes knowing Una held a real place among the cushions and puppets, and the ball waited safely against the wall for another slow turn when morning light returned.
