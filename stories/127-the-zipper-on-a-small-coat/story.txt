"Idris, the little blue coat wants to close before the breeze grows cooler," Grandma Nia said in her gentle voice while they stood together in the market lane lined with baskets, jars, and flower pots. Soft golden light from the lowering sun warmed Idris's shoulders and made the clay pots look rosy and friendly. Idris watched the small coat resting over Grandma Nia's arm and noticed the zipper had caught on a fold of fabric near the bottom. Grandma Nia held a wide basket of ripe pears in one hand and tugged carefully at the stuck zipper with the other. The air felt pleasantly mild against Idris's bare arms, and nearby vendors arranged bright marigolds in neat circles. Idris stayed right beside Grandma Nia, listening to quiet footsteps on the packed earth path.

Idris breathed in the smell of warm bread drifting from a baker's stall just a few steps away and felt how it mixed with the faint sweetness of the pears. Grandma Nia shifted her basket higher and tried again to free the zipper, her fingers moving slowly so nothing would tear. Idris saw that the stuck metal teeth made the job awkward for one person alone. The market lane hummed with soft talk and the clink of jars being set down, yet everything stayed calm and unhurried. Idris reached out and gently held the lower edge of the small coat steady without saying a word about helping. The fabric felt smooth under Idris's fingertips while the late light painted long friendly shadows across the flower pots.

Grandma Nia smiled when she felt the coat grow still in Idris's careful hands. "Thank you, Idris. That makes it much easier," she said, her tone warm and quiet. Idris kept holding the bottom of the zipper track so Grandma Nia could guide the pull tab upward with both free fingers once the basket rested safely on a low wooden bench. A soft sound of wind in the fig leaves floated down from the trees that shaded one end of the lane, cooling the air just a little. Idris noticed how the leaves shivered and made tiny dancing patterns of shade on the ground near their feet. Together they worked the zipper free of the fold, one small careful tug at a time, until the teeth lined up straight again.

Idris felt the cool ceramic rim of a nearby jar of honey when they leaned closer to steady the coat against the edge of the stall. The smooth round edge pressed lightly against Idris's wrist while Grandma Nia drew the zipper all the way up with a soft zipping whisper. The small coat now hung neatly closed over her arm, ready for whenever Idris might need it later. Idris let go and stepped back half a pace, simply glad the job was finished. Grandma Nia picked up her pear basket again and rested her free hand on Idris's shoulder for a moment. The market lane smelled faintly of earth and blossoms, and the light stayed gentle across every basket and pot.

"Shall we choose three yellow flowers for the table at home?" Grandma Nia asked. Idris nodded and walked a few steps farther along the lane where tall pots held cheerful blooms. They selected three bright stems together, Idris holding the stems carefully so the petals would not bruise while Grandma Nia paid the flower seller with quiet coins. The petals felt silky against Idris's palms and carried a faint fresh scent. Nearby jars of spices caught the light and glowed like tiny lanterns of their own. Idris and Grandma Nia moved slowly past more baskets filled with green beans and round red tomatoes, never rushing, simply enjoying the peaceful afternoon market.

Bodhi, who lived two doors down, waved from across the lane where he helped his own grandfather stack empty crates. "Hello, Idris. Those flowers look happy," Bodhi called in a friendly voice. Idris waved back and answered, "We found them near the honey jars." The two children smiled at each other for a moment before Bodhi returned to his quiet stacking. Grandma Nia and Idris continued along the lane, the closed small coat still draped safely over her arm and the pear basket balanced easily now. A mild temperature settled over everything as the sun slipped lower, and Idris felt content walking so close to Grandma Nia. They paused once more beside a row of smooth clay pots so Idris could run one finger along a curved side and feel its sun-warmed surface.

Idris noticed Grandma Nia glance at the sky and then at the small coat. Without waiting for any request, Idris simply held out both arms so Grandma Nia could slip the sleeves on and finish the last little bit of the zipper that still needed a final gentle tug at the top. The coat settled softly around Idris's shoulders and felt snug and comfortable at once. "There we are," Grandma Nia said. "Perfect fit." Idris did not ask for thanks or praise; they only took Grandma Nia's free hand again and walked on. The market lane grew quieter as a few vendors began packing their remaining jars into boxes, and the light turned a deeper gold across the flower pots. Idris listened to the soft shuffle of feet and felt peaceful.

They bought one small loaf of the warm bread to carry home and tucked it into the basket beside the pears. Idris held one side of the basket handle while Grandma Nia held the other, sharing the light weight between them as they left the market lane and followed the short path toward home. The flowers nodded gently with each step, and Idris watched their yellow faces bob against the evening air. A comfortable coolness touched Idris's cheeks now that the coat was zipped, and the bread gave off a cozy scent every time the basket swayed. Grandma Nia hummed a tiny wordless tune, and Idris hummed along softly. Soon the familiar front steps came into view, and they climbed them together without hurry.

Inside the quiet kitchen Idris set the flowers in a glass of water while Grandma Nia placed the pears in a bowl. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Idris carried the empty water pitcher back to its shelf, then returned to sit close beside Grandma Nia on the low couch where the last daylight mixed with the lantern's gentle shine. The small coat rested over the arm of the chair, its zipper smooth and free. Idris leaned against Grandma Nia's side and felt the calm of the room settle around them like a soft blanket, listening to the distant hush of evening outside and knowing the market lane of baskets, jars, and flower pots would wait peacefully for another day.
