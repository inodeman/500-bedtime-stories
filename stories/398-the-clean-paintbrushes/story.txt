The paintbrushes rested in a shallow wooden tray at the edge of the garden row, their wet bristles still soft and dark with leftover color, the fine hairs clumped together like tiny wet fans. Cool drops slid along the handles and fell onto the tray with soft ticks, and the late afternoon light made each damp tip gleam. Yael bent close enough to see the faint blue and gold streaks that had not quite rinsed away yet. The air near the tray smelled clean and watery, and Yael felt the smooth worn wood of the tray under their fingertips as they steadied it.

Yael stood beside the garden row where a long string stretched between two stakes, and two metal watering cans sat ready on the soil, their sides still cool from the shade. Wynn knelt opposite Yael, already holding one of the clean rags, and Miss Dalca waited a few steps back with her quiet smile. The string felt slightly rough where Yael touched it to keep it from swinging. A light breeze carried the warm smell of cocoa from the open kitchen window nearby, drifting over the leaves. Yael whispered, “We have to rinse them while the names are read, or the colors will mix.”

Miss Dalca began in a gentle voice, reading the color names slowly from a little card so the brushes could be cleaned one by one. “This one held cerulean,” she said, and Yael dipped the first brush carefully into the watering can. The water made a soft swirling sound against the metal. Wynn held the second can steady so nothing spilled. Yael felt the cool water on their wrists and watched the tinted swirls float away. If anyone hurried, the colors would muddy and the string of markers they were painting would smudge, so Yael kept every motion slow.

Yael and Wynn looked at each other across the string and decided without fuss to split the work into two quiet jobs. Yael would rinse each brush in turn while the name was spoken, and Wynn would wipe the handles dry and set them in a neat line on the clean cloth. Miss Dalca only offered a count when they needed it for the knot that would hold the finished markers. “Four loops for the hanging knot,” she said softly, and Yael nodded. The soil under Yael’s knees felt cool and crumbly, and a pale stripe of sunlight warmed the back of their neck.

Yael worked the first brush with slow circles inside the watering can, listening to the quiet drip of water and the distant bright sound of a cat’s collar bell as the cat walked along the garden path. Wynn waited until Yael lifted the brush free, then took it with both hands and patted the wooden handle dry. “Your turn for the next one,” Wynn said, and Yael answered, “I’m ready.” The bristles squeaked faintly against the metal rim, a small clean sound that pleased Yael. Miss Dalca read the next name, “marigold gold,” and the two friends kept their pace even and calm.

When the string needed securing so the wet markers could hang without touching, Yael’s fingers met the rough fibers and Miss Dalca simply named the knot once more. Yael and Wynn each held an end and pulled gently until the loops settled flat. The wool sleeve of Miss Dalca’s cardigan brushed Yael’s wrist for a moment as she leaned in to check the tidy knot, soft and warm against Yael’s skin. Yael smiled and said, “It holds just right.” Wynn added, “Now the last two brushes,” and the three of them stayed close while the watering cans rested steady on the ground.

Yael rinsed the final brushes with the same careful circles, watching the last traces of color leave the bristles and swirl away in pale clouds. The light had grown softer and gold along the garden row, and the leaves nearby gave off a green scent when Yael brushed past them. Wynn laid each clean brush side by side so the handles lined up like a little fence. Miss Dalca counted the finished markers hanging on the string in a low voice, and Yael felt the quiet satisfaction of every part finished without hurry. The two watering cans stood empty now, their metal sides cool under Yael’s palm when they touched them once more.

Together Yael and Wynn carried the tray of clean paintbrushes toward the back steps while Miss Dalca walked beside them, the string of bright markers swaying gently behind. Yael’s shoes made soft thuds on the wooden boards, and the evening air had turned a little cooler against their cheeks. Inside the kitchen the cocoa smell was stronger, wrapping around them like a blanket. Yael set the tray down carefully on the table and rocked once in the wooden chair by the counter, letting it settle still by itself. Wynn sat nearby, and Miss Dalca poured warm drinks into three cups. “We finished every brush,” Yael said, and Wynn answered, “And the knot held.”

Yael sipped the warm cocoa and felt the quiet of the room settle around them as the garden work stayed safely done outside. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
