Mama knelt beside the low wooden bench in the small school room, wiping soft dust from the edge of the stage with a cloth that left a faint warm scent of soap in the air. Quinn stood near her, toes curling against the cool floorboards, watching the way the late afternoon light slanted through the high windows and painted pale gold stripes across the empty chairs. The room felt quiet and still, like a place holding its breath before a song. Mama hummed under her breath while she worked, and Quinn listened, feeling the gentle heat of the day lingering on their cheeks. Farah sat cross-legged a little way off, turning the pages of a picture book with careful fingers. Quinn wanted to ask for the gentler song that always came at the end of music time, the one with the soft rising notes, but the words sat heavy and shy behind their teeth.

Quinn stepped closer to the low stage edge and rested one hand on the smooth wood, feeling how solid and steady it was under their palm. The air carried a light cedar smell from the freshly swept boards near the corner where the instruments waited in neat rows. Mama looked up and smiled without hurrying Quinn along. “We have a few quiet minutes before we pack the scarves away,” Mama said in her even voice. Quinn nodded and glanced toward Farah, who offered a small friendly wave. Outside, the wind in the fig leaves made a low rustling that drifted through the open window like a whispered invitation. Quinn breathed in and out, deciding they would try the smallest brave version of the request instead of pretending they did not care about the song at all.

Quinn walked over to the straw mat that lay near the stage and sat down carefully, running their fingers across the woven texture that felt dry and pleasant against their skin. Mama finished wiping the bench and came to sit nearby, her skirt settling with a soft swish. Farah closed the picture book and scooted a little closer so the three of them formed a gentle circle. The light in the room had grown warmer and more golden as the sun slipped lower. Quinn felt their heart beating a little faster, not from worry but from the quiet wish to hear that particular melody. They looked at the low stage edge and imagined the music floating out over it in calm waves.

“I would like the gentler song at the end, please,” Quinn said in a voice that could be heard, though it stayed soft and careful. Mama’s eyes brightened with kindness, and she did not push for any bigger show or louder words. “Of course, Quinn,” Mama answered, reaching for the small wooden recorder that rested on the bench. Farah clapped once, lightly, and the sound of her palms meeting made a gentle pop in the peaceful room. Quinn felt the temperature of the air cool just a touch as a breeze found its way inside, brushing across their arms like a friendly hand. They stayed seated on the mat and watched Mama lift the instrument.

Mama began to play the opening notes of the gentler song, and the melody rose slow and kind, filling the small school room with tones that seemed to glow against the walls. Quinn closed their eyes for a moment and listened to every rise and fall, feeling the music settle into their chest like warm tea. Farah hummed along quietly, matching the lower parts. The stage edge caught a long stripe of light that made the wood shine. Quinn opened their eyes again and smiled at Mama, who kept the tune steady and unhurried. No one asked Quinn to stand or to sing louder than they wished.

When the first verse ended, Mama paused and looked at Quinn with the same patient expression. “Would you like to hear it once more before we finish?” Mama asked. Quinn nodded and said, “Yes, please, just as soft.” Farah grinned and settled her hands in her lap. The second playing felt even calmer, the notes drifting up toward the ceiling and then down again like feathers. Quinn rested both palms on the floor beside the mat and felt the mild coolness of the boards. Outside the window the sky had turned a deeper blue, and the room grew dimmer in a comfortable way.

After the song faded, Mama set the recorder down and began to gather the colorful scarves into a basket. Quinn stood and helped by folding one scarf at a time, noticing how the fabric felt smooth and light between their fingers. Farah stacked the picture books on the low shelf near the stage. The three of them moved slowly, as if the gentle melody still floated in the corners of the room. Quinn felt proud in a quiet way for having spoken the request out loud, and Mama’s kind glance told them that the words had been exactly enough. A soft lamp near the door cast a pool of yellow light across the chairs.

Quinn noticed a cup of water sitting on the side table and turned the cup so the handle faced the room, making it ready for whoever needed it next. Mama gave a small nod of thanks. Farah stretched her arms and yawned a happy little yawn. The school room smelled faintly of the clean cloths and the wood, and the last of the daylight rested gently on the low stage edge. Quinn walked once more past the place where they had sat and felt the calm of the finished song still inside them. Mama held out her hand, and Quinn took it without rush.

They stood together near the window while Farah slipped on her sweater. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Quinn leaned against Mama’s side and listened to the quiet settle fully over the small school room, knowing the gentler song would wait for them again whenever they were ready to ask.
