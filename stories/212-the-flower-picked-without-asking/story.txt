The small yellow flower rested in Femi's cupped hands, its five silky petals still cool and slightly damp where the stem had snapped clean from the potting soil. Late afternoon light poured through the kitchen window and turned each petal a deeper gold, so Femi could see the tiny freckles of pollen dusting the center. The stem felt smooth and green between Femi's fingers, and a few dark crumbs of earth clung to the broken end. Femi turned the flower slowly, watching the light slide across it, and felt the quiet weight of knowing it had come from a pot that was not theirs.

At the kitchen table the jam jar sat open beside a soft slab of butter on a blue plate, and the shared pencil tin waited with its lid half off, pencils and crayons resting inside like sleepy sticks. Warm air from the oven brushed Femi's cheeks and smelled faintly of baking bread. The wooden tabletop felt smooth under Femi's elbows as they sat very still, the flower tucked close against their shirt. Outside, soft light kept changing on the wall, and Femi listened to the gentle tick that came from somewhere near the stove.

Femi almost slid the flower under the napkin pile so no one would see. Their hands hovered, ready to hide the bright yellow bloom among the folded cloth. The butter looked creamy and thick in the warm kitchen light, and the jam glowed ruby red. Femi's heart beat a little faster while they stared at the jam spoon and the butter knife lying side by side. The polished pebble Ms. Okonkwo kept as a paperweight for shopping lists felt cool and glassy when Femi's free hand brushed it by accident, and that smooth coolness made Femi pause.

Ms. Okonkwo stepped into the kitchen with a gentle smile, her apron still carrying the green smell of basil she had just snipped for supper. She set a basket of clean napkins on the table edge and looked at Femi with quiet kindness. "Femi, you seem thoughtful this afternoon," she said in a soft voice that matched the warm room. Femi's fingers tightened around the flower stem. Ren sat across the table drawing with a blue crayon from the shared pencil tin, the crayon tip making a quiet scrape on the paper. Soft light fell across Ren's page and across Femi's worried face.

Femi took a slow breath and held the flower up where the light could touch it. "I picked this flower from your pot by the window," Femi said, voice steady and clear. "It looked so bright and I wanted to hold it, but it was not mine to take." Ms. Okonkwo's eyes stayed gentle. She pulled out a chair and sat so her face was level with Femi's. The tin clock on the shelf gave one clear tick that marked the quiet moment. Butter and jam waited untouched while Femi waited too, the flower still warm from their hands.

"Thank you for telling me the plain truth, Femi," Ms. Okonkwo said, and her words felt as soft as the butter on the plate. "We can mend what can be mended." She reached for a small clean jar and a spoonful of water from the jug. Ren looked up from the drawing and said, "I can help hold the jar steady if you like." Femi nodded and carefully placed the stem into the water so the yellow petals floated upright again. The kitchen light made the water sparkle around the green stem, and the jam jar nearby caught the same glow.

Together they carried the little jar to the windowsill where the original pot sat, its soil still loose and dark. Ms. Okonkwo showed Femi how to press the remaining stem piece gently back into the soft earth beside its sister plants so the pot looked whole again. "The plant will grow new leaves," she said, "and your honesty helps everything feel right." Femi's shoulders loosened. Ren brought the shared pencil tin over so they could draw a small picture of the flower for the fridge. The butter knife clinked softly when Femi moved it aside to make room for the paper, and warm air from the oven still brushed their arms.

Femi and Ren folded the clean napkins so each stitched pear faced neatly upward, the cloth cool and crisp under their fingers. Ms. Okonkwo spread a thin layer of butter on toast and added a swirl of jam for each of them as a quiet thank-you snack. The yellow flower stood happily in its water jar on the sill, petals open to the fading light. Femi felt the smooth edge of the table and watched the jam shine like rubies. "I feel lighter now," Femi said, and Ren smiled around a bite of toast. The kitchen stayed peaceful, full of ordinary good things side by side.

Later the room grew softer as evening settled in. Femi sat once more at the table, the pencil tin closed, the jam lid screwed tight, and the butter covered for morning. They listened to the house settle into its nighttime hush and felt the last warmth of the day on their bare feet. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Femi rested both hands on the cool wood and thought of the yellow flower safe in water, the pot mended, and the kind quiet that followed telling the truth.
