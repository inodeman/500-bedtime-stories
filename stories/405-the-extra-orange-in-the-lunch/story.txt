Papa was already planing a long pine board across the low workbench when Ximena stepped into the workshop, the soft afternoon light falling in pale gold bars across the floorboards and warming the air like a quiet blanket. Tools hung ready on a low peg within easy reach, their metal edges catching little sparks of brightness. Papa’s hands moved in steady strokes, and the faint scent of cut wood mixed with something gentler nearby. Ximena stood still a moment, listening to the hush of the plane sliding back and forth. “The grain is opening up nicely today,” Papa said without looking up, his voice calm and low. Ximena nodded and set their small pack down near the bench, already glad to be there with him in the gentle warmth.

Ximena pulled out the lunch box Papa had packed that morning and rested it on a clear corner of the bench where the light made the lid shine. Inside, beside the sandwich wrapped in a neat square of cloth, sat an extra orange, round and bright as a small sun. The peel looked smooth and cool. Ximena touched it once with one fingertip and felt a quiet surprise rise in their chest. Papa had thought of the extra fruit without being asked. The workshop stayed still except for the distant click of a gate latch somewhere outside in the yard, a soft metal sound that settled into the quiet like a polite knock. Ximena closed the lid again and looked at Papa’s bent shoulders, deciding a quick thank-you would feel too thin for a kindness already waiting there.

They wandered a few steps toward the far wall while Papa kept working, and their palm brushed against a damp clay wall that stayed cool even in the afternoon warmth, the texture soft and faintly gritty under their skin. Ximena pressed their hand there a second longer, letting the coolness steady them. Around the low peg the tools waited in a loose row, a hammer handle sticking out farther than the rest and a small brush lying sideways on the floor. Ximena crouched, gathered the brush, and hung it back where it belonged so the peg held everything in easy order again. The clay coolness still lingered on their fingertips as they stood. Papa glanced over once, still planing, and the light on the board turned a deeper honey color while Ximena decided what else they might do without being told.

Back at the bench Ximena found a short length of clean fresh paper Papa had set aside for sketching measurements, and the crisp smell of fresh paper rose up when they smoothed it flat with both hands. They took the soft pencil lying nearby and carefully drew the outline of the pine board the way Papa liked, marking the places where the corners would meet. The pencil made a tiny whispering sound against the page. “I marked the corners the way you showed me last time,” Ximena said, holding the paper so Papa could see. Papa set the plane down, wiped his hands on a cloth, and studied the neat lines. “That will save us time when we fit the joints,” he answered, smiling with his eyes. The smell of the paper stayed light and clean in the air between them while the golden light continued its slow slide across the floor.

Ximena carried the paper to the other end of the bench and weighted it with a smooth river stone so it would not curl. Then they noticed the little pile of wood shavings gathering near Papa’s boots and fetched the short-handled broom from its place beside the low peg. They swept the shavings into a neat heap and tipped them into the scrap bin under the bench, the bristles making a soft whisking sound against the boards. The air felt cooler near the floor where the sweeping stirred a faint draft. Papa watched for a moment and then returned to checking the board’s edge with his thumb. Ximena kept the motion calm and thorough, wanting the space to feel ready before they spoke about the orange still waiting in the lunch box.

When the floor looked tidy again Ximena returned to the lunch box, opened it once more, and lifted the extra orange into the open so its bright skin caught the light. They set it gently beside the sandwich and rested both palms on their knees while they sat on the low stool Papa kept for them. The wood of the stool felt smooth and steady under them. Papa came over, wiped a last curl of shaving from his sleeve, and sat on the edge of the bench. The workshop held a peaceful quiet broken only by the soft creak of the stool. Ximena looked at the orange and then at Papa’s kind face, feeling the words line up plainly in their mind at last.

“I noticed the extra orange you packed beside the sandwich,” Ximena said, their voice steady and clear in the warm room. “It was already there when I opened the box, and I wanted to do something useful you did not ask for.” Papa’s eyes crinkled at the corners. He reached out and gave the orange a gentle roll so it turned once under the light. “I thought you might like it after we finish the edges,” he said. Ximena nodded and added, “The tools are back on the peg and the corners are marked on the paper, and the shavings are gone.” The orange smelled bright and sweet when Papa broke the peel just a little with his thumbnail, and the juice scent mixed with the earlier wood and paper in a friendly way.

They shared the orange sections slowly, the cool fruit tasting clean after the work, while the last of the afternoon light softened toward evening and the temperature in the workshop eased into a gentler coolness that felt kind on bare arms. Papa finished the final edge of the board and leaned it safely against the wall near the low peg. Ximena wiped their fingers on a clean cloth and placed the empty peel pieces into the scrap bin so nothing sticky remained on the bench. Outside, the yard had grown quieter. Inside, the two of them sat without hurry, the orange sweetness still on their tongues, and Ximena felt the workshop holding them both in its settled calm. Papa rested one hand on the finished board and said, “We made good progress together today.” Ximena answered, “I like working here with you,” and the words felt true and simple in the cooling air.

Later, when the board was covered with a soft cloth and the tools hung straight once more on the low peg, Ximena and Papa closed the lunch box and set it by the door ready for morning. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Its gentle circle of light reached across the bench and touched the edge of the stool where Ximena had rested both palms on their knees earlier, and the warm yellow glow made the whole workshop feel sleepy and safe as they stood to go.
