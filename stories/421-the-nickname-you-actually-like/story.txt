"Good morning, Yuri," Cousin Pio said from beside the stove, and the soft yellow light of the early sun slipped through the cottage kitchen window and rested on the wooden counters like warm honey. Yuri stood on the smooth cool floorboards and felt the gentle heat rising from the porridge pot as it made tiny popping sounds. The air around Yuri felt kind and steady on this ordinary kind morning. Yuri looked at Cousin Pio and heard the name spoken the full way they had asked, clear and unhurried, and the words settled softly inside Yuri like a favorite blanket being pulled up. The light brightened the hanging pans and the blue cups lined on the shelf. Yuri took one slow step closer, bare feet quiet, and watched steam curl upward in little silver ribbons that disappeared into the bright air.

Yuri moved nearer the table and noticed again how carefully Cousin Pio had said the name the full way. The kitchen smelled clean and welcoming, and Yuri rested one hand on the edge of the worn wooden table where the grain felt familiar under their fingers. Outside the open lower pane a soft breeze drifted in, carrying the faint smell of rain on dust from the path that had been wet overnight and was now drying in patches. Yuri breathed it in once and felt glad the morning stayed peaceful. Cousin Pio kept stirring without fuss, and Yuri stayed close, watching the spoon move in slow circles. The light on the floor made pale squares that Yuri stepped through carefully. Everything felt ordinary and kind, and Yuri kept the sound of their own full name close, like something precious already given.

Yuri thought a quick thank-you would feel too thin for something that mattered so much. The warmth from the stove reached Yuri’s cheeks in a gentle wave while they looked around for one useful thing Cousin Pio had not asked for. The cottage kitchen held quiet order, and Yuri spotted the stack of clean napkins waiting near the breadboard. A pale satin ribbon lay coiled beside them, smooth and cool when Yuri touched it. Yuri picked up the ribbon and felt its soft sliding texture between their fingers before deciding to fold each napkin neatly and tie a simple bow. The light from the window made the ribbon shine a little as Yuri worked. Cousin Pio hummed a low tune that matched the soft bubble of the porridge, and Yuri stayed focused, making the folds even and the bows tidy without needing any direction.

Yuri finished the last napkin and set the small stack beside the plates so the bows rested like quiet decorations. The air near the window felt cooler than the air by the stove, and Yuri liked the gentle change on their arms. Cousin Pio glanced over once with a calm smile but said nothing yet, still tending the pot. Yuri wiped a few crumbs from the breadboard next, brushing them into their palm and carrying them to the scrap bowl without being asked. The floorboards felt steady under Yuri’s feet as they moved back and forth. Outside, somewhere near the garden path, a gate latch clicked shut with a clear metal sound that drifted in on the breeze and then faded. Yuri paused only a moment to notice it before returning to the useful quiet work.

“Would you like the bowls set out now?” Yuri asked, keeping their voice even and kind. Cousin Pio turned and nodded. “Yes, please, Yuri,” Cousin Pio answered, using the full name again just as asked. Yuri felt the words land gently and carried three blue bowls to the table, placing them carefully so the rims caught the morning light. The pottery felt smooth and slightly cool against Yuri’s palms. Steam from the porridge still rose in soft curls, and Yuri watched it while arranging the spoons in a neat row. The cottage kitchen stayed peaceful, filled with ordinary sounds of wood and water and quiet breath. Yuri added the napkins with their satin bows last of all, stepping back once to see that everything looked ready and helpful.

Yuri poured a little water into the small pitcher and set it near the cups so no one would need to ask for it later. The temperature near the open window stayed pleasantly fresh against Yuri’s wrists. Cousin Pio lifted the pot and began to ladle the warm porridge into the bowls, and the soft sound of it landing was almost like a quiet song. Yuri stood nearby, close enough to smell the mild grain scent rising with the steam. The light had grown a little brighter now, turning the edges of the copper pans golden. Yuri kept their hands busy wiping one last spot from the counter with a clean cloth, still offering the useful help that had not been requested. Everything felt steady and kind inside the cottage kitchen on this ordinary morning.

“I noticed you said my name the full way I asked,” Yuri said plainly while standing near the table. Cousin Pio looked up and smiled again. “I remembered because it matters,” Cousin Pio replied in a calm voice. Yuri felt a quiet gladness settle in their chest like warm tea. The light painted a soft rectangle across the floor where Yuri’s shadow rested without hurry. Outside the window the path looked clean after the night’s light rain, and the air inside stayed gentle. Yuri reached for the cloth once more and folded it neatly before hanging it on its hook. The cottage kitchen held them both in its ordinary kindness, and Yuri stayed close, content that the useful thing had been done and the noticing had been spoken aloud without rush.

Yuri wandered to the window where a thin film of fog still clung to the cooler glass from the earlier chill. With one dry finger Yuri traced a slow careful circle through the soft mist, watching the clear path appear in the shape of a round quiet moon. The glass felt smooth and slightly cold under the fingertip. Cousin Pio carried the bowls the last short distance and set them down with soft clinks. “Thank you for the napkins and the ready table, Yuri,” Cousin Pio said, once more using the full name exactly as asked. Yuri smiled and kept the circle clear on the glass a moment longer, feeling the morning settle around them both like a gentle hold. The light through the traced circle looked brighter, and the cottage kitchen remained peaceful and ordinary and kind.

Yuri returned to the table and sat on the familiar wooden chair whose seat felt smooth and welcoming under them. The porridge smelled mild and comforting as Yuri lifted the spoon. Cousin Pio sat across and they began the quiet meal together without hurry. Outside the last of the night’s coolness faded, and the path dried further under the rising sun. Yuri glanced once more toward the window and the circle they had drawn, then back to the simple food and the calm face of Cousin Pio. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
