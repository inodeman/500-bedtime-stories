Mama stood at the kitchen table before breakfast with the chairs pulled close, already smoothing a folded paper against the wood while the pale morning light rested on the clean plates and made the air feel cool against bare arms. Ilaria climbed into the near chair and smelled the faint rain on dust that drifted in from the open back door after the night shower. Mama’s hands moved slowly, and the paper made a soft whisper each time a corner lifted. Ilaria felt the smooth seat under their legs and watched Mama’s calm face. Outside a gate latch clicked once and then fell quiet again. Mama looked up and smiled the way she did when the day was still soft and nothing had to hurry.

“I wrote a list of recital worries that needed the last one said too,” Mama said, tapping the paper with one finger so Ilaria could see the neat lines. Ilaria’s bigger news pressed at the front of their mind like a bright balloon, yet they kept their mouth closed and leaned closer because Mama’s voice sounded warm and steady. The kitchen light glowed gentle and yellow over the table. Ilaria rested both elbows near the edge and listened to the quiet tick of the wall clock. Mama’s sleeve brushed the paper with a light swish. The chairs sat so near that Ilaria could feel the steady warmth of Mama’s shoulder almost touching theirs.

Mama began near the top. “First I worried the notes might scatter if my fingers felt cold,” she said, and the words came slow enough for Ilaria to picture each one. Ilaria wanted to jump in with the news about Opal’s new song, yet they breathed in and out and stayed still. A thin ribbon of steam rose from Mama’s mug and carried a mild leafy scent. Ilaria’s bare feet found the cool floorboards and pressed gently. The paper looked ordinary, but Mama treated every line as if it mattered. Outside the window the sky stayed soft gray, and the light inside kept the room kind.

“Next I worried the satin ribbon on my music folder might slip,” Mama continued, and she let Ilaria touch the edge of the list where the ink sat smooth. Ilaria felt the satin ribbon that Mama had brought from the folder itself, cool and sleek between their fingers for a moment before letting go. The bigger news still waited behind Ilaria’s teeth, but they nodded and kept listening all the way. A soft clink sounded when Mama set her spoon down. The air near the table held a gentle morning chill that made Ilaria draw their cardigan closer. Mama’s eyes stayed kind, and the chairs stayed close.

Mama turned the paper a little. “Then I worried I might forget to smile at the end,” she said, and her voice stayed even. Ilaria’s shoulders loosened because the room felt safe and the light stayed steady on the tabletop. They could almost taste the quiet of the early hour. Mama’s handwriting looked careful, each worry written without hurry. Ilaria traced a small path on the wooden grain with one fingertip and heard the distant hush of a bird outside. The list kept unfolding, and Ilaria still held the bigger news inside like a secret pebble.

“I also worried the bench might creak too loudly,” Mama said, smiling a little at her own past self. Ilaria wanted to speak about the thing Opal had whispered yesterday, yet they waited because Mama had promised the last one needed saying too. The kitchen smelled faintly of toast beginning to warm in the toaster. Ilaria’s palms rested on the table and felt the smooth worn places where many breakfasts had happened. Soft light moved across Mama’s cheek when she shifted. The paper lay flat now, and only two lines remained.

Mama reached the next-to-last line and read it softly. “I worried I might look only at my own hands and miss the friendly faces,” she said. Ilaria stayed quiet and listened hard, noticing how Mama’s breath slowed between sentences. A cool draft touched Ilaria’s ankles for a second and then left. The chairs stayed close enough that their knees nearly brushed. Mama’s finger slid down to the final space on the list, and Ilaria felt ready for whatever came next. The room held a peaceful hush broken only by the gentle hum of the refrigerator.

“The last worry was that I might never say any of these out loud and then carry them alone,” Mama finished, folding the paper once with care. Ilaria asked one real question. “Did saying the last one make the others feel lighter too?” they said, voice small and clear in the quiet kitchen. Mama nodded and the morning light brightened a little across the plates. Ilaria felt the cool rim of their own empty cup under their hand. Only then did Ilaria share something small of their own. “Opal and I practiced the soft ending twice, and it sounded like rain on leaves,” Ilaria said, and the words came easy after all that listening. Mama’s smile grew warm, and the toaster clicked off with a tiny sound.

Ilaria leaned their head toward Mama’s shoulder while the chairs stayed close and the air felt gentle and still. They watched a faint circle of fog on the lower window pane and traced it once with a dry finger until it faded. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Mama set the folded list aside, and Ilaria smelled the clean morning kitchen again. Outside everything stayed calm, and inside the two of them sat together with nothing left unsaid on the paper and nothing heavy left between them.
