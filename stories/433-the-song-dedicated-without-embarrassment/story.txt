"Mr. Ade, may I sit in the library corner with the low shelves today?" Kito asked as they felt the mild warmth of sunlight on their shoulders through the tall glass. The corner held a sturdy wooden table and chairs, and soft golden light spilled across the floor in wide gentle bands that made everything look friendly and still. Kito walked forward on the quiet rug and rested one hand on the smooth tabletop, enjoying how cool the wood felt under their palm. Mr. Ade smiled and nodded from where he stood near a cart of books. A light breeze moved the curtain just enough to let more air in, and Kito settled into a seat ready to look at the volumes waiting nearby. They liked the peaceful way the light touched the paper edges and the calm hush that filled the space around them as they breathed slowly.

Kito noticed a book lying open with a pale paper ribbon marking a special page, and they lifted it carefully so the ribbon would not slip out. On that page someone had written the words of a song dedicated in a small voice that still counted, and the letters looked neat and cherished under Kito’s gaze. Kito ran a finger along the edge of the ribbon and felt how thin and smooth it was against their skin. The light from the window brightened the ink just enough for easy reading, and Kito could almost sense the quiet tune hanging in the air. Golda sat nearby turning pages of her own story, the soft sound of paper joining the stillness. Kito held the book open on their lap and studied the careful way the page had been kept flat and clean for anyone who came to visit this corner of the library.

It became clear to Kito that Mr. Ade had already taken care of the song dedicated in a small voice that still counted, because the page edges were mended with tiny neat strips and a fresh backing kept it from bending. Mr. Ade moved along the shelves wearing his soft cotton apron, the texture of the cloth brushing against book spines as he shelved returns with steady hands. Kito watched him and felt glad for the quiet order of the place. Cool air drifted from the window and touched Kito’s cheeks while they kept the book steady on the table. Outside a bird called once and then fell silent again. Kito closed the cover gently so the paper ribbon stayed exactly where it belonged and set the book in the middle of the table for others to find later if they wished.

A simple thank-you seemed too thin to Kito after seeing all that care, so they decided to do one useful thing Mr. Ade had not asked for at all. The chairs around the table sat at odd angles from people who had left earlier, and Kito stood up to set the chair square to the table, pushing each leg into place until the seats lined up neatly side by side. The wooden backs felt firm and slightly cool under their hands as they worked without hurry. Warm light still bathed the corner and made the polished floor gleam softly near their shoes. Golda looked up and said, "The table looks ready now." Kito smiled at their friend and finished adjusting the last chair, enjoying the small task in the calm room filled with gentle brightness.

Next Kito knelt by the lower shelf and stood several leaning picture books upright so their colorful covers faced outward in a straight cheerful row that pleased the eye. The glossy surfaces felt slick and cool against Kito’s fingertips as they slid each volume into place with care. While they worked the distant sound of a gate latch clicked once in the garden outside, a small clear noise that came and went without disturbing the library hush. Kito paused to listen and then continued until every book stood tall and welcoming. The air near the floor held a cooler temperature than higher up, and Kito liked the difference as they rose again to their feet. Mr. Ade kept sorting quietly across the room, still unaware of the extra help arriving in his corner.

Kito spotted a shallow tray of spare pencils and bookmarks on the side and lined the pencils up from shortest to longest so they would be easy to choose later. The tray wood felt smooth and dry under their touch as they finished the row. A gentle smell of toasted sesame drifted in through the window from the courtyard kitchen where someone prepared an afternoon treat, and Kito inhaled the warm nutty scent with quiet happiness that filled their chest. They looked toward Mr. Ade and knew the moment had come to speak plainly about what they had seen. Golda closed her book with a soft thump and waited patiently nearby. Kito wiped a speck of dust from the tray edge and stood ready, feeling the soft rug beneath their shoes as they took a calm step forward.

Kito approached Mr. Ade and said, "I noticed that you already took care of the song dedicated in a small voice that still counted in the book with the paper ribbon." Mr. Ade turned and gave Kito his full attention while the mild light rested on both of them like a shared blanket. Kito added, "The page is safe because of you, and I wanted you to know I saw it." The temperature near the shelves stayed comfortable and even around their shoulders. Mr. Ade answered, "Thank you for telling me, Kito. That song matters to me too." Kito felt the quiet satisfaction of words spoken clearly in the peaceful air. They stood together for a moment listening to the faint rustle of leaves beyond the glass before Kito stepped back toward the table with easy steps.

After that Kito returned to their squared chair and opened a picture book filled with drawings of meadows and gentle streams, letting their eyes follow the soft colors while the window light began to mellow toward late afternoon. The pages made a tiny whisper as they turned, a sound that fitted the peaceful corner perfectly and soothed Kito’s ears. Golda leaned over and whispered, "Shall we go soon?" but Kito shook their head slightly to ask for one more quiet minute among the books. The air felt pleasantly still around them, neither warm nor cool now that the strongest sun had moved farther along the sky. Kito rested their elbows on the table and enjoyed the solid feel of the wood supporting them while the last of the day’s brightness lingered on the spines of the nearby volumes.

Kito finally closed the picture book and looked across at Mr. Ade with a calm smile, glad they had done the useful thing and spoken about what they had noticed in the corner. Evening settled gently outside yet the library corner kept its welcome for anyone who lingered. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Kito breathed in the remaining hush and felt their shoulders relax in the mild temperature of the room as shadows grew longer on the rug. They knew the paper ribbon would mark the song for the next visitor, and the care Mr. Ade had given would continue without fuss. With one last glance at the tidy chairs and straight books, Kito rose ready for home, carrying the quiet of the corner with them like a soft blanket of light and stillness that stayed close.
