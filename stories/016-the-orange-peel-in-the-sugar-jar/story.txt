The soft evening rain brushed the bakery roof with a gentle patter and sent a cool breath of air through the half-open window into the kitchen where dough rested under cloths and jam cooled in pots. Bibi stepped inside and felt the warm air wrap around their cheeks like a soft blanket after the chill outside. Golden light from the overhead bulbs pooled on the flour-dusted counters and made the wooden floor look honey-colored. Bibi rubbed their palms together and smelled the rising yeast mixing with fruit sweetness. “Mama, everything smells so calm in here tonight,” Bibi said while walking farther in. Mama looked up from the far counter and nodded. Bibi’s fingers traced the smooth edge of a mixing bowl as the warmth soaked into their skin.

Bibi noticed the tall glass sugar jar sitting near the window sill, its lid resting loosely on top and thin strips of orange peel floating inside the thick syrup like little golden boats. The light from the bulbs caught the sugar crystals and made them sparkle softly against the glass. Bibi leaned closer and felt the slight heat rising from the jar against their nose. They wanted to lift the lid right then and check if the peels had turned chewy and sweet. “Mama, may I open the jar and see the orange peel now?” Bibi asked, voice quiet as the rain outside. Mama wiped her hands on her apron and came nearer so Bibi could hear her clearly. The kitchen felt snug and still around them both.

Mama rested a gentle hand on Bibi’s shoulder and spoke in her low evening voice. “The peel needs more time in the sugar before it is ready, Bibi. It is still candying slowly.” Bibi looked at the covered jar again and felt the warm air of the kitchen on their bare arms. They nodded and kept their hands at their sides instead of reaching. “I will leave it covered then,” Bibi said. Mama smiled and pointed toward a stack of clean recipe sheets waiting on the side table. Bibi walked over and stood still for a moment, listening to the quiet settling of the dough under its cloths. The light stayed soft and steady on the papers.

Bibi picked up the top sheet carefully and felt the sharp paper edge slide under their thumb as they straightened the pile into a neat stack. They moved slowly so the papers would not rustle too loud. The task felt small and peaceful in the warm kitchen. Mama measured a spoon of jam nearby and the fruit scent drifted over. Bibi kept stacking until every sheet lined up straight, their fingers brushing the dry paper again and again. “This helps the kitchen stay tidy while we wait,” Bibi said to Mama. Mama answered, “Yes, and the dough and jam are finishing in their own time too.” Bibi smiled and set the last paper down flat.

A wool scarf Mama had worn earlier hung drying on a chair back near the door, and the faint smell of wet wool rose from it as the warm air moved. Bibi noticed the scent while they stood by the table and it mixed with the bakery sweetness without hurry. They breathed in and out once, slow and even. The sugar jar still sat covered and quiet on the sill. Bibi walked back toward Mama and watched the dough cloths stay perfectly still. Soft light touched the jam pots and made their red and purple colors glow. Bibi felt calm all the way down to their toes.

The tin clock on the high shelf ticked once with a clear small sound that marked another minute passing. Bibi heard it while standing beside Mama and matched their breathing to the quiet rhythm. The kettle on the stove began its own soft tick as heat built under it. Bibi rested both hands on the cool counter edge and stayed still. Mama poured a little water for later tea and the steam rose in a thin white curl. “We can wait together,” Mama said. Bibi answered, “I like waiting this way.” The kitchen held them in its gentle heat and the rain kept its soft song on the roof.

Bibi moved to the window and looked out at the misty evening without touching the jar. The orange peel floated inside its sugar bath, still working slowly under the lid. Light from the bulbs reflected on the glass and made tiny rainbows on the sill. Bibi felt the cooler air near the window on their face and then stepped back into the warmer middle of the room. They folded a clean kitchen towel into a neat square and smoothed it flat. Mama checked the dough by lifting one corner of a cloth just enough to peek, then set it down again. Everything in the bakery kitchen kept finishing at its own pace while Bibi stayed close.

Time drifted like the slow sugar syrup itself. Bibi helped Mama wipe a spot of flour from the counter with a damp cloth that felt cool and then warm again under their hands. The jam pots gave off a soft fruit smell that filled the quiet space. Bibi glanced once more at the sugar jar but left the lid exactly where it belonged. “I think it might be ready soon,” Bibi whispered. Mama nodded and said, “We will know when the time is right.” They stood side by side listening to the gentle settling of the whole kitchen. The dough looked plump and finished under its cloths now, and the jam had thickened just enough.

Mama lifted the lid of the sugar jar at last and the candied orange peel gleamed translucent and ready inside the clear syrup. Bibi leaned in carefully and breathed the bright citrus scent that rose up. They touched one cooled strip with a fingertip and felt its sweet sticky surface. Mama placed a few peels on a small plate for later. Bibi and Mama stood together in the peaceful kitchen while the dough and jam rested complete. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
