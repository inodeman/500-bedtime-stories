The tin clock on the shelf gave a soft even tick that Tala noticed right away when they entered the workshop. Tala stood still and let the calm sound settle while warm yellow light from the bulb poured over the low peg board filled with tools. Cool air from the window brushed Tala's arms. The floor felt smooth under their socks as they stepped toward the workbench. A bright smell of orange peel floated near the jar of nails from Papa's afternoon treat. Tala breathed it in and looked at the neat hammers and chisels hanging within reach. Shadows rested gently against the walls and the room felt ready for evening. Tala touched the solid wood of the doorframe before moving inside.

Near the window a lantern waited on a sturdy stool and Tala walked straight to it. The glass felt cool when Tala lifted it to look inside. Papa had already trimmed the lantern wick short and flat so the flame would burn upright and the walk would stay even. Soft light from the bulb mixed with dusk outside and made the metal top shine. Tala heard Papa sorting small parts at the bench with quiet clinks. The air around the stool felt warmer where the light concentrated. Tools on the low peg hung straight. Tala set the lantern down carefully and thought about the care in that trimmed wick.

A quick thank-you would feel too thin for something so thoughtful, Tala decided. They wanted to do one useful thing Papa had not asked for. Several tool handles had tilted so Tala straightened each one on the low peg until they hung true. The wooden grips felt worn smooth against Tala's palms and the metal stayed cool. Steady light showed every adjustment and made the board look orderly. Tala noticed the side sink with its single drip and chose that next. The temperature near the pegs stayed comfortable as Tala worked. Outside the sky deepened into soft purple without any wind.

Tala picked up the linen napkin from the bench and felt its crisp woven texture between their fingers. They dampened a corner under the tap and wiped the porcelain rim clean. Then Tala let the water trickle and closed their eyes to count twelve drips without peeking. Cool water touched their fingertips as the clear plinks sounded in the quiet. When the twelfth drip fell Tala shut the tap and dried their hands on the napkin. Warm light reflected off the gleaming sink. Papa watched with a gentle expression. Tala hung the napkin back on its hook.

"I noticed the lantern wick, Papa," Tala said plainly while walking back. Papa looked up and answered in a warm low voice. "I trimmed it this afternoon so your walk would be even," he said. The air between them felt pleasant under the bulb's glow. Tala rested a hand on the cool workbench edge. "I straightened the tools on the low peg and cleaned the sink too," Tala told him. Papa smiled fully. "Thank you for doing those things, Tala," he replied with quiet pleasure. Light caught in his eyes and the room seemed softer. Tala felt the floor steady under their feet.

Together they checked the lantern again and Papa showed how the even wick sat in its holder. The glass chimney felt smooth under their touch. Mild warmth from the bulb kept chill away from their hands. Soft evening sounds drifted in from outside but stayed peaceful. Tala looked at the low peg board now perfectly arranged and felt glad for the help given freely. The workshop held a settled quiet. Papa closed the window halfway so cool air entered gently. Tala watched light pool on the bench like a calm surface. They stood without speaking for a short while and simply enjoyed the finished tasks.

Papa lifted the lantern and tested its balance while Tala stood close beside him. The metal base felt solid and ready for any even walk later. Golden light painted slow patterns across the tools and the clean sink. Tala rubbed their arms as the air cooled with full night. They matched Papa's calm breathing for several moments. Everything from the pegs to the stool seemed joined by the quiet care shared. Tala leaned lightly against Papa and felt the sturdy cloth of his shirt. The space smelled clean and woodsy with earlier scents gone. Soft light continued to hold the room in a friendly circle that felt safe and known.

They put the last screws into the proper drawer and wiped one remaining smudge from the bench edge. Tala's fingers traced the fine raised grain of the wood and felt its gentle texture. Steady light made the small task easy and pleasant rather than hurried. A comfortable warmth lingered in the air where they had worked side by side all this while. Outside a single leaf brushed against the window with the softest possible rustle. Tala handed Papa the last small tool for the low peg and watched him hang it exactly in place. The workshop now looked completely ready for night's rest and so did they both. Tala gave a small quiet yawn that came from deep contentment.

Papa rested one hand gently on Tala's shoulder as they both turned toward the window. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Cool night air slipped through the remaining gap and touched their faces with one last gentle brush. Tala felt the busy day folding quietly away into peace while the bulb overhead kept its low steady hum. They moved together toward the door that led to the rest of the house and the soft beds waiting. A thin path of light followed them across the worn floorboards. Tala stayed close beside Papa and carried the calm feeling of the workshop inside. All the useful care had been seen and met with care in return.
