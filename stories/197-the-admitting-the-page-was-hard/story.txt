The ferryman was already behind the bakery counter facing the street, lining up small jars of jam with quiet care while the fading daylight painted long stripes across the polished wood. Sora stood close enough to feel the leftover heat from the ovens on their bare arms and watched him place each jar just so. The page in Sora’s hand made a soft crinkle when they shifted their grip. Outside a bicycle bell tinkled once far away. Sora stayed silent at first, looking from the page to the kind face of the ferryman who moved with the same steady patience he used on the river.

Sora wanted to say the page had been hard, yet they still wished to try one sentence, but the words stuck behind their teeth. The counter felt smooth and slightly cool under Sora’s free hand as they leaned. A neighbor walked past the big window and the street light caught the silver of a button on a coat. Sora’s friend Kavi sat on a low stool nearby, humming a tiny tune that floated like steam through the shop. “I can wait,” the ferryman said gently without looking up from his jars. Sora pressed their lips together and felt shy heat rise in their cheeks while the humming continued soft and low.

From a nearby shelf the warm smell of honey drifted over as the ferryman lifted a sticky lid to check the pot of glaze. Sora’s nose twitched at the sweet scent and they almost smiled in surprise. Kavi swung their legs and the wooden stool creaked once under the motion. Sora unfolded the page carefully so the writing showed under the counter lamp’s glow. The letters looked big and a little crooked from earlier practice at home. Sora took a slow breath that carried that sweet scent and flour mixed together and held the page flat with both hands.

The ferryman wiped his chalky hand on his apron after marking a small list on the board beside him with a stub of white chalk. Sora noticed the dry white dust that lingered on his fingers before it vanished into the cloth folds. “That page looks important,” the ferryman said in his quiet river voice that never rushed. Sora nodded and felt the paper’s rough edge against their thumb pad. Soft light from the counter lamp made the written words glow gently on the cream paper. Sora’s heart beat a little faster but the bakery shop stayed calm and safe around them all.

A gate latch clicked shut somewhere in the back alley, a short metallic sound that settled into the quiet like a soft period at the end of a sentence. Sora heard it clearly and glanced toward the rear door for a second. The ferryman only smiled and kept stacking clean cups into a neat row. “You can tell me whatever you like,” he said, “or you can just try a little if that feels better.” Sora’s shoulders dropped a fraction at the kind tone. Warm air still curled around their ankles from the space under the counter where the ovens breathed.

Sora decided on the smallest brave version right then. They lifted their chin and spoke in a voice that could be heard clearly across the quiet counter space. “This page was hard,” Sora said, “but I still want to try one sentence.” The ferryman’s eyes crinkled with kindness and he did not ask for more or push for any show of reading the whole thing. He simply nodded once in understanding. Kavi clapped two soft hands together without making any real noise. Sora felt the smooth side of the page under their palm as they smoothed it flat on the wood.

The ferryman poured a little warm milk into a small cup and set it near Sora so the thin steam rose in a white ribbon that brushed their wrist. Sora smelled the plain milk and felt its gentle heat near their skin. “Would you like me to listen while you try?” the ferryman asked with no hurry in the words. Sora nodded again and pointed to the first line on the page. They sounded out the words slowly, one careful sound after another while the milk steamed. The street outside grew darker blue and a single moth tapped the window glass with a tiny tick that barely reached inside.

Sora finished the sentence and let the last word hang in the warm bakery air for a moment. The ferryman gave a small proud smile that reached all the way to his eyes. Kavi whispered, “That was good, Sora.” Sora felt light and quiet inside, as if a small knot had loosened in their chest. They rested their elbows on the counter and watched the jars of jam catch the lamp light like little jewels in a row. The page no longer felt quite so stiff between their fingers. Outside the bakery the evening cooled a degree more and the blue deepened.

Sora folded the page once more and tucked it carefully into their pocket while the ferryman finished lining the last of his jars. They waited through a single chime from the old clock on the wall, letting the clear note fade completely before they moved their feet. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Sora leaned against the counter one last time, feeling its solid wood under their cheek for a short rest, then straightened and breathed the last of the bakery air deep into their lungs. The ferryman waved them toward the door with a calm open hand, and Sora stepped out into the gentle night with Kavi walking quietly beside them, the tried sentence still warm and steady in their mind like a small lantern of its own.
