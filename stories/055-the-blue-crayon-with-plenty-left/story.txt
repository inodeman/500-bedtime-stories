The baker was already kneeling on the picnic cloth in the calm meadow, arranging small jars of jam and slices of bread into careful circles while the afternoon sun laid a mild golden warmth across everything. Isla walked up with Kei and felt that same gentle heat settle on their shoulders like a soft blanket. The cloth felt smooth under Isla’s palms as they sat down, and the baker glanced up with a calm expression. “I have set the picnic almost ready,” the baker said in a low voice. Isla watched the baker’s hands move without hurry and noticed how the light made the jam glow like tiny jewels. Kei settled beside Isla and rested their own hands on the cloth. A quiet breeze drifted past, carrying the open scent of grass, and Isla breathed it in while the baker placed one last spoon in the center.

Isla looked toward the middle of the cloth and saw a blue crayon that still had plenty left resting beside a sketchbook the baker had brought for drawing the shapes of fruit and flowers. The crayon’s paper wrapper felt slightly rough when Isla picked it up for a moment, and they turned it slowly between their fingers. Kei leaned closer so they could see the bright blue tip. “That crayon looks perfect for drawing the sky above our meadow,” Kei said softly. Isla held the crayon a little longer and smelled the faint beeswax that rose from its waxy surface. The baker continued unpacking cups without speaking, letting the two friends examine the crayon in the warm light. Isla noticed how the blue color matched the clear sky overhead and felt glad the crayon had so much still unused.

Isla set the blue crayon back down for a moment and listened as the wind in the fig leaves made a gentle rustling sound high above the picnic cloth. The leaves shifted and settled again, and Isla tilted their head to catch every quiet movement. Kei pointed toward the nearby fig tree whose branches reached over one edge of the meadow. “The leaves sound like they are whispering to each other,” Kei said. Isla nodded and watched a single leaf drift down to land near the cloth. The baker poured cool water into two cups and set them within easy reach. Isla felt the air grow a tiny bit cooler in the shade of the tree and rubbed their arms lightly. Everything stayed peaceful while the crayon waited in the center like an invitation.

Isla picked up the blue crayon once more and thought about how it would not feel fair if only one person was served first with the chance to draw. They looked at Kei and then at the long blue stick that still had plenty left. The light filtered softly through the fig branches and made little moving patterns on the cloth. Isla turned the crayon carefully and decided a smaller, even share would let both of them begin together. “I can break this into two shorter pieces so we each have one,” Isla said. Kei smiled and held out both hands ready. The baker watched from the far side of the cloth and simply nodded once, letting the choice stand without a speech. Isla felt the solid wax snap cleanly under gentle pressure and handed the even half to Kei right away.

Isla and Kei each held their smaller piece of blue crayon and began drawing simple shapes on the open pages of the sketchbook. Isla drew a wide circle for the sun while the cloth stayed smooth beneath their elbows. Kei added curved lines for clouds beside it. The baker passed them a plate of sliced peaches that smelled sweet and fresh. “Thank you for the peaches,” Isla said as they took a soft piece. The fruit felt cool and juicy against Isla’s tongue, and a drop of juice rested on their finger until they wiped it carefully. Kei colored a patch of blue near the bottom of the page to show the picnic cloth itself. Isla added little dots for crumbs and felt completely content sharing the crayon this way.

After a while Isla set their half of the crayon down and reached toward a fig leaf that had fallen close to the edge of the cloth. They turned the leaf over and touched a leaf’s soft underside, noticing how velvety it felt against the pad of their finger. The texture made Isla smile. Kei watched and then gently touched the same leaf too. The baker offered small squares of cheese that looked pale yellow in the light. Isla took one and chewed slowly while the meadow stayed calm all around. A tiny snail appeared on a flat stone near the cloth, and Isla and Kei paused their drawing to watch it glide patiently from one side to the other. The air remained pleasantly warm, and no one felt any need to rush.

Isla picked up the blue crayon half again and added a row of little flowers along the bottom of the page while Kei drew a basket beside them. The baker placed a small bowl of berries between the two friends so each could reach easily. “These berries are ripe and ready,” the baker said simply. Isla chose a dark one and felt its smooth skin before tasting the bright flavor. Kei did the same and then returned to coloring a blue bird in the corner of the paper. The light began to soften just a little as the afternoon moved forward, yet the meadow kept its gentle quiet. Isla listened to the soft scratch of crayon on paper and felt glad both of them could keep drawing side by side.

When the pictures felt finished, Isla and Kei placed their blue crayon pieces back in the middle of the cloth so they could rest together. The baker began packing the empty plates with unhurried movements. Isla stretched their legs and felt the cool grass beyond the cloth brush against their ankles. Kei closed the sketchbook carefully and handed it to the baker for safekeeping. A few remaining crumbs dotted the cloth, and Isla brushed them into a small pile with the side of their hand. The temperature stayed mild and comfortable. Isla looked once more at the even blue pieces and felt a quiet happiness that both friends had used them.

As the picnic ended Isla and Kei helped fold the cloth into neat layers while the baker gathered the basket. They walked slowly toward the edge of the meadow where a small path led home between the trees. The sky held the last soft light of day, and Isla carried the folded cloth against their chest so it felt warm from the sun. Kei walked close beside them, and the baker followed with steady steps. When they reached the familiar doorway Isla stepped inside first and saw that everything waited ready and calm. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
