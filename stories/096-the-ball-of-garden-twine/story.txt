The mild evening air slipped into the courtyard and rested on the long table beneath the fig tree, cool as fresh shade on bare skin. Niko walked across the worn stones and felt that same gentle coolness settle on their wrists and cheeks. Golden light from the low sun lay across the plates and the green figs hanging overhead, and Niko heard the soft rustle of leaves whenever a breath of wind moved them. Grandpa Sol sat near the middle of the table with his hands folded, and Walid already occupied the chair beside him, smiling at Niko. The air carried the quiet smell of warm bread and crushed mint from the bowls. Niko pulled out a chair and sat, pressing their palms against the smooth wood that still held a little daytime warmth.

Grandpa Sol reached into a basket and lifted a neat ball of garden twine meant for two bean rows, the brown cord wound tight and ready. Niko watched the ball turn in Grandpa Sol’s fingers and noticed a small paper tag still tucked into the outer loops. Niko brushed one fingertip along a paper edge and felt its dry, thin scrape before letting the hand fall back to the table. Walid leaned closer to look. “Is that for the beans tomorrow?” Walid asked. Niko nodded and answered, “Two straight rows beside the wall.” The light under the fig tree softened to honey color while the three of them looked at the twine together.

Supper plates arrived one by one, and the steam from the stew rose in pale ribbons that Niko could feel warming their chin. Grandpa Sol set the ball of garden twine in the open space near the bread so everyone could see it while they ate. Niko took a careful bite and tasted the mild herbs, then glanced at Walid’s empty spot for the serving spoon. A tin clock somewhere inside the house doorway began its steady little click, marking the quiet minutes. Niko listened to that gentle sound and felt glad the evening moved slowly. Grandpa Sol poured water into three cups and the liquid made a soft pouring note against the glass.

Niko looked again at the ball of garden twine and thought about the two bean rows. If the whole ball went first to only one person, Walid might wait and wait while the rows were already planned. Niko set down the spoon and said, “We can make smaller even pieces so everyone helps.” Grandpa Sol only watched with kind eyes and did not speak. Walid’s face brightened in the dimming light. Niko felt the cool evening air slip once more across their bare ankles under the table. The fig leaves overhead made a faint papery whisper as they settled.

Niko picked up the ball of garden twine and began to unwind a length with careful fingers, the cord sliding with a dry whisking sound. They measured two equal parts against the length of their own arm and looped each piece into its own smaller ball, pressing the soft fiber until it held shape. “One for you and one for me,” Niko said to Walid, placing the second little ball beside Walid’s plate. Walid touched it and smiled. “Thank you, Niko. We can plant side by side.” The warm light of the courtyard lamps began to glow against the cooling stones, and Niko felt the pleasant temperature change on their shoulders.

Grandpa Sol passed the bread basket without a single extra word, letting Niko’s choice stay just as it was. Niko tore a piece of bread and smelled the faint scent of wet wool rising from the folded sweater draped over the nearby bench, left there after an earlier wash. The smell mixed gently with the fig leaves and stew. Walid wound their smaller ball of twine once around a finger and laughed a quiet laugh. Niko felt the tablecloth’s slight roughness under their elbows and watched the last orange edge of sky fade beyond the courtyard wall. Everything felt steady and shared.

They finished the meal while the fig tree’s shadow grew longer and softer across the long table. Niko stacked the empty bowls carefully so the ceramic made only a muted clink. “Shall we leave the twine right here for morning?” Grandpa Sol asked at last. Niko answered, “Yes, both pieces together.” Walid nodded and set their small ball next to Niko’s. The night air now carried a cooler touch that Niko felt along the back of their neck, pleasant after the warm food. Fireflies began to blink near the lower branches, tiny and calm.

Niko helped fold the cloth napkins and brushed crumbs into a small pile, listening to the distant tin clock keep its soft count from inside. Grandpa Sol rose and stretched, then rested one hand lightly on Niko’s shoulder for a moment. Walid stood too and yawned behind a polite hand. Niko felt the smooth coolness of the stone bench against their legs when they shifted. The courtyard lamps threw steady circles of light that did not flicker. Niko breathed in the quiet mix of fig and leftover mint and felt ready for rest.

Niko and Walid carried the two small balls of garden twine to the edge of the table where morning light would find them, then followed Grandpa Sol toward the open doorway. Inside, the rooms held a gentle warmth that touched Niko’s cheeks as soon as they stepped through. Niko paused by the sill and looked back once at the dark courtyard and the still fig tree. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Niko listened to the slow settling of the house and felt the day close around them like a familiar blanket, calm and even.
