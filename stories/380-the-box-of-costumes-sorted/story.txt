The costume box sorted into sizes rested open on the cottage room floor, its sturdy cardboard sides cool under the soft yellow light cast by dozens of paper stars dangling from the ceiling beams. Inside, neat stacks of cloaks, hats, and sashes sat divided by thin paper labels reading small, medium, and large, each pile smooth and ready. Isla crouched so near that they could see the tiny stitches on a green cape and feel the faint warmth still held in the fabric from the sunny afternoon. The long tablecloth on the big table nearby hung in quiet folds almost to the floor. Isla breathed slowly and studied every edge of the box so nothing would spill before the careful work began.

Isla looked up toward the table and spoke in a gentle voice. “Zev, the piles look perfect now, but they will go wrong if anyone rushes.” Zev stepped closer across the wooden boards, their footsteps soft, and the air near them carried a light sweetness. Paper stars swayed just enough to send slow moving shadows across the tablecloth. Isla rested one hand on the box rim and felt how solid it stayed. The two friends stood side by side in the calm room while the temperature stayed pleasantly mild near the open window. Isla kept their eyes on the sorted costumes and waited for the right slow plan.

Zev answered with a quiet smile. “We can split it into two slow jobs so every piece stays where it belongs.” Isla nodded and the baker appeared from the kitchen doorway carrying a small tray, the warm light from the paper stars touching the flour dust still on their apron. A faint scent of orange peel drifted in with the baker and mixed with the cottage air. Isla felt glad the baker was near even if only for a moment of help. The long tablecloth brushed Isla's wrist like a soft curtain as they leaned to point at the medium pile. Everything in the room stayed peaceful while the three of them looked at the open box together.

Isla said, “I will move the small costumes one by one onto the left side of the table.” Zev replied, “And I will place the large ones gently on the right side.” The baker set the tray down and offered only a calm suggestion. “You might need the knot called a simple loop if any sash comes loose, or you can count three slow breaths before each fold.” Isla listened and felt the cool evening air slip across their bare arms. Paper stars glowed steadier now that dusk settled outside. The friends began their separate slow jobs while the baker stayed nearby without hurrying anyone.

Isla lifted a small velvet vest and carried it with both hands to the left edge of the long tablecloth, feeling how the fabric stayed light and warm from being inside the box. Zev did the same with a large feathered hat, walking at the same easy pace so nothing tipped. A tin clock on the shelf ticked once in a clear bright note that marked the quiet seconds. Isla matched their breathing to that gentle sound and set the vest down without a wrinkle. The baker watched from a chair and smiled when Isla and Zev paused at the same moment to check each other. The paper stars hung still above them like tiny moons.

Isla smoothed a medium sash next and noticed a loose end that needed tying. “Baker, is the simple loop the right name for this knot?” The baker nodded once. “Yes, the simple loop will hold it kindly if you make it slow.” Isla followed the name and looped the sash end through itself while Zev waited with the next large cloak. The texture of a linen napkin under the tray felt crisp when Isla brushed it by accident, cool and flat against their palm. Soft light from the paper stars made the sash shine. Isla and Zev kept their two jobs steady and never once moved faster than a sleepy cat.

Zev placed three more large pieces in a careful row while Isla finished the small pile with the same patient hands. The long tablecloth caught every costume without a single slip because both friends worked side by side yet on their own slow tasks. Isla felt the mild warmth of the room settle deeper as night came fully. The baker counted softly under their breath only when asked, offering the number four for how many folds a cape needed. Isla repeated the count aloud once and smiled at Zev. Paper stars cast gentle patterned light across all the sorted clothes now resting in new neat groups on the table.

Isla and Zev brought the last medium pieces together at the center of the tablecloth and checked every label one final time. “We finished every size without any rush,” Isla said. Zev answered, “The box can close now and stay ready for tomorrow.” The baker stood and gave a quiet clap of floury hands. Isla felt the smooth wood of the table under their fingers and heard only the soft settling of the house. The air held the last trace of orange sweetness while the friends folded the empty box flaps. Everything stayed safe and ordered because they had chosen the two slow jobs and shared them.

Isla and Zev slid the closed costume box under the end of the table where the long cloth hid it kindly, then sat on the floor cushions with the baker nearby. They watched the paper stars glow in the quiet dark and felt how the whole room had grown cooler and softer. Isla rested their head on a pillow and listened to Zev breathe evenly. The baker dimmed the last bright lamp. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
