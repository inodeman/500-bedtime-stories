The gentle smell of cedar rose from the wooden shelves behind the workbench as Chioma’s small hands already patted and pressed the dark wet soil in the seed tray that sat on the wide ceramic studio sill. Below the open window the quiet plaza lay still in the late afternoon light, and the soil had been watered so hard that it swam in little shiny puddles between the tiny seeds. Chioma felt the cool slippery mud cling to their fingers and almost slid the whole tray farther back behind a row of unfired bowls so no one would notice the mess. Warm air drifted across their cheeks while they worked, and the clay dust on the sill felt powdery under one elbow. Chioma kept smoothing the edges anyway, listening to their own quiet breathing and the soft drip of leftover water from the spout of the blue watering can.

Grandpa Sol stepped into the studio with slow careful steps, and the light from the low sun painted gold stripes across the floor. Chioma’s heart beat a little faster when they heard his familiar shuffle, yet the soft temperature of the room stayed kind. “Chioma, I brought us two cups of warm milk,” Grandpa Sol said in his easy voice, setting the cups down near the sill. Chioma wiped one muddy hand on their apron and looked at the seed tray where the soil still swam like a tiny brown lake. The air felt mild against Chioma’s neck as they stood very still. Grandpa Sol waited without hurrying, and the quiet plaza outside held only the distant coo of a pigeon.

Chioma turned toward Grandpa Sol and spoke the plain truth right away. “I watered the seed tray too hard, Grandpa Sol, and now the soil is swimming and some seeds might float,” Chioma said, their voice steady though a little soft. Grandpa Sol nodded and rested one warm hand on the sill beside the tray. The light from the window caught the wet soil and made it sparkle for a moment. Chioma felt relief loosen their shoulders when Grandpa Sol only smiled. “We can fix that together,” Grandpa Sol answered gently. Chioma watched him pick up a clean sponge from the drying rack, and the sponge looked pale and ready in the golden light.

Together they lifted the seed tray a little so the extra water could tip slowly into a waiting bowl. Chioma held one side carefully while Grandpa Sol held the other, and the cool damp wood of the tray pressed against Chioma’s palms. Outside, the wind in the fig leaves made a soft rushing sound that drifted in through the open window like a quiet song. Chioma listened to that peaceful rustle while they worked and felt glad the plaza stayed calm below. Grandpa Sol showed Chioma how to press a clean cloth along the edges so the swimming soil settled back into neat little hills around each seed. The cloth felt damp and useful in Chioma’s fingers, and no one laughed or frowned.

Chioma and Grandpa Sol used a small wooden stick to make gentle channels so any leftover water could run to one corner without washing the seeds away. The stick was smooth under Chioma’s grip, and the soil began to look less like a pond and more like a proper bed again. “See how the seeds stay put when we tip it slowly?” Grandpa Sol said. Chioma nodded and answered, “Yes, and now they have room to breathe.” Warm light continued to fill the studio, and the quiet plaza below seemed to listen. Chioma felt the mild air move across their arms as they patted the last loose bits of soil into place with careful fingertips.

They set the seed tray back in the middle of the sill where the afternoon light could reach every corner without glare. Chioma noticed a straw mat rolled near the base of the workbench and unrolled a corner of it so their feet could rest on its dry woven texture while they finished. The mat felt rough and friendly under Chioma’s socks, and it kept the cool floor from chilling their toes. Grandpa Sol poured a little fresh water from a clean jar, just enough this time, and Chioma watched the soil drink it calmly. “That looks better already,” Chioma said, smiling at the neat rows. The ceramic bowls nearby held the soft scent of drying clay, and everything felt peaceful again.

Grandpa Sol picked up one of the cups of warm milk and Chioma turned the cup so the handle faced the room before taking a careful sip. The milk tasted mild and comforting, and the cup’s smooth side warmed Chioma’s hands. They stood side by side looking out over the quiet plaza where the first soft shadows stretched across the stones. Chioma felt the gentle temperature of the milk inside them and the cooler air from the window on their face. No hurry touched the moment. Grandpa Sol simply stayed close, and Chioma felt safe telling the truth and mending the tray together.

Later they wiped the sill clean with a dry cloth until the ceramic surface felt smooth and free of mud again. Chioma stacked the damp cloths neatly and placed the blue watering can back on its hook where it belonged. The light outside grew softer and turned the quiet plaza the color of pale honey. Chioma leaned on the sill for one more look at the rescued seed tray and felt proud of the honest work. Grandpa Sol hummed a tiny tune without words while he rinsed the bowl that had caught the extra water. Everything in the studio smelled clean and settled now.

Chioma and Grandpa Sol sat on two low stools near the window while the last of the daylight faded into evening. Chioma rested their clean hands in their lap and watched the seed tray rest safely under the open air. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Grandpa Sol blew out the brighter lamp so only that gentle lantern light remained, and the straw mat still cushioned Chioma’s feet as the room grew quieter and ready for sleep.
