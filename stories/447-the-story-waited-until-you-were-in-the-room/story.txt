Grandma Nia was already in the bedroom with the hall light still on, her hands smoothing the quilt into neat folds while the golden strip of light from the hallway painted a soft path across the floorboards. Cosima stepped inside and felt the mild evening air brush their cheeks, cooler than the kitchen they had left behind. The room held a gentle hush broken only by the quiet rustle of fabric under Grandma Nia’s palms. Cosima watched those familiar hands pause, then rest on the open book whose first lines had waited patiently until they arrived. “I kept the beginning right here for you, Cosima,” Grandma Nia said, her voice low and even. Cosima breathed in and caught the faint, comforting smell of warm bread drifting from the tray on the side table.

Cosima sat on the edge of the mattress and let their bare feet dangle against the rug’s plush pile, which felt thick and welcoming under their soles. The hall light still glowed steadily through the doorway, casting long gentle shadows that did not hurry anyone. Grandma Nia lifted a small ceramic bowl that had held slices of the bread and a little milk, and the cool ceramic rim pressed briefly against her own fingers before she set it down again. Cosima noticed how everything had been prepared so the story could begin the moment they were truly present. Outside the open window the wind moved through the fig leaves with a soft, continuous whisper that sounded like pages turning far away. Cosima leaned closer to the book and felt the paper’s smooth surface under their fingertips.

“The story stayed quiet until you walked in, Cosima,” Grandma Nia said, smiling as she patted the pillow. Cosima nodded and answered, “I see that you made it wait for me.” The words felt true but still a little light, like a feather that might float off too soon. Cosima wanted to do something solid in return. They picked up the empty bowl, feeling again the cool ceramic rim against their palms, and carried it carefully down the short hall to the sink. Water ran cool over their hands as they rinsed it and left it neatly beside the faucet. When Cosima returned, the bedroom still held the soft yellow spill of the hall light and the same peaceful air.

Cosima climbed fully onto the bed now, the blanket’s cotton weave resting lightly against their legs and holding a trace of daytime warmth. Grandma Nia opened the book wider so both of them could see the drawings of quiet meadows and kind animals. The wind kept moving the fig leaves outside in that same gentle hush, a sound that settled into the corners of the room like a lullaby without words. Cosima rested their head against Grandma Nia’s shoulder and smelled the clean soap on her sleeve mixed with the last hint of warm bread still lingering near the tray. “You didn’t have to bring the bowl,” Grandma Nia said softly. Cosima replied, “I wanted to. You already took care of the beginning waiting until I was in the room.”

The two of them sat side by side while Grandma Nia began to read in a voice no louder than the settling house. Cosima listened to every word and watched the hall light remain steady, a warm rectangle that made the doorway look friendly rather than dark. Their fingers traced the embroidered edge of the pillowcase, feeling the raised threads that formed small flowers. The story unfolded without rush, full of slow walks under tall trees and shared snacks on wooden benches. Cosima felt the mattress give gently beneath them, cradling their weight the way a nest might hold a bird. Every so often Grandma Nia paused so Cosima could look longer at a picture of lanterns hanging among green leaves.

Cosima shifted a little closer and tucked their hands under the blanket’s soft fold, enjoying the quiet heat that gathered there. The cool ceramic rim of the bowl was already a finished kindness, and Cosima felt glad they had carried it without being asked. Grandma Nia turned another page, and the paper made a tiny dry sound like a leaf settling. Outside, the wind in the fig leaves continued its calm conversation with the night. Cosima said, “I noticed everything you did before I even sat down.” Grandma Nia answered, “That makes the story feel even better, Cosima.” The hall light still shone from behind them, steady and kind, wrapping the room in a simple glow that needed no brighter lamps yet.

They stayed that way while the tale moved forward through gentle hills and a shared picnic under wide sky. Cosima’s eyelids grew heavier, though they fought to keep them open just a little longer so they would not miss the next drawing. The air in the bedroom carried a mild coolness that made the blanket feel especially snug around their shoulders. Grandma Nia’s reading voice rose and fell like slow breathing, never startling, always near. Cosima pressed one palm flat against the sheet and felt its smooth cotton texture, cool where the blanket had not yet covered it. The smell of warm bread had faded to a memory, yet it still made Cosima think of kitchens full of patience and care.

Grandma Nia closed the book with a soft thump that sounded final and safe. Cosima stretched once under the covers and let out a quiet breath that matched the calm of the room. The hall light continued to spill its gentle rectangle across the floor, and Cosima watched dust motes drift slowly inside that beam. “Thank you for waiting with the beginning until I was here,” Cosima said plainly. Grandma Nia brushed a strand of hair from Cosima’s forehead with fingers that felt warm and dry. Cosima added, “I saw how you had already taken care of it.” The window curtain stirred once as the outdoor air moved past, and the wind in the fig leaves kept its soft steady rhythm like a distant friend saying good night.

Cosima settled deeper into the pillow and felt the last of the day’s warmth leave their toes in a pleasant way. Grandma Nia rose quietly and straightened the edge of the quilt one final time so it lay smooth across Cosima’s chest. The room held only peaceful sounds now: the faint settling of the house and the hush beyond the glass. Cosima’s eyes closed halfway, then opened again to check that Grandma Nia was still near. “Sleep well, Cosima,” Grandma Nia whispered. Cosima answered with a small sleepy sound that was almost a word. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
