"Come see before the light grows stronger," Kito whispered to Golda, their warm breath making a soft cloud against the cool air by the bedroom window on the frosty morning. Outside, pale silver light touched the glass where frost had drawn tiny white feathers along the corners, and the sill wood felt smooth and cold under Kito’s careful palm. The rest of the room still held a gentle leftover warmth from the night blankets. Kito stood on tiptoe in sock feet while Golda padded close, and together they peered into the quiet yard that looked soft and sleeping. Nothing moved fast. Kito kept their voice low so the hush would stay whole, and the cool pane pressed a clean chill against the air near their cheeks.

On the wide sill sat a short candle in a shallow dish, its wick dark and resting, and right beside it in the dark window glass lived its twin, a perfect quiet copy made only of reflection. Kito blinked with happy surprise at how real the twin looked, floating there against the frosted dark as if it too waited for morning. The smell of toasted sesame drifted faintly up the stairs from the kitchen where Auntie June had been busy earlier, curling into the bedroom like a kind hello. Kito’s fingers twitched with the real wish to pocket the candle or poke the twin to test if it would ripple, yet they stayed still and simply looked. Golda tilted their head, eyes wide, while a thin bright line grew along the far horizon and the twin candle seemed to keep its own secret shine that was only glass and dawn.

“We should not touch it,” Kito told Golda in a careful voice, “because the twin belongs to the window and the candle belongs to the sill.” Golda nodded, and Kito showed them how to cup both hands around their face without pressing the glass, so warm breath stayed back and the view stayed clear and kind. The texture of the cotton apron hanging on the nearby chair brushed Kito’s elbow when they shifted, soft and familiar from Auntie June’s morning work. They stood that way for a long quiet minute while the house settled around them with tiny wooden sounds. Outside light grew one shade brighter and turned the frost edges into silver lace that caught and held. Kito felt glad of the gentle way they were looking together.

Auntie June’s steps came lightly up the stairs, and she paused in the doorway with a warm smile. “What have my morning explorers found?” she asked, resting one hand against the doorframe. Kito pointed without jabbing and answered, “A candle and its twin in the dark window. We are looking without disturbing it.” Auntie June came closer, her presence steady, and knelt so her eyes met theirs at the same height. The air near the window still carried a chilly bite even while the room behind them stayed comfortable and soft. Together the three watched the twin shiver slightly whenever a tiny draft moved the air around the real candle, though no flame burned yet. Kito felt Golda’s shoulder lean gently against theirs in shared quiet wonder at the double that needed nothing more.

“May we leave it just like this?” Kito asked, and Auntie June nodded once. “Yes,” she said, “some things are loveliest when we let them stay exactly where they are.” Outside, a gate latch clicked softly in the distance as a neighbor opened their garden for the day, a small ordinary sound that somehow fit the calm morning. Kito breathed the cool clean scent of the pane and felt the earlier kitchen air still lingering gently on the stairs. Golda reached forward as if to poke and then pulled back because Kito had already shown the better slower way. Frost patterns caught little sparks of rising light and scattered them across the sill in tiny bright dots that danced without noise.

They stayed a while longer, and Kito named the shapes in the frost for Golda—a leaf, a star, a tiny boat with a curled sail. The temperature by their toes stayed warmer on the thick rug while their fingertips stayed cool near the wooden sill. Auntie June rested a light hand on Kito’s shoulder, steady and kind, and no one hurried the moment along. The twin candle in the glass held its place exactly as the real one did, two quiet friends sharing the sill and the early light. Kito felt the wish to pocket it drift away like steam from a cup, replaced by the simple gladness of seeing and showing. Golda hummed a little wordless tune that sat gently in the room without crowding anything.

When the light outside grew strong enough to turn the dark window into a softer gray, the twin became harder to see, and that felt right and peaceful too. Kito stepped back one careful pace so Golda could have the best view for a final slow look. The bedroom air smelled clean of cotton sheets and polished wood. Auntie June suggested they might go down for breakfast soon, and Kito agreed without any rush in their voice. They took one more long look at the candle sitting honest and still, its twin fading kindly as day arrived full. Nothing needed fixing or moving or claiming. The whole scene felt complete exactly as it waited.

Before they left the window, Kito straightened the little dish so it sat centered, not because it was wrong but because the motion felt like saying thank you with quiet hands. Golda copied the careful circle in the air without touching glass or wax. Auntie June smiled again and turned toward the door with easy steps. In the center of the room the table waited, and Kito paused to set the chair square to the table, a small neat action that made the space feel ready and peaceful for later. The floorboards were smooth and cool under their sock feet. Soft morning light now filled more of the room, golden and slow and kind to every corner.

Kito, Golda, and Auntie June went downstairs together, carrying the quiet of the twin candle in their minds like a shared secret that needed no pocket and no poke, only looking and leaving it be. The house welcomed them with warmer air and the promise of breakfast waiting. Kito held Golda’s hand for the last few steps and felt peaceful all the way. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
