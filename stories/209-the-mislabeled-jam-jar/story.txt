"Uncle Idris, I need to show you something on the bench," Paz said while the gentle afternoon light warmed their bare arms and made the wood grain shine. Paz stood close beside the long workshop bench scattered with curled shavings, open pots of glue, and neat piles of paper labels ready for sticking. The glass jam jar felt cool and solid in Paz's two hands as they turned it slowly under the light. They had wandered in looking for a quiet corner and found the jar resting among the tools as if it had arrived to help sort tiny brushes or nails. A mild draft from the open doorway cooled Paz's ankles while dust motes drifted like slow sparkles.

Paz studied the label that clearly read peach yet saw the jam inside glowed a rich golden orange that did not match. The wooden bench top felt firm and slightly ridged under their elbows when they leaned in for a better look. Paz thought for one short moment about sliding the jar behind a scrap of cloth so the wrong word might stay unseen. Soft floorboards creaked under a small shift of their feet and the quiet sound kept them company. The glue nearby stayed thick and clear in its pot while Paz kept holding the jar steady.

A gate latch clicked once outside in the yard and the plain metal sound reached Paz clearly across the still air. That single click told Paz that Uncle Idris would step back inside very soon from his short stroll. The jam jar sat right in the middle of the wood pieces and label stacks because Uncle Idris sometimes borrowed empty kitchen jars for holding odds and ends, and this one had traveled over with its old paper still attached. Paz brushed a fingertip along the dry paper edge and felt how it lifted a little at one corner. Warm light continued to pour across everything on the bench and kept the space bright.

Uncle Idris entered with quiet steps and the smell of cinnamon from the cookies cooling in the kitchen drifted in beside him. "Hello there, Paz," he said in his easy low voice that filled the workshop kindly. The room temperature rose a touch as he shut the door and the light from the window caught the glue bottles so they gleamed. Paz held the jar outward with both hands and felt their own breath stay calm. No hurry pressed on them while Uncle Idris set down his hat and came closer to the bench of wood, glue, and labels.

"This jam jar is labeled peach, but the jam inside is really apricot," Paz said in a steady voice. "I almost hid it behind the wood scraps so the mistake would stay out of sight." Uncle Idris listened without any rush and the soft brush of his sleeve whispered against the bench edge. Paz watched his patient face and felt the cool curve of the glass still resting safe in their palms. The stacked labels waited nearby under the same steady light while the air stayed mild and pleasant all around them.

"Thank you for telling me the plain truth, Paz," Uncle Idris answered as he reached for a fresh blank label and a small soft brush. Together they stayed at the wooden bench where the glue waited ready and sticky in its open pot. Paz held the jar firmly while Uncle Idris dampened the old label with a warm cloth until the paper loosened. The apricot jam inside caught the light and sparkled like tiny stars. Fresh paper from the new label gave off a clean dry scent as Paz helped peel the old one free with careful fingers.

Uncle Idris passed Paz a walnut-smooth spoon from the side drawer so they could lift just enough glue for the edges of the new label. The spoon felt silky and rounded under Paz's fingers while they worked without any drip or rush. Longer shadows began to stretch gently across the bench as daylight eased toward evening. Paz and Uncle Idris pressed the apricot label flat with slow even strokes until it sat true and neat. Everything on the workshop bench of wood, glue, and labels felt settled once more under their shared hands.

Paz hummed four notes and then left a rest while they both waited for the glue to firm on the glass. The quiet notes floated lightly in the warm air near the open window. Uncle Idris gave a small nod and rested his palm once on the bench with a soft thump. "We mended the label just fine together," he said. Paz placed the finished jar among the tidy tools where the clear apricot word looked honest and bright. Softer light filled the space as the day slowed without any hurry at all.

Later Paz sat on the low stool beside the bench and felt leftover warmth still held in the wood beneath their hands while Uncle Idris capped the glue pots. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Paz breathed the cool evening air that brushed their cheeks and listened to the deep quiet of the finished work. The apricot jar stood ready for toast in the morning or for holding brushes another afternoon. Paz smiled across at Uncle Idris and the whole workshop held them both in calm peace.
