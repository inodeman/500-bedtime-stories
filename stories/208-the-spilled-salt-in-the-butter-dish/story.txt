A soft tick reached Kito first, gentle as a fingertip tapping glass, and they paused with one hand on the smooth edge of the library table by the round window. Afternoon light lay in a pale circle on the wood, warm against Kito’s wrists, and the quiet room smelled faintly of old paper and clean polish. Kito settled deeper into the chair and listened again, letting the small sound settle their shoulders. Books stood in neat rows nearby, their spines cool to the eye, and a little butter dish waited near a plate of soft bread meant for a peaceful reading snack. Kito breathed slowly and watched dust float like golden flecks in the beam. The chair creaked once under them, a friendly sound, and Kito smiled at how still everything felt.

Kito reached for the bread and the little silver knife that rested beside the butter dish, ready to spread a thin layer before turning another page. The dish sat open, pale butter gleaming in the window light, and a small salt cellar stood too close, its lid already loosened from earlier use. When Kito’s elbow brushed the cellar, a fine white scatter slid across the rim and tumbled into the butter in a soft hiss of grains. Kito froze, feeling the cool air from the round window touch their cheek. The salt lay bright against the yellow butter like tiny stars, and Kito’s stomach gave a quiet flutter of surprise. They stared at the dish, hearing only their own careful breathing and the far hush of turning pages somewhere deeper in the library.

For a long moment Kito thought of sliding the dish behind a tall stack of picture books so no one would see. The idea rose quickly, warm and tempting, and Kito’s fingers hovered near the rim while the window light made the spilled salt sparkle. They could almost hide it, push the evidence away, and pretend the butter remained perfect. Kito’s sleeve brushed the table, catching on a rough little knot in the wood that scraped lightly against the cloth. Heat rose in their face, not from fear but from the tangled wish to make the mess vanish. Outside the round window a cloud drifted, and the light on the table cooled by a single degree, soft and gray for a breath before brightening again.

Kito set both hands flat on the table and decided the plain truth felt steadier than any hiding place. They drew a slow breath that carried a sudden calm thread of lavender from a small cloth sachet tucked near the reading cushions, and the gentle smell eased the tightness in their chest. “I spilled the salt into the butter,” Kito whispered to themselves first, practicing the words until they sounded simple and true. Then Kito looked toward the aisle where Miss Dalca usually walked between the shelves. The butter dish sat openly now, salt and all, and Kito kept it there on purpose. Their fingertips rested on the cool porcelain, feeling its smooth curve, while the round window threw a quiet oval of light across their lap.

Miss Dalca appeared with soft steps and a kind tilt of her head, stopping beside the library table by the round window. Kito looked up and spoke clearly. “Miss Dalca, I bumped the salt cellar, and the salt went into the butter dish.” Miss Dalca’s eyes rested on the dish without hurry, and the air between them felt pleasantly warm from the afternoon sun. “Thank you for telling me straightaway, Kito,” Miss Dalca said in a voice as even as poured milk. “We can mend what can be mended together.” Kito’s shoulders lowered, and they noticed the faint tick of a tin clock on a nearby shelf, steady and bright, keeping time like a friendly heartbeat. No teasing came, only the shared glance at the butter and the open space of the table.

Together they lifted the dish and carried it to a small side counter where clean spoons and a fresh napkin waited. Miss Dalca showed Kito how to scoop the top layer carefully, salt and all, into a scrap bowl, and Kito felt the cool weight of the spoon handle against their palm. “We will keep the clean butter underneath,” Miss Dalca said, “and set out a new pinch of salt in its own little dish.” Kito nodded and worked slowly, scraping with gentle strokes while the window light warmed the side of their neck. The scrap bowl filled with the salty top, and the remaining butter looked smooth again, pale and ready. Kito rinsed the spoon under a trickle of water that sounded like rain on a leaf, then dried it on a soft cloth that smelled faintly of soap.

When they returned to the library table by the round window, Hadi was already settling into the next chair with a picture book about rivers, smiling as if nothing had ever been wrong. “We fixed the butter,” Kito told Hadi, and Hadi answered, “That sounds fair and good.” Miss Dalca set the mended dish down, along with a clean salt cellar, and poured a thin ribbon of fresh butter onto Kito’s bread. The bread felt springy under Kito’s fingers, and the first bite tasted mild and comforting. Light from the round window slid across the page of Hadi’s book, and Kito watched a narrow shadow move the width of a thumb as a branch outside swayed once. The three of them sat without rush, turning pages, and the room held a gentle hush broken only by the soft rustle of paper.

Kito leaned back and let one hand rest on the reading cushion beside the chair, discovering a quilt stitch raised like a tiny path under their fingertips, firm and even. The texture grounded them while Miss Dalca read a short paragraph aloud about a boat that drifted past green banks. Kito listened, tasting the last crumb of bread, and felt the table’s wood still warm from the sun. “I like sitting here with the window round like a moon,” Kito said, and Miss Dalca replied, “It is a fine place for steady work and steady words.” Hadi turned another page, and the three stayed close, the butter dish now tidy, the salt safe in its cellar, and the afternoon stretching quiet and kind around the library table.

Later the light grew softer and gold along the floorboards, and Kito stacked their book neatly before sliding the chair in with care. Miss Dalca gathered the plates with a soft clink of porcelain, and Hadi waved a quiet goodbye as they headed toward the door. Kito lingered one last moment at the round window, feeling the cooler evening air slip through a narrow crack and brush their forehead like a feather. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Kito breathed in the peaceful room, let their hands rest calm at their sides, and walked out beneath the gentle light, ready for home and sleep.
