"Would you like to sit by the drum for a little while, Ines?" Auntie Luz asked in a warm voice that floated like honey through the quiet room. Ines stood near the music corner where a smooth wooden drum waited beside a pale recorder, and the afternoon light lay soft and gold across the floorboards. They could smell beeswax on the polished drum rim, a gentle clean scent that made the corner feel safe and ready. Auntie Luz did not hurry. She only smiled and rested one hand on the back of a low chair. Outside the open window the air felt mild against Ines’s cheeks. Ines touched the chair with two fingers and felt the cool wood. They were thinking about the substitute teacher who would arrive soon, and the thought of saying their own name out loud made their stomach flutter like a small bird.

Ines sat down slowly. The cushion under them was thick and springy. Auntie Luz stayed close without crowding, and the room stayed peaceful. From somewhere beyond the window came the soft rustle of wind in the fig leaves, a steady hush that matched Ines’s breathing. They looked at the drum’s round top and at the recorder’s neat holes. A friend named Jorie had visited earlier and left a tiny drawing of a star on a scrap of paper near the music stand. Ines picked up the paper and felt its dry edges. “I can hear the leaves,” Ines whispered. Auntie Luz nodded. “They sound friendly today,” she answered. Ines kept listening. The sound helped their shoulders drop a little lower.

The substitute teacher stepped into the doorway a short while later, holding a simple clipboard and wearing a kind expression. She smelled faintly of clean soap and stood where the light from the window touched her shoes. Ines watched her without looking away too fast. Auntie Luz said, “This is Ines, and this is our music corner.” The substitute teacher smiled and waited. Ines’s hands rested on their knees. They felt the warm fabric of their own sleeves. Saying a name still felt big inside their chest, like a song that wanted to start but had not found its first note. Auntie Luz did not push. She only kept her calm face and let the quiet stretch gently.

Ines remembered that courage could be small. They looked at the drum again and tapped it once with one fingertip. The sound was low and round, a single soft boom that filled the corner and then faded. The substitute teacher glanced toward the drum with interest but did not demand anything. Ines felt the smooth skin of the drumhead under their finger, cool at first and then warmer. “My name is Ines,” they said, not loud like a shout, yet clear enough that the words traveled across the short space and could be heard. The words sat in the air like quiet beads. Auntie Luz’s eyes shone with ordinary kindness. The substitute teacher wrote the name carefully and said, “Thank you, Ines. I am glad to meet you.”

After that, the room felt lighter. Ines picked up the recorder and held it. The wood was smooth against their palms. They did not play a full song. They only blew one gentle note that rose and fell like a breath. Auntie Luz sat nearby and listened without clapping or making a fuss. The substitute teacher rested her clipboard on a shelf and watched with soft attention. Ines noticed a small green leaf that had drifted in near the windowsill. They touched a leaf's soft underside and felt how velvety it was, almost like a tiny blanket. That small touch helped their hands stay steady. “Would you like to hear the drum once more?” Auntie Luz asked. Ines nodded. “Yes, please,” they said, and the second tap was a little stronger than the first, still calm.

They stayed in the music corner while the light shifted toward late afternoon. The air grew cooler near the floor. Ines and Auntie Luz took turns making quiet sounds, never racing, never trying to impress. The substitute teacher asked if she might hold the recorder for a moment, and Ines handed it over with both hands. When it came back, Ines wiped the mouthpiece with a soft cloth Auntie Luz kept in a basket. The cloth smelled clean and felt thick between their fingers. Jorie’s star drawing stayed on the stand like a small bright companion. Ines spoke again, simply, “I like the drum best right now.” Auntie Luz answered, “Then the drum can be yours for this quiet time.” The words felt easy now, not heavy.

Outside, a snail began a slow journey across one whole brick on the low wall visible from the window. Ines watched it for a long peaceful stretch while Auntie Luz sat with them. The snail’s shell caught a pale glint of light, and its path never hurried. Ines described what they saw in a soft voice so the substitute teacher could look too. “It is going all the way across,” Ines said. The teacher smiled and said she was glad to share the watching. The brick looked warm from the day’s sun, and the snail left a thin silver line that shone briefly. Ines felt proud in a quiet way for having offered their name earlier. The pride did not shout. It rested inside like a warm stone in a pocket.

As the sky softened, Auntie Luz brought a small cup of warm milk for Ines and a second cup for herself. The milk tasted mild and comforting. Steam rose in thin curls that brushed Ines’s nose with gentle heat. The substitute teacher said goodbye with a wave and a promise to remember the name Ines had shared. After she left, the music corner felt even cozier. Ines set the recorder back in its place and covered the drum with its cloth. They pressed their cheek for a moment against Auntie Luz’s sleeve and felt the familiar weave of the fabric. “Thank you for waiting with me,” Ines said. Auntie Luz replied, “I liked waiting with you.” The room held only ordinary evening sounds now, soft footsteps and the faint settling of wood.

Later, when the lamps were low and the day had folded itself away, Ines sat by the same window with a blanket over their knees. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Its light made a calm circle on the floor and touched the edge of the drum and the recorder one last time. Ines breathed in the quiet house air and remembered the way their own name had sounded when they chose to offer it. Auntie Luz brushed a strand of hair from Ines’s forehead with a cool gentle hand. Outside, the fig tree stood still. Inside, the blanket felt heavy and safe. Ines closed their eyes while the lantern kept its steady shine, and the music corner rested nearby like a friendly neighbor ready for another calm day.
