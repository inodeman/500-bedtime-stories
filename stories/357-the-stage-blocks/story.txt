The warm scent of rising dough drifted through the bakery kitchen while Remy’s hands already smoothed the painted edges of the first wooden stage block on the cool tiled floor. Soft gray light from the high windows rested on Remy’s sleeves and made the wood look pale and friendly. Remy ran their fingertips along each side, feeling every smooth plane and tiny corner. Kito knelt close by, sleeves rolled, watching the same block with quiet interest. Ms. Okonkwo walked past the long counters in her flour-dusted apron, her steps light and even. “We still have time before the early table is set,” she said gently. Remy kept working, palms open and steady, and the quiet kitchen felt safe and wide around them.

Remy and Kito lifted the second block together and set it near the first, the wood making a soft dull sound against the tiles. A faint draft from the back door carried the clean smell of soap and cool air. Remy noticed how the blocks were meant to stack into one low step only when their edges matched perfectly, like puzzle pieces that needed patience. “If we hurry the edges will slip,” Remy said. Kito answered, “Then we go slow.” Ms. Okonkwo paused by the mixing bowls and offered nothing more than a soft count under her breath, naming the spaces between the blocks as if they were simple numbers. Remy listened and felt the cool morning air on their cheeks while they adjusted the second piece by tiny finger-widths.

The third block waited against the wall, and Remy touched its surface with both hands before sliding it forward. Kito held one side while Remy guided the other, their movements matching without rush. Warm light brightened a little as the sky outside turned softer blue. Remy could hear the quiet tick of the wall clock and the far hush of the street. Ms. Okonkwo stood near the tall flour bin and said, “This knot of edges is called the careful seam.” Remy repeated the name under their breath and felt how the words helped their hands stay patient. The three blocks sat close now, almost ready to become one step, yet still a little crooked if anyone pushed too fast.

Remy and Kito decided to split the work into two slow jobs so nothing would tip. Remy took the job of lining the front edges while Kito took the job of steadying the back corners. Each child worked only on their part, pausing often to look and wait. The tiles felt smooth under Remy’s knees, and a thin band of sunlight warmed the backs of their hands. “I am ready on this side,” Remy whispered. Kito whispered back, “I am ready on mine.” Ms. Okonkwo simply counted aloud once more, slow and clear, giving them the same calm name for the place where the blocks must meet. Remy breathed evenly and kept every motion small.

While they worked, the air near the cooling racks carried a gentle thread of honey that made the whole kitchen smell sweet and golden for a moment. Remy noticed it and smiled without speaking, still guiding their block with open palms. Kito’s chalky hand brushed Remy’s sleeve by accident as they both reached to test the middle seam; the dry powdery texture felt soft and harmless, like the dust left after drawing on a sidewalk. Remy and Kito glanced at each other and kept going, neither one pulling ahead. Ms. Okonkwo remained by the counter, offering only another quiet count when the edges needed a final name. The three blocks began to sit level, their painted sides lining up into one short, safe step.

Remy sat back on their heels for a breath and listened. Somewhere beyond the open rear door a gate latch clicked shut with a single clean sound, then silence returned. The small noise reminded Remy that the morning outside was beginning, yet inside the kitchen everything stayed unhurried. Remy and Kito returned to their two slow jobs, Remy pressing the front faces flush while Kito pressed the rear faces firm. “Almost,” Remy said. “Almost,” Kito agreed. Ms. Okonkwo spoke the name of the careful seam one last time, and Remy felt the words settle like a steady hand on the work. The blocks locked into place with a gentle wooden sigh, becoming one even step that reached just high enough toward the flour bin.

They tested the step together, first Remy placing one careful foot, then Kito placing one careful foot beside them. The wood held firm and quiet under their shared weight. Warmth from the nearby oven wrapped around Remy’s ankles like a soft blanket. Remy rested both hands on the top block and felt how solid and true the alignment had become. “We did the two jobs,” Remy said. Kito nodded and answered, “And we finished them together.” Ms. Okonkwo smiled from the counter and simply said the number of the final count, nothing more. Remy climbed down slowly and brushed a loose curl from their forehead, feeling proud of the quiet pace they had kept.

Before the early table was rolled into place, Remy and Kito swept the tiny wood chips into a dustpan while Ms. Okonkwo wiped the counters with a clean cloth that smelled faintly of lemon. The new step stood ready beside the tall bin, sturdy and still. Remy pressed one palm once more against the top surface and felt the cool smooth wood. Light continued to grow at the windows, soft and silver-blue. Remy and Kito sat for a moment on the lowest edge of the step, side by side, listening to the kitchen settle around them. No one rushed. Everything waited in the same gentle rhythm they had chosen.

Remy leaned their shoulder lightly against Kito’s and watched Ms. Okonkwo begin to set the first bowls for the early table. The air stayed warm and kind. Remy’s hands rested open on their knees, still remembering the grain of the wood. Outside the sky brightened another shade. Inside, the three stage blocks remained perfectly aligned, a small solid step born of two slow jobs and one shared finish. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
