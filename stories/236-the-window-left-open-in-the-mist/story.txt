The open window hung ajar on its hinges above the ceramic studio sill, and a cool film of dampness spread across the smooth ledge where mist had drifted in from the quiet plaza below. Tiny beads of moisture clung to the pale clay dust that always powdered that sill, catching the last soft gray light. Brisa stood close enough to feel the chill rising from the wood as their fingertips brushed the wet surface and found it slick and unexpected. The air near the sill carried a faint outdoor freshness that made the studio feel suddenly larger. Brisa remembered leaving the window that way after shaping a small cup earlier, wanting a breeze for the drying clay. Now the damp had pooled in one corner beside a waiting bowl, and the cool temperature settled into Brisa’s palms like a quiet fact.

Brisa pulled their hand back and glanced toward the shelves of unfinished pots, the soft clay scent mixing with the cooler air that still drifted through the open frame. For a moment Brisa thought of sliding the window shut quickly and wiping the sill so no one would notice the small mess. The idea felt heavy in their chest, and they stayed still instead, listening to the hush of the plaza stones far below. Warm light from a single lamp inside the studio glowed against the growing dusk and made the damp patch shine. Brisa rubbed their arms against the mild chill and decided the truth would sit better than any hiding. They took a slow breath and turned toward the doorway where footsteps already approached.

Grandpa Sol stepped into the ceramic studio with his usual gentle pace, the soft wool of his sweater brushing the doorframe as he entered. Brisa caught the clean smell of wet wool that clung to the fabric from the misty walk across the plaza. The familiar scent wrapped around the room like a calm blanket and made Brisa’s shoulders loosen. Grandpa Sol set a small kettle on the stove and smiled at Brisa without hurry. “The plaza looks peaceful tonight,” he said in his low warm voice. Brisa nodded and stayed near the sill, feeling the cool air still moving past their ankles. The lamp light painted soft circles on the floorboards while the kettle began its quiet tick.

Brisa watched the kettle for a moment and breathed in time with its soft ticking sound, letting the warm steam idea settle their thoughts. Then they turned fully toward Grandpa Sol and spoke the plain words. “I left the window open, Grandpa Sol, and the sill got damp from the mist.” The sentence came out steady, and Brisa felt the cool dampness still on their fingertips as they pointed to the ledge. Grandpa Sol looked at the open frame and the wet wood without any frown. “Thank you for telling me straightaway, Brisa,” he answered. “We can mend what needs mending together.” The quiet plaza below stayed still, and the studio air held that gentle mix of clay and wool.

They moved side by side to the sill, and Grandpa Sol handed Brisa a soft cloth that felt dry and ready. Brisa pressed the cloth along the damp wood and felt the moisture soak in slowly while the cool evening light faded outside. “The clay pieces are safe,” Grandpa Sol said as he checked the nearby bowls. Brisa nodded and kept wiping until the ledge turned matte again under their careful hands. The texture of a paper edge brushed Brisa’s wrist when they shifted a drying sketch that had been left near the window, its thin crisp border catching for a second against their skin. Together they eased the window closed until the latch clicked softly, shutting out the last of the mist.

With the sill dry and the frame snug, Brisa and Grandpa Sol stepped back and looked at the neat ledge. “It feels better this way,” Brisa said, still holding the damp cloth. Grandpa Sol took the cloth and wrung it gently over the sink so the water ran clear and quiet. “You did the right thing by speaking up, Brisa,” he replied, his voice even and kind. No teasing followed, only the shared work of hanging the cloth to dry near the warm stove. The tin clock on the shelf gave one clear soft chime as the hour turned, its bright note settling into the calm room. Brisa felt the last cool air leave their cheeks and smiled at the tidy sill.

They poured warm water from the kettle into two mugs, and the rising steam carried a mild herbal scent that filled the ceramic studio with comfort. Brisa wrapped their hands around the mug and felt the pleasant heat spread through their fingers while Grandpa Sol settled onto the wooden stool nearby. Outside the closed window the quiet plaza held only the faint glow of street lamps far below. Brisa spoke again, softer now. “I almost hid it, but telling felt lighter.” Grandpa Sol nodded and sipped his drink. “Honesty keeps the evening gentle,” he said, though the words simply floated and did not press. The warm mugs and the dry sill made the whole room feel settled and safe.

Brisa leaned against the worktable and traced a slow circle in a bit of leftover clay dust with one fingertip, enjoying the fine powdery feel under their skin. Grandpa Sol brought over a small finished cup that had cooled completely and set it between them as a quiet companion. The lamp light glowed warmer as full dusk arrived, and the closed window reflected the two of them in soft shapes. No rush remained in the studio, only the easy company of mugs and shelves and the mended sill. Brisa breathed the calm air and felt the earlier chill completely gone. The plaza stayed peaceful under the mist that no longer entered.

Later Brisa sat on the low bench by the wall while Grandpa Sol rinsed the last cloth, and the whole ceramic studio held a deep evening stillness broken only by ordinary small sounds. The shelves of pots stood ready for morning, and the dry sill looked ordinary again under the lamp. Brisa’s eyelids grew heavy in the gentle heat that now filled every corner. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
