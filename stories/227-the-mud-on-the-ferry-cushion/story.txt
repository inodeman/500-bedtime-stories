"Ms. Okonkwo, there is mud from my heel stuck on the ferry cushion," Idris said in a soft voice while they sat very still at the kitchen table. Golden afternoon light poured across the wood and made the jam jar gleam like a little ruby beside the butter dish and the shared pencil tin stuffed with short colored pencils. Idris had carried the cushion inside after the ferry ride because it felt damp and needed a safe spot, and now the dried mud from their heel made a dusty gray patch on the blue fabric right next to the butter. They brushed one finger over the rough spot and felt how the mud had dried into tiny crumbs that clung to the weave. The air near the open window carried a gentle coolness that touched Idris’s cheeks while they looked at the cushion and wondered what to do next.

Idris almost slid the cushion under their chair so no one would notice the mud. Their hands rested on the table edge where the jam spoon had left a faint sticky ring, and the butter smelled faintly sweet next to the open pencil tin. Idris thought about how the ferry ride had been calm with water sliding past the rails, yet their heel had pressed into soft earth on the dock and left this mark behind. They pulled the cushion back into the light instead of hiding it and watched dust motes float above the jam jar. The kitchen felt warm from the oven, and Idris’s bare feet pressed against the smooth floorboards as they decided the plain truth would feel better than keeping the muddy secret.

"I stepped in mud on the dock and it came home on the cushion," Idris told Ms. Okonkwo when she walked closer with an empty bowl in her hands. Ms. Okonkwo set the bowl down and leaned near so the smell of warm bread drifted from the counter where loaves cooled on a rack. Idris pointed to the gray patch and kept their voice steady while the shared pencil tin sat quietly between the butter and the jam. Ms. Okonkwo nodded without any teasing and touched the cushion lightly with one careful finger. Idris watched her face stay kind in the soft kitchen glow and felt their shoulders loosen as the truth settled between them like another calm breath.

"We can brush it clean and then rinse the spot," Ms. Okonkwo said, and Idris answered, "I will help hold it steady." Together they carried the cushion a few steps to the sink while the wind in the fig leaves made a soft rustling sound outside the open window. Idris held the fabric edges and felt the mud loosen under the gentle brush Ms. Okonkwo used, tiny flakes falling into the water with quiet plinks. The butter dish and jam jar waited back on the table beside the pencil tin as if keeping the place ready for their return. Cool water ran over Idris’s wrists while they worked, and the kitchen light stayed gentle on the wet blue cloth.

Idris and Ms. Okonkwo patted the clean spot with a soft towel until most of the dampness left the fabric. They returned to the kitchen table where the jam still shone and the butter looked creamy under the pencil tin lid that someone had left half open. Idris sat again and rested one hand against a mug that had a cool ceramic rim, feeling the smooth circle while Ms. Okonkwo smiled. Bodhi, who had been drawing quietly nearby, looked up and offered a green pencil from the tin so Idris could sketch a little ferry on scrap paper. The towel felt fluffy under Idris’s other hand as they dried their fingers, and the whole room stayed peaceful with no hurry left in it.

"The mud is gone now and the cushion can dry by the chair," Idris said, and Ms. Okonkwo replied, "You told me right away and that helped us fix it together." Idris nodded and picked up the green pencil, drawing slow waves beside the jam jar while Bodhi added a small bird on the same paper. The butter knife rested idle, and the shared pencil tin gave a tiny rattle when Idris reached for a blue stub next. Warmth from the cooling bread oven still lingered in the air around their shoulders. Idris pressed the pencil lightly and listened to the quiet scratch it made on the paper while the cushion rested clean on the nearby stool.

After a few more quiet minutes Idris carried the empty bowl to the sink and came back to the table with clean hands. They sat down once more beside the jam and butter and the pencil tin, feeling the wood grain under their palms. Ms. Okonkwo poured a little milk into two cups and the liquid made a soft pouring sound that blended with the kitchen calm. Bodhi slid the drawing closer so Idris could add a tiny dock under the ferry sketch. The afternoon light had turned a deeper gold across the cushion’s clean blue surface, and Idris’s bare toes curled happily against the floor as they finished the small picture.

Idris leaned their cheek for a moment against the cool ceramic rim of the milk cup before taking a sip. The cushion now smelled only of clean fabric and river air that had washed away, and the jam jar lid clicked shut when Ms. Okonkwo sealed it for later. Bodhi put the pencils neatly back into the shared tin with a gentle clink of wood on wood. Idris watched the last of the daylight stretch across the butter dish and felt how easy the whole afternoon had become once the mud was spoken of and mended. Their chair creaked softly when they shifted, a friendly little sound in the settled kitchen.

Later Idris helped fold the dry cushion and set it on the stool where it looked soft again under the fading light. Ms. Okonkwo turned the oven light off so only the window glow remained, and Bodhi closed the pencil tin with a quiet snap. Idris yawned behind one hand and listened to the house settle around the jam jar and butter dish that still waited on the table. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Idris rested their head on their arms for a peaceful moment while the kitchen stayed warm and the clean cushion waited nearby for tomorrow’s gentle adventures.
