Ms. Okonkwo was already at the kitchen table, turning the butter dish so its soft yellow face caught the late light while she wiped a faint smear from the rim of the jam jar. Cosima stepped closer and felt the gentle heat still rising from the counter near the toaster. The shared pencil tin rested between the two plates, its lid half open to show stubs of green and blue from earlier doodles. Outside the window the leaves of the fig tree made a soft continuous hush as wind moved through them. Cosima rested one hand on the back of a chair and listened to that quiet rustle for a moment. Ms. Okonkwo glanced up with a calm smile and kept arranging the small spoons. Cosima noticed how the wood of the table felt smooth under their fingertips. The air in the kitchen stayed warm and steady.

Cosima had carried the toast over on a plain plate and set it down carefully beside the pencil tin. The toast was darker than Cosima had meant it to be, edges scraped with a butter knife until the worst black flakes fell away into the sink. Cosima almost slid the plate forward without saying a word, as if the jam and butter might hide the uneven color. Warm bread scent still floated up from the toast in a friendly way, even after the scraping. Cosima looked at Ms. Okonkwo’s quiet hands and felt a little twist of worry. The light from the window made the scraped surface look less harsh. Cosima swallowed and kept one finger on the edge of the plate. Everything on the table waited without hurry.

Cosima sat down and wrapped both hands around the cool ceramic rim of a small empty bowl that had held berries earlier. The smooth chill of the glaze settled Cosima’s fingers. Ms. Okonkwo continued buttering nothing yet, just waiting with easy patience. Cosima thought about sliding the toast under a napkin and pretending everything was perfect. The pencil tin clicked faintly when Cosima bumped it by accident. Outside the same easy wind kept brushing the fig leaves. Cosima took a slow breath and decided the napkin idea felt heavier than the truth. The kitchen smelled clean and ordinary. Cosima lifted their eyes.

“Ms. Okonkwo,” Cosima said, “I burned the toast a little and scraped it, and I almost just put it here without telling you.”  
Ms. Okonkwo set down the butter knife and looked at Cosima with kind attention.  
“Thank you for saying the plain thing,” Ms. Okonkwo answered.  
“We can mend what can be mended,” she added.  
Cosima felt the cool ceramic rim again under their thumbs and nodded. The light on the table stayed soft and even. No teasing came, only the ordinary quiet of two people deciding what to do next. Cosima’s shoulders loosened.

Together they examined the toast. Ms. Okonkwo showed Cosima how a little more gentle scraping could smooth the darkest corners without crumbling the whole slice. Cosima held the plate steady while the knife moved carefully. Bits of dark crumb landed on a napkin instead of the table. Then Ms. Okonkwo spread a thin layer of butter that melted into the warm surface and filled the tiny gaps. Cosima spooned a small swirl of jam onto one corner so the red sweetness balanced the toasty taste. The pencil tin stayed nearby like a quiet friend. Cosima watched the butter shine. The room felt peaceful and useful at the same time.

Cosima carried the empty berry bowl to the sink, rinsed it under cool water, and came back to the table with damp hands. Ms. Okonkwo had already poured two small cups of milk that caught the window light. Cosima sat again and noticed how the toast now looked ready and ordinary, no longer something to hide. They both took slow bites. The jam tasted bright against the mild butter. Cosima chewed and felt glad the truth had been spoken aloud. Ms. Okonkwo smiled once more without needing many words. The pencil stubs in the tin stayed still. Everything on the table belonged together in a calm way.

“It tastes better when we fix it together,” Cosima said after another bite.  
“Yes,” Ms. Okonkwo replied, “honest hands make good mending.”  
Cosima grinned a little and reached for the pencil tin, drawing a tiny toast shape on a scrap of paper just for fun. The graphite whispered across the paper. Outside the fig leaves kept their soft conversation with the wind. Cosima colored the drawn toast lightly with a yellow stub. Ms. Okonkwo watched without correcting anything. The kitchen held its gentle temperature. Cosima felt the day settling like a blanket.

They finished the toast in unhurried silence broken only by the small sounds of cups and the distant leaves. Cosima stacked the plates neatly and wiped a stray crumb from the table near the jam jar. Ms. Okonkwo closed the pencil tin with a soft click. Cosima’s bare feet felt the smooth floorboards. The last of the afternoon light turned the butter dish into a small golden moon. Cosima breathed in the leftover warmth of the kitchen and felt steady. No one needed to hurry. The scraped toast had become simply breakfast shared without any shadow left behind.

Cosima and Ms. Okonkwo sat a little longer while the light faded toward evening. Cosima rested both elbows on the table and listened to the quiet house. The jam jar lid made a tiny metal sound when Ms. Okonkwo twisted it closed. Cosima’s hands remembered the cool ceramic rim from earlier and felt content. Everything that needed mending had been mended with ordinary care. Cosima yawned once, a slow sleepy stretch. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
