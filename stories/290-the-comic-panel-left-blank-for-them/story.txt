Soft evening air drifted in through the open window and brushed the bedroom rug of cushions, puppets, and paper with a mild warmth that felt like a slow breath. Kei settled into the center of the cushions first, pressing their palms into the yielding cloth and feeling how the cool patches of shadow mixed with the last golden light on the floor. Paper rustled under Kei’s knees as they spread the sheets into a neat half-circle, and the puppets leaned against one another like sleepy friends waiting their turn. Kei patted a spot beside them and glanced toward the wooden bench that stood just at the edge of the rug, its smooth surface catching the same gentle light. The air carried a faint green scent from outside, and Kei breathed it in while they waited for the game to begin.

Papa knelt near the far cushion and laid out colored pencils in a careful row, the graphite tips clicking softly against one another. Kei watched Papa’s hands move and then reached for a sheet that already held the outlines of three comic panels. One panel in the middle remained completely blank on purpose, a clean white square waiting for whoever needed it most. Kei ran a fingertip along the edge of that empty space and felt the dry tooth of the paper. Outside the window the tomato vines released their sharp green smell into the room, and Kei noticed it curling around the cushions like an invisible ribbon. Jasper stood just beyond the circle of cushions, close enough to see the blank panel yet still outside the fun, their socks quiet on the wooden floorboards.

Kei looked up and saw Jasper’s small smile that stayed a little too careful. The cat padded past the doorway then, and the tiny silver sound of a cat’s collar bell chimed once before fading down the hall. Kei shifted a red cushion sideways with both hands so that a clear open place appeared right beside the blank panel. “Jasper, this spot is yours if you want it,” Kei said in a voice soft enough to match the evening light. Papa stayed quiet on his cushion and only watched, letting the children find their own way. Kei patted the new space twice so the fabric made a muted thump, inviting without any big announcement.

Jasper stepped forward and sat carefully, knees brushing the edge of the paper. Kei felt the grain of the bench under their other hand where they had leaned for balance, the fine ridges cool and steady against their skin. “We can draw anything that fits inside the boxes,” Kei explained, holding out a blue pencil. Jasper took it and studied the blank panel with slow interest. Papa smiled but said nothing, his shoulders relaxed against a puppet that smelled faintly of clean cotton. Kei moved another cushion so their elbows would not bump, changing the seating without making a show of it, and the room felt warmer for the small adjustment.

Kei began sketching a simple tree in the first panel while Jasper hovered the pencil over the empty middle square. The paper gave a quiet whisper under the lead. “Maybe the tree can have a swing that two friends share,” Kei offered, glancing sideways. Jasper nodded and started a careful curve that became a rope. Papa shifted only enough to pass a green pencil across the rug when it was needed, still letting the children solve the quiet problem themselves. Kei slowed the pace of their own drawing so Jasper never felt hurried, and the golden light on the floor softened further into a calm glow that touched every cushion.

“I like how the swing leaves room for whoever wants to sit,” Jasper said after a long peaceful minute. Kei answered, “Me too,” and added a second seat to the swing without erasing anything Jasper had drawn. The puppets seemed to lean closer as if listening. Kei reached for a yellow pencil and colored a patch of sunshine across the third panel so the whole page felt connected. Outside, the tomato vines kept their green smell drifting in, steady and familiar. Kei changed one small rule then by saying the blank panel could stay blank a little longer if Jasper wanted to think, and that made the game kinder without anyone needing to explain why.

Jasper filled the empty square with two figures side by side on the swing, their lines light and sure. Kei watched the pencil move and felt glad that the seating change and the slower pace had opened a real place instead of a prize. Papa stayed nearby on his cushion, hands folded, eyes gentle, trusting the children completely. Kei whispered, “We can add stars next if you like,” and Jasper smiled a fuller smile this time. The cat’s collar bell was long gone, yet the room held its own soft quiet. Kei rested their cheek against a cushion and listened past the near ticking of the little desk clock, hearing instead the far clock down the hall that marked slower, kinder minutes.

The drawings finished themselves almost without effort. Kei and Jasper set the finished comic page in the center of the rug where the puppets could “see” it, and the paper lay smooth under the cooling light. Papa gathered the unused pencils into their tin with a soft metallic click. Kei stretched their legs across two cushions and felt the fabric warm from their own body heat. Jasper copied the stretch, and for a while no one needed to speak. The tomato-vine smell had gentled into something almost sweet. Kei closed their eyes for three breaths and opened them again to the safe circle of cushions, paper, and friends who now sat fully inside the fun.

Kei stacked the comic page carefully on top of a clean sheet so the blank panel’s gift would not crease. Jasper helped smooth the corners. Papa rose only to switch on the small lantern by the window and then returned to his cushion without a word. Kei listened once more for the far clock and not the near one, letting its distant pulse settle their heartbeat. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
