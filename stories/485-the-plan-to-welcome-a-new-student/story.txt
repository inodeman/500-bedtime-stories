Auntie Luz sat already on the low stool beside Eko’s bed, her hands busy smoothing a neat stack of cream-colored sheets under the gentle circle of lamp light that made the whole bedroom feel warm as toast. Soft yellow light pooled across the quilt and caught the quiet dust motes drifting near the ceiling. Eko slipped under the covers and felt the cool pillow against their cheek while Auntie Luz kept arranging the pages with careful fingers. Outside, night had settled, yet the room stayed cozy, and Eko watched the way the lamp glow rested on Auntie Luz’s kind face. “Eko, I brought these so we can talk before any story begins,” Auntie Luz said, her voice low and steady. Eko nodded and rested both palms on their knees, feeling the soft fabric of their pajamas under their hands as they waited.

Auntie Luz lifted the top page so Eko could see the simple drawings of smiling children and an open door. The faint smell of fresh paper rose between them like a clean new notebook waiting for crayons. “Tomorrow a new student arrives,” Auntie Luz began, “and our plan is to welcome them with open hearts and quiet ears.” Eko’s chest fluttered because they had bigger news of their own bubbling up, yet they kept their lips closed and listened. The lamp hummed a tiny friendly note. Eko watched Auntie Luz’s finger trace a circle drawn around two stick figures holding hands. Warm air from the hallway brushed Eko’s ankles under the blanket. Auntie Luz smiled and turned the page slowly so the paper whispered.

“The first part of the plan is simple,” Auntie Luz continued, looking right at Eko. “We say hello with our eyes and our names, then we show them the coat hooks and the quiet corner.” Eko wanted to blurt out the surprise about the painted rock they had found that afternoon, but they pressed their lips together and stayed still. The bedroom air felt pleasantly cool on Eko’s forehead where the covers stopped. Auntie Luz pointed to a little sketch of chairs in a circle. Outside somewhere a gate latch clicked once, soft and metallic, then fell silent again. Eko breathed in and out and kept their attention on Auntie Luz’s calm words.

Auntie Luz turned another sheet and showed a drawing of children sitting near a low wall. “Here is the listening part, Eko,” she said. “When the new student talks, we stay quiet and let every word land like a leaf. We do not rush in with our own stories yet.” Eko’s fingers curled into the quilt, feeling its bumpy stitches, while the urge to interrupt rose and then settled again. The lamp light glowed steady and golden across Auntie Luz’s hands. Eko imagined the new student and felt curious instead of impatient. Auntie Luz’s sleeve brushed the paper with a soft hush. Eko kept both palms resting lightly on their knees and listened all the way through the next quiet sentence.

“We will invite them to touch the small clay garden model we made together last week,” Auntie Luz explained, and she reached into her bag to lift the little piece so Eko could see. Eko stretched out one careful hand and felt the damp clay wall of the miniature garden, cool and slightly sticky under their fingertip, the tiny grooves still soft from recent shaping. The texture surprised Eko in a gentle way and helped them stay patient. Auntie Luz set the model on the nightstand where the lamp light made its edges shine. “Listening means we notice what they like about the wall and the little gate,” she said. Eko nodded slowly, still holding back their bigger news, and the bedroom stayed peaceful around them.

Auntie Luz unfolded the last wide page that showed a path of footsteps leading to a shared snack table. “After we listen, Eko, we offer a seat and a cup of water, and we ask only one gentle question at a time,” she said. Eko’s shoulders relaxed under the soft weight of the blanket. The warm lamp light painted a circle on the ceiling that looked almost like a moon. Eko could hear their own quiet breathing and the far-off sigh of the house settling. Auntie Luz’s voice stayed even and kind. Eko wanted more than anything to share the painted rock and the funny cloud they had seen, yet they waited through every single word, watching Auntie Luz’s eyes crinkle with care.

“When the new student finishes speaking, we clap softly once with our hands so they know we heard them,” Auntie Luz finished, tapping the final drawing. “That is the whole plan, Eko, from hello to the listening part to the quiet clap.” Eko sat very still, the cool night air touching their nose, and realized the last sentence had truly arrived. The papers lay smooth and ready on Auntie Luz’s lap. Eko felt proud for holding the bigger news inside until now. Auntie Luz folded her hands and looked at Eko with patient warmth. The lamp hummed its tiny song again, and the bedroom felt safe and full of soft light.

Eko took a slow breath and asked their one real question. “Auntie Luz, what if the new student feels shy and does not want to talk right away?” Auntie Luz smiled and answered that waiting was also listening, and silence could be a kind of welcome too. Only then did Eko share something small of their own. “I found a smooth gray rock today and painted a tiny sun on it,” Eko said. “Maybe we can leave it near the clay garden so the new student can see it.” Auntie Luz’s eyes lit up. The quilt felt thick and comforting under Eko’s legs. Outside the window the night stayed gentle, and Eko leaned a little closer so their shoulder touched Auntie Luz’s arm.

Auntie Luz set the papers aside and smoothed Eko’s hair with a warm palm. Eko snuggled deeper into the pillow, the room smelling faintly of clean sheets now, and the quiet felt complete. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Auntie Luz whispered that tomorrow they would use the plan together, and Eko closed their eyes while the lamp light rested like a friendly hand across the covers, steady and calm until sleep arrived.
