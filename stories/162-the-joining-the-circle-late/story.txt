The circle rested on the bakery floor like a soft ring of pale chalk and smooth wooden beads strung loosely enough that one side still gaped open, inviting a careful step. Warm yellow light from the street lamps spilled through the big front window and made the chalk edges glow the color of fresh butter. Nim stood very still behind the bakery counter facing the street and watched the open place in the circle, smelling the sweet rise of dough baking in the ovens behind them. The air felt gently warm against Nim’s cheeks, and the wooden counter under their fingertips was smooth and cool.

Nim shifted their weight and listened to the quiet clink of the cash drawer closing. Outside, the evening street held only a few slow footsteps and the soft rush of the river nearby. The ferryman had come in for a loaf of seed bread, his coat still carrying the mild damp of the water, and he waited patiently with a kind half-smile. Brigid stood near the circle already, her braid swinging as she hummed a tiny tune. Nim felt the familiar flutter of wanting to join and not quite knowing how, yet the bakery smelled safe and the light stayed steady.

A small cat wandered past the counter, and the bright chime of a cat's collar bell rang once in the quiet room. Nim’s shoulders eased a little at the friendly sound. The ferryman rested one hand on the counter and spoke in a low, easy voice. “Evening, Nim. That circle looks ready for whoever feels like stepping near.” Nim nodded and traced a slow circle on the wood with one finger, feeling the cool grain. The open gap in the chalk ring waited without hurry, and the warm air from the ovens brushed the back of Nim’s neck like a soft scarf.

Brigid turned and waved, the motion gentle. “We saved a space right by the open side, Nim. You can come whenever you like.” Nim heard the words clearly and tasted the leftover mint leaves that someone had crushed earlier near the tea jars, a clean bright scent that mixed with the bread. The ferryman did not move closer or raise his voice. He simply stayed by the counter, kind and unhurried, as if he had all the time in the river. Nim drew a slow breath and felt the temperature of the room settle around them like a favorite blanket.

Nim’s hand found a loose glass bead that had rolled near the counter edge, smooth and cool as river water under their thumb. They rolled it once, twice, then set it carefully beside the register. The street lamps outside threw long soft rectangles of light across the floorboards, reaching almost to the chalk circle. Nim looked at the open place again and decided the smallest brave version would be enough. No one needed a big leap. Just a step and a voice that could be heard.

Nim cleared their throat softly and spoke so the words carried across the quiet bakery. “I would like to try the open side, please.” The ferryman smiled wider, the corners of his eyes crinkling. “That sounds just right,” he answered. Brigid clapped once, lightly. “Come on, then. The space is still waiting.” Nim walked around the end of the counter, shoes whispering on the floorboards that felt warm underfoot now from the oven heat. The chalk circle seemed larger up close, yet the gap stayed friendly and wide.

Nim stepped carefully into the open place, feeling the slight grit of chalk dust under one shoe and the smoother wood under the other. The circle closed gently around them as Brigid shifted to make room. Outside, a bicycle bell rang far down the street, faint and cheerful. Nim stood inside the ring and spoke again, voice steady enough to hear. “It feels nice in here.” The ferryman nodded from his place at the counter. “Good to hear your voice, Nim.” Warmth rose from the floor near the ovens, and the light through the window turned the whole circle golden for a moment.

Brigid offered her hand, palm open, and Nim took it. Together they swayed a little in place, nothing fancy, just a quiet side-to-side that matched the soft hum still coming from the ovens. Nim named three smells quietly to themself—the bread, the clean mint leaves still lingering, and the faint soap on Brigid’s sleeve—without speaking any worry aloud. The ferryman stayed exactly where he was, kind and still, watching with quiet pleasure. A cool draft slipped under the door and brushed Nim’s ankles once, then faded. The circle held them all without needing anything more.

Nim rested their free hand on the wooden bead strand that formed part of the ring and felt its smooth curve. The bakery grew quieter as the last customers left, and the street outside settled into deeper blue evening light. “Thank you for waiting,” Nim said to both of them. The ferryman answered, “Anytime the circle opens, it stays open for you.” Brigid squeezed Nim’s fingers gently. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Nim leaned a little into the calm space they had chosen, breathing the warm air, and the night felt ready for sleep.
