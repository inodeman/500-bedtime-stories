The mild evening breeze slipped through the open workshop window and settled gently over the bench of wood, glue, and labels, bringing a soft coolness that brushed Sable's cheeks like a friendly breath. Sable stood close beside Uncle Idris on their sturdy stool, feeling the smooth grain of the wooden bench under their hands as they surveyed the neat rows of paper labels and the pots of glue that shone in the fading light. Soft amber light from the western sky poured across the tools and rested warmly on Sable's shoulders. "Shall we paint the little sailboat this evening?" Uncle Idris asked in his quiet voice. Sable nodded with a small smile and reached for the brush, already enjoying the peaceful start of their shared time among the wood shavings that still held a faint outdoor freshness.

The room stayed hushed except for the steady tick of a tin clock that sat on the high shelf and counted the slow minutes of their work. Sable dipped the brush into the bright blue paint and drew it carefully along the wooden side of the boat, feeling the cool wetness of the color as it covered the grain. Uncle Idris worked nearby sanding a second piece, and the soft hush of sandpaper mixed with the clock's calm sound. When Sable turned to ask a question they reached out for balance and pressed their thumb, still carrying a smear of blue from adjusting the pot, firmly against the pale doorframe that stood just beside the bench. A clear thumb of blue paint now marked the wood, and Sable's face grew warm under the lingering daylight.

Sable stared at the blue mark for a long second while the workshop lamp cast a steady yellow glow over the labels and the open glue. They thought about covering it with a sleeve or turning away so no one would notice right then. The leftover paint on their thumb felt sticky and cool. Around them the air held the quiet of late day, and Sable almost chose to stay silent. Yet they remembered how good it felt to work honestly with Uncle Idris on every other small task. Their fingers curled lightly around the edge of the bench as they decided what to do next in the gentle light.

Then Sable breathed in and caught the gentle smell of chamomile rising from Uncle Idris's half-finished mug of tea that rested safely away from the paint and glue on the far end of the bench. The warm floral scent drifted over and made the whole space feel kinder. "Uncle Idris, I left a thumb of blue paint on the doorframe by mistake," Sable said clearly, pointing with their clean hand so he could see. The words came out steady and true. Uncle Idris looked up at once from his sanding, and the soft window light showed only kindness in his face as he set his work aside to join Sable.

"I am glad you told me the plain truth, Sable," Uncle Idris said, and there was no teasing anywhere in his tone, only calm care. He came to stand beside the stool so they could both examine the blue thumb mark together under the clear evening light that still filled the workshop. Sable felt the slightly rough texture of the doorframe wood under their fingertip as they showed exactly where the paint had landed. "We will mend it together right now," Uncle Idris added with a small nod. He lifted a soft cloth from a drawer and brought down a little tin of gentle cleaner that lived on the shelf above the labels.

Sable took the cloth and watched as Uncle Idris twisted open the cleaner, then they held the cool round shape of the tin lid in their own palm for a moment before setting it safely on the bench among the wood scraps and glue pots. The lid felt smooth and solid against their skin. Together they dabbed the cloth with a bit of cleaner and pressed it lightly to the blue mark, rubbing in slow circles while the paint loosened and transferred to the fabric. A mild coolness from the cleaner touched the wood and Sable's knuckles. "Look how the blue is lifting away," Sable said with quiet wonder, keeping their strokes even and careful so the doorframe stayed smooth.

Uncle Idris smiled and offered Sable a dry part of the cloth so they could polish the spot until no blue remained and the wood matched the rest of the frame once more. The polished place felt clean and almost warm from the gentle friction of their shared effort. Soft shadows lengthened across the floor as the light outside grew softer, and the glue bottles caught little gleams along their sides. Sable stepped back on the stool to admire the mended doorframe and felt their breathing ease fully. "We fixed what needed fixing," Uncle Idris said, giving Sable's shoulder a brief light pat that carried only pride. The workshop held a peaceful hush around them both.

They returned then to the half-painted boat that waited on the bench of wood, glue, and labels, and Sable finished the blue hull with long steady strokes while Uncle Idris prepared a thin mast and dabbed fresh glue that smelled clean along its base. Evening light turned the whole room a gentle blue-gray, and Sable felt the smooth worn handle of the paintbrush rest comfortably between their fingers. They spoke of floating the finished boat on the little garden pond the next morning when the water would be still. Sable's free hand rested on the cool edge of the bench as they worked, and no hurry pressed them. Everything felt settled and right after the simple truth and the quiet mending.

When the boat stood ready to dry on a clean sheet of labels, Sable helped gather the brushes and close the paint pot, then rinsed their hands in the basin of warm water that Uncle Idris filled for them by the wall. The workshop dimmed in a friendly way as night approached, and Uncle Idris struck a match to light the lantern that hung near the glass. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Sable leaned for a moment against Uncle Idris's side, feeling the calm of the finished work and the mended doorframe nearby, and knew the evening ahead would stay just as gentle as the breeze that still brushed the open sill.
