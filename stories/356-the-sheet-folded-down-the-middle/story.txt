The bed sheet waited in a pale heap on the scuffed floorboards of the school hall, its cotton surface catching the honey-colored light from the tall side windows while faint chalk dust drifted in the air. One long edge curled like a sleepy wave, and the middle crease already whispered that it wanted two steady hands and one slow fold from end to end. Milo crouched close enough to feel the cool cloth against their fingertips and watched how the fabric settled without a wrinkle when left alone. The hall itself smelled clean and ready, with tables being wheeled into gentle rows for the fair that would open after supper. Milo brushed a loose thread free and stayed still a moment longer, listening to the soft shuffle of sneakers elsewhere in the big room.

Milo stood and called softly across the space where Yara was stacking paper cups. “Yara, this sheet needs us both,” Milo said, and the warm air from the open side door touched their cheeks as they spoke. Yara came over at once, her steps quiet on the wood. Grandpa Sol followed more slowly, carrying a small cardboard box of string and labels, the dry cardboard scent mixing with the open-door breeze. The three of them looked down together at the sheet that wanted company for its fold. Outside a distant bird chirped once and then fell quiet again, leaving only the calm inside sounds of the hall.

They tried the first corner lift too quickly, and the sheet slid sideways in a soft rush of air that brushed Milo’s wrists like cool silk. “If we hurry it will twist,” Milo said, feeling the temperature of the cloth change where their hands held it. Yara nodded and stepped back half a pace so the fabric could rest flat again under the even ceiling lights. Grandpa Sol set his box down with a gentle thud and only watched, offering no push. The hall felt spacious and safe around them while other helpers moved far away near the refreshment tables, their voices low and kind.

Milo and Yara decided then to split the work into two slow jobs that would meet in the middle. Milo took the left half and began smoothing every ripple with the flat of their palm, feeling the faint nap of the weave rise and fall. Yara took the right half and matched each stroke, the two of them moving like quiet mirrors. The paper edge of a folded fair program brushed Milo’s knuckle when they reached for a clearer spot on the floor, its thin crisp line surprising and neat against their skin. They kept the pace even so the sheet never pulled or bunched.

“Count with me to eight before the next lift,” Grandpa Sol said in his low voice that carried only as far as their circle. Milo breathed once and answered, “One, two,” while the sheet’s weight rested easy between them. Yara joined on three and four, their voices soft as the distant scrape of a chair being set straight. The count stretched longer than any rush would allow, and the fabric answered by hanging true between their careful holds. Warmth from the high windows rested on Milo’s shoulders like a light blanket while they waited for the numbers to finish.

They lowered the doubled sheet onto the floor again and prepared the long center fold that the cloth had seemed to want from the start. Milo kept their side taut with both hands while Yara mirrored the tension exactly, neither of them tugging ahead. “The knot we need is just a simple square,” Grandpa Sol offered when the ends came near the string, naming it clearly without reaching in. Milo repeated the name under their breath and felt the string’s slight roughness against their palm as they began the first loop. The hall stayed mild and still around the three of them, only the faint scent of wet wool drifting past from a damp coat hung near the doorway to dry.

A tin clock on the far wall gave one clear tick that marked the quiet minute while Milo and Yara finished the square knot together. They checked the fold one last time by running free hands down the new double edge, the cloth now neat and even under the steady overhead glow. “It feels right now,” Yara said, and Milo smiled without hurry. Grandpa Sol nodded once and stepped back so the finished sheet could rest in its place beside the reading nook tables. The air near the floor felt cooler than the air near their faces, a small difference that kept Milo aware of every calm breath.

They carried the folded sheet the short distance to the low display table and smoothed it once more so the fair guests would find a soft landing place for picture books later. Milo pressed their cheek briefly to the top layer and smelled the clean cotton still holding a trace of afternoon light. Yara tucked the labeled string end out of sight while Grandpa Sol only watched the last small motions with quiet eyes. No one spoke for a handful of heartbeats; the hall itself seemed to settle around the finished work as distant helpers laughed gently over a crate of polished apples.

Milo stood back with Yara and Grandpa Sol and felt the whole gentle fair taking shape around their quiet corner, tables aligned and soft cloth ready. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Milo rested one hand on the cool table edge and listened to the last soft footsteps fading toward the kitchen while the three of them shared the settled peace of work finished side by side.
