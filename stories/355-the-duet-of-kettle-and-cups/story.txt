The baker was already smoothing the long tablecloth across the wooden table in the cottage room, pressing each fold flat with careful hands while the paper stars overhead cast gentle patterned light onto the cloth. Isla stepped closer and felt the cool evening air that slipped in through the open window, brushing their cheeks like a quiet hello. The baker hummed a low tune that matched the slow work, and Isla watched the cloth settle into place without a single wrinkle. Kei arrived right then and stood beside Isla, both of them breathing in the peaceful room. “The kettle and the tray of cups must come to the table together,” the baker said softly, still working. Isla nodded and kept their eyes on the neat rows of paper stars that glowed like tiny moons.

Isla reached toward the sideboard where the shining kettle waited next to the tray filled with small cups, and the metal felt cool under their fingertips while Kei held the tray steady. The two friends knew the kettle and the tray had to arrive as one pair so nothing would tip or clatter on the long tablecloth. Soft yellow light from the paper stars touched the kettle’s curve and made it look friendly and ready. Isla spoke in a calm voice and said, “We will carry them slowly so they stay side by side.” Kei smiled and agreed without hurrying. The cottage room felt warm around them as they planned the careful steps.

Isla and Kei decided the work would go wrong if anyone rushed, so they split the task into two slow jobs right there beside the table. Isla would guide the kettle while Kei managed the tray, moving only when both were ready. The air near the window carried a faint coolness that kept them steady and patient. Paper stars turned slowly above as a tiny draft moved them, and Isla felt glad the baker stayed nearby without taking over. “We can take turns counting our steps,” Isla whispered to Kei. The friends stood close and watched each other so the kettle and cups would travel as a perfect pair.

Isla practiced the soft loop once on a spare corner of cloth and felt how the fabric slid easily into place. Warmth from the nearby stove reached Isla’s arms and made the room feel cozy and safe. Kei held the tray still while Isla finished the knot, and together they checked that everything stayed level. The paper stars cast their quiet light over the whole scene like a gentle blanket.

Each step made a soft sound on the wood, and Isla matched Kei’s pace so the kettle and cups stayed perfectly even. Outside the window the wind in the fig leaves made a gentle rustling that filled the quiet pauses between steps. Isla felt proud of the careful way they moved together without any hurry. The baker counted aloud in a calm voice from the side of the table, giving them a steady rhythm. “Three more steps,” the baker said, and Isla and Kei finished those steps side by side.

When they reached the edge of the table, Isla set the kettle down first in the center of the long tablecloth while Kei placed the tray of cups right beside it so the pair arrived together exactly as planned. The cups made a tiny friendly clink that sounded like a soft song under the paper stars. Isla brushed a fallen leaf that had drifted in from outside and noticed a leaf’s soft underside against their palm, smooth and cool like velvet. Kei helped straighten the napkin with its soft loop knot so everything looked neat. The baker nodded once and continued smoothing the tablecloth corners. Isla breathed slowly and felt the calm settle deeper into the cottage room.

Isla and Kei stood back a little and watched the kettle and the tray rest together under the hanging paper stars, the whole arrangement glowing in the mild light. A quiet beeswax smell rose from the polished wooden edges of the table where the baker had worked earlier, sweet and clean. Isla reached out once more to touch the kettle handle and found it still cool and smooth. “We did it without rushing,” Isla said to Kei in a happy whisper.
