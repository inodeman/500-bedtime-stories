Ines sat cross-legged on the cool kitchen floor when a small sound drifted through the open door. It was the soft, bright tinkle of a cat's collar bell, ringing once as the cat stepped along the porch boards. Ines lifted their chin and noticed the gentle sound at once. Late sunlight lay in a warm stripe across Ines's bare feet, and the air from outside felt mild against their cheeks. Ines stood and brushed a crumb from their sleeve. “I hear the cat going out,” Ines said. They walked to the doorway and smelled the clean bread the baker had set to rest on the counter. Beyond the garden path the calm meadow waited with its picnic cloth already spread. Ines called, “Nia, the meadow is ready,” and waited while their friend hurried near.

Ines and Nia stepped outside together and followed the baker down the short path where tomato vines grew along the low fence and sent up their green, leafy smell into the soft air. The baker carried a basket that bumped lightly against one knee. Ines walked close enough to feel the dry grass tickle their ankles and to see how the white picnic cloth lay smooth and inviting on the quiet ground. Nia skipped once and then matched Ines’s slower pace. “The cloth looks like a little cloud,” Nia said. Ines nodded and breathed the warm meadow air. A wooden bench sat at the edge of the cloth, its surface pale in the light. Ines rested one hand on the bench for a moment and felt a grain of the bench under their fingertip, rough and steady. The baker smiled and set the basket down without hurry.

Ines settled on the picnic cloth first, smoothing a corner so Nia could sit beside them. The cloth felt smooth and slightly cool under Ines’s palms. The baker opened the basket and lifted out a round bowl of strawberries still cool from the rinse. Tiny beads of water clung to the red skins and caught the low sun. Ines leaned close and felt the chill rising from the bowl against their nose. “They look perfect,” Ines said. Nia’s eyes grew wide with delight. The baker placed the bowl in the middle of the cloth where the light made the berries glow. Far away a clock chimed faintly, and Ines chose to listen for that far clock and not the nearer sounds of the house. The meadow stayed calm and green all around them.

Ines looked at the full bowl and then at Nia and then at the baker. A quiet thought rose in Ines’s mind. If the first strawberry went only to one person, the share would not feel even. Ines wanted Nia to taste the cool fruit at the same moment. Ines reached gently toward the bowl, careful not to tip it. The strawberries smelled sweet and fresh. “May I make smaller shares so we both begin together?” Ines asked the baker. The baker simply nodded and let the choice stand without a speech. Ines felt gladness settle warm in their chest. Nia watched with quiet interest while Ines began to divide the berries.

Ines picked up one strawberry after another and set them in two small even piles on clean napkins the baker had brought. The berries remained cool against Ines’s fingers, and juice left a faint pink shine on their thumbs. Ines counted softly under their breath so each pile matched. Nia held still and waited. “These two are the same,” Ines said at last, sliding one napkin toward Nia and keeping the other close. The baker rested on the bench nearby and watched with calm eyes. A soft breeze moved across the meadow and cooled the backs of Ines’s hands. Ines and Nia each lifted a strawberry at the same slow pace and bit into the cool fruit together. Sweetness filled Ines’s mouth while the sun rested gently on their hair.

Ines chewed and listened to the quiet meadow. A bee hummed far off among the clover, and the sound blended with the rustle of the cloth when Nia shifted. Ines wiped a drop of juice from their chin with the napkin edge. “They taste like summer rain,” Nia said. Ines smiled and agreed with a small nod. The baker opened a second basket and set out soft rolls that still held a little oven warmth. Ines tore a piece of roll and felt its tender crumb between their fingers. They shared the bread the same careful way they had shared the strawberries. Light turned gold along the tops of the grasses. Ines sat cross-legged and felt the cloth’s weave press lightly against their knees while the cool strawberry bowl sat empty and peaceful in the center.

Ines leaned back on both hands and watched a white cloud drift high and slow. The air grew a shade cooler as the sun slipped lower. Nia stretched out one leg and hummed a tune without words. Ines closed their eyes for a breath and opened them again to the soft green of the meadow. “Shall we save the last two strawberries for the walk home?” Ines asked. Nia nodded, and Ines wrapped the remaining berries in a clean cloth so they would stay cool a little longer. The baker rose and brushed a few crumbs from the bench. Ines stood too and felt the grass spring under their feet. Together they folded the picnic cloth into neat squares while the evening light turned the whole meadow the color of honey.

Ines carried the folded cloth while Nia carried the empty bowl. The path home led past the same low fence where the garden waited in quiet shadow. Ines felt the evening air cool their arms and heard only the soft sound of their own steps on the packed earth. The baker walked a little ahead, basket swinging gently. At the porch the cat appeared again and brushed against Ines’s ankle with warm fur. Ines paused to stroke the cat once and then followed everyone inside. The kitchen held the last of the day’s warmth. Ines set the cloth on the table and washed the pink berry juice from their hands under cool water. Nia waited by the window seat. Ines dried their hands and sat down close to their friend while the house settled into evening quiet.

Ines rested their head against the soft cushion of the window seat and felt Nia’s shoulder warm beside them. Outside the meadow had grown dim and peaceful. Inside the room the air smelled faintly of the clean cloth and the last sweet berries. Ines spoke in a voice almost as soft as breath. “Tomorrow we can share again.” Nia answered with a sleepy hum of agreement. The baker moved quietly through the room and left a small lamp glowing near the door. Ines watched the gentle light and felt their eyelids grow heavy. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Ines closed their eyes and let the calm of the shared picnic settle around them like a light blanket while the house remained still and kind.
