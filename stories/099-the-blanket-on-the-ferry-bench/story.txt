The sweet smell of pear skins drifted from the snack crate as Delia’s small hands already tugged at the sleeves of hanging coats in the school cloakroom, lining each cuff so the hooks would hold them neat. Cool afternoon light slid across the floorboards and warmed the backs of Delia’s fingers while they worked. Beyond the row of pegs the lunch mats waited in soft blue squares, and the ferry bench sat against the far wall with its wooden sides painted like a little boat. Delia hummed under their breath and kept smoothing fabric, feeling the quiet of the room settle around their shoulders like a second jacket.

A faint breeze carried the soft rustle of wind in the fig leaves outside the high window, and Delia paused to listen before stepping closer to the ferry bench. The folded blanket rested there in tidy layers, thick and ready, its edge catching the same pale light that touched the mats. Delia ran one palm along the top fold and felt how the cloth held the day’s gentle heat. Uncle Idris stood near the mats with a quiet smile, and Ilaria waited beside him, shoes already off so toes could press the soft mat surface. Delia’s name floated once in the air when Uncle Idris said, “Delia, the blanket looks ready for resting time.”

Delia turned the warm coin that someone had left as a play token on the bench armrest, feeling its smooth metal edges against their thumb while they thought. The coin’s warmth matched the quiet temperature of the room, neither too cool nor too warm. Ilaria shifted from foot to foot on the lunch mat and watched Delia with open eyes. Delia knew the blanket on the ferry bench would not feel fair if only one person received it first and kept the whole soft pile. The idea of an uneven start made Delia’s chest feel tight in a mild way, so they kept turning the coin and looking from the folded layers to the open mats just beyond the cloakroom doorway.

“Ilaria can sit with me if we make the blanket smaller for two,” Delia said, voice steady and clear in the calm air. The light from the window grew a little softer as clouds passed, painting slow shapes across the lunch mats. Uncle Idris nodded once but spoke no long words, only waited. Delia began unfolding the blanket with careful hands, smelling the clean cotton that still held a trace of outdoor air. Each layer opened with a quiet whisper of cloth, and Delia laid the middle section flat so both sides stayed even. The texture of the weave felt friendly under Delia’s palms, and they kept working until the blanket stretched just far enough for two small resting places side by side.

Ilaria stepped forward onto the nearest lunch mat and said, “Thank you, Delia, that looks just right for both of us.” Delia answered, “We can each have the same amount of soft space,” and smoothed one half toward Ilaria while keeping the other half close. The air near the mats carried a mild coolness from the floor, pleasant against bare ankles. Uncle Idris watched without adding any speech, simply letting Delia’s choice stand as the blanket settled into place. Delia pressed a corner flat with one knee and felt the firm give of the mat underneath, then invited Ilaria with a small wave of the hand. Together they sat, the shared blanket covering knees and laps in matching warmth.

“Does your side feel cozy too?” Delia asked, glancing across the even fold. Ilaria nodded and replied, “Yes, Delia, it feels the same as yours.” The light in the cloakroom corner grew honey-colored as the afternoon eased forward, and Delia noticed how the painted ferry sides of the bench glowed a little brighter in that glow. Delia reached out once more to tuck a loose edge so nothing hung uneven, then rested both hands in their own lap. The quiet of the room wrapped around the three of them like the blanket itself. Uncle Idris stayed nearby on a low stool, eyes kind, and the choice remained simple and open with no extra talk needed.

Delia stretched one foot and used a toe to straighten the rug corner that had curled near the lunch mats, pressing the fabric flat against the smooth floor. The small motion felt good and finished the space. Temperature stayed gentle, the kind that invites still shoulders and slow breathing. Ilaria copied the calm by settling deeper into the shared blanket, and Delia felt glad the folds matched so neither side looked larger. The ferry bench behind them held the leftover fold of cloth like a quiet pillow, and Delia glanced back once to make sure everything stayed tidy. Uncle Idris’s quiet presence made the whole moment feel steady, as if the room itself approved of the even share.

Outside the high window the light continued its slow change, and Delia listened to the soft hush that filled the cloakroom now that coats hung straight and mats waited ready. Delia’s hands rested open on the blanket, feeling its steady thickness, while Ilaria’s shoulder touched Delia’s in a friendly way. “We can rest here until the bell,” Delia said softly. Ilaria smiled and stayed quiet after that, content. Uncle Idris let the arrangement remain exactly as Delia had made it, offering only a small nod that needed no words. The pear-skin sweetness had faded into the background, replaced by the clean smell of mats and cotton, and Delia breathed it in with easy calm.

Delia leaned back against the ferry bench edge and felt the solid wood support their spine while the shared blanket kept both children warm in equal measure. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Ilaria’s breathing slowed to match Delia’s, and the whole cloakroom seemed to hold its peaceful air around the lunch mats. Uncle Idris remained nearby without speaking further, simply present as the even folds stayed true. Delia closed their eyes for a moment and felt the quiet settle deeper, the blanket smooth and fair beneath their hands, the day ending in gentle light and shared rest.
