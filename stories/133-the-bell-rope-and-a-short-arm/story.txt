"May I hold the lower stack while you manage the rest?" Kito said, resting both hands on the cool edge of the library return desk where soft afternoon light pooled across the wood.

Mr. Ade stood behind the desk in a cotton apron that felt smooth and familiar under Kito’s brief touch as they steadied a pile of picture books, and the mild indoor air carried a quiet hush broken only by the turning of pages somewhere deeper in the room. Outside the side door the rainy steps glistened under a gentle silver light that never grew harsh, and the bell rope that signaled the evening close hung a little high for a shorter arm, swaying just out of easy reach whenever Mr. Ade stretched. Kito noticed the careful way Mr. Ade balanced on the balls of his feet and how the rope brushed his fingertips without settling into his palm. The desk lamp threw a steady golden circle that warmed Kito’s cheeks while they waited without speaking again.

Rain whispered against the glass in a soft continuous hush, and Kito felt the cool draft that slipped under the door each time someone returned a book. Mr. Ade tried once more for the bell rope, his shoulder rising, yet the height remained awkward, so Kito simply stepped closer and lifted the lower half of the heavy return bin so Mr. Ade could free both hands for better balance. “I can keep these steady,” Kito said, and the words floated gently into the warm air above the desk. Mr. Ade’s eyes crinkled with quiet relief as he accepted the shared weight without needing to ask. The bin’s cardboard edge felt dry and firm against Kito’s palms while the rainy light outside turned the steps into soft gray mirrors.

Golda appeared from the children’s corner carrying two slender volumes, her shoes making almost no sound on the carpet, and the temperature near the desk stayed pleasantly mild. “The steps look shiny,” Golda murmured, setting her books on the counter so Kito could slide them into the already steady bin. Kito kept their arms firm and their gaze kind, never glancing up for praise, only watching the way the bell rope still waited high and unused. Mr. Ade shifted his stance, and Kito shifted with him, taking the useful half of the job as if it were the most ordinary thing in the world. A faint warmth rose from the floor vents and brushed Kito’s ankles while the three of them worked in unhurried rhythm.

When the bin grew lighter, Kito guided it toward the sorting shelf and felt the smooth grain of the wood shelf under their fingers. “We can finish the returns first,” Mr. Ade said, his voice low and even, and Kito nodded, still holding the side so nothing tipped. The rainy steps beyond the door remained empty of hurry; only the steady silver sheen of water marked them. Kito breathed in the faint toasted sesame that drifted from a small plate of crackers left on the volunteer cart, a gentle scent that made the library feel even more like a calm kitchen corner. Golda wiped a single drop of rain from a book cover with the cuff of her sleeve, and Kito smiled at the careful motion without needing any words.

Together they carried the sorted stack to the cart nearest the side door, where the cooler air from the rainy steps touched Kito’s wrists like a polite greeting. Mr. Ade reached again for the bell rope, and this time Kito simply cupped both hands beneath his elbow in a steady, wordless brace so the shorter arm could rise the final inch. The rope settled into Mr. Ade’s palm with a soft scrape of fiber against fiber, and the small bell above gave one clear note that rang through the quiet shelves. Kito lowered their hands at once and stepped back, cheeks cool from the draft, heart easy because the work was shared. No one spoke of thanks; the finished task itself felt complete.

They paused beside the door while the last returns found their places, and Kito listened to the distant click of a gate latch somewhere in the garden beyond the library wall, a neat metallic sound that arrived and faded like a polite knock. The rainy steps shone under the same gentle light, and Kito noticed how the water made tiny rivers between the stones without ever seeming fierce. Mr. Ade hung the bell rope back on its hook at a height that would wait for morning, and Kito straightened the cart wheels so they pointed neatly forward. Golda slipped her hand into Kito’s for a moment, the skin of her palm warm, then let go again as they all turned toward the reading tables.

Inside once more, Kito felt the milder indoor temperature settle around their shoulders like a folded blanket. “Shall we put the chairs ready for tomorrow?” Mr. Ade asked, and Kito answered, “Yes, we can,” already moving toward the nearest table. They set the chair square to the table so its legs lined up cleanly with the wood grain, the seat smooth and cool beneath their hands. Golda matched the angle on the opposite side, and the three of them worked until every chair sat quiet and even. The library lights glowed low and honey-colored, and the rainy steps outside continued their soft silver shine through the glass without needing anyone to watch them.

Later Kito stood by the front window while Mr. Ade locked the side latch and Golda waved a small good-night. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Kito breathed the calm remaining air, felt the last cool draft fade, and watched the rainy steps settle into deeper dusk, knowing the shared work had left everything steady and kind for the morning to find.
