"Grandpa Sol, may we lift the big map together before the chairs fill the middle of the floor?" Niko asked, their voice soft in the wide school hall where strings of paper lanterns waited unlit and the air still held the cool hush of late afternoon. Dust motes drifted through a slant of golden light from the high windows, and Niko could feel the smooth polish of the wooden floor under their socks as they stood beside the rolled map that would show every region in gentle colors for the fair. Anil knelt nearby, smiling, while Grandpa Sol rested one hand on a folding table and nodded, already ready to help without hurrying anyone. Niko breathed in the quiet and felt glad the three of them had arrived early enough to set things carefully.

Niko and Anil unrolled the big map colored so the edges matched, spreading it across two long tables pushed end to end so the coastlines and mountain bands would line up without a gap. The paper smelled faintly of the storage cupboard, and Niko ran a careful finger along a blue river that crossed from one half to the other, listening to the soft rustle as the sheet settled. If someone rushed and pulled only one side, the matching edges would slip and the picture of the land would look crooked under the fair lights later. Niko looked at Anil and said, "We should go slowly, one side each." Anil answered, "Yes, Niko, slow is better," and the warm light from the west window painted their hands gold as they held the corners steady.

Grandpa Sol brought a small wooden box from the side room and set it down with a quiet thud that echoed gently against the high ceiling. Inside the box lay soft cotton cords and brass clips meant to hold the map edges true once the colors lined up. Niko caught a clean breath of cedar rising from the open box, a calm woodland scent that made the busy hall feel like a quiet cabin for a moment. Grandpa Sol only smiled and did not reach for the cords yet. He waited while Niko and Anil decided how to share the work, the soft temperature of the hall air neither too warm nor too cool against their arms.

Outside the open side door the afternoon moved, and Niko heard the wind in the fig leaves as a low shushing sound that came and went like a friendly breath. The sound drifted across the straw-colored floorboards and made Niko pause with both hands flat on the map so nothing would shift. Anil held the far edge and waited too. Grandpa Sol named nothing yet; he simply stood near enough that Niko felt safe and unhurried. The light grew softer by degrees, turning the paper from bright white to a mellow cream while the colored regions glowed like small painted gardens.

Niko noticed a straw mat someone had placed near the doorway for visitors to wipe their shoes later, and the rough woven texture brushed Niko's ankle when they stepped sideways to straighten a corner. The mat felt dry and friendly under their toes, nothing sharp, only the honest weave of summer grass. Anil said, "Niko, you hold the left edge and I will hold the right, and we can ease them together a little at a time." Niko nodded and answered, "That way the colors will meet like friends." Grandpa Sol watched with quiet eyes and kept his hands free, ready only if they asked.

They split the work into two slow jobs the way they had promised themselves. Niko took charge of aligning the northern bands of green and gold while Anil matched the southern blues and browns, each child moving only an inch at a time and checking the printed edges after every small slide. The paper made a tiny whispering sound under their palms, and the hall lights had not yet been switched on, so the last daylight was all they needed. Niko felt the slight coolness of the tabletop through the map and kept breathing evenly. Nothing tore. Nothing wrinkled. The matching edges began to kiss like puzzle pieces that already knew each other.

Halfway along the middle seam a short cord had twisted into a soft knot that kept one clip from sliding free. Niko paused and looked up. Grandpa Sol stepped closer and offered only a quiet count, saying, "One loop, two loops, then the little tail," so Niko could ease the knot open without tugging hard. Anil held the map steady while Niko followed the gentle names of the loops, and the cord loosened like a sleepy ribbon. The temperature near the floor stayed mild, and Niko smelled only the clean paper and the lingering cedar from the box. They smiled at each other across the wide colored land.

With the knot free, Niko and Anil finished the last slow inches together, pressing the matched edges flat until every region sat true from border to border. Grandpa Sol handed them the brass clips one by one, and Niko fixed the top while Anil fixed the bottom, both working at the same calm pace. A paper cup of water sat on the nearest windowsill; Niko turned the cup so the handle faced the room so no one would bump it later. The hall felt peaceful, filled with the low creak of settling tables and the distant hush of leaves. Niko whispered, "We did it side by side," and Anil whispered back, "Together is the nicest way."

They stepped back to look at the finished map glowing in the evening light, every color edge neat and ready for the gentle fair. Grandpa Sol rested a kind hand on Niko's shoulder, and Anil stood close enough that their sleeves touched. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Niko felt the smooth floor again under their feet, heard the last quiet breath of air move through the high space, and knew the three of them had set the map right by sharing the slow work until it was done.
