Ms. Okonkwo stood at the long wooden counter in the bakery kitchen, her hands already dusted white as she pressed soft dough into neat rounds before the early table was set for the morning. The air held the smell of warm bread drifting from the low ovens, and Uma felt that gentle heat on their cheeks the moment they stepped inside. Beside a clean board sat the model bridge of sticks, the one that stood only when both sides were held and then not, its thin wooden pieces waiting in two small piles. Yael waited near the window where pale light touched the jam jars, and outside the wind in the fig leaves made a soft rustling that mixed with the quiet of the room. "Good morning, Uma," Ms. Okonkwo said without looking up, her voice steady and kind. Uma walked closer across the cool tiles and rested one hand lightly on the edge of the board, ready to begin the careful work.

Uma and Yael looked at the sticks together while Ms. Okonkwo kept shaping dough nearby. The bridge would go wrong if one person rushed, so they decided at once to split the building into two slow jobs that matched each other. Uma would hold the left side steady and fit the cross pieces one by one, and Yael would hold the right side and place the matching sticks with the same gentle pace. Ms. Okonkwo glanced over once and offered only a calm name for the knot they would need later. "That loop is called a baker’s hitch," she said, then returned to her dough. Uma felt the smooth grain of the first stick under their fingers and breathed in the quiet kitchen air that still carried warmth from the ovens.

They began with slow hands. Uma pressed the left base pieces into place while Yael mirrored the motion on the right, and neither of them hurried even when a stick slipped a little. The model bridge that stood only when both sides were held and then not needed both of them at every step, so Uma whispered the count of each piece as they worked. Outside the window the wind in the fig leaves kept its soft sound, a gentle company for their careful breathing. Ms. Okonkwo finished one tray of dough and wiped her hands on a clean cloth, watching without stepping closer. "You are keeping good time," she said in a low voice. Uma smiled and felt the cool ceramic rim of a nearby mixing bowl when their elbow brushed it by accident, a smooth circle that helped them stay steady.

Uma and Yael moved to the middle section next, still splitting the work so that one never pulled ahead of the other. Uma held the left uprights while Yael fitted the short crossbars, then they switched roles without speaking so each side stayed even. The sticks made tiny clicking sounds when they met, soft as breadcrumbs falling on wood. The kitchen light grew a little brighter as the early hour advanced, gold resting on the flour-dusted counter. Ms. Okonkwo set a finished loaf pan aside and simply counted aloud once, giving them a slow rhythm to match. "One, two, pause," she said, and Uma matched their next placement to that gentle count.

When the upper beams were ready, Uma felt a small wish to finish faster, but they remembered that rushing would make the bridge lean. Instead Uma and Yael kept their two slow jobs, Uma supporting the left span while Yael lowered the right span into place. The model bridge that stood only when both sides were held and then not began to look like a real little crossing, steady because both children stayed patient. The smell of warm bread grew stronger as another tray finished baking, filling the space with comfort. Yael’s hands were careful and sure, and Uma felt proud of their shared pace. Ms. Okonkwo nodded once from her place at the counter and offered nothing more than the quiet name of the next knot. "Baker’s hitch again," she murmured.

Uma tied the left knot slowly while Yael held both sides firm, then they traded so Yael could tie the right knot with the same care. The string felt smooth and dry between Uma’s fingers, and the finished knots sat neat and even. They released their hold together and watched. The bridge stood on its own, balanced and still, because neither side had been rushed. Soft light from the window rested on the little wooden span, and the kitchen felt peaceful around them. Uma let out a quiet breath and heard only the distant wind in the fig leaves beyond the glass. Ms. Okonkwo smiled without coming closer. "Well done, both of you," she said.

Uma carried the empty bowl that had held spare sticks over to the sink, set it down gently, and came back to the board where the finished bridge waited. The cool ceramic rim of another bowl still rested near their hand when they returned, smooth and pleasant under their palm. Yael stood beside them, and together they admired the steady little structure that had needed two slow jobs instead of one hurried pair of hands. Ms. Okonkwo moved a tray of cooling loaves farther down the counter, and the warm kitchen air wrapped around all three of them. Uma felt the soft flour dust on the wood under their wrists and knew the early table would soon be ready. The bridge stayed upright, quiet proof of their shared care.

They cleaned the last loose sticks into a small box while Ms. Okonkwo began setting plates for the early table. Uma wiped the board with a dry cloth, feeling the grain of the wood and the leftover warmth of the room. Yael helped fold the cloth afterward, and the two friends stood side by side looking at the model bridge one more time. Outside, the fig leaves still moved with a soft sound that seemed to praise their patience. Ms. Okonkwo poured water into glasses with a gentle clink. "The table is nearly ready," she said. Uma felt calm and happy, their shoulders loose after the careful work.

Uma and Yael stepped back so the finished bridge could sit safely near the window while the bakery kitchen filled with the last preparations for the early table. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Uma rested both hands on the cool edge of the counter and listened to the quiet settle around the warm bread scent and the distant leaves. Ms. Okonkwo dimmed the brighter lights one by one, leaving only the gentle glow. Yael yawned a small sleepy yawn, and Uma felt their own eyes grow heavy in the peaceful room. They had finished together, and the little bridge stood ready for morning, held first by four careful hands and then by balance alone.
