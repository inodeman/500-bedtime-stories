A tiny silver tinkle drifted across the soft rug near the window seat, and Dahlia paused mid-step to notice it. Warm lamplight pooled on the polished floorboards of the hall just inside the cottage, and the air felt gently cool on Dahlia’s bare arms after the walk home. They turned slowly, curious, and watched a gray tail slip under the bench. The little sound came again, clearer now, and Dahlia knew it was the cat’s collar bell ringing as the cat curled into a nap. Dahlia breathed in and caught a faint clean smell of soapy wool from the knitted blanket folded there, and they felt glad the cat was settled. Beyond the open front door the porch waited in honey-colored evening light, and Dahlia could already hear Cousin Pio’s careful footsteps on the boards outside.

Dahlia stepped onto the cottage porch where the wooden planks still held the day’s mild warmth under their socks. Cousin Pio stood near the steps holding two large herb pots wrapped in brown paper that had begun to tear along every fold. The paper crackled softly each time Pio shifted grip, and bright green leaves poked through the gaps like tiny flags. Dahlia saw how the pots tilted unevenly and how Pio’s elbows stuck out while they tried to keep both from slipping. A cooler evening breeze brushed past Dahlia’s ears and carried the damp earth scent of the pots. “Those look heavy,” Dahlia said gently, staying close so Pio would not feel alone with the awkward load.

Dahlia reached out and slid both hands under one pot without waiting for anyone to ask. Through a tear in the wrapping the ceramic felt cool and solid against their palms, and they steadied the base with care. “I can take this one,” Dahlia said. Cousin Pio let out a small breath of relief and settled the remaining pot more securely against their chest. Together they turned toward the doorway, and the light from inside spilled golden across the threshold onto Dahlia’s shoes. The temperature dropped a little as they moved from the open porch air into the sheltered hall, and Dahlia kept both arms steady around their share of the herbs while the paper rustled like quiet leaves.

They carried the pots carefully across the hall just inside, where the floor felt smooth and cool under Dahlia’s feet. Cousin Pio set their pot down first on the low table near the wall, and the paper tore a little more with a soft ripping whisper. Dahlia placed theirs beside it and smoothed one torn flap so the dark soil would stay safely inside. “Thank you for helping with the basil and the mint,” Cousin Pio said. Dahlia only nodded and patted the side of the pot, feeling the clay firm and pleasantly chill. A long stripe of evening light stretched across the hall floor and touched the edge of Dahlia’s sleeve with a gentle glow that made the green leaves shine.

Dahlia noticed that the leather lace tying one paper wrapping had come loose and dangled beside the pot. They ran a finger along the leather lace, which felt smooth and slightly worn from earlier knots, and they retied it in a neat bow so the paper would hold through the night. The hall filled with the sharp green scent of the herbs, and Dahlia liked how it mixed with the quiet of the cottage. “Shall we put them by the sunny window for tomorrow?” Cousin Pio asked. Dahlia answered, “Yes, the light will be good there in the morning.” They lifted the lighter pot again and walked slowly, listening to their own careful steps on the boards and feeling the steady weight against their chest.

They set both pots on the wide sill just inside the hall where morning sun would reach first. The clay remained cool under Dahlia’s fingertips, and a few crumbs of potting soil dusted the sill with a dry, powdery feel. Cousin Pio peeled away the worst of the torn paper and folded it into a small neat square that fit in one hand. Dahlia fetched a soft cloth from the hook and wiped the table clean, the cloth still warm from sitting near the kitchen stove. Outside the porch grew quieter as evening settled deeper, and the air in the hall stayed pleasantly mild against their skin. Dahlia and Cousin Pio stood side by side for a calm moment watching the green leaves settle after their journey from market.

Dahlia sat on the low bench in the hall and rested both hands on the smooth wood that felt solid and familiar. The herbs on the sill cast small leafy shadows in the fading light, and Dahlia watched them shift whenever a thin breeze found the open door. Cousin Pio brought two cups of warm milk from the kitchen, and the cups felt pleasantly hot against Dahlia’s palms as they wrapped their fingers around one. They sipped the mild sweetness together in easy silence and set the cups down carefully on the table edge. Dahlia swung their legs a little and listened to the soft creak of the bench, content that the pots now stood steady and safe after the walk home. The hall held a gentle hush broken only by the occasional settling of old wood.

As the sky beyond the porch turned a soft deep blue, Dahlia rose and closed the front door most of the way so the hall would stay cozy. The wooden door handle felt familiar and solid in their grip, and a thin line of cooler outdoor air still slipped under the sill like a quiet ribbon. They checked the herb pots once more, making sure neither leaned, and brushed a stray scrap of paper into their pocket. Cousin Pio hummed a low quiet tune while folding leftover wrapping, and the soft sound filled the hall like distant bees. Dahlia felt sleepy contentment settle over their shoulders, warm and easy, and they smiled at the green leaves waiting for morning water in the dimming room.

Dahlia curled onto the window seat cushion with the knitted throw nearby and listened to the house grow still. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Its gentle light painted a golden circle on the floor near the herb pots, and Dahlia watched the leaves gleam faintly before they closed their eyes. The hall held a peaceful quiet broken only by the distant settling of porch boards, and the temperature felt perfect for resting. Cousin Pio whispered, “Sleep well, Dahlia,” from the doorway, and Dahlia answered with a sleepy, “Good night.” They breathed slowly and let the calm of the cottage porch and the safe hall carry them toward quiet dreams of green gardens.
