Auntie Luz sat cross-legged in the music corner already, wiping the slender recorder with a folded cloth while soft yellow light from the high window warmed the wooden floor. Eko walked in on quiet feet and felt the mild coolness of the air near the instruments. Auntie Luz looked up and smiled without stopping her careful work. “Come sit with me, Eko,” she said in her ordinary gentle voice. Eko lowered themself onto the round cushion next to the wide drum and rested both palms on their knees for a moment, feeling the steady calm of the room. The recorder gleamed a little brighter under Auntie Luz’s cloth, and the drum’s skin looked ready under the same kind light.

Eko glanced at the drum and then at their own left hand resting open on one knee. They remembered the short pattern they had practiced only with the right hand before, and a shy feeling rose like a small cloud. The music corner held the drum close and the recorder on its stand, both waiting without hurry. Auntie Luz set the cloth aside and breathed in the quiet. Eko smelled the clean scent of fresh paper from the open notebook of simple notes lying nearby. “The drum is here whenever you want it,” Auntie Luz said, still kind and unhurried. Eko kept both palms on their knees and listened to the soft hush of the house around them.

Outside a gate latch clicked once with a clear small sound that drifted through the open window and then faded. Eko lifted their left hand a little and let it hover above the drum skin. The shy cloud stayed, yet Eko did not look away or pretend the drum was only for looking. Auntie Luz hummed a tiny note on the recorder, just enough to make the air feel friendly. Eko felt the cushion under them and the warm floor beyond. “I might try the pattern with my left hand,” Eko said in a voice that could be heard, small but steady. Auntie Luz nodded once and stayed right where she was.

Eko placed their left palm flat on the drum and noticed how smooth and cool the skin felt. They thought of the three soft taps they knew, the ones that made a round little bounce of sound. Auntie Luz watched without leaning forward or asking for more. Eko drew a slow breath and tapped once with the left fingers, then twice more in the short pattern. The sound was quieter than usual yet clear in the corner. “That was the pattern,” Eko said, still in a voice meant to be heard. Auntie Luz smiled and answered, “I heard every part of it, Eko.”

The wall beside the cushions felt cool and slightly damp under Eko’s shoulder when they leaned back for a rest after the taps. It was a damp clay wall that held the day’s mild temperature and made the music corner feel snug. Eko kept the left hand near the drum and tried the same three taps again, a little more even this time. Auntie Luz picked up the recorder and played one long gentle note that floated like a soft ribbon. Eko listened and felt the shy cloud grow thinner. The light from the window stayed steady and kind across the floorboards.

Eko rested both palms on their knees again and looked at Auntie Luz. “Can I try it once more while you play a tiny bit?” they asked. Auntie Luz answered right away, “Of course, Eko. I will keep it soft.” She blew a slow quiet phrase on the recorder while Eko used the left hand for the short drum pattern. The two sounds met without rushing. Eko felt the cushion under them and the calm air moving a little with each breath. The drum answered the left fingers with the same round bounce, and Eko heard it clearly.

Una appeared in the doorway for a short visit, carrying nothing but a smile, and sat on the edge of another cushion without speaking at first. Eko showed them the left-hand taps once more, still using the small brave voice. “This is the pattern I tried with my left hand,” Eko said. Una nodded and listened. Auntie Luz kept the recorder quiet so the drum could be heard. The light stayed golden and easy on everyone’s faces. Eko felt glad that the shy feeling had not stopped the taps.

After Una waved and stepped away again, Eko and Auntie Luz stayed in the music corner a little longer. Eko tried the left-hand pattern one last slow time, then let both hands rest open on the drum skin. Auntie Luz set the recorder down and spoke softly. “You chose the smallest brave try, Eko, and I am glad you let me hear it.” Eko smiled and felt the warm floor under their feet. The air smelled clean and the corner held its quiet shape around them. No one asked for a bigger show or a faster beat.

Eko leaned back once more, closed their eyes for a slow breath, and opened them to the familiar room. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Auntie Luz folded the cloth and the notebook of notes. Eko kept the memory of the left-hand taps close, along with the kind voice that had answered without pushing. The cushion stayed comfortable, the drum skin cool, and the whole music corner ready for tomorrow’s ordinary calm.
