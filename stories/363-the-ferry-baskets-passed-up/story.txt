The bright smell of lemon soap filled the air as Bodhi stood at the little outdoor basin, their hands already busy turning the smooth bar over and over under the cool running water. Tiny bubbles clung to Bodhi’s wrists and sparkled in the mild afternoon light before they rinsed them away with a quiet splash. The wooden planks under Bodhi’s bare feet felt sun-warmed and slightly rough as they dried their fingers and stepped toward the garden. Ahead lay the neat garden row where green leaves climbed the taut string lines, and two watering cans rested side by side in the dappled shade. Just beyond, the low dock met the gentle water, and the raised wooden deck waited above for the baskets that needed careful passing. Bodhi walked slowly, listening to the soft creak of the boards and feeling ready to help.

Bodhi reached the garden row and touched one of the cool metal watering cans, noticing how the string stretched straight between the wooden stakes like quiet guidelines. The baskets sat stacked on the dock below the deck, filled with soft packets of seeds and small potted herbs that smelled faintly of earth after the lemon soap had faded from their hands. Nim arrived with quiet steps and stood beside Bodhi, both of them looking at the short distance the baskets had to travel upward. Miss Dalca waited near the string, her hands resting lightly on the second watering can. The light slanted gently across the leaves, and the air felt pleasantly warm without being heavy. Bodhi knew the baskets would tip or tangle if anyone hurried, so they stayed still and thought about a slower way.

“We should not rush the baskets,” Bodhi said softly, their voice calm in the quiet garden air. Nim nodded and answered, “We can split the work into two slow jobs instead.” Miss Dalca smiled and offered, “I can give you a steady count, or I can name the simple knot you need for the string.” Bodhi chose the count for the lifting and the knot name for the securing, and the three of them began without hurry. The deck boards felt solid and slightly cool under Bodhi’s palms as they leaned to receive the first basket. Sunlight filtered through the branches in soft patches, and the watering cans stayed ready nearby for later. Everything moved at the pace of quiet breathing.

Bodhi took the job of waiting on the deck edge and guiding each basket onto the wide planks once it rose, while Nim stayed lower to lift and steady from the dock side. They passed the first basket with careful hands, the woven sides feeling firm and dry against Bodhi’s fingers. Miss Dalca spoke the name of the knot clearly so Bodhi could loop the garden string through the handle without twisting. “It is called a simple loop hitch,” Miss Dalca said, and Bodhi repeated the motion slowly until the knot held. The light stayed mild and even, and no one spoke above a gentle tone. Bodhi felt the solid wood of the deck under their knees as they settled each basket in place.

The second basket rose just as slowly, and Bodhi guided it with both hands while Nim supported the bottom until it cleared the edge. Wind in the fig leaves made a soft rustling sound that drifted across the garden row and mixed with the quiet scrape of the baskets on wood. Bodhi checked the string line once more and adjusted a loose end so nothing pulled too tight. Miss Dalca kept her calm count going in a low voice that matched their movements. “One for the lift, two for the rest, three for the set-down,” she said, and Bodhi followed each number without skipping ahead. The two watering cans gleamed dully in the shade, waiting for the moment the baskets were safe.

When the third basket came up, Bodhi and Nim switched their slow jobs for a moment so each could feel both parts of the work. Bodhi knelt on the dock boards that felt damp and cool from the nearby water, while Nim waited above on the deck. They moved together so the basket never swung or tilted. Miss Dalca named the knot again when the string was needed, and Bodhi tied it with steady fingers. The air carried a light warmth that settled on Bodhi’s neck like a soft scarf. Every basket settled in a neat row on the deck, ready for the garden later, and no one hurried even once.

Bodhi carried one lighter basket down the short steps into the garden row itself and set it beside the string lines where the plants would soon receive the seeds. Nim brought the watering cans closer, the metal sides cool and smooth under their shared grip. Miss Dalca watched the placement and offered one more quiet count while they arranged everything. Bodhi smoothed a bit of soil with their palm and felt the fine grains slip between their fingers. The light had grown softer and lower, painting long gentle shadows across the path. They worked side by side until every basket sat exactly where it belonged.

Later Bodhi and Nim wiped the small wooden table on the deck in slow circles with a floured towel that felt dry and powdery against their hands, clearing away a few stray leaves. Miss Dalca poured a little water from one can into the other so both would be ready for morning. Bodhi listened to the quiet drip and felt the evening air turn pleasantly cooler on their arms. “We finished every basket together,” Bodhi said, and Nim smiled in answer. The garden row looked peaceful with its string still straight and the baskets safe on the deck above the dock. Everything smelled clean and earthy in the fading light.

Bodhi sat on the edge of the deck with Nim close by while Miss Dalca gathered the empty string scraps into one neat bundle. The boards under Bodhi felt smooth now that the day had cooled, and the last soft light touched the tops of the plants below. They watched the water lap gently against the dock posts without any hurry left in the air. Bodhi rested their hands in their lap and breathed in the calm of the finished work. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Soon Bodhi would go inside for sleep, carrying the quiet feeling of the shared slow jobs all the way to their pillow.
