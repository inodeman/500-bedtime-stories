"Can we start the swing count one more time before sleep?" Kei asked, their voice quiet as the warm yellow light from the small lamp pooled across the bedroom rug of cushions, puppets, and paper.

Kei sat cross-legged in the middle of the soft cushions and smoothed a long strip of fresh paper that still carried its clean, sharp smell. Papa leaned against the low bookcase and watched without speaking, his hands folded. Ximena stood just beyond the edge of the rug near the doorway, holding a paper puppet whose string hung loose. Earlier the children had drawn a row of swings on the paper and taken turns naming who would pretend to ride each one, yet when Kei and the puppets reached the end of the list the count had skipped a name and left Ximena standing still. The air in the room felt pleasantly cool against Kei’s bare feet, and they glanced once at Ximena’s quiet face before looking down at the crayons scattered like tiny lanterns of color.

Kei picked up a red crayon and rolled it between their fingers, feeling the smooth wax while the paper rustled under their knees. They remembered how the count had jumped from their own name straight to a puppet called Blue Bird and then to Papa’s name for fun, never landing on Ximena. The cushions smelled faintly of laundry soap and held the gentle warmth of the afternoon sun that had left them earlier. Kei did not want anyone left outside the circle of play. They shifted one round blue cushion closer to the open space and then another, making a wider nest of soft shapes that faced the paper swings. Papa’s quiet breathing stayed steady; he did not interrupt.

Ximena took a single step nearer when Kei patted the new open cushion. The paper crackled again as Kei unfolded a second sheet and drew one more swing with careful loops. “This swing belongs right here in the middle,” Kei said, tapping the fresh drawing so the crayon made a soft scratching sound. The bedroom felt snug, the temperature even and calm, and the puppets lay in a friendly heap of cloth and buttons. Kei looked at Ximena and then at the empty cushion. “You can sit with us now and choose the next name yourself if you like.” Papa only nodded once, a small movement that told the children the choice was theirs alone.

Kei waited while Ximena settled onto the cushion, the fabric sighing under her. They slowed the whole game on purpose, speaking each name more slowly than before and leaving a long soft pause between them. “Kei swings first,” Kei whispered, then waited. “Ximena swings next.” The words felt round and easy in the quiet room. A distant gate latch clicked once outside the open window, a small metal sound that drifted in with the evening air and made both children glance toward the curtains before they smiled. The paper swings now held every name in turn, and Kei pushed the crayons into a neat row so nothing felt rushed.

They began the pretend ride together. Kei held one edge of the paper while Ximena held the other, and the two of them lifted and lowered the strip in a gentle rhythm that matched a real swing’s soft arc. The puppets rode along the crease, their cloth bodies warm from being held. Kei rested both palms on their knees for a moment and breathed in the calm of the rug. “Your turn to say how high the swing goes,” Kei told Ximena, and Ximena answered in a voice no louder than a moth’s wing, “Just a little higher, then slow again.” Papa stayed where he was, letting the slow pace and the new seating do all the work.

Kei reached for a small clay figure that sat near the puppet pile, a tiny house they had shaped earlier. The outer wall of the clay house still felt cool and damp under their fingertips, smooth and yielding like river mud after rain. They set the clay house beside the paper swings so the puppets could pretend to rest there between rides. Light from the lamp touched the damp clay and made it shine faintly. Ximena placed her puppet on the roof of the little house and laughed a quiet laugh that sounded like beads rolling. Kei laughed too, the sound low and happy, and the cushions under them held their weight without a single crease of worry.

The game continued until the paper swings had carried every name three full times. Kei kept the counting slow and even, never hurrying past anyone. “We can stop when the last swing lands softly,” Kei said, and Ximena replied, “It already feels soft.” They lowered the paper together onto the rug. The room had grown a little cooler as night settled, yet the cushions kept a pocket of warmth around their legs. Papa rose only long enough to pull the curtain halfway closed so the outdoor sounds stayed gentle, then returned to his place by the books. Kei stacked the puppets into a careful tower and smoothed the paper one last time so the drawn swings lay flat and peaceful.

Kei and Ximena leaned back against the largest cushion and listened to the quiet house. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Its light reached the clay house, the paper, and the resting puppets in a calm golden wash, and Kei felt the last of the day’s play settle into something still and safe.
