Neighbor Sami stood midway along the ferry deck, already wiping the wooden snack counter with slow circles of a damp cloth that left cool streaks in the warm afternoon air. The rail beside them shone under the low slanting light, and only two elbows of room remained between the counter and the water-side edge where the boards smelled faintly of clean soap. Dori walked up and rested both hands on the smooth rail, feeling the gentle rock of the boat beneath their shoes while Neighbor Sami kept working without hurry. A small line of children waited near the counter for fruit cups and crackers, and someone had already left a folded napkin marking a held place right in the middle. Milo stood just outside that little gathering, close enough to see the napkin yet not quite inside the circle of elbows and soft talk.

Dori noticed the way Milo shifted from one foot to the other and heard the quiet scrape of sneakers on the deck planks. The sunlight made bright coins of reflection dance across the water below the rail. “The pears look good today,” Neighbor Sami said in a calm voice while still wiping, and the air near the counter carried the fresh smell of pear skins where a few had been sliced ready for the cups. Dori watched Milo’s eyes follow the napkin that held the spot in line and decided the space needed a small change that would not point anyone out. The ferry engines hummed low and steady, a sound that mixed with the soft lap of water against the hull.

Dori stepped nearer the counter and felt the mild heat rising from the sun-warmed boards through the soles of their shoes. “Milo, will you help me carry the cups once they are ready?” Dori asked, keeping the words ordinary and even. Milo nodded and moved one step closer so that an elbow of room opened beside the held place. Neighbor Sami simply rinsed the cloth in a basin of cool water and let the children arrange themselves, offering no extra directions. The light had turned a softer gold now, and it touched the edges of the rail like a quiet blanket.

Dori reached into a pocket and found a warm coin left from earlier, the metal smooth and pleasant against their fingers as they turned it once. That small texture helped Dori think of a slower pace. Instead of rushing forward, Dori shifted the folded napkin a little sideways so that two places sat side by side without anyone needing to call it special. “We can stand here together and wait,” Dori said to Milo, and the words floated gently over the deck. Milo’s shoulders eased, and the two of them fit into the two elbows of room with room enough to breathe. The breeze lifted a few loose leaves from the potted fig trees set along the far rail for shade.

The sound of wind in the fig leaves arrived then, a soft rustling that mixed with the water and made the whole deck feel slower and kinder. Dori and Milo watched Neighbor Sami set out the fruit cups in a neat row while the line moved at an easy pace. No one hurried, and the held place became simply the spot where Dori and Milo stood shoulder to shoulder. The air cooled a degree as a thin cloud passed the sun, and the change felt pleasant on bare arms. “Thank you for saving the space with me,” Milo said quietly, and Dori answered, “It fits us both just right.”

Neighbor Sami poured a little water for the fig trees and left the children to finish the small plan themselves. Dori used one toe to nudge a loose coil of rope so it lay straight against the rail, a calm motion that cleared a few more inches. The snack cups smelled sweet when they were handed over, and Dori and Milo took theirs without leaving the comfortable spot they had shaped. Light from the lowering sun painted long gentle stripes across the deck boards. The ferry rocked in a slow rhythm that matched the way they stood, elbows free and faces easy.

Dori felt the warm coin again while tucking it deeper into the pocket, then offered Milo first choice of the pear slices still fragrant inside the cup. The two of them leaned on the rail together and watched the water glitter. “The boat moves like a big rocking chair,” Dori said, and Milo smiled at the idea. Neighbor Sami stacked the empty trays with soft clicks of wood on wood and stayed near enough to be present yet far enough to let the new arrangement stay the children’s own. Temperature along the deck had settled into a mild evening coolness that made every breath feel clean.

Shadows lengthened in a friendly way across the two elbows of room, and the held place in the snack line had become simply the place where friends stood. Dori and Milo finished the last sweet bites while the fig leaves kept their quiet motion overhead. The rail felt solid and smooth under resting hands. Neighbor Sami gave one small nod that needed no words, and the ferry continued its calm path across the water. Everything about the deck felt settled and right, as if the space had always been meant for three quiet people and two small cups.

Later the children sat on a low bench near the cabin door while the sky turned the color of soft peaches. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Dori listened to the steady hush of the engines and felt the last warmth of the day on their cheeks as Milo rested nearby and Neighbor Sami folded the clean cloths with slow care.
