A mild evening breeze brushed the wooden harbor step and cooled the planks where the water lapped with a quiet hush. Violeta stood close beside the step and felt the soft air move across their cheeks while seagulls called far off. The sky held a pale pink light that made every crate glow gently. Papa waited there with a stack of neat parcels ready to change hands, and Violeta watched the way the light touched his shoulders. The boards under Violeta’s shoes felt smooth and a little damp from the sea spray that never quite dried. They breathed in the clean salt air and stayed near Papa without rushing.

Papa wore a long white apron over his clothes because he helped pack the evening parcels of fresh bread and fruit for the boats. The apron strings hung loose behind him and needed a proper bow so they would not flap while he worked. Violeta noticed how the strings tangled whenever Papa reached for another wrapped box. A faint scent of orange peel drifted from one open crate where someone had peeled a fruit earlier for a quick snack. Violeta stepped nearer and saw Papa try once more to gather both ends with one hand while holding a parcel steady with the other. The light from the low sun warmed the side of Violeta’s face as they waited quietly.

“These strings will not stay put tonight,” Papa said with a small smile that showed he was not upset, only busy. Violeta heard the soft splash of a rope against a post and felt the cool evening settle deeper around them. They reached out and took one end of the apron string in their own careful fingers. The cotton felt smooth and a little stiff from many washings. Papa kept hold of the heavy parcel so nothing would tip. Violeta guided their half of the string around and waited for Papa to meet them with the other half. Together they made the first loop without hurry while the harbor water tapped gently against the step.

“I can hold this part steady for you,” Violeta said in a low voice that matched the calm of the place. Papa nodded and passed the second string into place so Violeta could finish the bow. Their hands moved slowly and the knot sat flat and neat against Papa’s back. A linen napkin that had been tucked under one parcel brushed Violeta’s wrist with its crisp dry weave when they shifted a box aside. The texture felt clean and familiar, like the cloths at home. Violeta smoothed the finished bow once more so it would not loosen while Papa handed the next parcel across the step to the waiting boatman.

The boatman took the parcel with a nod and the exchange felt easy and kind. Violeta stayed close and helped lift the lighter packages one by one so Papa’s hands stayed free. “You make the work lighter when you stand right here,” Papa said, and Violeta felt a quiet warmth spread through their chest at the simple words. The air cooled another degree and the pink light softened toward gold. They heard a tin clock tick from the little harbor office nearby, its steady metal sound marking the peaceful minutes. Violeta counted the soft ticks for a moment while they waited for the next box, just to keep their mind calm and present.

Ciro arrived then with a small empty basket and offered to carry the last two light parcels down the short ramp. “I can take these to the lower dock if you like,” Ciro said, and Violeta smiled at their friend. Papa thanked Ciro and handed over the basket while Violeta checked that the apron bow still held firm. The three of them stood together on the step for a quiet breath while the water made tiny ripples that caught the fading light. Violeta felt the wood grain under their palm when they rested a hand on the railing. Everything around them smelled clean and the orange peel note still lingered faintly from the earlier crate.

When the last parcel had changed hands, Papa untied the apron carefully so it would not drag. Violeta helped fold it into a neat square and tucked the strings inside. “Shall we walk home before the lamps come on fully?” Papa asked, and Violeta answered, “Yes, the path feels nice this evening.” They left the harbor step side by side with Ciro waving goodbye from the ramp. The temperature dropped just enough that Violeta pulled their light sweater closer and felt the soft knit against their arms. The path home wound past quiet houses where windows already showed warm squares of light.

Inside their own kitchen the air held a gentle warmth from the stove that had been banked low. Violeta set the folded apron on the chair and washed their hands at the sink while Papa poured two cups of warm milk. They did not count drips but instead watched the steam rise in slow curls and felt the smooth ceramic of the mug. Papa spoke once more in a peaceful tone. “Your hands made the bow just right today,” he said, and Violeta only nodded and sipped, glad the work had gone smoothly. The kitchen light was low and golden and the whole room felt settled and safe after the harbor air.

Later Violeta climbed into bed while the house grew quieter still. Papa sat nearby for a short while and the mattress felt firm and welcoming under them. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Violeta closed their eyes and heard only the distant hush of the harbor carried on the night air, and the last of the day’s coolness drifted away as sleep came easy and deep.
