"Vera, come closer to the hearth while the clock keeps its slow and friendly time," Auntie June said, and the low fire painted soft golden light across the cottage stones as warm air rose and brushed Vera's bare toes.

Vera sat cross-legged on the thick rug and felt the gentle heat soak into their knees while Auntie June lifted a floured towel from the bread board and folded it with careful hands. The cloth felt dry and powdery under her fingers, and a little cloud of white dust drifted down like quiet snow. Vera wanted to jump up and tell about the bright feather they had found that afternoon, yet the steady ticking of the unhurried clock and the mild crackle of the fire made them stay still and listen instead. Outside the partly open window the night air moved, and the scent of lemon soap still clung to Auntie June's sleeves from washing the baking bowls. Vera watched the flames dance and breathed in that clean citrus smell mixed with wood smoke.

Auntie June smoothed the floured towel once more and began in a voice as calm as the hearth itself. "Long ago this cottage window learned to stay open just one finger wide," she said, "so the cool breath of evening could slip inside without scattering the papers or waking the sleeping cat." Vera pressed their palms against the warm floorboards and noticed how the light from the fire made tiny shadows sway on the wall. They almost spoke about Uma's new blue kite, yet they kept the words tucked away and leaned nearer so every soft syllable could reach them. The temperature near the stones felt perfect, neither too hot nor chilly, and Vera's shoulders relaxed as they watched Auntie June's kind face.

The story continued while the unhurried clock counted each peaceful minute. "The finger-wide space lets the garden talk to us," Auntie June explained, and just then the sound of wind in the fig leaves rose like a low green whisper through the gap. Vera heard the gentle rustle clearly and felt the faintest cool current touch their cheek. They wanted to burst out with news of the feather's shiny edges, but they waited, eyes fixed on Auntie June, and the fire's orange light flickered across their lap. The cottage felt safe and round, filled with the quiet company of wood and stone and the soft lemon soap scent that still floated near the washing basin.

Auntie June paused to brush a speck of flour from her apron, then went on. "When the window stays open that little way, the morning birds can send their first songs inside, and the bread dough rises happier because the air never grows heavy." Vera nodded slowly and ran one fingertip along the rug's rough weave, feeling every loop of yarn. The hearth stones radiated steady warmth that reached their ankles, and they kept their bigger news folded inside like a secret pebble. Outside the fig leaves answered the breeze again, a dry silvery hush that mixed with the fire's low pop. Vera breathed the lingering lemon soap and stayed quiet all the way through.

"Even on the stillest nights the open finger of space reminds us we share the cottage with the garden," Auntie June said, her words slow as the clock's hands. Vera watched the flames curl around a log and felt the air shift from warm to cooler near the window gap. They longed to speak of how Uma had laughed when the feather spun in the sunlight, yet they held the wish and listened to the very last sentence. The floured towel lay folded on the table, its powdery texture catching the firelight in pale patches, and Vera's ears stayed open for every gentle detail Auntie June offered.

When the tale ended, Auntie June rested her hands in her lap and smiled at Vera across the glowing hearth. Vera waited one more heartbeat, feeling the rug's soft give under their legs and hearing only the unhurried clock and the distant wind in the fig leaves. Then they asked their one real question in a small clear voice. "Auntie June, does the window ever feel lonely if the finger space is closed all the way?" The lemon soap smell drifted once more as Auntie June shook her head kindly. "No, Vera, it simply rests, knowing the garden will wait." Warm light bathed both of them, and the temperature stayed kind.

Vera thought for a moment while the fire sighed and the floured towel kept its quiet place on the table. They wiped a crumb from the wooden surface in slow circles with one careful hand, feeling the smooth grain under their fingers. Only then did they share something small of their own. "I found a feather today," Vera said softly. "It was gray with a silver tip, and Uma said it looked like a tiny sail." Auntie June nodded, and the cottage air felt gentle against Vera's arms as the clock ticked on without hurry. The sound of wind in the fig leaves brushed the open window again, light as a sigh.

Vera leaned their head against Auntie June's knee and felt the last of the day's warmth settle into the room. The hearth glowed lower now, painting everything the color of honey, and the lemon soap scent faded to a clean memory. Outside the fig leaves rested too. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
