Soft evening light rested on the wooden bars of the meadow gate like a warm blanket just before supper, and the tall grass beyond it swayed only a little as if the day itself were settling down. Yelena walked up the slight slope with careful steps and felt the cool air of late day brush their cheeks. They paused at the open gate where every afternoon game usually began, noticing how the boards felt smooth under their fingers when they rested a hand there. Farther out the meadow waited green and open, and the first stars had not yet decided to appear. Yelena breathed in the gentle temperature of the place and smiled because the gate always smelled faintly of dry wood and leaves. They waited a moment longer, listening to the quiet hush that meant the fun could start soon.

Just outside the circle of ready feet stood Zia, close enough to watch yet not quite inside, and near Zia’s shoes lay an open notebook that already held one careful doodle of two people holding hands under a little sun. The paper still showed empty white space beside that first drawing, room for another picture if anyone chose to add it. Mr. Ade sat on the low stone bench by the gate post with his own quiet smile, letting the children decide what came next. Yelena saw the notebook pages lift once in a tiny breeze and felt the soft temperature of the air change against their bare arms. They looked from Zia to the empty half of the page and back again. The light from the lowering sun painted everything gold and calm.

“I brought the notebook so we could draw our team before we run,” Yelena said, keeping their voice even and friendly. Zia glanced down at the open pages but did not step forward yet. The meadow gate held its usual place as the starting line, yet something about the seating of the children left a small gap right beside the notebook. Mr. Ade only watched with kind eyes and did not speak, trusting them to find their own way. Yelena noticed how the grass near the gate felt cool against their ankles when they shifted closer. They wanted the space to feel open for everyone without any loud announcement.

Yelena gently slid the notebook a little farther into the middle so its blank side faced the group and then patted the ground right next to Zia. “We can all sit in a new half-circle here by the gate,” they said. “That way the drawing and the first game share the same starting spot.” Zia’s shoulders eased as they settled onto the grass. The wool sleeve of Mr. Ade’s jacket brushed the edge of the bench when he leaned forward just enough to see, and the fabric felt thick and soft whenever anyone’s hand happened to touch it by accident. Light from the open meadow still reached them in long gentle beams.

A small cat trotted past the gate post, and the clear ring of a cat's collar bell floated once through the quiet air before the animal wandered off again into the taller grass. Yelena picked up the pencil that lay in the notebook’s fold and held it out so both they and Zia could share it. “Your turn for the second doodle,” Yelena offered without making it sound like a special prize. Zia took the pencil and began a simple smiling face beside the first drawing of two people. The other children scooted nearer until everyone sat in the new half-circle Yelena had shaped. The temperature near the ground stayed mild and pleasant against their legs.

Mr. Ade remained on the bench and only nodded once, happy to let the children solve the seating and the pace themselves. “Maybe we walk the first round instead of racing,” Yelena suggested, changing the usual speed so no one felt hurried. “We can look at the doodles while we go.” Zia finished the second little person on the page, and now the notebook held a complete pair of friends under the same sun. The pages felt smooth and slightly warm from the afternoon light when Yelena closed it halfway to keep the drawings safe. Everyone agreed with soft voices that the slower start felt just right.

They rose together and stepped through the meadow gate side by side, the notebook carried carefully in Yelena’s hands so both doodles stayed visible. “Look how the two people match now,” Zia said, and Yelena answered, “They do, and there is still a little corner left if we want flowers later.” The group moved at the gentle pace Yelena had chosen, feeling the cool tips of the grass brush their shoes. Mr. Ade walked a few steps behind them, still letting the children lead. The light softened further into a dusty rose color across the open field.

After one calm loop they returned to the gate and sat once more in the half-circle Yelena had made, the notebook open again between them. The air carried a faint sweet smell of cocoa from the thermos Mr. Ade now opened on the bench, and he poured small warm cups for anyone who wanted one. Yelena held their cup carefully and watched the steam rise in thin curls. Zia leaned close so both could look at the finished doodles together. The children talked in quiet turns about what game might come next, and every idea left room for every person. The temperature of the cocoa warmed their fingers through the cups.

When the last of the daylight slipped lower, Yelena rocked once on the broad gate rail and let the motion stop by itself while the notebook rested safely on their knees. They felt full of the easy company of the afternoon. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Everyone gathered their things without hurry, the meadow gate still standing open behind them like a friendly doorway, and the two doodles stayed side by side on the page as they walked home under the first true stars.
