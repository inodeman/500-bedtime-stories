The warm smell of cedar rose from the porch beams as Chioma’s small hands already busied themselves among the dry leaves that had drifted against the cottage steps. Soft golden light from the late afternoon rested on the boards, and Chioma felt the mild air brush their arms while they gathered a few crackling leaves into a neat heap. Cousin Pio stood a little farther along the porch, frowning at the thick skirt of dry leaves that hid the smooth path leading straight to the open door. Chioma glanced up and saw how the leaves made every step awkward for Pio, who kept pausing to kick them aside with the side of one shoe. “The path is still under here somewhere,” Pio said, voice quiet and a bit tired. Chioma smiled and kept working, already deciding to share the job without waiting to be asked.

Cool shadows stretched across the porch boards while Chioma and Pio began to clear the leaves together, the dry rustle rising under their fingers. Chioma took the left side of the hidden path and swept the leaves into small piles with careful strokes of both hands, feeling the rough edges of each leaf against their palms. Pio worked the right side, and soon a smooth pale strip of stone appeared between them like a quiet ribbon. The light grew softer and warmer as the sun slipped lower, painting the cottage wall in gentle orange. “You are making that half look so tidy,” Pio said, glancing over with a grateful little grin. Chioma only nodded and kept brushing, happy that the path was becoming easy to walk again for both of them.

A faint breeze carried the sound of wind in the fig leaves from the tree beside the porch, a steady hush that mixed with the soft scrape of clearing. Chioma paused for a moment to listen, then bent again so their shoulders stayed near the ground where the remaining leaves still covered the smooth stones. Pio stretched and rubbed both hands together, the air around them turning a touch cooler as evening settled closer. Chioma noticed the piles growing tall and carefully shifted their own heap farther from the doorway so no one would trip. “Shall we carry these piles to the garden edge together?” Pio asked. Chioma answered, “Yes, that will keep the path open all the way to the hall.”

Inside the hall just beyond the porch door the air felt still and gently warm, and Chioma stepped onto a straw mat that lay across the wooden floor, its woven surface firm and dry under their bare feet. They set down an armful of leaves they had carried in by mistake and then turned back to help Pio finish the last stretch of the path. The lantern light from deeper in the cottage spilled faintly into the hall, making the smooth stones outside glow a soft gray. Chioma brushed the final leaves aside so the whole path lay clear and even, wide enough for two people to walk side by side without stumbling. Pio came through the doorway carrying the last small pile and stopped on the straw mat beside Chioma. “Thank you for taking half without me even asking,” Pio said, voice low and kind. Chioma felt the mat’s texture once more and simply answered, “It felt good to do it together.”

The hall smelled faintly of the cedar porch beams still drifting inward, and Chioma stood quietly while the cooler evening air from outside brushed past their ankles. They watched Pio set the last leaves in a basket near the wall, the basket’s rim catching a thin stripe of golden light from the window. Chioma reached for a small cup that sat on the low table and turned the cup so the handle faced the room, making it ready for whoever might want a drink later. The stone floor held a pleasant coolness that rose through the straw mat and touched Chioma’s toes. Outside, the cleared path shone smooth and open under the fading sky, no longer hidden by any skirt of leaves. Chioma felt calm and useful, knowing the porch and hall now welcomed every careful step.

Warm lamplight began to fill the hall more fully while Chioma and Pio sat for a moment on the edge of the straw mat, listening to the quiet house settle around them. Chioma ran one hand slowly along the mat’s even weave, enjoying the dry texture that stayed steady under their fingers. Pio stretched both legs toward the open door and looked out at the smooth path that now led cleanly from the porch straight inside. A soft breath of cooler air moved through the hall and lifted a loose curl on Chioma’s forehead. “The path looks ready for morning already,” Pio said with a peaceful smile. Chioma nodded and rested both hands in their lap, feeling the gentle temperature of the room wrap around their shoulders like a light blanket.

Chioma rose and walked a few steps deeper into the hall where the wooden walls held the day’s leftover warmth, the boards smooth beneath their palms when they touched them. They could still see the porch through the doorway and the clear path gleaming faintly under the first hints of dusk. Pio followed and stood nearby, both of them quiet while the house made only the smallest settling sounds. Chioma noticed a second cup on the shelf and turned that handle toward the room as well, matching the first one they had fixed. The air carried a mild woody scent that reminded them of the porch beams, and Chioma breathed it in slowly. Everything felt settled and kind after the shared work of uncovering the smooth stones.

Outside the last of the daylight rested in pale bands across the cleared path, and Chioma stepped once more onto the porch to look at the neat result of their half of the job. The boards underfoot still held a trace of afternoon heat even as the air cooled, and Chioma pressed both bare feet flat to feel it. Pio joined them and together they admired how the stones now lay open and inviting all the way to the hall. A gentle hush moved through the nearby trees, and Chioma stood still long enough to let the peaceful feeling sink in. They had not waited for praise; they had simply seen the struggle and taken a useful share. Now the porch and the hall belonged to easy footsteps and quiet evening.

Back inside, Chioma settled onto the straw mat again while Pio closed the door most of the way, leaving only a soft stripe of outdoor light. The hall grew dimmer and cozier, the temperature kind against Chioma’s cheeks and hands. They folded their legs neatly and watched the familiar shapes of the room become gentle shadows. Pio sat close by, and neither of them spoke for a long calm minute. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Chioma felt their eyelids grow heavy in the quiet light and knew the smooth path waited ready for tomorrow, clear and kind beneath the quiet stars.
