Mama was already on the garden path after the gentle rain, brushing beads of water from the wooden bench with slow strokes of a soft cloth. Cool evening air touched Bibi's cheeks as they stepped closer along the shining stones, both hands wrapped around a thick mug of cocoa that sent up gentle curls of steam. The steam drifted toward a cold glass pane set in the wall of the garden shed beside the path, a quiet place Mama used for watching young plants. Shallow puddles held the last pale silver light of the sky. Mama looked up and said, "I brought your cocoa outside so we could sit while the garden dries, Bibi." Bibi nodded and felt the solid warmth of the mug against their palms and fingers.

Pema came along a moment later, and the sound of a gate latch clicked softly as they closed the garden gate behind them. Bibi turned and waved with one free hand, careful not to tip the cocoa. The air near the flower beds carried the smell of rain on dust where the drops had mixed with the dry edges of the soil. Pema hurried over the cool wet stones and stopped beside Bibi with bright eyes. "Your cocoa looks like it is making tiny clouds," Pema said in a hushed voice. Bibi smiled and held the mug so Pema could feel the heat rising without taking it. The path felt smooth and damp under their shoes as they stood together.

Mama finished drying the bench and sat down, patting the clean wood so Bibi and Pema could climb up beside her. A satin ribbon hung from Bibi's cuff and brushed soft and smooth against their wrist each time they lifted the mug. Steam from the cocoa reached the cold pane and at first wrote nothing at all, only a pale foggy blur that spread like quiet breath across the glass. Bibi watched the empty mist gather in the dim light and kept very still. The evening temperature settled cool around their shoulders. "Look at the glass with me, Pema," Bibi said softly. "The steam is touching it now."

For a long moment the steam wrote nothing, just a cloudy soft patch that stayed blank and gentle on the cold pane. Then slowly a shape began to form inside the mist, a calm curve that looked a little like a sleeping moon or a curled leaf resting. Bibi felt a real wish to poke the shape or somehow pocket the whole soft picture, and their free fingers twitched once in the cool air. The cocoa gave off a warm chocolate scent that mixed with the damp garden. Pema's eyes grew round and still. The light on the glass was silver and faint, and the stones of the path held a leftover hush after the rain.

Bibi took a slow breath and turned toward Pema with care. "We can look without touching it," Bibi said. "If we poke the steam shape, it will fade away too soon. Come stand here with me and just watch." Pema nodded and stepped closer on quiet feet, hands tucked safely into pockets. Together they stood very still while the soft curve held its place on the glass. Mama watched them from the bench with the cloth folded neatly in her lap. A light breeze moved the cool air across Bibi's forehead like a gentle comb. Bibi felt glad they had chosen to keep the picture whole.

Mama rose and came to stand behind Bibi and Pema, resting one warm hand on Bibi's shoulder. "I see it too," Mama said in a low kind voice. "The steam drew something quiet for us on the cold pane." The three of them looked together at the gentle shape while more thin steam rose from the mug Bibi still held steady. Somewhere in the damp bushes a small bird made one soft chirp and then fell silent. The light shifted on the glass as dusk deepened above the garden path. Bibi leaned a little toward Pema so they could share the view without crowding the precious mist.

Bibi showed Pema how to look by holding still and breathing slowly, letting the steam keep its soft lines without a poke or a grab of any kind. Pema copied Bibi and kept both hands away from the glass. The wish to disturb the shape faded as they simply watched the quiet curve rest on the cold pane. Mama's presence felt steady and warm at their backs. The garden path after the gentle rain lay peaceful under the growing dusk, and the cocoa steam continued its small work. Bibi whispered, "It stays because we leave it alone." Pema smiled and answered, "I like leaving it just where it is."

They stayed a little longer until the shape began to fade on its own as the glass slowly warmed from the air. Bibi and Pema and Mama left the steam picture where it was and turned back toward the house along the garden path, carrying the half-finished cocoa with care. Cool air moved gently past their faces and through their hair. The wet stones made soft sounds under their slow steps. Bibi walked close to Mama and felt the solid mug handle under their fingers. The purple sky rested above them like a quiet blanket as they left the shed and the cold pane behind without another touch.

When they reached the back steps, Mama opened the door and warm indoor air wrapped around Bibi like a kind hug after the cool garden. Bibi set the mug on the low table and sat with Pema on the soft rug near the window. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Mama tucked a light blanket around their knees, and the room held a faint chocolate warmth from the cocoa along with the clean smell of wood. Outside, the garden path rested in the dark, and the cold pane kept whatever quiet secret remained. Bibi closed their eyes and listened to the slow tick of the clock while the gentle light stayed and the evening grew still and safe.
