The ferryman stood already at the cottage windowsill, mid-task, turning trays of drying wool so each strand could catch the drifting air while he slid glass jars into even rows that shone in the pale afternoon light. Cool drafts slipped through the open panes and brushed Golda's bare arms as they stepped close and watched his careful hands. Golda rested their palms on the smooth wooden sill and felt its solid coolness under their skin. "I am setting every jar straight so the wool can rest without tipping," the ferryman said in his low calm voice. Golda nodded and stayed near him, noticing how the light made soft golden edges on the jars and how the whole windowsill smelled clean and peaceful with the wool laid out so neatly.

Golda looked at the cloth-covered paper lantern resting among the trays and jars and felt a strong wish to lift the cover and check the seam that had to dry shut. Warm rectangles of sunlight stretched across the floor near Golda's feet and made the boards feel gentle under their toes. "May I see if the paper lantern seam is ready yet?" Golda asked softly, their eyes fixed on the quiet shape under the cloth. The ferryman kept one steady hand on a tray and shook his head with a kind look. "Not yet, Golda. The seam still needs time to dry shut all the way, so we leave the cover in place," he answered. Golda took a slow breath of the mild air and decided they would wait.

Golda left the paper lantern covered exactly as it was and chose a quiet task instead, picking up a small soft brush to smooth the drying wool in long even strokes. The scent of honey rose gently from an open jar the ferryman had set nearby for later tea, and Golda breathed it in with a small smile. Sunlight warmed Golda's back as they worked, turning the wool fluffier with every pass of the brush. "This feels good to do while the seam finishes," Golda said to the ferryman. He nodded and kept arranging trays without any hurry, the two of them sharing the calm space by the window.

A clear click of a gate latch sounded from the garden path outside, and Golda paused to listen before returning to the wool. Cooler shadows drifted across the sill as a thin cloud passed, changing the light to a softer silver that rested lightly on Golda's hair. Their friend Gita appeared in the doorway with quiet steps and came to stand beside the jars and trays. "May I brush the wool with you, Golda?" Gita whispered. Golda handed her another brush so they could work side by side, the drying strands growing smoother under their shared care while the covered lantern waited.

Golda and Gita brushed together while the ferryman watched over everything on the sill. Golda's hand felt chalky from the fine dry dust that clung to the wool fibers, a powdery texture coating their fingers as they worked. They wiped the chalky hand once on a clean cloth the ferryman offered, then continued with easy motions that made the wool lie flat and ready. The air near the window stayed mild and pleasant against their skin. "The seam will be ready in its own time," the ferryman said gently. Golda agreed and kept making neat rows among the jars.

Golda noticed the small wooden clock on the wall and decided their quiet task could include waiting through its single chime. They slowed the brush strokes and listened, feeling the solid edge of a tray under their free hand. Soft colored dots of light from the jars danced across Golda's sleeves as the sun shifted. When the chime rang out once, clear and sweet, Golda counted it silently and smiled. "That one chime means we have waited just right," Golda told Gita. Gita set her brush down carefully and stood still with them in the peaceful room.

The ferryman glanced at the cloth cover but did not lift it, giving the paper lantern seam the last minutes it needed. Golda leaned their arms on the sill and felt the cool wood press steadily against them while birds called short cheerful notes through the open window. "You found a fine quiet way to spend the waiting time, Golda," the ferryman said with warm approval. Golda felt a quiet gladness settle inside and looked at the perfectly smoothed wool now resting in its trays beside the shining jars.

Gita stayed close while Golda and the ferryman shared the last stretch of stillness, the light growing a little softer and more golden across the windowsill. Golda ran one finger along a jar rim and felt its smooth cool curve, breathing the mild cottage air. "I am glad we left the cover on," Golda said. The ferryman smiled and answered, "Now the seam has finished drying shut exactly as it should." Golda stood ready, hands calm at their sides, knowing the quiet task had filled the time well.

The ferryman lifted the cloth at last so Golda could see the paper lantern seam closed neat and strong among the jars and trays of drying wool. Golda touched the finished lantern with careful fingers and felt its light paper surface, warm from the long rest by the window. Gita clapped once very softly in delight. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
