A soft drip of water fell from a hanging fern onto the edge of the greenhouse bench with a gentle plink. Lior noticed the sound immediately and lifted their eyes toward the south window. Golden afternoon light poured through the glass and warmed the rows of seed pots on the long wooden bench. Auntie June stood nearby pressing soft earth around a young sprout. The wooden whistle lay hidden under a folded blue cloth among the pots. Lior knew Auntie June had rubbed it with oil that still needed to sink all the way into the wood. Cool air from the vent brushed Lior's cheeks as they sat still and watched the light make the cloth glow. They wondered about the whistle but stayed without touching anything yet.

Lior leaned a little closer to the cloth. "Auntie June, may I look at the whistle now?" they asked in a soft voice. Auntie June wiped her hands and sat on the low stool beside Lior. "The oil is still sinking in, dear Lior," she said with a warm smile. "It needs more time to finish its quiet work." Lior nodded slowly. Just then they caught the rich smell of cocoa rising from two mugs on a small tray near the window. The chocolate scent filled the air in a cozy way. Lior pulled their wool sleeve down over one hand and rubbed the soft fuzzy texture against their palm while they thought. The light from the south window shifted to a deeper gold across the seed pots.

"What can I do while the oil sinks?" Lior asked. Auntie June pointed toward a small green watering can by the pots. "You could give each seed pot three slow drops of water," she answered kindly. Lior stood and picked up the can with both hands. The handle felt smooth and cool. A thin stream of water made a light tinkling as it dropped into the first pot. Lior moved from one pot to the next counting softly. Auntie June stayed close and never rushed them. The greenhouse air felt pleasantly warm on Lior's neck and arms as they finished the last pot and set the can down carefully.

Lior glanced once more at the blue cloth but left it covered. They walked over to the wooden rocking chair in the corner near some empty trays. Lior climbed into the seat and pushed off to rock once very slowly forward. Then they let the chair sway until it stopped all by itself in stillness. That small action felt just right. Lior smiled and rested back against the wood. Through the south window the sky began to turn soft peach and pink. Auntie June continued her quiet work, and the room held a peaceful hush with only an occasional creak of wood.

A clear little tinkle sounded as the greenhouse door opened a bit. Lior turned and saw the family cat step inside, the small bell on its collar chiming with every step. The cat came to the rocking chair and brushed against Lior's legs. Lior reached down to stroke the fur and felt a steady purr. "Hello, friend," Lior said in a low tone. The cat curled onto the floor nearby. Auntie June said, "The cat has come to wait with us too." Lior laughed a quiet laugh. The last orange light from the south window touched the cat's whiskers and made them shine.

Lior stayed seated and watched Auntie June stack the colorful seed packets into a neat pile. The packets slid with a dry whispery sound. Lior stretched their legs and drew them back. They felt glad the whistle remained covered. The temperature grew cooler as the sun sank, so Lior wrapped their arms around themselves. Auntie June finished and stood by the chair. Together they looked out the south window at the changing colors. A single leaf fluttered down from the fern and landed on the bench far from the whistle.

After a while Lior slipped from the chair and returned to the bench. They laid both hands flat near the cloth but kept them still. "I believe the oil has almost finished sinking in now," Lior said. Auntie June placed a hand on Lior's shoulder and answered, "We shall look when the daylight grows softer." Lior felt happy and began to gather the empty seed trays into a tower. Each tray scraped lightly as Lior stacked them. Cooler air filled the greenhouse and Lior saw their breath in a faint cloud when they puffed. The cat watched from its spot with half-closed eyes in the dimming light.

Side by side they made sure every tray stood steady. Then Lior found a small soft brush and swept soil crumbs from the bench around the cloth. The bristles created a faint swishing that matched the evening. Auntie June prepared a fresh cloth just in case. Lior kept sweeping until the wood looked clean. Their eyelids felt heavy yet they finished the task. Only a pale silver glow remained at the south window and long shadows lay across the seed pots. Lior put the brush away and stood waiting quietly.

At last Auntie June gave a small nod. Lior lifted the blue cloth and revealed the wooden whistle. The surface looked smooth, all the oil having sunk deep into the wood in its own time. Lior held the whistle and blew one very soft note that drifted like a lullaby. Auntie June said, "There it is, finished and fine." Lior placed the whistle safely next to a pot of seeds and yawned. They leaned into Auntie June's side and felt the day settle. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Its warm light mixed with the silver dusk and wrapped Lior in gentle rest while the cat purred and the greenhouse grew still.
