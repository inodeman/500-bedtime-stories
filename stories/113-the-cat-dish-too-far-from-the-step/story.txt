The warm smell of toasted sesame drifted from the small kitchen corner beside the library return desk as Lior’s hands already slid along the edges of returned picture books, stacking them in careful piles on the smooth wooden counter. Cool damp air touched their wrists whenever the outer door swung a little in the soft rain. Lior pressed each cover flat and listened to the quiet hush of pages settling together. Soft yellow light from the desk lamp made the book spines shine gently while they worked. Lior kept their shoulders relaxed and their feet planted on the dry floor mat, happy to stay busy in the calm space.

Outside the open doorway the rainy steps glistened under the overhang lights, and the cat dish that had been left too far from the dry step sat alone on a wet stone, its rim catching small drops. Lior glanced out and saw Mr. Ade trying to balance a tall stack of books in one arm while reaching for the dish with the other. The air felt cooler near the door, and Lior noticed how the rain made tiny silver trails on the steps. Mr. Ade’s shoes stayed on the dry part, yet the dish remained just beyond easy reach. Lior watched without rushing, feeling the steady warmth of the indoor air on their back.

Mr. Ade shifted his weight and the books wobbled. “These volumes need to go straight onto the cart,” he said in a gentle voice, “but that dish keeps sliding farther each time the wind lifts a little.” Lior stepped closer to the threshold where the floor changed from carpet to stone and felt the slight chill rising from the wet ground. Soft rain sounds pattered on the leaves beyond the steps. Lior saw that one person alone found the stretch awkward. They stayed quiet for a moment, simply noticing how the light from inside reached only halfway across the top step.

Lior moved forward and took the useful half of the job without a word of request. They held out both hands and said, “I can carry the books to the cart for you.” Mr. Ade let the stack rest carefully into Lior’s waiting arms. The books felt cool and solid against Lior’s shirt. Together they kept everything steady while rain whispered on the roof above. Lior walked the short distance inside and set the books down with a soft thud on the rolling cart, then returned at once to the doorway. The temperature near the steps stayed mild under the shelter.

Now free of the heavy stack, Mr. Ade knelt and stretched toward the cat dish. Lior knelt too on the dry edge of the top step and slid the dish closer with both palms until it rested safely under the overhang. The metal felt slick from rain yet Lior guided it smoothly. “There,” Lior said softly, “now it sits where the dry step begins.” Mr. Ade smiled and brushed his hands on the front of his clothing. A gate latch clicked shut somewhere in the garden path beyond the library wall, a small clear sound that floated in with the rain. Lior wiped their damp fingertips on their own sleeve and stood again.

Inside once more, Lior noticed Mr. Ade’s cotton apron had collected a few tiny drops near the hem, the fabric soft when Lior’s hand brushed it by accident while passing the cart. They both returned to the return desk where the lamp still cast its friendly glow. Lior began lining up the remaining books in size order, feeling the slight roughness of older covers under their thumbs. The air inside smelled clean and paper-sweet again. Mr. Ade rolled the cart a little farther and said, “Thank you for seeing what was needed.” Lior only nodded and kept their eyes on the neat row forming under the light.

Femi appeared from the children’s corner carrying two thin storybooks and paused beside Lior. “The rain makes everything shiny,” Femi whispered. Lior answered, “It does, and the steps stay dry under the roof.” They spoke in low tones so the quiet of the library held. Lior felt the mild indoor warmth settle around their shoulders while they finished the last stack. Together the two children watched Mr. Ade check the cat dish one more time through the doorway, making certain it stayed on the dry stone. Soft rain continued its gentle rhythm against the windows.

Lior walked to a nearby reading table, took hold of a wooden chair, and set the chair square to the table so it waited neatly for the next visitor. The legs made a tiny scrape on the floor that faded quickly. Warm light from the desk lamp reached across the tabletop and touched Lior’s hands as they smoothed the surface. Mr. Ade returned fully inside and closed the door most of the way, leaving only a narrow gap for fresh air. Lior felt calm and complete after the small shared work. Femi settled into another seat and opened a book with a soft rustle of pages.

Later Lior sat in the squared chair with a favorite picture book open across their knees, turning each page slowly while the library grew even quieter. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Cool evening air drifted in thin ribbons near the floor, and Lior rested their cheek for a moment against the warm page. Outside the rain had slowed to a hush, and the cat dish remained safe on the dry step where they had placed it. Lior’s breathing matched the slow turn of pages, and the whole room felt peaceful and ready for rest.
