Auntie Luz sat already at the quiet craft corner, her hands steady as she smoothed thick paste along the edges of wide paper banners that draped from the wooden rack. Soft afternoon light pooled across the table and warmed the cool plate where a pale butter curl rested under a clean cloth, waiting to soften on its own for later spreading. Ximena stood nearby and smelled the faint sweetness of the paste mixing with the room’s still air while they watched Auntie Luz work. The wooden rack creaked gently when a banner shifted. “This paste holds best when we let each strip rest a moment,” Auntie Luz said without looking up. Ximena nodded and felt the smooth edge of the table under their fingertips, the temperature of the wood cool and pleasant. Outside the open doorway a soft breeze carried the quiet of the yard, and Ximena’s shoulders eased as they settled onto the low stool beside their auntie.

Ximena kept glancing toward the covered butter curl on its cool plate, wondering if the pale swirl had begun to soften yet. They leaned forward a little and heard the tiny rustle of paper scraps under their own sleeve. The craft corner held stacks of fresh paper that gave off a clean crisp smell each time Auntie Luz lifted a new sheet. “May I peek under the cloth just once?” Ximena asked in a hushed voice. Auntie Luz smiled and kept pasting another banner. “The butter curl needs its own quiet time under the cover, Ximena,” she answered gently. Ximena sat back and felt the stool’s woven seat press evenly against their legs. Light from the window painted pale squares on the floorboards, and the air stayed comfortably cool around them both.

Auntie Luz handed Ximena a square of soft paper and a small dish of paste so they could work side by side. Ximena dipped one finger carefully and spread a thin layer that felt cool and slightly sticky. They pressed the paper onto a longer banner hanging from the wooden rack and listened to the quiet sigh of the paste settling. The butter curl stayed covered on its cool plate a short distance away, still meant to soften without anyone lifting the cloth. “I want it to be ready soon,” Ximena whispered. Auntie Luz nodded and said, “We can keep our hands busy with these banners until it is.” Ximena smoothed another edge and noticed how the paste dried slowly into a matte sheen under the gentle indoor light.

They worked in easy silence for a while, Ximena aligning paper corners while Auntie Luz hung finished pieces higher on the wooden rack. The rack’s smooth rungs felt warm where sunlight had touched them earlier. Ximena’s breath slowed to match the calm rhythm of the room. A distant gate latch clicked shut in the yard, a clear metal sound that drifted in and faded. Ximena paused with paste still on one fingertip and listened until the quiet returned fully. They wiped their hands on a soft cloth and looked again toward the covered plate, yet left the cloth undisturbed. The craft corner stayed peaceful, filled with the mild scent of drying paste and the steady presence of Auntie Luz beside them.

Ximena decided to arrange leftover paper strips into a neat fan shape on the table’s far edge. Each strip carried a slight texture under their careful fingers. Auntie Luz watched with kind eyes and offered another clean cloth for wiping. “Your fan looks tidy already,” Auntie Luz said. Ximena answered, “I like making the edges match.” They kept their voice soft so the butter curl could keep softening on its own under the cover. Cool air drifted past their ankles from the open doorway. The wooden rack held its hanging banners without sway, and the whole corner felt settled and safe around the two of them as they continued their quiet work.

After a few more careful placements, Ximena rested both palms on their knees and simply watched the light move slowly across the floor. The cloth over the butter curl stayed exactly where Auntie Luz had placed it. Time stretched gently while paste dried on the banners and the cool plate held its quiet charge. Ximena felt the steady warmth of their own hands against the fabric of their trousers. Auntie Luz reached over and lightly patted Ximena’s shoulder. The craft corner remained filled with soft sounds of paper sliding and the wooden rack holding everything in place. Ximena stayed still a little longer, letting the moments pass without hurrying the butter.

When Ximena rose again they stepped closer to the low wall that framed one side of the craft corner and brushed their fingers along its surface. The damp clay wall felt cool and slightly yielding under their touch, like earth after gentle rain. They returned to the table and chose one last paper strip to paste near the top of a hanging banner. Auntie Luz hummed a tiny wordless tune while she adjusted the wooden rack. Ximena smiled at the familiar sound and kept their movements slow. The covered butter curl waited patiently on its cool plate, still finishing in its own time. Soft light continued to fill the corner without glare or shadow, and Ximena breathed in the calm of the shared space.

At last Auntie Luz set down her paste brush and turned toward the plate. Ximena waited without reaching. “I think the butter curl has softened now,” Auntie Luz said. Together they lifted the cloth and saw the pale swirl ready and smooth. Ximena felt a quiet gladness settle in their chest. They helped carry the cool plate a few careful steps toward the kitchen doorway while the craft corner stayed neat behind them, paper and paste and wooden rack all in their places. The air smelled faintly of the finished paste work, and the temperature remained pleasantly even as they moved.

Ximena and Auntie Luz returned the empty plate later and sat once more among the hanging banners. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Ximena leaned against Auntie Luz’s side and felt the gentle warmth of the shared stool. Outside the yard had grown quieter still. They watched the last of the daylight fade while the wooden rack held its colorful paper shapes in perfect peace, and the whole craft corner seemed to breathe with them in the kindly evening hush.
