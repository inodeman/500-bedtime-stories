The seed packet lay on the wide sill of Femi’s bedroom window, its thin paper warm under the spill of hall light that still glowed from the doorway. Tiny printed leaves curled across the front, and the flap had been opened once and then neatly closed again so that no seeds could tumble free. Femi leaned so near that their breath fogged a small circle on the glass beside it, and they could see the faint pencil mark someone had added along the edge. The paper felt dry and light when Femi finally touched one corner with the tip of one finger, careful not to crinkle it. Soft shadows stretched across the quilt from the open door, and the air near the sill held a gentle coolness from the night outside. Femi stayed still for a long moment, simply looking, while the hall light kept the room from ever growing fully dark.

Femi turned and saw that Grandma Nia had already set a shallow clay dish of dark soil on the low table beside the bed, ready for whatever the packet held. The soil smelled faintly of basil, as if someone had brushed fresh leaves across it earlier while working. Femi’s eyes moved from the dish back to the packet and then to Grandma Nia, who sat in the wooden chair near the foot of the bed with her knitting resting quiet in her lap. “You found the seeds I talked about,” Femi said, voice low so the calm of the room stayed whole. Grandma Nia’s smile reached her eyes, and she nodded once without rushing. The hall light painted a soft path across the rug between them, and Femi felt the cool of the floorboards under bare feet as they stepped closer to the table.

Grandma Nia lifted the packet with both hands and set it gently beside the clay dish so Femi could see how the two things belonged together now. “I thought they might like a place waiting for them,” she said, the words steady and warm. Femi listened to the quiet tick of a tin clock on the dresser, each small sound marking the peaceful minutes. The packet’s paper caught the hall light again and glowed a little, and Femi noticed that Grandma Nia had even placed a small spoon nearby for scooping soil later. Everything felt cared for without anyone asking for it. Femi’s shoulders lowered, and they breathed in the quiet of the room while the clock kept its gentle rhythm.

A quick thank-you rose in Femi’s mind and then felt too thin for what Grandma Nia had done. Femi looked around the bedroom for one useful thing that had not been requested. On the nightstand sat a short stack of clean cloth napkins someone had brought up earlier with a cup of warm milk, each napkin stitched with a small pear near the corner. Femi picked them up one by one and folded them so every stitched pear faced neatly upward, smoothing each crease with the flat of their hand until the stack looked tidy and ready. The cloth felt smooth and cool against Femi’s palms. Hall light reached the nightstand too, turning the white fabric soft gold at the edges. Grandma Nia watched without speaking, her knitting still quiet in her lap, while Femi finished the small stack and set it straight.

When the napkins stood in their careful pile, Femi reached into the shallow dish of pebbles Grandma Nia kept for window decorations and chose one polished pebble that shone under the hall light. The stone felt cool and glassy smooth between Femi’s fingers, rounded by water long ago. Femi placed the pebble beside the seed packet so it could keep the paper from sliding if a breeze came later. “I noticed what you did,” Femi said plainly, looking at Grandma Nia. “You took care of the seeds I only mentioned once.” Grandma Nia’s hands rested on the knitting, and the room held the words like a soft blanket. Femi’s voice stayed even and clear, and the pebble caught a tiny spark of light as if it agreed.

Grandma Nia set her knitting aside and opened her arms so Femi could lean against the chair. “I heard you wishing for them while we washed the supper bowls,” she answered, the sound of her voice low and kind. Femi rested there for a breath, feeling the warm fabric of Grandma Nia’s sleeve against their cheek. The hall light made a gentle rectangle on the ceiling above them both. Outside, leaves brushed the glass in a slow hush that matched the quiet inside. Femi stayed close, glad the seed packet no longer waited alone on the sill, and glad the clay dish waited with it.

Femi walked back to the window and checked that the packet sat safe between the pebble and the dish. They smoothed the quilt where it had wrinkled near the pillows, tucking the edge under so the bed looked ready for sleep. The fabric of the quilt felt thick and familiar under Femi’s hands, still holding a little daytime warmth. “Will we plant them in the morning?” Femi asked, glancing toward Grandma Nia again. Grandma Nia nodded, and the hall light caught the silver in her hair. Femi smiled at the thought of tiny roots finding the soil, and the bedroom seemed to settle deeper into its calm.

Ren had left a small drawing of a garden on the dresser earlier that afternoon, and Femi moved it closer to the tin clock so the two friends’ ideas could keep each other company through the night. The paper of the drawing felt lightly textured under Femi’s thumb. Hall light reached even that far corner, soft and steady. Femi whispered good night to the seed packet the way someone might speak to a sleeping kitten, then turned toward the bed. Grandma Nia rose and helped pull the covers back so Femi could climb in without a tangle. The sheets smelled clean and cool, and Femi slid between them with a long easy breath.

Femi lay still while Grandma Nia brushed a gentle hand across their forehead and then stepped toward the doorway where the hall light waited. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Femi watched its calm circle of light touch the seed packet, the polished pebble, and the edge of the clay dish one last time before their eyes grew heavy. Grandma Nia’s footsteps faded down the hall like quiet rain on dry ground, and the bedroom held its warm stillness around Femi as sleep came near.
