The warm smell of honey drifted from the open oven as Remy’s small hands stayed busy patting soft dough into neat rounds on the bakery counter that faced the street. Flour dusted Remy’s fingers like tiny clouds, and the wood felt smooth and slightly warm under their palms. Golden light poured through the wide front window and rested on the glass jars of jam lined up like quiet soldiers. Outside, the low swing hung from a sturdy branch near the ferry path, its wooden seat empty and waiting in the gentle breeze. Remy watched it sway just a little while they kept pressing the dough, feeling the quiet of the afternoon settle around their shoulders like a soft blanket.

Remy glanced again through the glass and saw the ferryman standing by the counter with a basket of fresh rolls, his coat smelling faintly of river water and his boots dusty from the path. The low swing sat just beyond the bakery door, close enough that anyone inside could hear the ropes creak if the wind caught them. Remy’s cheeks felt warm from the ovens, and they tucked a loose strand of hair behind one ear with a floury fingertip. Kito leaned against the counter too, holding a small paper bag that rustled whenever they shifted. Remy wanted a turn on that low swing more than anything just then, yet the words felt heavy on their tongue like sticky honey still cooling.

The bakery door stood open to the street sounds, and Remy’s ears caught the soft click of a gate latch from the little fence that kept the swing area neat and safe. Remy’s stomach fluttered with a shy feeling that made them look down at their busy hands again. They could pretend the swing did not matter and keep shaping dough forever, but something quiet inside wanted to try. The ferryman smiled in a calm way that did not hurry anyone, and the afternoon light made the street look friendly and wide. Remy breathed in the sweet air and felt the counter’s edge cool against their wrists while they thought about the smallest brave step they could take.

Kito whispered near Remy’s shoulder, “The swing looks perfect right now,” and the words floated gentle as steam. Remy’s heart beat a little faster, but they kept their voice soft when they answered, “I know. I want a turn.” The ferryman heard them and turned with kind eyes, never making a big show or calling any attention that felt too large. Remy swallowed once and felt the warm bakery air brush their face. Outside the window the low swing waited under the dappled light, its seat smooth and inviting. Remy wiped their hands on the apron hanging nearby and decided the shy feeling did not have to win the whole afternoon.

Remy stepped a little closer to the open doorway where the street breeze mixed with the bakery warmth and made the air taste sweet. Their voice came out quiet but clear enough to be heard over the soft clink of trays. “Excuse me,” Remy said to the ferryman, “may I please have a turn on the low swing?” The ferryman nodded right away and answered in a low friendly tone, “Of course you may, Remy. It is ready whenever you are.” Kito gave a small happy bounce and said, “I’ll hold your spot at the counter.” Remy felt a chalky hand brush theirs for a second as Kito passed a bit of sidewalk chalk they had been holding, the dry dusty texture lingering on Remy’s palm like a secret hello.

Remy walked out into the mild daylight and crossed the short path to the low swing, feeling the wooden seat solid and smooth under them as they sat. The ropes felt sturdy in their grip, and a light breeze cooled their bare arms. They pushed off gently with both feet and swung in a slow easy arc that made the world tilt just enough to feel like floating. The ferryman stayed by the bakery counter and called softly, “Take all the time you like.” Remy answered back, “Thank you,” and the words came easier now. Honey still lingered in the air even outside, mixing with the fresh leaf smell from the oak. Kito waved from the doorway, and Remy swung a little higher, then a little lower, matching the calm rhythm of their own breathing.

After several gentle swings Remy slowed the ropes and let their feet touch the soft ground again, feeling proud in a quiet way that needed no big words. The ferryman held the bakery door open with one kind hand and said, “You asked just right.” Remy smiled and walked back inside where the counter still held the unfinished dough rounds. They washed their hands under cool water that sparkled in the light, then returned to patting the soft shapes. Kito offered a shared bite of a warm roll, and the two of them stood side by side watching people pass on the street. The low swing swayed empty once more, peaceful and ready for whoever came next.

Remy finished the last dough round and brushed the extra flour away, listening to the quiet hum of the ovens and the distant lap of river water near the ferry. The ferryman packed his basket and gave one more easy nod before heading out toward his boat. Remy felt the bakery’s gentle heat on their skin and the contented stillness that followed a small brave ask. Kito leaned close and murmured, “That was nice,” and Remy simply nodded, happy to let the moment rest. Outside the light began to soften toward evening, turning the street gold and rose while the low swing hung still under its branch.

Later Remy sat on a tall stool by the bakery window with a cup of warm milk that smelled faintly sweet, watching the first tiny stars appear above the ferry path. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Remy’s hands rested quiet in their lap, no longer busy, and the whole room felt calm and safe as the evening settled deeper around them.
