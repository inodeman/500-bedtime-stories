Grandpa Sol already sat on the wide ceramic studio sill above the quiet plaza, his hands turning a soft lump of clay under the pale afternoon light that slid across the cool tiles. The air felt mild against Bibi’s cheeks as they climbed up beside him, and the clay made a gentle wet whisper each time Grandpa Sol pressed his thumbs into it. Far below, the plaza stones looked smooth and empty, and a soft breeze carried the faint smell of rain on dust up from the ground. Bibi settled their knees on the warm sill and watched the gray clay become a small bowl shape, round and careful. Grandpa Sol hummed a low tune without words while the light shifted slowly over the blue and white tiles set into the sill edge. Bibi rested one palm on a smooth blue square and felt how cool it stayed even in the sun.

Bibi reached for the wooden ladle that rested near a shallow dish of water, meaning only to drip a little moisture onto Grandpa Sol’s clay so the bowl would stay soft. The ladle handle felt smooth and light in Bibi’s fingers, and they tipped it gently. A single clear drop fell with a tiny plink. Then the ladle slipped, tapped the edge of a blue tile, and a small chip broke free with a short dry click. The chip lay like a tiny blue leaf on the sill. Warm light touched its broken edge. Bibi’s stomach gave a quiet flutter. They glanced at Grandpa Sol, who was still shaping the clay and had not seen. The plaza below stayed hushed, and Bibi could hear only the soft scrape of clay against Grandpa Sol’s hands.

Bibi’s fingers closed over the chip. It felt smooth on one side and rough on the break. For a moment Bibi thought of sliding it into a pocket and saying nothing at all. The sill tiles felt cool under Bibi’s other hand. A satin ribbon that held back Bibi’s hair brushed softly against their neck when they bent closer to look. Grandpa Sol kept working, the clay making a slow squish. Bibi held the chip tightly and felt its small weight. The almost-hiding idea sat in Bibi’s chest like a held breath. Outside, the light on the plaza grew a little softer, and the studio air stayed calm and warm.

Bibi looked at the empty place on the blue tile where the chip had been. The missing corner made a pale jagged line in the glaze. Bibi’s thumb rubbed the chip once more. Then Bibi loosened their fingers and set the chip carefully on the sill between them and Grandpa Sol. The clay bowl sat unfinished, still damp and cool to the eye. Bibi took a small breath that smelled clean from the open window. “Grandpa Sol,” Bibi said, voice steady though quiet, “I dropped the ladle and a chip came off the blue tile.” The words felt plain and true in the mild air. Grandpa Sol’s hands paused on the clay. He turned his kind face toward Bibi and the little blue piece.

“Thank you for telling me, Bibi,” Grandpa Sol said, and his voice held only warmth, no teasing at all. He wiped one clay-dusted hand on a cloth and picked up the chip between careful fingers. The broken edge caught a thin stripe of light. Bibi watched his face and felt the sill’s coolness under their legs. “We can mend what can be mended,” Grandpa Sol added. From somewhere down near the plaza a gate latch clicked shut once, a short metal sound that faded quickly. Bibi nodded. “I almost hid it,” Bibi said, “but I wanted you to know the plain truth.” Grandpa Sol smiled a small smile and set the chip beside a dish of clear glaze. “That was brave and kind, Bibi,” he said. “Come, we will work slowly.”

Together they cleaned the broken edge with a soft damp sponge. The sponge felt springy and cool against Bibi’s fingertips when Grandpa Sol let them try. A thin line of special paste went onto the chip, smelling faintly of clean earth and water. Grandpa Sol held the blue piece steady while Bibi guided it back into the missing corner of the tile. The fit was nearly perfect. Soft studio light made the wet paste shine for a moment. Bibi pressed very gently, just as Grandpa Sol showed, and the chip settled into place. Below them the quiet plaza held still. Bibi’s shoulders eased. “Will it stay?” Bibi asked. Grandpa Sol nodded. “It will hold while it dries, and later we can smooth the seam.”

They left the tile to rest and returned to the clay bowl. Grandpa Sol showed Bibi how to drip water with the ladle held firmly this time, and the clay stayed soft under their shared hands. The bowl grew rounder. Cool dampness touched Bibi’s wrists. Outside the window a pale fog began to gather on the glass where the warmer studio air met the cooler evening. Bibi lifted a dry finger and traced a slow circle in the fog, watching the clear path appear. The circle caught a little of the fading light. Grandpa Sol finished the rim of the bowl with one last smooth press. “You did careful work today, Bibi,” he said. Bibi smiled and wiped their hands on the same cloth, feeling the cloth’s rough weave.

The paste on the blue tile began to set, turning from shiny to matte under the soft indoor glow. Bibi leaned close and saw how the chip sat snug again, the break almost invisible except for a thin honest line. The sill felt pleasantly cool now that the sun had slipped lower. Grandpa Sol rinsed the ladle and set it back in its place. A gentle quiet filled the studio, broken only by the drip of clean water into the basin. Bibi rested their cheek for a second against Grandpa Sol’s sleeve and smelled the clean soap he always used. The plaza below looked peaceful in the blue hour. Bibi felt glad the truth had been spoken and the tile had been mended side by side.

Night settled fully over the quiet plaza, and the ceramic studio sill held its calm blue tiles and the mended chip safe under the soft indoor light. Bibi and Grandpa Sol sat a little longer with the finished clay bowl between them, both pairs of hands clean and still. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Bibi watched the warm circle of light rest on the repaired tile and felt the cool evening air drift across their bare feet. Grandpa Sol patted Bibi’s shoulder once, gentle and sure. Outside everything stayed peaceful, and inside the studio the air smelled faintly of drying clay and the earlier rain on dust that had risen from the stones. Bibi yawned a small yawn, ready for sleep, while the mended blue tile kept its quiet place on the sill.
