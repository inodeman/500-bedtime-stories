Grandpa Sol was already smoothing the bright cloth down the long wooden table when Quinn came barefoot into the courtyard, and the stones still held a mild warmth from the day. He moved from place to place, setting out shallow bowls and folding napkins into neat triangles, the faint smell of cedar rising from the open chest where the special dishes were kept. Quinn stood close beside him and watched the way his hands worked without hurry. Soft gold light slipped between the leaves of the fig tree and patterned the tabletop. “The market umbrella is ready, Quinn,” Grandpa Sol said, nodding toward the wide canvas that stood open near the far end. “I put it up so we can keep the cool air near us while we eat.” Quinn reached out and touched the edge of the cloth he had just laid, feeling how smooth it was under their fingers.

Quinn walked the length of the table and paused where the circle of shade under the market umbrella rested on the ground like a quiet round room of its own. The air inside that circle felt cooler against Quinn’s cheeks and arms, a gentle difference from the open courtyard. Farah would arrive soon, and Grandpa Sol had already set three chairs near the shaded spot so everyone could sit together. Quinn listened for a moment and heard the soft rustle of wind in the fig leaves high above. “It feels nice here,” Quinn said, almost to themselves, and then a little louder to Grandpa Sol. “Farah will like this cool place too.” Grandpa Sol only smiled and kept arranging the spoons in a straight line, letting Quinn decide how the space would be used.

Quinn knelt on a straw mat that Grandpa Sol had unrolled beside the shaded chairs so no one would have to sit on bare stone. The woven surface felt dry and slightly scratchy in a friendly way under Quinn’s knees and palms. On a low tray inside the circle of shade under the market umbrella sat a large platter of ripe fruit and soft cheese, already cut into generous pieces because the evening air would keep them fresh. Quinn looked at the platter and then at the three empty plates waiting nearby. If the biggest pieces went to only one person first, the circle of shade would suddenly feel uneven, as if the coolness itself belonged to a single seat. Quinn wanted Farah to feel just as welcome the moment she stepped into the courtyard.

Farah’s footsteps sounded lightly on the path, and soon she stood at the edge of the table, her cheeks pink from the walk. Quinn stood up from the mat and held out a hand. “Come sit with us under the umbrella,” Quinn said. “I saved a place right in the middle of the shade.” Farah’s eyes brightened as she felt the cooler air on her face. Grandpa Sol brought a pitcher of water that still held a faint chill, and the three of them settled into the chairs. The canvas overhead made a soft continuous shadow that smelled faintly of sun-warmed cloth, and Quinn could see how the circle of shade held them all together like a quiet invitation.

Quinn picked up the serving spoon and looked again at the large pieces on the platter. Carefully, Quinn divided each piece into smaller, even shares so that three plates would hold the same amount. The fruit glistened in the filtered light, and a thin slice of cheese went onto every plate with the same care. Farah watched with a little smile as Quinn set the third plate in front of her. “Now we all start together,” Quinn said. Farah nodded and answered, “This feels just right.” Grandpa Sol watched the careful hands at work and said nothing more than a quiet “Mm,” which meant he was content to let the choice stand exactly as Quinn had made it.

They ate slowly while the courtyard settled into evening. The temperature dropped a little more, and the coolness under the market umbrella wrapped around their shoulders like a light blanket. Quinn passed the water cup to Farah and, almost without thinking, turned the cup so the handle faced the room, making it easy for anyone to reach. Farah took a sip and set it back within the shared circle of shade. Grandpa Sol refilled the pitcher once, the clear water catching tiny sparks of remaining daylight. No one rushed, and the long table stretched away into softer shadows where the fig tree stood guard.

After the plates were empty, Quinn helped gather the spoons and stack the bowls. The stones underfoot had grown cooler now, and a gentle hush filled the courtyard. Farah rested her chin on her hands and looked up at the underside of the umbrella, where the canvas glowed faintly. “I like how the shade belongs to all of us,” Farah said. Quinn answered, “Me too.” Grandpa Sol only nodded once more, still offering no speech, simply letting the evening continue in the calm way Quinn had shaped it. The three of them stayed seated a little longer, listening to the quiet and feeling the last mild warmth leave the air.

When the sky turned the color of soft blueberries, Grandpa Sol rose and began folding the outer edges of the tablecloth. Quinn and Farah carried the empty pitcher indoors together, their bare feet quiet on the threshold. Inside the house the rooms smelled clean and still, and Quinn felt the familiar comfort of home settling around them. Farah waved good night from the doorway and promised to come again when the fig leaves next danced. Quinn walked with Grandpa Sol down the short hall, the day’s last light fading behind the curtains, and everything felt peaceful and complete.

Quinn climbed into bed while Grandpa Sol adjusted the window latch with careful fingers. The sheets were smooth and cool against Quinn’s legs, and the pillow held the faint clean scent of laundry dried in open air. Outside, the courtyard rested under the dark shape of the fig tree and the folded market umbrella. Quinn closed their eyes and pictured the even plates again, the shared circle of shade, and Farah’s quiet smile. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
