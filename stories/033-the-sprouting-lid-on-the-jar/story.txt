"Auntie June, may I lift the cloth lid on the sprouting jar just for one quick look?" Kito asked in a hushed voice near the greenhouse bench. Soft golden light from the south window spilled across the rows of seed pots and warmed the backs of Kito's hands as they stood very still. The air inside felt gently humid, and Kito could see tiny beads of moisture on the glass. Auntie June looked over with a kind smile while she wiped a leaf. Kito rocked on their heels, eyes fixed on the round jar and its snug cloth cover, wanting so much to know what grew inside yet sensing it was still too soon. The quiet hum of the space wrapped around them both like a soft blanket of afternoon calm.

Auntie June rested one hand on the cotton apron tied at her waist, the fabric cool and lightly worn under her palm, and answered, "Not yet, Kito. The sprouts finish best when we leave the lid right where it is." Kito nodded slowly and traced a fingertip along the edge of a nearby seed pot, feeling the faint grit of dry soil. A faint draft carried the smell of toasted sesame from a small paper packet Auntie June had opened earlier for a shared snack on the bench. Kito breathed it in and let their shoulders drop. Outside, the latch of the garden gate clicked once with a clean metallic note as someone closed it gently far down the path, then all grew still again. Kito stayed close to the jar without touching it.

Kito picked up a clean cloth and began dusting the rims of the empty seed pots one by one, moving with careful strokes so no dirt fell. The south window light shifted a little lower and painted longer pale rectangles across the wooden bench surface. "I can wait with you, Auntie June," Kito said, voice steady now. Auntie June hummed a quiet tune while she arranged tiny labels in a neat row. Kito felt the smooth grain of the bench against their wrists each time they leaned forward. The greenhouse stayed pleasantly warm, never too hot, and the sprouting jar sat patiently in its place with the cloth lid undisturbed. Kito counted the pots aloud under their breath just to keep hands busy and thoughts gentle.

After the pots shone clean, Kito decided to set the small wooden chair square to the table at the end of the bench so everything looked tidy and ready. The legs scraped softly once against the floorboards before settling firm. Auntie June watched and said, "That looks just right, Kito. Thank you for helping the space feel settled." Kito smiled and pressed their palms flat on the chair seat, noticing how solid and cool the wood felt after sitting in shade. Light from the south window now glowed more amber than gold, and Kito glanced again at the cloth lid without reaching. The jar remained closed, doing its quiet work. Kito turned back to the seed packets and stacked them by size, taking long slow breaths of the still greenhouse air.

Kito and Auntie June sat side by side for a stretch of peaceful minutes, watching dust motes drift in the window light. "Do you think the little roots are stretching yet?" Kito asked softly. Auntie June replied, "They are taking exactly the time they need, and we are keeping them company." Kito leaned their head against Auntie June's shoulder for a moment and felt the steady rise and fall of her breathing. The temperature inside stayed mild and kind on bare arms. Somewhere a watering can dripped once into a tray with a tiny plink. Kito kept their gaze on the cloth lid of the sprouting jar and practiced staying still, hands folded in their lap like two quiet birds. Time moved in easy circles rather than rushes.

When the light softened further, Kito fetched a small brush and swept stray soil grains into a neat pile at the corner of the bench. Each bristle made a faint whisper against the wood. Auntie June offered Kito a sip of cool water from a blue cup, and the liquid tasted clean and refreshing going down. Kito set the cup aside carefully so it would not tip. The sprouting jar waited in the same spot, cloth lid still perfect and unmoved. Kito felt proud of leaving it alone. The south window framed a patch of soft sky that had turned the color of pale peaches. Everything around the bench smelled fresh and green, and Kito's fingers found a smooth river stone Auntie June kept for luck and turned it slowly.

Auntie June stood and checked the soil in a few pots with one gentle finger. "The day is winding down, Kito. Shall we see if the jar is ready now?" she asked. Kito's heart gave a happy little skip, yet they answered, "Only if you think the time has come." Together they leaned close. Kito lifted the cloth lid at last with both careful hands. Inside, the seeds had opened into pale tender sprouts curled like tiny green commas, damp and perfect. A mild earthy scent rose up, cooler than the rest of the room. Kito laughed once, soft as a moth wing, and whispered, "They waited for us, and we waited for them." Auntie June nodded and rested a light hand on Kito's back while the last window light touched the new sprouts with gentle gold.

Kito replaced the cloth lid loosely so the sprouts could keep breathing and then helped Auntie June gather the seed packets into a wooden box. The box lid closed with a quiet wooden sigh. Outside the glass the garden had grown blue with evening, yet inside the greenhouse the air held its pleasant warmth. Kito wiped the bench one final time with slow circles, feeling the last grains of soil catch in the cloth. They spoke almost to themselves, "I am glad we left it covered until it finished." Auntie June answered only with a warm look that needed no extra words. Kito tucked the brush away and stood looking at the south window where the sky had deepened. The whole space felt settled and kind, ready for rest.

Kito took Auntie June's hand and they walked the short path from the bench toward the little door that led back to the house, leaving the seed pots and the finished sprouting jar in their peaceful row. The evening air brushed cooler against Kito's cheeks yet stayed friendly. Inside the house the rooms glowed with low lamps. Kito climbed onto the waiting bed, still smelling faint green from the greenhouse on their sleeves, and pulled the soft cover up to their chin. Auntie June smoothed the blanket once. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
