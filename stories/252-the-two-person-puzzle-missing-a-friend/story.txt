The soft tick of a tin clock reached Tamsin's ears first, steady and small like a quiet heart. Tamsin sat still on the library rug under the round window and noticed the calm rhythm rising from near the low shelves, making the tall room feel safer and quieter. Cool air drifted from the window and touched Tamsin's bare arms while soft evening light painted a silver circle across the thick woven rug. Grandma Nia settled beside them with a wooden puzzle box, her sweater brushing Tamsin's shoulder with a warm woolly feel. Tamsin pressed their palms into the rug and felt its loops give gently under their weight. "The clock keeps time for our quiet games," Grandma Nia said, her voice low and even. Tamsin smiled and watched pale dust motes float in the fading light.

Tamsin spilled the puzzle pieces across the center of the rug and felt each one cool and flat against their fingertips. The round window glowed with deep blue dusk now, and Tamsin sorted the straight edges while Grandma Nia watched without reaching in. A soft footstep approached from the doorway, and Tamsin looked up to see Pilar standing just outside the circle of light on the rug, holding a thin book against their chest. The air smelled faintly green from the basil pot resting on the sill, its leaves still and glossy in the dimness. Tamsin waved a hello but kept sorting the sky pieces of the puzzle that showed a winding garden path. Pilar shifted from foot to foot on the bare wood floor beyond the rug's edge, close enough to see all the colors yet not quite inside the fun. Grandma Nia only nodded once and let the children decide what came next.

Tamsin looked at the empty space on the rug and at Pilar's quiet face lit only by the dimmer glow near the shelves. The puzzle had been more fun with the friend who had just arrived, and now it felt unfinished without Pilar. Tamsin scooted sideways on the thick rug so a clear warm patch opened right beside the scattered pieces. "Come sit here, Pilar," Tamsin said, patting the spot with an open hand. The temperature near the floor stayed mild even as the window glass cooled against the night. Pilar stepped onto the rug and sank down cross-legged, knees almost touching Tamsin's. Grandma Nia smiled but said nothing more, giving the children full room to work. Tamsin handed Pilar a small handful of green pieces that matched the tree leaves in the picture.

Pilar's fingers closed carefully around the pieces, and Tamsin noticed how the light from the round window made every cardboard edge shine. Together they turned the pieces slowly, listening to the tiny clicks when two edges locked true. The rug held their weight evenly and felt soft under their elbows as they leaned closer. Tamsin slowed their own searching so Pilar could try a corner piece first without any hurry. "This blue one might be sky," Pilar whispered, holding it up between them. Tamsin nodded and waited while Pilar tested the fit against the top row. Grandma Nia poured warm tea into two small cups on a side table, the steam rising in thin white curls that caught the last light. The whole scene stayed gentle and unhurried, only the shared work of matching colors and quiet shapes.

Tamsin found a polished pebble resting near the edge of the rug, smooth and cool as river glass, and set it carefully on a finished corner of the puzzle so the pieces would not slide. The texture of the pebble felt round and satiny against Tamsin's thumb before they left it there as a quiet marker. Pilar smiled at the little stone weight and fitted another piece beside it. "We can take turns with the middle parts," Tamsin said, changing the pace so each of them tried two pieces then paused. The tall library shelves cast long soft shadows across the floor, and the air held a faint warmth from the lamps farther down the aisle. Grandma Nia stayed seated nearby with hands folded, simply watching the children invent their own way of sharing. Tamsin felt glad that Pilar now sat fully inside the circle of the game.

They worked in easy silence for a while, the only sounds the soft scrape of cardboard and their own slow breathing. Tamsin shifted the seating once more by sliding the box lid between them so both could reach every remaining piece without stretching far. Cool night air pressed lightly against the round window and fogged a thin crescent at the bottom, yet the rug stayed cozy underneath. Pilar found the missing flower petal and clicked it home with a happy little sound. "Look, Tamsin, it fits," Pilar said, eyes bright in the soft light. Tamsin clapped once, quietly, palms warm against each other. Grandma Nia let them keep solving without any grown-up directions, trusting the two friends to finish the garden picture on their own. The puzzle grew clearer with every shared turn.

Tamsin rubbed their eyes and felt the day growing heavy in their limbs, yet the calm of the rug kept them content. The round window now showed only dark sky and one early silver star. Pilar yawned behind a polite hand, and Tamsin slowed the game still more by suggesting they sort the last pieces by color first. "We can finish the path last," Tamsin offered, voice barely above a whisper. The gentle change of rules kept neither of them left behind or rushed. Warmth rose from the thick rug fibers straight into Tamsin's legs as they sat side by side. Grandma Nia rose once to draw a thin curtain halfway across the window, dimming the room further into sleepy dusk. The children nodded their thanks without looking up from the almost-complete puzzle.

When the last piece slid into place, Tamsin and Pilar both leaned back on their hands and looked at the whole garden scene glowing faintly under the window light. The picture of winding stones and green leaves matched the quiet feeling inside them. Tamsin touched the finished edges and felt a soft contentment settle. Pilar carefully returned the small wooden box lid. "Thank you for making space," Pilar said, soft and sure. Tamsin answered, "It is better when we both belong right here." Grandma Nia gathered the empty cups with slow movements so she would not hurry them. The three of them sat together a moment longer on the library rug under the round window while the evening deepened around their shoulders.

Grandma Nia helped them slide the finished puzzle back into its box without breaking the picture, and Tamsin carried the light load over to the low shelf. The room had grown dim and kind, perfect for resting. Tamsin and Pilar stretched out side by side on the rug, heads near the round window where cooler air still drifted gently. Grandma Nia tucked a folded blanket over their legs, the fabric smooth and heavy enough to hold the day's last warmth. Tamsin closed their eyes and listened to the slow breaths nearby. The lantern by the window glowed softly. In that gentle light Tamsin felt the friendly evening settle around them all, ready for sleep.
