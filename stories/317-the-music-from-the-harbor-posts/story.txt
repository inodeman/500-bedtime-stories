The ferryman was already there at the harbor wall at low tide, his hands mid-task as he carefully knotted new ropes onto the mooring rings with practiced fingers that moved without hurry. Golda approached along the wall with Gita walking close beside them, and the cool evening air touched Golda's cheeks with a gentle chill that made them pull their jacket a little tighter. The stones felt firm and slightly uneven under their feet while pale light from the west spread across the exposed muddy flats in long soft bars. "Hello, Golda. Hello, Gita. The tide is out nice and far today," the ferryman said as he pulled a knot tight and tested it with a tug. Golda watched his work carefully and noticed the clean scent of wet wood rising from the boat nearby as they stood for a long moment taking in the wide quiet space left by the water that had gone out.

Golda wandered a few steps farther with Gita, looking down at the rows of thick harbor posts that stuck up from the sand like sturdy old friends whose surfaces were rough and weathered by many seasons. Golda ran a chalky hand along the nearby wall where white powder from the crumbling stone coated their fingers lightly and left a dry dusty feel. Suddenly the sweet smell of honey drifted past from a baker's cart that had stopped up the lane, making Golda think of warm toast at breakfast. The posts themselves gave off no smell, only a quiet solid presence in the fading day. Soft cries of seagulls calling far across the flats filled the air as Golda and Gita paused to study the scene more carefully and feel the open space around them.

Then the wind shifted and came sideways across the open harbor, and right away the harbor posts started humming with a deep steady tone that rose from their hollow insides and filled the air. Golda stopped short and listened hard, their ears filling with the unexpected music that seemed to come from the wood itself. Gita's eyes grew round as the humming continued like a soft orchestra of wood and air that rose and fell with each puff. "Listen to that sound, Golda. The posts are singing," Gita said in a hushed voice full of quiet surprise. The light flickered a little as thin clouds moved slowly overhead, and the temperature stayed even and pleasant on Golda's bare arms and neck. They both leaned forward just a bit without touching anything, letting the sideways wind keep up its gentle work on the posts.

Inside Golda the wish to pocket a small bit of the humming wood or to poke the posts to see what would happen felt strong and real for a second or two. But Golda kept their hands at their sides and just looked with careful eyes. The breeze carried a light warmth now that mixed with the cooler patches near the water and brushed softly against skin. Farther up the path they heard a gate latch click once as someone closed a garden entrance with a neat metal sound. Golda turned to Gita and smiled a quiet smile full of interest in the ongoing notes. The posts hummed on without pause under the steady sideways push of the air that made the music keep coming.

Golda showed Gita how to look without disturbing the posts by taking one slow step back and cupping a hand behind one ear to catch more of the tone. "If we stand like this and stay very still, Gita, we can hear every part of the hum without changing it at all," Golda said in a soft clear voice. Gita tried the same pose and nodded with matching care. The fabric of Golda's sleeve felt soft as they raised their arm in the shared stance. Evening light turned the wet sand into a sheet of silver that shone gently near their feet. "It is better this way," Gita answered after a short pause filled with listening. Golda felt glad they had chosen to watch carefully together in the open space.

The ferryman set down his ropes and came to stand near Golda and Gita, tilting his head to catch the music from the harbor posts that still sang in the wind. "When the wind comes sideways like that, these posts have always hummed for anyone who stops to notice," the ferryman said with a warm and steady tone. Golda looked up at him with bright eyes. "We are going to leave them right where they are so the humming can go on," Golda replied in firm soft words. The air around them held a faint damp coolness from the low tide flats that touched every cheek. He nodded in full agreement and they all listened side by side as the notes rolled gently out over the sand and stones.

For a short time they waited through a single chime that sounded from a marker buoy rocking out beyond the wall, letting the clear ring blend with the posts for one peaceful stretch. Golda kept perfectly still during the wait, breathing slowly and evenly while the note lasted. The light softened further as the sun sank lower toward the far edge of the water. Gita's shoulder brushed Golda's with a friendly solid warmth that felt nice. After the chime faded the humming posts continued alone once more in their own way. Golda felt the smooth cool surface of a nearby stone under their resting palm and knew the posts belonged exactly there in the harbor.

Soon Golda and Gita waved farewell to the ferryman and began the walk home along the quiet path, their shoes making soft scuffs on the packed earth that led away from the water. Behind them the harbor wall and its humming posts grew smaller with each step they took together. Cooler air settled around Golda's neck and hands as night drew slowly near and the sky deepened. They talked in low voices about the sideways wind and the music it had made among the wood. Lamps began to shine in windows along the street, casting pools of yellow light on the ground that guided their way. Golda felt peaceful recalling how they had all looked without touching and left everything in place.

At home Golda changed into soft night clothes and sat on the edge of the bed while the house settled into evening quiet around them. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Outside a last light breeze moved the curtains with a faint rustle that sounded like far-off water, and Golda remembered the posts left humming in their place under any wind that might still blow. The blanket felt thick and comforting over their legs as warmth spread through the room. They closed their eyes with Gita's shared looking still close in mind and the ferryman's kind words echoing gently, ready for sleep in the calm and steady space.
