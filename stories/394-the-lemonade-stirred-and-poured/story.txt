A faint jingle of a cat's collar bell drifted from under the wooden chair in the little room beside the water. Esme noticed the small sound at once and tilted their head, listening while the cool evening air brushed softly across their bare arms. Pale gold light slipped through the open window and lay in long quiet stripes on the floorboards. Esme rose, smoothed one wrinkle out of the cloth on the table, and stepped outside where the harbor dock stretched wide and steady under the settling sky.

Esme walked along the worn planks with Teo close beside them. Uncle Idris waited near the edge where the boats rocked gently. The air held a warm hint of tomato soup from the supper pot still simmering in the room behind them. Baskets of fresh rolls and folded napkins waited in neat rows, ready to be passed up from the lower boats to the higher landing. Esme touched the smooth rail and felt the last heat of the day leaving the wood. Teo pointed at the tall stack and said, “We can finish these if we keep the lemonade ready too.” Esme nodded and answered, “We will do it slowly so nothing spills.”

Uncle Idris showed them the clear glass pitcher and the long wooden spoon resting on a clean towel. He said only, “Call the knot a simple clove hitch if the rope slips,” then stepped back to watch the baskets rise. Esme lifted the pitcher while Teo held the spoon. The lemonade looked pale and bright under the open sky. Esme began to stir in slow circles so the sweet slices of lemon turned without splash. Teo waited with both hands steady on the rim of the first cup. A light breeze moved across Esme’s face and carried the soft creak of rope.

Esme kept the spoon moving in the same calm rhythm while Teo watched the surface. If either of them hurried the liquid would slosh over the edge and wet the napkins. Esme said, “I will stir until the ice settles low.” Teo answered, “Then I will pour just to the line and no farther.” Uncle Idris counted under his breath in an easy voice, giving them a quiet pace. Esme felt the cool glass under their fingers and smelled the clean citrus rising with every turn of the spoon. The baskets kept coming up one after another, handed from boat to dock with careful arms.

Teo took the pitcher when Esme finished stirring and began to pour in a thin steady stream. Esme held each cup still so the lemonade filled without a drip. The boards under their feet felt solid and slightly rough. Light from the low sun turned the surface of the drink to soft gold. Another basket rose, and Esme reached for its wicker handle, feeling the firm woven texture against their palm before setting it safely on the dock. Teo smiled and said, “That one is full enough.” Esme replied, “We can do the next the same way.”

They worked side by side without rushing. Esme stirred a fresh pitcher while Teo poured the finished one into waiting cups for the people who climbed the short ladder. Uncle Idris offered the name of the knot once more when a rope loosened, and Esme and Teo repeated it together while their hands stayed busy. The water beside the dock made a low continuous hush. Cool air drifted up from the surface and touched Esme’s ankles. Every cup stood steady on the planks, and every basket found its place in the growing row.

When the last basket came up, Esme and Teo poured the final cup of lemonade and set the empty pitcher down on the towel. Uncle Idris nodded once. The three of them stood quiet for a moment while the pink light faded into soft blue. Esme felt the calm that comes after work done together. Teo handed Esme a cup, and they both took slow sips. The lemonade tasted bright and cold. Around them the dock grew stiller, and the boats settled into gentle rocking.

Esme helped gather the empty cups and the spoon while Teo folded the towel. Uncle Idris coiled the spare rope with the clove hitch he had named. The evening air turned cooler against Esme’s cheeks, and the planks held a faint leftover warmth under their feet. Esme looked at the neat line of baskets and the clean pitcher and felt glad they had chosen the slow way. Teo brushed a stray lemon seed from the rail. No one spoke loudly. The harbor held only the quiet sounds of water and wood.

Esme carried the last clean cup back toward the little room with Teo and Uncle Idris walking just behind. Inside, the air still held the gentle warmth of earlier. Esme set the cup down and sat on the cushion again. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Outside the water kept its steady hush, and Esme closed their eyes with the calm feeling of a day finished well, the lemonade shared, the baskets safe, and every slow job done together.
