"Papa, the little pot is already steaming," Tala said as they stepped through the workshop doorway and felt the gentle heat rising from the corner burner. Soft afternoon light slanted across the wooden floor and touched the low pegs where hammers and chisels hung in neat rows. Tala walked closer and noticed the wooden spoon resting beside the pot, its tip still faintly wet, and they understood at once that Papa had tasted the soup first and only then added the salt. The air near the burner felt pleasantly warm against Tala’s cheeks while the rest of the room stayed cooler. Tala stood still for a moment, listening to the quiet tick of cooling metal, and thought how carefully Papa had waited.

Tala moved along the workbench and brushed one finger across a stack of pale sheets that smelled of fresh paper waiting for sketches. They remembered how Papa always measured twice before cutting, and how the same patience had gone into the soup. The light from the high window made the paper edges glow almost silver. Tala picked up a short pencil, tested its point, and set it back exactly where it belonged. A slow drip of water from the outdoor faucet marked time outside, steady and soft. Tala’s shoulders relaxed as they watched Papa wipe his hands on a clean cloth and smile without speaking.

Near the back wall Tala rested one palm against the cool, damp clay wall and felt its smooth grain under their skin. The wall held the memory of recent rain yet stayed firm and quiet. Tala traced a small circle there, then turned to the low pegs again. Every tool hung within easy reach, handles polished by years of use. Tala noticed a loose coil of twine dangling near the floor and decided it needed a better home. They wound the twine into a neat loop and slipped it onto an empty peg. The motion felt useful and calm, something Papa had not asked for yet clearly needed.

Outside a gate latch clicked shut with a gentle metallic sound as a neighbor closed the yard for the evening. Tala heard it clearly through the open side door and felt the workshop grow stiller afterward. Papa ladled a small taste of soup into a cup and set it aside to cool. Tala watched the steam curl upward in slow silver ribbons. The temperature near the burner stayed cozy while a faint breeze from the doorway carried the last outdoor air inside. Tala thought a plain thank-you would feel too thin after all the quiet care Papa had shown.

Tala found a soft brush and swept the fine wood shavings from the bench into a little tin box Papa kept for kindling. The shavings whispered against the bristles like dry leaves. Golden light from the west window stretched longer across the floorboards and warmed the tops of Tala’s shoes. They worked slowly so the brush made almost no noise. When the surface shone clean, Tala returned the brush to its own low peg. Papa glanced over and nodded once, still stirring the pot with patient circles.

“I saw you tasted it first,” Tala said, voice steady and clear. Papa looked up, eyes kind. “I wanted the salt just right for everyone,” Papa answered. Tala smiled and added, “That was thoughtful.” The words hung gently in the warm air. A soft clink sounded when Papa set the ladle against the pot rim. Tala felt the workshop settle around them like a familiar blanket. They rested both palms on their knees for a quiet moment, feeling the sturdy cloth of their trousers, then rose again.

Tala carried the cooled tasting cup to the sink and rinsed it under a thin stream of water that felt cool on their wrists. The light had shifted to a deeper honey color, painting the tool handles gold. They dried the cup with a folded towel whose weave felt thick and kind against their fingers. Papa hummed a low tune while he adjusted the burner flame lower. Tala placed the clean cup on its shelf and straightened a row of small jars filled with nails and screws. Each jar made a tiny glass note when it touched its neighbor.

“I put the twine away and cleared the bench,” Tala said. Papa turned fully toward them. “I noticed you already took care of the soup the careful way,” Tala continued. “Thank you for waiting to salt it.” Papa’s voice came warm and even. “You saw that, and you helped without being asked. That means a great deal.” Tala felt a quiet gladness spread through their chest like warm tea. The burner made one last soft sigh as the flame settled lower still. Outside the sky turned the color of ripe plums, and the workshop lamps began to seem brighter by comparison.

Tala walked once more past the low pegs, checking that every handle hung straight, then stood beside Papa while the soup finished its gentle simmer. The air held the mild scent of carrots and broth mixed with wood. Papa poured two small bowls and they sat on the sturdy stools near the window. The first spoonful tasted balanced and kind. Tala rested both palms on their knees again while they waited for the second bowl to cool. Evening quiet filled every corner. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
