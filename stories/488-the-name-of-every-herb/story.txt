A soft pop rose from a log in the fire, and Bodhi noticed it right away while sitting on the rug near the cottage hearth. The sound was small and friendly. Warm golden light washed over Bodhi's hands and made their freckles look brighter. Auntie June sat in her favorite chair close by, and the heat from the stones of the hearth felt steady against Bodhi's side. Bodhi stretched out their legs and let the comfort sink in. The cottage held a deep quiet. Bodhi watched the flames curl and straighten without any rush. They felt safe and ready for whatever story might come next. The air carried a mild dry warmth that made Bodhi's eyelids grow heavy.

Auntie June cleared her throat gently and began. "I want to tell you about the names of some herbs, Bodhi," Auntie June said. Bodhi felt a surge of bigger news about the tall oak tree and the wide view from its branches, yet they chose to stay silent and listen. The firelight flickered across Auntie June's face and cast a soft glow on the basket of dried plants at her feet. Bodhi's cheeks felt the pleasant heat radiating in slow waves. They folded their hands and focused on every word. The room smelled clean of wood and home. Auntie June waited a moment, and Bodhi gave a small nod. Everything in the cottage seemed to slow down without haste.

"These herbs have names that sound nearly alike, Bodhi," Auntie June continued in her even voice. Bodhi kept the oak tree news tucked behind their teeth and watched her lift the first small bunch. A faint draft cooled the back of Bodhi's neck for an instant before the hearth warmth returned. "The first is called sweet mint," she said. Bodhi repeated the name silently and pictured green leaves waving. The light from the fire made the dried stems look as if they still held summer. Bodhi leaned their shoulder against the chair leg and felt how solid it was. Auntie June's story unfolded like a slow path, and Bodhi walked it with care.

"Next comes garden mint, which sounds so much like the first," Auntie June said, holding up another stem so Bodhi could see. Bodhi listened hard and heard how the names chimed together. The flames made a continuous low whisper that filled the spaces between her sentences. Bodhi's bigger news waited farther back now, growing quieter. They studied the way Auntie June's fingers handled the herbs with gentleness. Heat soaked through Bodhi's sweater and made them feel wrapped in comfort. The cottage hearth stood as the glowing heart of the room. Bodhi almost forgot the oak tree as the second name settled in.

"Then there is wild mint, and still the names keep sounding alike," Auntie June went on. Bodhi drew a deep breath and suddenly noticed the smell of lavender coming from a small pouch on the windowsill. It drifted over in a sweet calm wave. They had listened through three names already and felt glad of their patience. The golden light softened as brighter flames died back to coals, yet the temperature stayed kind and even. Bodhi's eyes followed Auntie June's every movement. The bigger news about climbing high no longer pushed so hard. Instead Bodhi rested in the flow of the soft words and the peaceful air.

Auntie June paused and then said, "All those minty names sound so much the same until the last one." Bodhi stayed perfectly quiet, waiting for the final name with their whole heart. A log settled deeper into the coals with a muted sigh of ash. Bodhi felt the warmth increase for a second across their shins. They watched Auntie June select the special last stem from the basket. The light danced over the walls in lazy loops. Bodhi realized they had come all the way through without interrupting, and a quiet gladness filled them. The evening felt long and safe.

"The last herb is called quiet leaf," Auntie June said, and the different sound of it rang clear and true. Bodhi had listened all the way to the last sentence just as they meant to. The tin clock on the mantelpiece ticked once with a clear bright note that fit the moment. Bodhi reached toward the quilt folded over the arm of Auntie June's chair and ran one finger along a single quilt stitch, feeling the firm bumpy texture of the careful sewing. That small raised line felt strong and neat. The fire's glow lit the quilt's soft colors. Bodhi sat back, full of the sounding-alike names and the one that stood apart.

Now Bodhi could ask one real question. "Why does quiet leaf sound so different from the others, Auntie June?" Bodhi asked in a voice just above a whisper. Auntie June answered, "Because it grows where the shade is deepest and likes a name that belongs only to itself." Bodhi nodded, understanding in their own young way. They watched a shadow from the fire move the width of a thumb across the hearthstone as the flames shifted. The air near the floor stayed cool enough for comfort while upper warmth hovered higher. Bodhi felt their own small news ready now, gentler than before. Everything in the room seemed to approve of the waiting.

Bodhi shared something small of their own. "I saw a tiny white moth resting on the porch rail this morning," they said. Auntie June smiled and reached out to smooth Bodhi's hair with a light touch. Bodhi leaned their head against the chair and felt the last bits of the day melt into ease. The coals in the cottage hearth pulsed with a deep red light that painted the floor in soft shapes. A gentle heat still rose and brushed Bodhi's face. They thought of sweet mint and garden mint and wild mint and then quiet leaf, all held safely. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Bodhi let their eyes close halfway and rested in the warm quiet.
