Neighbor Sami was already there in the harbor shelter on the lee side of the wind, mid-task with a stack of folded blankets that they patted smooth on the wide bench near the inner doorway. The cool temperature inside the wooden walls felt steady and kind, free from any push of air, and the filtered afternoon light lay in quiet bars across the floorboards. Sable stepped in and paused, watching Neighbor Sami tuck the last corner into place with slow hands. Sable smelled the clean salt of the boards underfoot and heard only the far soft wash of water against the pilings outside. Neighbor Sami glanced up with a small smile and kept working, making certain the path to the next room stayed open and peaceful. Sable thought of the songs they had practiced earlier beyond that doorway and felt how still everything remained now.

Sable moved closer to the bench and rested one hand on the smooth wood, feeling its worn grain under their palm while the dim light shifted gently along the walls. Neighbor Sami had already taken care of the quiet made in the next room while Sable practiced, keeping footsteps soft and voices low so the practice time could settle without any break. The hush hung there like a held breath, warm and ready. Sable remembered the careful notes they had tried again and again, and how the quiet had wrapped around each one. Neighbor Sami lifted a small tray from the side shelf and set it down without a clatter. “The next room is ready whenever you want it again,” Neighbor Sami said in a low voice. Sable nodded and looked toward the open doorway where the light fell softer still.

A faint scent of cinnamon drifted from a covered tin on the table, sweet and familiar, and Sable breathed it in while they stood beside Neighbor Sami. The air stayed cool against Sable’s wrists as they watched Neighbor Sami arrange two mugs beside the tin. Sable knew a quick thank-you would feel too thin for the care already given to that quiet space. They looked around the shelter for one useful thing Neighbor Sami had not asked for. The low shelves held cups and a few spoons, and the floor near the entrance needed a gentle sweep of the scattered sand that had blown in earlier. Sable picked up the short broom and began to gather the grains into a neat pile, moving slowly so the hush stayed whole.

Neighbor Sami noticed the sweeping and tilted their head but said nothing at first, only poured warm water into the mugs so the cinnamon smell grew a little fuller in the still air. Sable finished the small sweep and set the broom back against the wall, then reached for the tray to carry it nearer the window seat where the light was gentlest. The wood of the tray felt dry and light in Sable’s hands. “You did not have to clear the sand,” Neighbor Sami said softly. Sable set the tray down and answered, “I wanted to help the same way you helped the quiet.” The words came plain and steady. Outside a gate latch clicked shut with a single clear sound as someone passed along the path, and then the shelter returned to its deep calm.

Sable lifted one of the spoons from the tray and felt its walnut-smooth spoon surface against their fingers, cool and polished from many careful washings. They stirred the warm drink in slow circles so the cinnamon scent rose again in soft curls. Neighbor Sami sat on the bench and watched the light slide lower along the opposite wall. Sable handed one mug across and kept the other, then sat as well with their knees tucked close. The temperature of the mug warmed Sable’s palms while the shelter held its even coolness around them both. “I noticed how you kept everything still while I practiced,” Sable said. Neighbor Sami’s eyes crinkled at the corners. “I heard the careful notes,” Neighbor Sami replied. “They sounded happy in the quiet.”

Chioma appeared at the open doorway for only a moment, her steps light on the threshold, and she left a small folded cloth of extra napkins before she waved and went on toward the boats. The brief visit brought a thread of brighter light that faded again as she moved away. Sable unfolded one napkin and placed it beside each mug, another small task no one had requested. The cloth felt soft under Sable’s fingertips. Neighbor Sami hummed four notes under their breath and then left a rest so the silence could settle back like a feather. Sable smiled and matched the same four notes in a quieter voice before the rest came again. The harbor water whispered farther off, and the shelter stayed wrapped in its safe lee-side stillness.

Sable rose once more and checked the latch on the inner door to the next room, making sure it rested open just enough for air to drift without noise. The wood felt cool and solid under their hand. Neighbor Sami watched and gave a quiet nod of thanks that needed no extra words. Sable returned to the bench and sipped from their mug, tasting the gentle cinnamon warmth while the light outside softened toward evening. “The quiet felt bigger because you were already taking care of it,” Sable said plainly. Neighbor Sami answered, “And now the whole shelter feels tended by both of us.” They sat together without hurry, listening to the distant creak of a mooring rope and the even hush that filled every corner.

The shadows lengthened across the floor in slow blue bands while Sable and Neighbor Sami finished their drinks in matching calm. Sable stacked the empty mugs and the walnut-smooth spoon back onto the tray and carried everything to the side shelf where it belonged. The air against Sable’s bare arms stayed pleasantly cool, and the last of the cinnamon scent lingered like a kind memory. Neighbor Sami rose and shook out one blanket so it lay ready for anyone who might rest later. Sable helped smooth the edges until no wrinkle remained. “I saw what you did for the practice quiet, and I wanted you to know,” Sable said one more time. Neighbor Sami rested a hand briefly on Sable’s shoulder and answered, “I know now, and it feels complete.”

They stood a little longer in the growing dusk while the shelter held its peaceful temperature and the softest sounds of the harbor continued outside. Sable looked once more toward the next room and felt glad the quiet had been kept so well. Neighbor Sami lit the small lantern that waited on the sill. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Sable watched the warm circle of light spread across the nearest boards and settled back onto the bench for a final shared breath of stillness before the evening deepened all the way into night.
