Papa stood already on the wide stone harbor step in the mellow afternoon light, his arms stretched awkwardly as he tried to keep the swinging wooden window of the little parcel booth propped open with one shoulder while he accepted a stack of brown-wrapped packages from the boatman. The cool damp air rising from the water brushed against his bare forearms and made the papers rustle. The wooden prop that belonged in the window frame had slipped free earlier and lay nearby on the step, so Papa kept shifting his weight to hold everything steady. Una walked up beside him and saw at once how the open window tilted and threatened to close on the parcels. The stone felt smooth and sun-warmed under Una's bare feet as they stopped close to Papa.

Una reached down without a word and picked up the smooth wooden prop, turning it in their hands so the light caught its polished surface. A soft scent of beeswax rose from the wood, leftover from the careful rubbing Papa had given it last week. Una slid the prop into place beneath the window sash, holding it firm with both palms while Papa freed his shoulder and settled the parcels safely on the inner counter. The water lapped gently against the harbor wall in a slow rhythm. “There,” Una said quietly, “now it will stay open for the rest.” Papa smiled down and answered, “That helps more than you know, Una.” Una kept one hand steady on the prop a moment longer, feeling how solid and sure it sat once it was seated right.

With the window held open by its wooden prop, Papa could pass the parcels through one by one, and Una stood ready on the outer step to receive each bundle and set it neatly on the waiting cart. The late light turned soft and honey-colored across the boards of the booth, warming Una's cheeks. A boat horn sounded far out on the water, low and friendly. “Hand me the next one when you are ready,” Una told Papa, and he replied, “Here it comes, steady now.” Una took the weight of the package carefully, the brown paper rough against their fingers, and placed it beside the others. They worked side by side in that easy way, neither of them speaking of praise, just sharing the job so the parcels moved safely from boat to shore.

Between packages Una glanced aside and noticed a small snail making its slow shining path across one whole brick of the harbor step. The brick felt cool and slightly gritty where Una rested a fingertip near the snail's trail. Soft green light filtered down through the branches of the fig tree that grew beside the booth. Una watched the snail finish its crossing while Papa sorted the last labels inside. The air stayed mild and kind. Una turned back and said, “The snail made it all the way,” and Papa looked out with a quiet chuckle. “So it did,” he answered. Then they both returned to the remaining parcels, Una holding the cart steady so nothing would tip.

Once the cart was full, Papa closed the window carefully over its wooden prop and locked the simple latch. Una ran a hand along the outer sill, feeling the grain of the wood still warm from the sun. The water reflected the sky in gentle blues and pinks. Nearby the fig tree stirred, and Una heard the wind in the fig leaves making a dry, whispery music above their heads. They stood together for a moment on the harbor step, listening. Una reached up and touched one low leaf, turning it to feel a leaf's soft underside against their thumb. The texture was cool and almost velvety. Papa rested a hand on Una's shoulder and they watched the boats rock quietly at their moorings.

Papa lifted the lighter packages while Una guided the cart wheels over the uneven stones of the step. The evening air carried a faint salt smell mixed with the last sweetness of the day. Lanterns began to glow along the far docks, one after another. “Shall we roll it home together?” Papa asked, and Una nodded and said, “Yes, I can push from this side.” Their voices stayed low and calm in the open air. The wooden wheels of the cart clicked softly over each crack. Una kept close to Papa the whole short way along the waterfront path, matching his slower steps so the load stayed even.

At the end of the short path they reached the little house that looked out toward the harbor. Una helped ease the cart through the gate and into the shed while the last pink light faded from the sky. Inside the kitchen the air felt still and mild. Papa set the parcels on the table to sort in the morning. Una washed their hands at the basin, feeling the cool water slip over their wrists. From the open doorway came the distant creak of ropes on the boats. They moved without hurry, putting away the day in small careful motions.

Later Una sat on the low bench by their bedroom window while Papa brought a cup of warm milk that steamed gently in the quiet room. The milk smelled plain and comforting. Outside the fig leaves had grown still. Una sipped slowly and watched the harbor lights wink across the dark water. Papa sat nearby and spoke of tomorrow's simple plans in a soft voice. Una answered with a sleepy word or two. The room held only the low hum of evening and the faint clink of the cup.

Una climbed into bed and pulled the soft blanket up to their chin, feeling the gentle weight settle around them. Papa dimmed the main lamp and stepped to the doorway. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Una closed their eyes and listened to Papa's quiet footsteps fade down the hall, already half dreaming of parcels and open water and the steady wooden prop that had made the work easy for two.
