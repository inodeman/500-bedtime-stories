The smell of warm bread drifted from the kitchen as Nim’s hands already smoothed the big paper map flat across the library rug under the round window. Nim pressed their palms along every edge so nothing would curl up, and the paper felt dry and slightly dusty under their fingers while soft light from the round glass warmed the tops of their wrists. Grandma Nia sat in the nearby chair with her knitting, and the room stayed quiet except for the gentle click of her needles. Nim leaned closer and traced the drawn paths of the backyard with one careful finger, already deciding where the marks would go. The rug’s thick weave cushioned Nim’s knees, and the air stayed pleasantly mild.

Nim looked up through the round window and saw the fig tree outside, then turned back to the map that needed two X marks, not one. Yelena stood just beyond the edge of the rug, holding a blue crayon and watching without stepping onto the soft circle yet. Grandma Nia smiled but stayed quiet so the children could decide things themselves. Nim felt the cool ceramic rim of the empty snack bowl resting near their foot and nudged it aside with a careful toe. The late light made pale circles on the floor around them, and Nim’s shoulders stayed relaxed as they thought about the map’s blank spots.

“Yelena, the map is big enough for both of us,” Nim said in a calm voice. Yelena stepped one foot closer but still stayed half off the rug, the blue crayon warm in her hand. Nim shifted their own seat sideways so a clear space opened right beside the map’s middle. The round window let in a gentle brightness that touched Yelena’s hair. Grandma Nia kept knitting without speaking, letting the two children work it out. Nim patted the open spot on the rug twice so the invitation felt real and steady.

Yelena sat down fully on the library rug, and Nim slid the map a little so both pairs of hands could reach the middle easily. “We need two X marks because the yard has two special trees,” Nim explained softly. Yelena nodded and held out the blue crayon. Outside, the sound of wind in the fig leaves brushed against the glass in a slow hush. Nim took the crayon and waited so Yelena could choose the first spot together. The rug held their weight evenly, and the air near the floor felt cool and still.

They decided the first X would mark the fig tree itself and the second would mark the little bench under the apple tree. Nim changed the pace by slowing every motion so Yelena could draw at the same time. “You put your X here, and I will put mine right next to it,” Nim said. Yelena pressed the crayon down carefully, and the blue line formed a neat X on the paper. Nim added the second X just a finger-width away so both marks sat clear and equal. The paper made a quiet scratch under the crayon, and the light from the round window stayed steady on their shared work.

Grandma Nia set her knitting down and watched without interrupting while Nim and Yelena checked both X marks once more. “Does the map feel finished now?” Grandma Nia asked in her low kind tone. Nim nodded and answered, “Yes, because Yelena helped make both places important.” Yelena smiled and rested her hand lightly on the corner of the map. The children leaned back on their heels, and the soft pile of the rug warmed under their legs. A faint sweetness still hung in the air from earlier, and the room felt peaceful around the three of them.

Nim picked up the empty bowl by its cool edge and carried it carefully to the sink, then walked back to the rug in slow steps so nothing felt rushed. Yelena had already smoothed the map again, and both children sat side by side once more. “Tomorrow we can follow the two X marks outside if the sun is out,” Yelena said. Nim agreed with a quiet hum and folded the map into neat halves so it would stay safe. The round window showed the sky turning softer blue, and the temperature near the floor stayed comfortable. Grandma Nia rose and fetched two small cushions for their backs.

Nim and Yelena rested against the cushions while Grandma Nia dimmed the brighter lamp, leaving only the gentler glow near the window. Nim kept one hand on the folded map so it stayed close between them. The library rug felt thick and kind under their whole bodies now. Outside the leaves had grown quieter, and inside the room the air smelled clean and settled. Nim’s eyelids grew heavier, yet they still glanced once at Yelena to make sure she looked just as calm. Grandma Nia spoke one last soft sentence about how well the two marks sat together on the paper.

Nim curled a little closer to the map and to Yelena while the cushions held them both. Grandma Nia settled back into her chair with a small sigh of contentment. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Nim breathed in the quiet room and felt the last warmth of the day fade into night, ready for sleep beside their friend on the soft rug under the round window.
