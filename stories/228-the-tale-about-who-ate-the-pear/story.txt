The afternoon breeze brushed the round window so lightly that the glass only shimmered, and pale sunlight warmed the library table beneath it like a quiet blanket. Noemi sat close to that curve of glass, resting their cheek for a moment against the smooth wood that still held a trace of outdoor coolness. They set down a soft cloth bag and drew out one round pear whose skin felt cool and firm. Noemi turned the fruit slowly, watching how the light made a tiny bright spot on its side, and they thought how nicely it might sit inside the picture-story they planned to invent about orchard friends.

Noemi took a careful bite, and the juice tasted sweet while a tin clock on the far shelf kept its steady soft tick across the quiet room. They chewed and listened to that gentle metal sound, already half-dreaming of offering the pear a cameo in their tale. “Maybe you could roll down a hill and wake the sleepy owl,” Noemi murmured to the pear, smiling at the idea. Another bite followed, then another, until only a slender core and a few pale bits remained. The sticky sweetness clung to Noemi’s fingers, and they glanced around the empty chairs, almost sliding the core beneath a stack of open books so no one would notice.

Warm air drifted from a nearby vent and brushed Noemi’s neck while they hesitated, the half-hidden pear core still cool against their palm under the pages. They thought of the story they had nearly given the pear to, the way the fruit might have rested on the table as a real prop while they spoke the words aloud. Yet the sticky place on their hand made them pause. Noemi lifted the books again, feeling the paper edges, and drew the core back into plain sight. They wiped their fingers on a spare napkin from the bag, the paper rasping gently, and sat very still as the round window painted a slow gold circle on the tabletop.

Miss Dalca appeared between the tall shelves, her steps soft on the carpet, and the faint smell of lavender floated from the sachet tucked in her cardigan pocket. She carried a small stack of picture books and set them down near Noemi with a friendly nod. “Hello, Noemi,” she said in her calm low voice. “I see you found a good seat by the round window today.” Noemi looked up, the pear core resting openly now beside their notebook, and felt the cool wood under their wrists. They opened their mouth, closed it, then opened it once more.

“I ate the pear I brought,” Noemi said plainly, the words coming out steady. “I almost gave it a part in my story, and then I almost hid what was left under the books because my hands got sticky.” Miss Dalca listened without hurry, her eyes kind in the soft window light. She pulled out the chair across from Noemi and sat, the lavender scent drifting a little stronger for a moment. “Thank you for telling me the plain truth, Noemi,” she answered. “Sticky fingers happen, and we can mend what needs mending together.”

They worked side by side at the library table. Miss Dalca brought a damp cloth that felt smoothly cool, and Noemi wiped every spot of juice from the wood until the surface shone again under the gentle light. Together they wrapped the pear core in a clean scrap of paper and set it safely inside Noemi’s bag so it would not touch any books. Noemi’s friend Priya wandered over from the next table, her own book held open, and offered a quiet smile. “Want to draw a new pear for your story instead?” Priya asked. Noemi nodded, and the three of them shared the quiet scratch of colored pencils across fresh paper while the round window kept its calm glow.

Noemi pressed a finger along the edge of a borrowed reading quilt that Miss Dalca had draped over the spare chair, feeling one neat quilt stitch that rose like a tiny raised path under their fingertip. They traced that single stitch back and forth while the new pear took shape on the page, round and green with a cheerful leaf. Miss Dalca added a few soft lines of her own, and Priya colored a small basket beneath the fruit. “The story pear can stay right here on the paper,” Noemi said, voice light. “It will never get sticky, and I can still tell about it whenever I want.” The pencils whispered, the window light held steady, and the whole table felt peaceful again.

Noemi watched the shadow of the round window’s frame creep the width of a thumb across their drawing while the air stayed comfortably warm. They leaned back in the chair, shoulders loose, and listened to the faint rustle of pages turning somewhere deeper in the library. Miss Dalca gathered the extra pencils into a tin cup that clicked once, softly. Priya returned to her own book with a little wave. Noemi slipped their finished drawing into the notebook, the paper cool and smooth, and rested both hands flat on the clean table as contentment settled over them like quiet snowfall.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Noemi breathed in the last faint sweetness that still clung near their bag, felt the solid chair beneath them, and let their eyes follow the gentle gold light until everything grew slow and easy, ready for whatever calm came next.
