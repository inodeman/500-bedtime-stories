"Yuri, will you help me with these cushions before the mist soaks them all the way through?" asked Cousin Pio from the cottage porch where the cool evening air brushed softly against bare arms. Yuri stepped out onto the worn boards and felt the gentle chill settle on their skin like a light blanket while the silver mist drifted low and turned the open bench pale in the fading light. The cushions sat in a soft heap, already darkening at the edges where the mist had kissed them, and Cousin Pio tugged at one end with both hands. Yuri watched the fabric shift and saw how awkward the whole pile looked for just one person to lift. A quiet scent of rain on dust rose from the path beyond the railing and mixed with the clean wood smell of the porch.

Cousin Pio pulled again and the cushions slid only a little, their weight making the open bench creak under the slow drag. Yuri walked closer and rested one palm on the damp surface, feeling how the cloth had grown cool and heavy. The mist swirled in soft gray ribbons around their ankles and made the porch lantern glow with a warmer yellow against the pale air. "These are heavier than they look," Cousin Pio said, breathing out a small cloud that vanished at once. Yuri nodded and took hold of the other end without saying anything about praise or thanks, simply lifting so the load became lighter for both of them. Together they eased the first cushion free of the bench and carried it toward the open doorway that led into the hall just inside.

Warm air from the hall met them like a kind welcome, and the floorboards felt smooth under Yuri’s socks as they stepped across the threshold. Cousin Pio set their end down carefully near the wall, and Yuri matched the motion so the cushion landed without a bump. Soft lamplight spilled across the hallway rug and showed the faint sheen of mist still clinging to the cloth. "One more trip should do it," Cousin Pio murmured, and Yuri answered, "I can take this side again." They turned back to the porch where the remaining cushions waited in the cool gray air, and the mist had grown a little thicker, painting tiny beads on the wooden railing.

Outside once more, Yuri felt the temperature drop just enough to make their fingers tingle as they gripped the next cushion. Cousin Pio lifted the opposite corner, and together they swung the soft weight clear of the open bench. The fabric brushed Yuri’s wrist with a cool, slightly damp touch while they walked side by side toward the door. Inside the hall the air held a gentle warmth that dried the mist from their cheeks, and they placed the second cushion beside the first. Yuri noticed a satin ribbon tied around one corner of the pile, smooth and cool under their thumb as they straightened the edge so everything sat neat against the wall.

"Almost finished," Cousin Pio said with a quiet smile that showed only gladness, never surprise. Yuri simply nodded and followed back to the porch for the last of the cushions. The mist had left a thin film on the bench itself, and Yuri paused long enough to trace a small circle of fog with one dry finger before wiping the spot clean. Cool droplets beaded on the wood and caught the porch light like tiny stars. They lifted the final cushion between them, the weight now familiar and easy, and carried it into the hall where the air smelled faintly of cedar and warm cloth.

In the hallway Yuri and Cousin Pio stacked the last cushion on top of the others, pressing gently so the pile stayed steady. The satin ribbon on the lower cushion still felt smooth when Yuri tucked it out of the way. Soft footsteps sounded from deeper in the cottage, and Paz appeared with a folded towel, offering it without a word. Yuri took the towel and patted the top cushion until the surface felt only cool instead of wet. Cousin Pio smoothed the sides, and the three of them stood for a moment in the quiet glow of the hall lamp while the porch mist stayed politely outside.

Yuri heard a gate latch click shut somewhere near the garden path, a small metal sound that floated in through the open door and then faded. The porch boards still held a light sheen of moisture, but the cushions were safe now, warm and dry in their new place against the hall wall. Cousin Pio stretched their arms and smiled at Yuri, saying only, "That went much faster with two." Yuri felt the pleasant tiredness in their hands and the soft warmth of the indoor air on their face. They glanced once more at the neat stack and felt glad the open bench outside was empty of anything that needed saving.

Paz handed Yuri a second dry cloth, and together they wiped a few remaining beads from the cushion corners until every surface felt smooth and ready for rest. The hall light cast a calm golden circle on the floor, and the air stayed comfortably warm around their shoulders. Cousin Pio closed the porch door with a gentle push so the mist stayed outside where it belonged. Yuri stood near the cushions and listened to the quiet house settle into evening, the boards underfoot solid and familiar. No one spoke of who had done more; the work simply felt finished and kind.

Yuri sat for a short while on the hall bench beside the rescued cushions, letting the warmth soak into their legs while Cousin Pio and Paz moved softly about the room. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Outside the mist continued its slow silver dance across the empty open bench, and inside the air held the quiet peace of a job shared without any need for words of praise.
