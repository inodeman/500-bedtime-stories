In the sitting room a faint creak rose from the floorboard near the window, and Vera noticed it right away as they sat in the low chair with afternoon light pooling gold across the rug. The air felt pleasantly warm on their cheeks while they listened again, then stood and walked toward the open back door that led straight to the garden. Outside the rows stretched neat and green, and Vera saw the white string already stretched along one row beside two blue watering cans that waited in the mild sun. Miss Dalca stood there with a folded paper recipe in her hands, and Arun waved from the far end of the row where the soil looked dark and ready. Vera stepped closer and smelled the fresh earth mixed with something sweeter drifting from the kitchen window. They smiled at the familiar faces and felt ready for whatever quiet work waited.

Vera reached the garden row and brushed their fingers along the taut string that marked the plants, feeling how the fiber held steady in the soft breeze that cooled their wrists. Miss Dalca held up the recipe and said, “This one splits into stirring the mix and reading the steps, and it will go wrong if anyone rushes.” Arun nodded and looked at Vera with calm eyes. Vera answered, “Then we can take the two slow jobs together.” The light slanted gently across the watering cans, making their metal sides shine like quiet mirrors. Vera noticed how the leaves nearby trembled just a little, and they breathed in the warm outdoor air that carried a hint of cut grass. Everything felt peaceful as they decided to begin without hurry.

Miss Dalca handed the top half of the recipe to Arun so they could read each line aloud while Vera took the wooden spoon for stirring the small bowl of special plant food set on a flat stone. Vera said, “I will stir only when you finish each sentence.” Arun replied, “And I will read only after you stop the spoon.” The texture of the damp mixture felt cool against the spoon as Vera moved it in slow circles. Sunlight rested warm on Vera’s shoulders while a nearby bee hummed past without landing. Miss Dalca stayed close but did not take either job, simply watching the string and the cans with a kind expression. Vera kept their movements easy and even so nothing spilled.

Arun read the first step in a soft voice that matched the hush of the garden, and Vera stirred three careful turns before waiting. The mixture released a gentle scent of cocoa that rose on the breeze and made Vera smile as they worked. Miss Dalca pointed to the loose end of the garden string and offered, “You will need a square knot for that corner, or you may count to four while you loop it.” Vera glanced at Arun and they both chose to finish the stirring and reading first. The watering cans stood ready with cool water inside that would come later. Light flickered through the bean leaves and painted small moving shapes on the soil near Vera’s shoes. They felt glad the jobs stayed separate and slow.

Vera stirred again after Arun finished the next line, and the spoon made a quiet scraping sound against the bowl that blended with the distant leaves. Arun read the measuring note twice so Vera could keep the pace steady. A cat walked along the far path and its collar bell gave one clear bright ring that Vera heard and liked. Miss Dalca only named the knot once more when they looked toward the string, saying nothing else that might hurry them. The air stayed mild and the soil underfoot felt firm yet soft. Vera and Arun kept trading the small pauses so the recipe stayed even and right. Together they poured the finished mix into one watering can while the second waited with plain water.

Vera held the can steady while Arun guided the spout along the row, and the water made a gentle trickling sound as it soaked into the earth beside each plant. Miss Dalca showed them where the string needed the knot and counted softly to four while Vera’s fingers worked the loops. Vera said, “The square knot holds well when we go slow.” Arun added, “Now the reading and stirring are both done.” The wool sleeve of Miss Dalca’s cardigan brushed Vera’s arm for a moment as she steadied the string, and the fabric felt thick and comforting. Warm light continued to rest on the leaves. They finished watering the whole row without a single splash wasted, and the second can emptied last of all.

Vera stepped back with Arun and looked at the neat damp soil and the tied string that now sat firm along the garden row. Miss Dalca smiled and said the recipe had come out just as it should because no one had rushed the parts. The temperature of the late air cooled a little against Vera’s bare wrists while they stood together. A soft rustle moved through the taller plants behind them like a quiet thank-you. Vera felt the satisfaction of the shared work settle in their chest. They rinsed the spoon under the last drops from a can and set the bowl aside on the stone. Everything in the row looked cared for and calm.

Vera, Arun, and Miss Dalca walked back toward the house as the garden light turned softer and the doorway glowed ahead. Inside again Vera returned to the low chair near the window and rocked once in the chair and let it stop by itself while the others settled nearby. The room held a peaceful hush broken only by the distant settling of the house. Vera felt the smooth wood of the chair arms under their palms and watched the sky outside deepen by degrees. Arun sat on the rug and Miss Dalca poured a small shared cup that still carried the faint kitchen warmth. Vera rested easy knowing the garden jobs were complete and the row would sleep well under the coming evening.

Vera leaned their head against the chair back and listened to the quiet breathing of the room while the last light faded outside. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Arun spoke one more gentle word of good night and Miss Dalca answered with a warm nod. Vera closed their eyes to the familiar shapes and the settled stillness that wrapped the house like a blanket.
