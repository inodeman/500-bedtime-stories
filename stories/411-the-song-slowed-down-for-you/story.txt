Cousin Pio stood already at the stove in the cottage kitchen, stirring a pot of creamy porridge with slow circles while the pale morning light warmed the blue tiles. The air felt gently heated near the burner, and Pio sang the old river song in a careful, drawn-out way, holding each syllable so the words stayed easy to hear. Brisa walked in and paused by the doorway, listening as the stretched melody floated over the soft clink of the spoon. Outside the open window the leaves stirred, but inside everything smelled fresh and kind. Brisa watched Cousin Pio’s free hand rest near a small wooden box that held the music they both loved.

Brisa crossed the floor and felt the rough weave of a straw mat under their toes, a welcome texture after the smooth boards. Cousin Pio kept singing the slowed song without looking up, shaping the lines about willow banks and quiet boats so Brisa could catch every single word. The kitchen held the faint scent of cedar from the open shelves that lined one wall. Brisa smiled at the thoughtful pace of the tune and reached for the stack of clean bowls waiting on the counter. Steam rose in soft curls from the pot, carrying a mild grainy warmth that brushed Brisa’s cheeks.

“Good morning, Brisa,” Cousin Pio said in a voice that matched the gentle tempo of the song. “I thought we might learn the verses properly today.” Brisa nodded and began setting two places at the little table, placing spoons just so. The light from the window brightened a patch of yellow on the wall, and Brisa heard the wind in the fig leaves brushing the glass in a soft rustle. They liked how Cousin Pio had already taken care of the song slowed down so you could catch the words, making the morning feel open and unrushed. Brisa poured a little milk into a small jug and set it near the bowls.

Brisa decided a quick thank-you would feel too thin for such a careful gift, so they looked around for one useful thing Cousin Pio had not asked for. The cups sat on the drying rack with their handles turned every which way. Brisa picked up the nearest cup, turned it so the handle faced the room, and did the same for the second cup. The porcelain felt smooth and cool against their fingers. Cousin Pio kept the melody flowing, each slowed word landing clearly like pebbles in a still pond. Brisa breathed in the cedar again and felt glad to be helping without being told.

“I turned the cup handles toward us,” Brisa said plainly, “so they are ready when we sit.” Cousin Pio glanced over and gave a quiet smile that reached their eyes. “That is kind, Brisa,” Cousin Pio answered, still keeping the song gentle and unhurried. Brisa carried the milk jug the rest of the way and placed it carefully in the center of the table. The porridge made a low bubbling sound now and then, soft as a cat’s purr. Warm light continued to fill the cottage kitchen on this ordinary kind morning, settling on Brisa’s shoulders like a light shawl.

Brisa returned to the counter and wiped a small spill of water with a clean cloth, another small task that needed doing. The cloth felt damp and slightly rough in their hand. Cousin Pio finished the last slow verse and let the final note fade into the quiet. “Did you catch the part about the silver fish?” Cousin Pio asked. Brisa nodded and repeated the line in a soft voice of their own. Outside, the wind in the fig leaves kept its calm shushing, while inside the stove’s warmth stayed steady and friendly. Brisa felt the morning stretch comfortably around them both.

They sat together at the table once the bowls were filled. Brisa tasted the first spoonful and found it smooth and mild. Cousin Pio hummed a few more slowed phrases between bites, and Brisa joined in on the easy words they had now learned. The straw mat still rested under the table legs, its woven ridges a quiet reminder of home. Brisa looked at Cousin Pio and spoke the noticing they had held ready. “I saw that you slowed the song so I could catch the words,” Brisa said. “Thank you for doing that before I even asked.”

Cousin Pio set down their spoon and rested both hands on the table edge. “I hoped it would help, Brisa,” they replied. The kitchen filled with the gentle clink of spoons against bowls and the continued soft light from the window. Brisa felt the smooth wood of the chair under their palms and smiled again. Everything stayed calm and ordinary in the best way. They finished their breakfast without hurry, trading a few more clear lines of the river song that now belonged to both of them.

After the bowls were rinsed and set to dry, Brisa and Cousin Pio stood for a moment by the window. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Brisa leaned lightly against the sill and listened once more to the quiet that followed the song, feeling the last of the stove’s warmth on their back and the cool morning air on their face.
