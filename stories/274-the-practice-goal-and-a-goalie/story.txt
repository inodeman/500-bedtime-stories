The practice goal stood tucked against the ferry rail, its white netting glowing in the gentle afternoon light that poured across the deck, and Delia reached out to touch the frame which felt cool and firm under their palm. Tiny shadows from the mesh crossed Delia's shoes as they leaned in for a closer look at the empty space inside, where a soft ball could roll if someone stood ready. The deck boards warmed Delia's feet through their socks, and the whole goal took up just enough room that only two elbows of space remained for moving around carefully. Delia kept one hand on the pole and smiled at how the netting whispered when the ferry swayed a little in the calm water.

Willa waited just outside the small patch of fun, standing close to that practice goal with no one who wanted to stand in it alone, their hands resting lightly at their sides while they watched Delia and the soft orange ball. The warm smell of tomato soup drifted from a nearby thermos that Neighbor Sami had opened, mixing with the river air in a comforting way that made the whole deck feel homey. Delia glanced over and saw how Willa shifted from foot to foot on the sun-warmed wood, not quite in and not quite out of the circle. The light from the low sun made Willa's hair shine softly, and Delia felt the steady gentle motion of the ferry under everyone as the rail stayed solid nearby.

Neighbor Sami held a picnic basket by its wicker handle, which felt bumpy and dry against their fingers as they sat back on the bench and let the children work things out for themselves. "You three can make the game whatever you like," Neighbor Sami said softly, the words carrying easily over the quiet deck without any hurry. Delia turned toward Willa and felt a cool breeze brush their cheek like a friendly pat. The water sparkled beyond the rail in shades of blue and silver, and Delia knew the space was tight but still held plenty of room for sharing.

"Willa, will you come stand by the goal with me?" Delia asked, their voice warm and steady as they kept one hand on the cool pole so the netting stayed close. Willa stepped nearer, the boards creaking lightly under their shoes in a friendly rhythm, and the air around them felt pleasantly mild without any chill. Delia shifted their own feet so there was space for both of them right in front of the netting, changing the way they stood so no one had to guard the goal all alone. The sunlight lay soft and golden on their shoulders while they settled into the new side-by-side places without any fuss.

"I can roll the ball very slowly," Delia said next, changing the pace so everyone could join without any rush or squeeze. Willa nodded and answered, "I like slow rolls best of all." A faint tinkling sound of a cat's collar bell floated from somewhere down the ferry aisle, light and cheerful like a tiny hello that made Delia smile wider. Delia felt the solid rail at their back and the open goal behind both of them, and together they made a shared spot that felt just right for two. The deck's gentle rock made their knees sway in easy time with the water below.

Neighbor Sami watched without stepping in, only smiling as Delia arranged everything with quiet care. Delia smoothed one wrinkle out of the cloth napkin that had fluttered near their feet, pressing the fabric flat with careful fingers that felt the soft weave under the tips. "We can take turns rolling and catching together," Delia told Willa, making the rules fit the small space so Willa had a real place right there beside them instead of waiting. The netting brushed softly against Willa's sleeve when they both leaned in a little, and the temperature of the air stayed kind and even around them on the open deck.

Willa picked up the soft ball and felt its fuzzy cover under their hands, then rolled it gently toward the open net while Delia stood ready right next to them so the goal never felt empty. "Your turn next after this one," Willa said with a quiet laugh that matched the easy sway of the ferry. Delia caught the ball against their middle and the contact felt light and friendly like a shared secret. Beyond the rail the water made soft slapping sounds that kept time with their game, and everything stayed peaceful under the fading golden light that touched Delia's hair.

They kept the game slow and close, Delia changing the seating by sitting down cross-legged right in front of the goal so Willa could sit too, knees almost touching in the two elbows of room left by the rail. "This way we both guard and both roll," Delia said, and Willa settled in with a happy nod as the soft ball passed between them once more. Neighbor Sami nodded once from the bench without saying more, letting the children keep their own calm pace. The wooden deck felt smooth and solid beneath Delia as they sat, and a cool shadow from the upper cabin slipped over their hands for a moment before the friendly light returned to warm them again.

Later the ferry docked with a gentle bump and everyone walked home under the first evening stars, the soft ball tucked safely away and the practice goal folded for another day. Delia climbed into bed still thinking of Willa beside them at the rail and the easy turns they had shared without anyone left out. The room held a quiet hush broken only by the distant hush of night wind, and Delia pulled the blanket up to their chin where it felt warm and familiar against their skin. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
