Cousin Pio crouched already in the schoolyard, mid-task, running a soft cloth along the wooden ferry rail that had been set near the circle of stones so two sets of elbows could rest comfortably side by side. The wood felt cool and smooth under the cloth as Cousin Pio worked in the mild afternoon light that dappled the ground. Quinn walked closer and watched the careful polishing, noticing how the low swing hung still a few steps away with its seat just the right height. Farah stood a short distance off, near enough to see everything yet outside the open space where play usually gathered. Quinn felt the gentle warmth of the air on their bare arms and stayed close to Cousin Pio without speaking yet.

Quinn saw that Farah kept near the ferry rail but did not step fully into the circle of stones or reach for the low swing. A light breeze drifted past and carried the clean smell of cedar from the boards of the rail itself. Quinn rested a hand on the wood and thought about the empty stretch beside it. Cousin Pio kept polishing in steady strokes and did not direct anyone, simply letting the children notice one another. The stones formed a neat round shape that felt firm when Quinn tapped one with the toe of a shoe, and the whole schoolyard seemed quiet and ready.

“Pio, may we lean on the rail today?” Quinn asked in a calm voice. The wind in the fig leaves made a soft steady rustle high above the schoolyard. Cousin Pio looked up once, nodded, and answered, “You children can decide the space together.” Quinn turned toward Farah while the low swing gave one quiet creak in the same breeze. Soft patches of sunlight shifted across the stones and made the rail look welcoming. Quinn took a small step closer to the open stretch of wood.

Quinn placed one elbow on the ferry rail and left clear room beside it. “Farah, there is space here for two sets of elbows if you would like to lean with me,” Quinn said gently, without any loud call. Farah moved forward and the packed earth felt solid and warm under their shoes. Quinn shifted a little farther along so the place felt open rather than crowded. A straw mat lay folded on the grass nearby, its woven surface dry and even, ready if anyone chose to sit later. Cousin Pio finished the last wipe of the cloth and sat quietly on a low bench, watching without stepping in.

Farah set an elbow on the rail right next to Quinn’s and the wood held a pleasant leftover warmth from the sun. “This feels steady and nice,” Farah said. Quinn nodded and they both gazed at the circle of stones and the waiting low swing. Cousin Pio remained on the bench with hands folded, letting the children arrange the moment themselves. Quinn spoke again in the same easy tone. “We can take turns on the swing after we count slowly to ten, so the pace stays gentle for everyone.” The air around them stayed mild and comfortable.

Quinn and Farah leaned together along the ferry rail that had room for two sets of elbows, their arms resting easily while they looked at the smooth stones. Quinn kept the new slower pace by counting softly under breath before anyone moved toward the swing. A thin cloud passed and the air cooled by a single degree, pleasant against bare skin. Farah’s sleeve brushed Quinn’s arm with a light cotton touch that felt friendly. Cousin Pio smiled from the bench and stayed silent, trusting the children to keep the space kind. The low swing waited without motion, its ropes hanging straight.

After several quiet minutes Quinn unfolded the straw mat and spread it carefully beside the rail so feet could rest between turns. “We can sit here while we wait,” Quinn told Farah. Light slipped through the leaves in gentle moving patterns across the schoolyard and over the stones. Farah sat first and Quinn settled beside them, elbows still near the sturdy rail. They spoke in low voices about the round shapes of the stones and how the swing seat looked soft. Cousin Pio watched the calm order of it all and did not add any extra words.

Cousin Pio later carried a small cup of cool water and set it on one of the flat stones. Quinn turned the cup so the handle faced the open part of the schoolyard, making it simple for either of them to reach. The water felt fresh and cool when Quinn offered it first to Farah. “Thank you for the room on the rail,” Farah whispered. They sipped in turn and watched the low swing sway the smallest bit. The ferry rail stayed firm beside their elbows, holding the shared place without hurry, and the whole schoolyard felt peaceful under the softening light.

As the afternoon eased toward evening Quinn and Farah walked with Cousin Pio toward the low building at the edge of the schoolyard. Inside, cushions waited in a quiet corner and the children sat close, still thinking of the circle of stones and the steady rail. Quinn felt glad that Farah had leaned right beside them the whole time. The lantern by the window glowed softly. A mild indoor warmth filled the room while Quinn rested against a familiar cushion and listened to the slow quiet breathing of friends nearby.
