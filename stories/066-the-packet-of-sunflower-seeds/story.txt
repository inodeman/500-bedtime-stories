The warm evening air brushed the courtyard walls gently like a quiet breath, and pale gold light spilled through the fig tree leaves onto the long supper table. Quinn stepped across the smooth stones and felt their coolness under bare toes while a soft breeze stirred the edges of the tablecloth. Grandpa Sol had already set out plates and bowls, and the air held the faint sweetness of ripe figs hanging just overhead. Quinn paused beside a chair and watched the light shift across the wood grain. A cat padded past with a tiny ring of sound from its collar bell, then settled under a bench. Quinn smiled at the calm space and pulled their chair in close, ready for the meal that waited in the fading daylight.

Quinn helped straighten the napkins while Grandpa Sol carried a basket of bread from the kitchen doorway. The bread smelled fresh and warm, and Quinn noticed a folded packet of sunflower seeds resting near the butter dish, half still folded shut as if waiting for someone to open it fully. Jaleh arrived then with a small cloth bag over one shoulder and waved hello across the long table. “I brought extra spoons in case we need them,” Jaleh said, setting the bag down carefully. Quinn reached for the spoons and began to line them up by size from longest to shortest along the edge of a clean plate. The metal felt cool and smooth under their fingers. Soft shadows from the fig leaves danced slowly across the table while everyone settled into their seats.

Grandpa Sol poured cool water into the glasses and the sound of it filling each one made a gentle trickle. Quinn picked up a thick sweater that had been left on the back of a chair and breathed in the clean smell of soapy wool that clung to its soft folds. They folded the sweater neatly and set it aside so no one would sit on it by mistake. Jaleh passed the bread basket and Quinn took a small piece, feeling the crust crackle lightly. The packet of sunflower seeds still sat half folded shut in the middle of the table, its paper edges catching the last bits of sunlight. Quinn looked at it and thought about how the seeds inside might feel if only one person received them first while others waited.

Quinn lifted the packet gently and felt its light weight in both hands. Inside the half open fold they could see the striped seeds resting together like tiny boats. “These seeds look ready to share,” Quinn said softly, glancing toward Jaleh and then toward Grandpa Sol. The evening air cooled a little more as the sun slipped lower, and a faint hush settled over the courtyard. Grandpa Sol nodded once but said nothing yet, simply watching with kind eyes. Quinn opened the fold the rest of the way and poured a few seeds into a small empty dish, then divided them carefully into three even little piles so that no one pile looked bigger than the others. The paper packet crinkled quietly under their careful fingers.

Jaleh watched the small piles form and smiled across the table. “That way everyone can try some together,” Jaleh said, voice warm and clear. Quinn felt glad that the choice felt right and passed the first little pile toward Jaleh’s plate. The fig tree leaves rustled once overhead in a mild breath of air, and the long table seemed to hold the three of them in a quiet circle of light. Grandpa Sol accepted his own small share without adding any words of his own, simply letting the even piles remain just as Quinn had made them. Quinn tasted one seed and noticed its mild nutty flavor while the cool stones under the table kept their feet comfortable.

After the seeds came the rest of the supper, simple bowls of soft vegetables and cheese that Grandpa Sol had prepared earlier. Quinn tore a piece of bread and dipped it carefully, listening to the quiet clink of spoons against bowls. The leather lace on the handle of Jaleh’s cloth bag brushed against Quinn’s wrist when they reached for the water pitcher, and the smooth worn texture felt familiar and steady. Light from a nearby window mixed with the outdoor glow and made the edges of the plates shine faintly. Quinn spoke again while passing the pitcher. “I like when the shares match,” they said, and Jaleh answered, “Me too, it feels friendly.” The three of them ate slowly while the courtyard stayed peaceful around them.

As the bowls emptied, Quinn helped gather the used spoons and lined them once more by size beside the empty bread basket. The air had grown cooler still, and a thin shawl of dusk settled over the fig tree branches. Grandpa Sol rose to light a small lantern near the house wall, and the soft flame threw a round pool of brightness onto the stones. Quinn carried their empty plate to the side table and felt the wood cool beneath their palms. Jaleh hummed a short quiet tune while folding the napkins into neat squares. No one hurried, and the courtyard held only the gentle sounds of dishes being stacked and the distant evening birds settling for the night.

Quinn sat back down for a moment longer and looked at the empty seed packet now lying flat and open. The half folded shut part had opened completely under their earlier care, and the table looked balanced with every place equal. “Thank you for letting me divide them,” Quinn said to Grandpa Sol, who simply smiled and rested a hand briefly on the back of Quinn’s chair. The temperature of the air felt just right against bare arms, neither warm nor chilly. Jaleh pointed toward the fig tree where a few leaves still caught the last silver light. Everything about the supper felt settled and kind, and Quinn breathed in the quiet happiness of the shared evening under the spreading branches.

Later when the plates were rinsed and the long table cleared, Quinn walked with Jaleh and Grandpa Sol toward the doorway that led inside. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Quinn paused to look back once at the courtyard where the fig tree stood guard over the quiet stones and the empty chairs. A final cool drift of air touched their cheek, and the whole place seemed ready for rest. They stepped inside together, carrying the calm of the even shares and the gentle supper with them into the softer lights of home.
