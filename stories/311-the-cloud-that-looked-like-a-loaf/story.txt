Mama was already kneeling on the garden path after a gentle rain, her hands patting the damp earth firmly around the stems of the young herbs so they would stand tall again. Brisa walked out from the house and felt the cool evening air settle gently on their bare arms like a soft scarf. Little silver puddles dotted the stones and caught the last warm light of day. “Mama, may I help too?” Brisa asked in a quiet voice. Mama nodded and kept smoothing the soil while Brisa crouched nearby, listening to the slow drip of leftover rain falling from the eaves onto the path below.

Brisa lifted their eyes and noticed a low cloud hanging above the fig tree that looked exactly like a round loaf of bread, plump and golden along its edges. A real wish rose in Brisa to stretch up and pocket the cloud or poke it lightly just to feel if it was soft. The air carried the clean smell of cedar from the freshly trimmed fence boards. “Esme, hurry and see,” Brisa called softly when their friend appeared at the garden gate. Esme came along the path with careful steps and stood shoulder to shoulder with Brisa, both of them gazing upward under the open sky that still held a pale blue glow.

Esme’s eyes grew wide at the loaf-shaped cloud and she lifted one hand toward it. Brisa gently guided Esme’s hand back down and spoke in a calm tone. “We can look without disturbing it,” Brisa said. The two friends stayed very still together while a mild breeze brushed past their cheeks. Brisa felt glad that Esme understood at once and copied the quiet watching. Soft light filtered through the cloud’s edges and made them look creamy and thick, almost good enough to taste if clouds could be bread.

Mama finished pressing the last bit of earth and rose to join Brisa and Esme on the path. She tipped her head and studied the same floating shape. “It truly does look like a fresh loaf,” Mama said with a warm smile. Brisa leaned lightly against Mama’s side and felt the cozy knit of her sweater. All three stood without reaching or shouting, simply sharing the view. Beneath their feet the garden stones remained cool and slick from the rain, and small reflected pieces of sky shimmered in every shallow puddle.

While they watched, the cloud began to stretch and thin. The neat loaf shape slowly melted into a longer curl and then into a soft free form that no longer resembled bread at all. Brisa felt a tiny pull of wanting to keep the first shape forever, yet they stayed quiet and still beside their friend. Esme whispered, “It is changing right before us.” Brisa nodded and kept their gaze steady on the drifting white. High above them the wind in the fig leaves made a gentle continuous rustle that sounded like quiet pages turning.

Brisa showed Esme how to shape their hands into soft circles around their eyes so they could look even more carefully. “Just hold your hands like this and keep them low,” Brisa explained. Esme followed the motion with slow care and both children smiled. When Brisa stepped back a little for a wider view their bare toes brushed the edge of a straw mat that Mama had left near the bench; the dry woven surface felt scratchy and interesting against their skin. Mama watched the two of them with kind eyes while the mild air of evening rested easily on everyone’s shoulders.

They remained a few peaceful moments longer on the garden path as the cloud rose higher and became only an ordinary soft puff among the others. Brisa no longer wished to pocket it or poke it because simply looking together felt complete and enough. “I am glad we left it right where it is,” Brisa told Mama and Esme. Mama rested a gentle hand on Brisa’s shoulder and Esme gave a small happy nod. Nearby a single leftover drop fell from a leaf with a tiny clear plink, and the whole garden seemed hushed and clean under the deepening light.

Before long Mama suggested they walk inside for a warm drink before bedtime. Brisa and Esme followed her along the path toward the open kitchen door. Inside the rooms glowed with low golden light from the lamps. Brisa carefully turned the cup so the handle faced the room before pouring warm milk for each of them. They sat close at the wooden table and spoke softly about the cloud that had looked like a loaf and then not. Esme smiled across the table and said she would carry the picture of it home with her. Brisa felt peaceful and ready for rest as the house grew quiet around them.

Later Brisa climbed into bed while Mama tucked the blanket snug and smooth. The bedroom air felt pleasantly cool against their face. Esme had already waved goodbye at the door and gone home. Brisa closed their eyes and once more saw the shining garden path and the changing cloud. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Mama kissed Brisa’s forehead and stayed a moment longer so the gentle light could fill every corner with calm before sleep arrived.
