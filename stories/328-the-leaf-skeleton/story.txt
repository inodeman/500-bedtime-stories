Frost touched the bedroom window with a quiet white lace that glittered when the early light found it, and the glass felt cool as a smooth stone under Noemi’s palm. Noemi stood close enough to fog a small circle with their breath, watching the pale sparkle settle on the sill like sugar that would not melt. The air in the room held a soft chill near the glass, yet farther back the blankets still kept a gentle warmth. Noemi pressed their nose nearer and smelled the clean cold, then drew back and wiped the fog with one careful finger so the view stayed clear. Outside, thin frost painted every twig, and inside, the quiet felt round and safe. Noemi’s bare toes curled against the rug, and they stayed still, listening to the house wake in tiny sounds.

On the wide sill itself rested a leaf skeleton as fine as a map, its veins spread like tiny pale roads across a paper-thin shape that the night frost must have left behind. Noemi leaned until their eyes were level with it, seeing how the light shone through every open space between the veins, making a soft gold pattern on the wood. The leaf looked lighter than a feather and more delicate than lace, yet it held its shape perfectly, as if drawn by a careful hand. Noemi’s fingers hovered in the air, wanting very much to pocket the treasure or to poke one tiny hole just to see if it would flutter. The wish sat warm in their chest, bright and curious. Still Noemi kept their hands tucked against their own sweater, studying the leaf the way one studies a sleeping bird.

Priya’s soft steps crossed the floorboards, and soon Priya stood shoulder to shoulder with Noemi at the same frosty window, both of them breathing quiet clouds. “Look how the light makes little windows inside it,” Noemi whispered, pointing with a steady finger that never touched. Priya’s eyes widened, and they nodded without reaching either. “It looks like a map of a secret garden,” Priya said in a hush that matched the morning. Together they watched a thin shadow slide the width of a thumb across the sill as the sun climbed a little higher, and the leaf skeleton stayed exactly where it was, safe and whole. The cool air near the glass brushed their cheeks while the rest of the room stayed kindly warm.

Auntie June appeared in the doorway with a folded blanket over one arm, the lavender scent of her morning tea still clinging gently to her sleeves as she walked nearer. She moved slowly so the floor would not creak too loud, and she joined them at the window without asking questions at first. Noemi turned and said, “Auntie June, we are looking only with our eyes so the leaf can stay perfect.” Auntie June smiled the soft smile that made her eyes crinkle, and she bent until her face was level with theirs. “That is a fine way to visit a fragile thing,” she answered, her voice low and even. The three of them formed a quiet half-circle, shoulders almost touching, all gazing at the same pale map of veins.

Noemi explained carefully how the frost must have thinned the leaf during the night and left only the strongest lines behind, and Priya added that the pattern reminded them of rivers drawn on paper. Auntie June rested one hand on the quilt folded at the foot of the bed, feeling a single raised quilt stitch under her thumb while she listened. The tin clock on the shelf ticked a steady gentle note that filled the pauses between their words. Noemi’s voice stayed soft as they showed Priya how to tip their head left and right so the light changed inside the leaf skeleton without anyone moving it. “If we leave it here, the sun can keep drawing on it all morning,” Noemi said, and Priya agreed with a quiet yes.

They stayed at the window long enough for the frost lace outside to glitter brighter, and the leaf skeleton caught every new sparkle between its open veins. Noemi felt the cool glass once more with the back of their hand, then pulled away so the warmth of the room could return to their skin. Priya described the way one long vein ran like a road from tip to stem, and Auntie June pointed out a smaller branch that looked like a path to a tiny house. No one reached. No one poked. The wish to pocket the leaf still flickered once in Noemi’s mind, yet they chose again to keep both hands busy only with pointing and with holding the edge of their own sleeve. The air smelled clean and cold near the sill, and farther back it smelled only of ordinary morning.

Auntie June settled the folded blanket across the chair so it would be ready later, and the three of them returned their full attention to the sill. Noemi noticed how the leaf skeleton cast the softest lace of shadow onto the wood, a shadow so light it seemed made of breath. Priya leaned just close enough to feel the chill rising from the glass, then stepped back half a step so their sweater would not brush the treasure. “We can come look again after breakfast,” Noemi told them both, and the words felt like a promise that kept the leaf safe. Auntie June rested a calm hand on Noemi’s shoulder, warm through the knit, and the quiet filled every corner of the room like kind water.

Outside, the frost held steady under the rising light, and inside the bedroom the quiet grew even softer. Noemi, Priya, and Auntie June watched the leaf skeleton one last long moment, letting their eyes travel every pale road and every open space. The wish to touch had become a different kind of wanting—the wanting to remember exactly how it looked while it rested free. Noemi breathed out slowly so the fog would not hide the view, and Priya copied the same careful breath. Auntie June’s presence made the whole scene feel held, as if the morning itself had decided to stay gentle. The leaf remained on the sill, fine as any map, untouched and shining.

They left the leaf skeleton exactly where the frost and the night had placed it, and the room settled around that small choice like a blanket pulled smooth. Noemi climbed back under the covers for one more quiet minute, feeling the familiar weight and the cool pillowcase against their cheek, while Priya sat on the rug and Auntie June dimmed the brighter lamp. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Its mild gold light reached the sill without waking the leaf, and the three of them listened to the house breathe while the frosty morning kept its gentle hold on the glass and the treasure stayed free for any later looking that would still be only looking.
