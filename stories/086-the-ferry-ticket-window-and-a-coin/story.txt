The small coin lay bright in Ilaria's cupped hands, its silver surface smooth and cool where their thumbs rubbed the raised edge over and over. Late sunlight poured across the metal and made a tiny star dance on the boat shape pressed into its center. Ilaria had kept the coin safe all afternoon after the ferry ride because it was exactly enough for a second small fare, enough for one more gentle trip across the quiet water someday. They felt the solid weight settle into their palm like a hidden promise they could choose to keep or share. Warm evening air brushed their wrists while they studied every faint scratch and turned the coin so it flashed again.

In the courtyard a long wooden table stretched under the broad fig tree, its leaves hanging low and deep green in the soft dusk. Grandpa Sol had set out plates that still held a faint warmth from the kitchen stove, and the air carried the gentle scent of baked bread mixed with ripe fruit. Kesi sat already on the bench with bare feet swinging slowly, waiting for supper to begin under the leafy roof. Ilaria walked closer across the smooth stones, the coin tucked carefully into their pocket so it would not jingle. Soft golden light filtered through the fig leaves and painted gentle shifting shapes across the white tablecloth and the waiting bowls.

"Grandpa Sol, the ferry brought us right to your gate this afternoon," Ilaria said, their voice quiet and glad as they climbed onto the bench. Grandpa Sol smiled and nodded while he placed a large bowl of warm stew carefully in the middle of the long table. Kesi reached for a napkin that felt soft and slightly damp against their fingers from the evening air. The three of them settled along the wooden benches while a light breeze moved the hanging leaves and made them whisper. Ilaria could hear the distant soft slap of harbor water far beyond the courtyard walls as the first stars waited above.

As the first spoonfuls of stew cooled on their tongues, Ilaria thought again of the coin that covered a second small ferry fare waiting in their pocket. It seemed odd that something meant for two journeys should sit unused while supper was shared among three. The stew tasted rich and earthy, and steam rose in thin curls that brushed their cheeks with gentle heat before drifting away. Grandpa Sol passed crusty bread that cracked softly under their hands, and the table felt friendly and steady under the fading peach-colored sky. Ilaria watched how the portions landed first near one plate and then another and began to wonder about fairness.

Ilaria realized that if the coin bought only one extra treat for the first person served, it would not feel fair to leave Kesi waiting with an empty space. They pulled the coin out again and set it on the table near the bread basket where everyone could see the little boat stamped on its face. "This coin can cover a second small ferry fare, but tonight we are three at the table," Ilaria said softly while the metal made a tiny clear click against the wood. Kesi looked over with curious bright eyes while the fig tree rustled above them in the mild moving air and the light grew gentler still.

Grandpa Sol watched without speaking and simply waited with calm hands resting on the table edge. Ilaria decided to make a smaller even share so everyone could join the same sweet moment. They asked for three little bowls and carefully divided a sweet fig pudding that Grandpa Sol had brought warm from the kitchen, using the idea of the coin's two-fare value but splitting everything into three gentle equal parts. "Kesi, this part is for you too," Ilaria said as they slid the third bowl across. The pudding smelled sweet and sunny, the spoons clinked lightly together, and warmth from the bowls rose pleasantly to their faces in the cooling dusk.

A leather lace hung from the corner of Ilaria's small satchel where it rested on the bench, and they brushed the rough-smooth strip with one finger while the bowls were passed from hand to hand. Kesi took their share with both hands and grinned across the table. "Thank you, Ilaria. It tastes like sunshine and figs," Kesi answered in a happy whisper. The three of them ate the smaller portions slowly and with care, and the courtyard stayed peaceful under the wide leaves. Grandpa Sol let the choice stand without any speech, only a quiet nod that felt like warm approval resting over them all.

After the pudding bowls were empty and wiped clean, Ilaria lined the spoons up by size along the edge of the table, smallest to largest, so they shone in a neat silver row that caught the last light. Somewhere near the low wall a cat's collar bell gave one clear tiny ring as the cat walked past on quiet paws. The air cooled a little more against their bare arms and made them draw closer on the bench. Grandpa Sol poured water into cups that felt cool and smooth in their hands. Ilaria tucked the coin back into their pocket for another day, glad that the evening had made room for everyone at once.

The smell of soapy wool drifted from the knitted blanket Grandpa Sol draped over the bench backs when the air grew softer and kinder. Ilaria leaned against the clean folds and felt the whole evening settle around their shoulders like a hug. Fireflies began to blink near the lower branches of the fig tree in tiny yellow sparks. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Kesi hummed a little wordless tune while the last crumbs were brushed away into a neat pile, and Ilaria closed their eyes for a long quiet moment before bed, the coin warm again against their side and the courtyard calm under the darkening friendly sky.
