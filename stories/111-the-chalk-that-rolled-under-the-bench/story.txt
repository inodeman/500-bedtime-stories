Cousin Pio was already on his knees on the cottage porch when Brisa came through the open door, the late sun laying a soft gold stripe across the boards. Pio had one shoulder pressed low and was sweeping his hand back and forth under the long wooden bench, hunting for something that had slipped away. Brisa felt the mild evening air brush their arms and heard the quiet scrape of Pio’s sleeve. “The box of chalk rolled clean under the bench,” Pio said, sounding a little breathless, “and I can’t quite catch the corner.” Brisa stepped closer and saw the pale cardboard edge far back in the dim space. They rested one palm on the warm bench top and watched their cousin reach again.

Brisa crouched beside Pio and studied the narrow gap. The porch boards felt smooth and slightly dusty under their bare knees. They noticed how the bench legs sat close together, making the stretch awkward for one pair of arms alone. Without a word Brisa scooted to the far end of the bench and gently pushed the opposite leg a finger’s width outward so the opening grew. “There,” Brisa said softly, “now you can slide farther.” Pio’s fingers brushed the box at last. The two of them worked side by side in the gentle light, neither rushing, while a faint breeze moved across the porch and cooled the backs of their necks.

Pio tugged, yet the box still snagged on a crosspiece. Brisa lay flat on their stomach and stretched their own arm under from the other side until their fingertips met Pio’s. Together they guided the box of chalk forward an inch at a time. Brisa smelled the clean cedar of the porch posts as they worked, a dry sweet scent that mixed with the outdoor air. “We’ve almost got it,” Pio whispered. Brisa only nodded and kept their hand steady, feeling the cardboard edge grow closer until the whole box slid free into the open.

They sat up together on the porch, the box resting between them. Brisa brushed a bit of dust from the lid and felt the fine grit against their palm. The chalk pieces inside made a soft rattle when Pio tilted the box. Brisa stood and carried it carefully through the doorway into the hall just inside, where the light turned cooler and dimmer. Pio followed. On the floor of the hall lay a straw mat that felt dry and slightly prickly under Brisa’s bare feet as they stepped across it. They set the box on a low shelf so it would not roll again.

In the quiet hall Brisa noticed a small cup of water left on the windowsill from earlier drawing. They turned the cup so the handle faced the room, then looked back at Pio. “We can keep the chalk here until morning,” Brisa said. Pio smiled and agreed. Outside, the sound of wind in the fig leaves rose and fell like a slow breath, drifting through the open door and making the hall curtains sway just a little. Brisa listened while they smoothed the straw mat flat with one careful hand.

Pio opened the box and chose a single stick of blue chalk. Brisa chose yellow. They returned to the porch edge and sat on the top step where the last sunlight still warmed the wood. Brisa drew a simple sun and a path of dots while Pio added a small house beside it. The chalk felt dry and powdery between Brisa’s fingers. Neither of them spoke for a long stretch; they only listened to the quiet scratch of chalk on stone and the soft evening air moving past their ears. When the drawings were finished Brisa blew the loose dust away and watched it sparkle briefly in the light.

They carried the chalk box back inside and placed it once more on the shelf. Brisa ran one fingertip along the cool plaster wall of the hall and felt how smooth it was after the rough chalk. Pio closed the lid gently. “Thank you for helping me reach it,” Pio said. Brisa only gave a small nod and padded farther into the hall, where a lamp already waited. The temperature indoors felt a degree cooler than the porch, pleasant against their skin. They straightened a folded blanket on the bench just inside the door so everything looked ready for night.

Brisa and Pio sat together on that indoor bench for a few calm minutes, swinging their feet slowly. Brisa leaned their shoulder lightly against Pio’s and listened to the distant hush of the leaves outside. The wood of the bench held a faint leftover warmth from the day. They talked in low voices about the colors they might use tomorrow and about the shapes they had made. No one asked for thanks or praise; the work had simply been shared. Brisa felt the quiet settle around them like a soft shawl while the last of the porch light faded through the doorway.

Later Brisa rose and walked to the window in the hall, checking that the cup still faced the proper way. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Brisa stood for a moment in that gentle circle of light, feeling the smooth floor under their feet and hearing only the faintest evening sounds. Pio joined them and they both looked out at the darkening porch where the chalk drawings waited safely until morning. Brisa breathed in the quiet air of the cottage and rested one hand on the cool windowsill, ready for sleep.
