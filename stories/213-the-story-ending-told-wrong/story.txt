The warm smell of toasted sesame drifted from a small open bag near the stack of picture books as Lior’s busy hands smoothed the wrinkled sheet of paper that held the story ending they had changed to sound braver. Soft afternoon light poured through the round window and made golden circles on the library table where Lior sat alone for the quiet hour. The paper felt slightly rough under their fingertips while they read the new brave lines once more, and the cool air from the high shelves brushed their cheeks. Lior kept sliding the pencil eraser back and forth, erasing a smudge and rewriting a word, completely tangled in the feeling that the changed ending no longer matched the gentle beginning of the tale. Outside the glass the garden looked peaceful, yet Lior’s shoulders stayed hunched over the page.

Lior reread how the little fox in the story now leaped across a wide stream without any help and shouted cheerful words instead of waiting quietly for a friend, and the bright window light warmed the side of their face while they worked. The paper crackled softly each time they folded a corner, and the wooden table felt smooth and solid beneath their elbows. They had wanted the ending to sound braver so the fox would seem strong, yet the new sentences sat oddly beside the soft pictures already printed in the open book. A faint chill rose from the stone floor and made Lior tuck their feet onto the chair rung. They almost slipped the changed page under the heavy atlas that rested nearby.

Just then the clear click of a gate latch sounded from the garden path beyond the round window, and Lior froze with the paper half-hidden under one palm. Warm light still painted the tabletop in slow arcs while dust motes drifted like tiny lanterns in the air. Lior’s heart beat a little faster, not from fear but from the muddled wish to keep the braver ending secret for a moment longer. They straightened the paper again, then paused, listening to the quiet library hush that followed the latch sound. The round window showed Miss Dalca walking up the path with a gentle smile.

Miss Dalca stepped inside the reading room wearing her soft cotton apron that brushed lightly against the chair backs as she moved, and the fabric felt cool and familiar when Lior’s arm brushed it in greeting. “Hello, Lior,” Miss Dalca said in a calm voice that matched the steady glow of the window light. Lior’s fingers stayed curled around the edge of the changed story ending while the mild library air carried the last trace of sesame. Miss Dalca set a fresh stack of books on the far corner of the table and waited without hurry. Lior looked at the round glass, then at the paper, feeling the tangle loosen just a little.

Lior drew a slow breath of the cool room air and said, “Miss Dalca, I changed the ending of this story so the fox would sound braver, and I almost hid the paper under the atlas.” The words came out plain and clear while afternoon light rested warmly on both their hands. Miss Dalca nodded once and answered, “Thank you for telling me the plain truth, Lior. We can look at it together.” Lior slid the sheet into the open space between them so the window glow fell across every line. No teasing came, only the quiet scrape of a chair leg as Miss Dalca sat down nearby.

“I wanted the fox to leap alone and shout,” Lior explained, tapping the brave new sentences with one careful finger while the table surface stayed smooth and cool under their wrist. Miss Dalca smiled and said, “The first ending already held a quiet kind of courage when the fox waited and then walked home with its friend.” Together they smoothed the paper flat again, and Lior felt the soft warmth of the sunlight shift across their sleeves. Femi, who had been reading at the next table, wandered over with quiet steps and peered at the page without speaking yet. The three of them listened to the peaceful turning of distant pages in the tall stacks.

Miss Dalca took a clean sheet from her pocket and said, “Shall we mend what can be mended by writing the true ending once more beside the pictures?” Lior nodded and answered, “Yes, please, I want the fox to wait for its friend the way the pictures show.” The pencil moved slowly across the fresh paper while cool air drifted from the open window and brushed Lior’s hair. Femi handed over a green crayon so they could add a small bridge the fox and friend could cross side by side. The cotton apron brushed the table edge again as Miss Dalca leaned in to help spell a gentle word.

Lior set the chair square to the table so everything lined up neatly, then copied the mended ending in careful letters while the round window light grew softer and golden on the fresh page. The paper felt smooth now under the pencil point, and the library air carried a mild coolness that kept everyone comfortable. Femi whispered, “The fox looks happy walking home,” and Lior smiled at the quiet sound of the crayon finishing a green leaf. Miss Dalca rested one hand lightly on the finished sheet and everything felt settled and right. Outside, the garden stayed still and kind.

When the last word was written, Lior placed both papers side by side under the gentle window light and felt the calm quiet settle around the library table. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Lior tucked the mended ending into the storybook, closed the cover with a soft thump, and rested both hands on the warm wooden surface while the room filled with the peaceful hush of turning pages and far-away footsteps.
