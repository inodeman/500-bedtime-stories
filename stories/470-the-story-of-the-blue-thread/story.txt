A gentle tick came from the far corner of the room, soft as a fingertip on wood, and Ines noticed it right away while they sat cross-legged on the quilt. The lamp by the bed cast a honey-colored light that made the walls look friendly and warm. Cool evening air drifted through the open window and brushed Ines’s cheeks. Auntie Luz smiled from the wooden bench at the foot of the bed, her hands folded loosely in her lap. Ines could hear their own quiet breathing mix with that far tick. “Are you ready for a story, Ines?” Auntie Luz asked in a low voice. Ines nodded but kept the bigger news tucked inside for now, feeling the quilt’s smooth top under their palms and the steady warmth of the pillow behind them.

Auntie Luz began slowly, her words calm as the room. The faint smell of tomato vines floated in from the garden outside, green and fresh, reminding Ines of summer leaves even at lamp time. Ines shifted and felt the cool sheet against their legs. “This is the story of where the blue thread came from,” Auntie Luz said. Ines wanted to jump in with news about the quiet reading prize, but they pressed their lips together and listened. The lamp hummed a tiny steady note. Soft shadows rested on the rug like friendly shapes. Ines watched Auntie Luz’s kind face in the gentle glow and decided to wait all the way through, breathing the evening air and holding still.

“Long ago,” Auntie Luz continued, “a little blue thread waited on a high shelf in a quiet shop.” Ines imagined the shop while staying still in the real bedroom where the air felt clean and cool. The bench under Auntie Luz had a smooth grain that her fingers traced while she spoke, and Ines noticed the warm light catching on those fingers. Ines’s toes curled into the mattress’s springy give. They wanted so much to tell about the surprise at school, yet they kept listening to every word about the blue thread. A moth tapped lightly once against the screen and then was still, and Ines stayed quiet beside the soft lamp glow.

“The shopkeeper sold that blue thread to a woman who loved to sew coats for cold mornings,” Auntie Luz said. Ines felt the pillow’s cool case against their cheek as they leaned sideways to hear better. Outside a soft breeze moved the curtains with a whispery hush that sounded like far-away leaves. Ines pictured the blue thread wound neat on its spool, bright as a piece of sky on a clear day. Their own bigger news pressed at their tongue, but Ines breathed in and out and let Auntie Luz keep going. The bedroom stayed peaceful, filled with the low steady light that painted gold edges on the books. Ines’s hand rested on the quilt’s bumpy stitches and stayed patient.

“That woman gave a length of the blue thread to her neighbor, who used it to mend a favorite scarf,” Auntie Luz went on. Ines listened to the rise and fall of the familiar voice and felt safe in the warm circle of lamp light. Their pajamas felt soft and slightly fuzzy against their skin. Somewhere down the hall a clock ticked, and Ines tried to listen for the far clock and not the near one, just to keep their mind steady and calm. The blue thread traveled from scarf to a child’s mitten in the telling, always gentle and useful. Ines almost spoke then, but held the words back and watched the shadows stay quiet on the ceiling while the air remained pleasantly cool.

“One day the blue thread found its way into a sewing box that belonged to a girl named Nia,” Auntie Luz said with a smile toward Ines. The room felt cozy, the temperature just right under the covers that draped Ines’s shoulders. Ines heard a tiny jingle then, the sound of a cat’s collar bell as the household cat walked past the doorway and settled somewhere soft on the hall rug. Ines’s eyes followed the sound for a second before returning to Auntie Luz. They still kept their own news quiet, letting the story of the blue thread unwind completely. The lamp light made Auntie Luz’s hair shine silver at the edges, and Ines breathed the calm air and waited with open ears.

“Nia used the blue thread to stitch a tiny star onto her pillowcase, and that is how it came to rest in a happy home,” Auntie Luz finished gently. “And that is the last of the story of where the blue thread came from.” The words settled into the quiet bedroom like soft feathers drifting down. Ines felt the mattress hold them steady and warm through the blankets. A pale band of light from the hall edged under the door and lay quiet on the floor. Ines had listened all the way to that last sentence without interrupting once, though the bigger news still waited inside. Their fingers smoothed a fold in the blanket, feeling its woven ridges and the gentle heat trapped underneath.

Ines sat up a little against the pillows and asked one real question. “Auntie Luz, did Nia keep the leftover blue thread safe in a special place?” The question floated out clear and curious in the hushed room. Auntie Luz nodded and the wooden bench creaked faintly under her as she leaned forward into the light. Cool air from the window touched Ines’s forehead like a soft hand. “Yes, Ines, she kept it in a small tin with buttons,” Auntie Luz answered kindly. Ines smiled at the answer and felt ready now, hearing the quiet settle again around the bed. The lamp’s glow stayed steady and kind across the floorboards while Ines gathered a small thought.

Then Ines shared something small of their own. “Today I found a smooth pebble in my pocket and it felt nice when I held it,” Ines said softly. Auntie Luz listened with the same care Ines had shown all evening. The covers felt heavy and comforting over Ines’s legs as sleep began to tug at their eyelids. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Outside the night stayed quiet, and Ines closed their eyes while the warm light stayed near, the sheets smelling faintly of clean cotton and the room holding only slow breaths and the far clock’s gentle mark of time.
