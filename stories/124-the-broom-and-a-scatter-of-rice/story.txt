The grains of rice lay scattered like pale seeds across the cool kitchen stones that formed the floor of the long school hallway, each tiny piece catching the mild yellow light from the high windows and glinting softly beside the wooden legs of the stools. Kira bent close and studied how the scatter reached almost to the row of coat pegs and curled around the heavy bases of the music stands waiting quietly for practice. The stones felt smooth and slightly chilly under the brush of Kira’s fingertips as they traced one grain without lifting it yet. A gentle warmth rose from the nearby radiators and mixed with the quiet of the empty corridor. Kira breathed slowly and noticed the way the rice made faint white trails that someone would need to clear before the next morning’s footsteps arrived.

Neighbor Sami stood farther along the hallway with a broom, pushing at the awkward spill yet finding the grains slipped under stools and rolled toward the music stands no matter how carefully the bristles moved. Kira heard the soft swish of straw against stone and saw Sami’s careful steps around the pegs that lined the wall. The air stayed pleasantly mild, neither hot nor cold, while afternoon light striped the floor in long gentle bands. “This scatter of rice across the kitchen stones is awkward for one person,” Neighbor Sami said, pausing to rest the broom. Kira watched the grains tumble again and felt the solid coolness of the floor through their shoes as they took a step closer.

Kira walked forward without hurry and took in the full stretch of the mess between the pegs and the stands. The faint smell of chamomile rose from a forgotten mug perched on one stool, soft and calming like a cup left from earlier tea. Kira lifted a small dustpan that rested near a music stand and spoke in a steady voice. “I can gather the half near the coat pegs while you sweep the middle.” Neighbor Sami nodded once and answered, “That would make the work even and simple.” The tin clock on the wall above the pegs ticked with a clear steady sound that filled the quiet pauses between their movements. Kira began scooping rice from their chosen side, feeling each hard grain click lightly into the pan.

They worked side by side in the calm light that slanted between the music stands, Kira covering the stones closest to the pegs while Neighbor Sami guided the broom toward the center stools. When Kira shifted a small container to clear more space, the texture of its tin lid felt smooth and cool against their palm before they set it neatly aside. “The grains hide well under these stool legs,” Kira said softly while reaching. Neighbor Sami replied, “They do, yet with both of us the pile grows steady.” No praise was sought or given as Kira kept to the useful half of the job, the hallway remaining peaceful except for the faint rustle of rice and broom. Warmth from the sun-soaked stones rose gently against their hands each time they touched the floor.

Kira moved around one tall music stand with care, keeping the dustpan level so no grain escaped, and felt the mild temperature of the air brush their cheeks like a quiet breath. The light grew softer as clouds passed outside yet still lit every corner of the hallway without glare. Neighbor Sami swept a final cluster toward Kira’s pan and said, “We are nearly finished now.” Kira answered, “Yes, the stones look clear again already.” Together they tipped the gathered rice into an open cloth bag that waited on a low stool, the grains whispering as they fell. Kira smoothed the bag closed with slow fingers and glanced along the tidy row of pegs that no longer guarded any stray white pieces.

The last few grains rested near a music stand base, and Kira knelt once more on the kitchen stones that still held a faint coolness in their shaded spots. Neighbor Sami held the broom ready but waited while Kira claimed those final bits for their shared pile. A soft draft moved through the hallway and carried the settled quiet of late afternoon. “Shall we check once more under the stools?” Neighbor Sami asked in a low tone. Kira smiled a little and said, “I already looked, and the floor is smooth.” They stood together for a moment, the bag of rice between them, and the hallway of pegs, stools, and music stands felt open and ready once more. Kira brushed a single speck of dust from their sleeve and felt the calm of work done evenly.

Kira and Neighbor Sami carried the bag to a side shelf near the far pegs, placing it where it would stay dry and neat until someone needed it later. The light had turned the color of pale honey across the kitchen stones, and the music stands cast long gentle shadows that did not trouble anyone. Kira ran one hand along the edge of a stool and felt its worn wooden smoothness leftover from many quiet days. Neighbor Sami set the broom back in its corner and stretched once without hurry. No words of thanks piled up; the shared task itself had been enough. Kira listened to the soft settling sounds of the building and knew the hallway could rest easily now.

Outside the tall windows the sky softened toward evening while inside the air cooled just enough to feel fresh against Kira’s face. They walked the length of the cleared stones once more, passing the stools and the waiting music stands, and noticed how empty of clutter the space had become. Neighbor Sami fastened a loose coat on one peg with a small click of the hanger. Kira matched the unhurried pace and felt the solid quiet settle into their shoulders like a light blanket. Everything in the hallway stood ready for tomorrow without fuss or leftover grains. Kira paused by the windowsill where a small lantern waited, its glass clean and its base steady on the wood.

Kira rested both hands on the sill for a breath and watched the last of the daylight fade beyond the glass while the hallway behind them stayed peaceful and swept. Neighbor Sami gave a quiet nod from near the pegs and turned toward the door with the calm of someone whose work had been shared. The stones no longer held any scatter, and the stools sat evenly spaced once more. Kira felt the mild evening air touch their wrists and knew the quiet task was complete. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
