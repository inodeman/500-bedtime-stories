The beetle shone green as a bottle while it crossed the broad green leaf that lay flat on the cool damp stones of the harbor wall at low tide. Its shell caught the slanted gold light and threw back tiny sparks, and each careful step of its legs made a faint tick against the leaf that sounded almost like a very small tin clock being wound. The leaf felt papery and cool where the breeze lifted its edge, and the air carried a clean green smell of basil from a little pot someone had set nearby on the wall. Femi crouched so near that the beetle’s rounded back filled their whole view, and they watched the creature pause, turn, and keep walking as if the leaf were a wide green road just for it.

Femi stayed very still and felt the cool stone under their bare knees and the smooth weight of a polished pebble they had picked up earlier and now held loosely in one hand. The pebble’s surface was glassy and round from long tumbling in the water, and it warmed slowly against Femi’s palm while they breathed in the basil scent again. Below the wall the water lay quiet and silver in the low tide, and little bubbles popped with soft wet sounds. Femi’s friend Ren knelt beside them, and Femi whispered, “Look how green it is, Ren, just like the bottle we found last week.” Ren nodded without speaking yet, eyes wide at the shining beetle.

The wish to pick the beetle up or give it a gentle poke rose quick and real inside Femi’s chest, but they kept both hands open and quiet around the polished pebble instead. Warm late light poured over the wall and made the beetle’s shell look almost liquid, and the far-off cry of a gull drifted across the harbor. Femi turned the pebble once between their fingers, feeling its perfect smooth coolness, then set it down carefully so both hands were free. “We can look without touching,” Femi said softly to Ren. “It is already busy walking, and the leaf is its path right now.” Ren smiled and breathed out a quiet “Yes,” matching Femi’s still shoulders.

Together Femi and Ren leaned a little closer, never reaching, while the beetle continued its slow journey from one side of the leaf to the other. The stones under them held the day’s leftover warmth even as the air cooled, and the basil smell rose sweeter each time a tiny breeze stirred the pot. Somewhere behind them a real tin clock ticked steadily from the ferry office window, its clear metallic sound carrying over the quiet water. Femi pointed with one finger held far back and said, “See the way the light slides across its back when it moves? It looks like little green stars.” Ren answered, “I see them too. They wink and then they hide again.”

The ferryman came walking along the top of the wall with slow easy steps, his boots making soft thuds on the stone. He stopped a few paces away so the shadow of his hat fell gentle and cool across the leaf without covering the beetle. The air smelled more strongly of basil now that he had brushed past the pot, and the tin clock kept its calm ticking behind them all. Femi looked up and said, “We are watching the green beetle cross the leaf. It is safe where it is.” The ferryman crouched carefully, hands resting on his knees, and answered in a low voice, “That is a fine bright traveler. Best left to finish its walk.”

Femi showed Ren how to cup their hands around their eyes like little binoculars so they could look even closer without leaning too near the leaf. The polished pebble lay forgotten for a moment beside them, still smooth and round. Warmth from the stones rose through Femi’s legs while the harbor water made soft shushing sounds far below. Ren tried the same cupped-hand trick and whispered, “Now I can see the thin lines on its shell.” The ferryman smiled and kept his own hands quiet, watching the beetle reach the far edge of the leaf and step onto a tiny curl of seaweed that had dried pale in the sun.

No one moved to pocket the beetle or poke it or lift the leaf. They simply stayed in a gentle half-circle of quiet looking while the late light turned softer and pinker across the water. The basil scent floated around them like a green ribbon, and the tin clock marked each slow second with its bright little sound. Femi felt the cool evening air brush their bare arms and was glad the polished pebble waited nearby so they could hold it again later. The ferryman rose first, still smiling, and Femi and Ren stood up too, leaving the beetle exactly where it belonged on its leaf road.

Before they left the wall Femi found the picnic cloth they had brought earlier and began to fold the cloth napkins one by one so that the stitched pear on each corner faced neatly upward. The cloth felt soft and a little sun-warmed under their fingers, and the folded squares made a tidy stack that smelled faintly of the basil leaves tucked inside the basket. Ren helped with the last napkin, and the ferryman waited patiently while the tin clock ticked on. Femi said, “The beetle can keep walking while we pack,” and Ren answered, “It already knows the way.”

They walked home along the quiet path as the sky deepened, and Femi carried the polished pebble in one pocket where it rested smooth and cool against their side. Inside the house the rooms felt gentle and ready for sleep, and Femi placed the pebble on the windowsill beside a small pot of basil that still gave off its fresh green smell. Ren waved good night from the door, and the ferryman’s boat lanterns winked far out on the darkening water. Femi climbed into bed while the soft house sounds settled around them. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
