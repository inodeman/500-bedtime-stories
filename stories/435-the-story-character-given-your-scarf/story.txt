Papa stood at the low wooden bench in the workshop already, turning a soft cloth in slow circles over the little wooden rabbit from the bedtime tales they loved. Warm afternoon light poured through the open doorway and made the tools on the low peg gleam like quiet coins. Una stepped inside and felt the cool stone under their feet while Papa hummed a low tune and brushed dust from the rabbit’s carved paws. The rabbit wore Una’s own knitted scarf, the blue one with white stars, tied gently under its chin so it would not slip. A thin golden beam rested on Papa’s shoulders as he worked. “Just a few more smooth places,” Papa said, his voice calm as still water. Una stayed near the doorway and listened to the quiet scrape of cloth on wood, breathing the warm workshop air that smelled faintly of pine shavings.

Una moved closer and noticed how carefully Papa had already taken care of the story character given their scarf. The rabbit’s loose ear had been glued firm again, and its scarf had been straightened so the stars showed clear. The beeswax scent rose gently from the open tin on the bench where Papa had polished the wood until it shone. Una ran one finger along the edge of the bench and felt the smooth finish. Outside the doorway the wind in the fig leaves made a soft shushing sound that drifted in like a lullaby. “It looks happy again,” Una said, watching the scarf hang neat and bright. Papa smiled without stopping his slow circles. The light on the rabbit’s face seemed kinder now, as if the little figure had rested well.

Una thought a quick thank-you would feel too thin for all the quiet care Papa had given. They looked around the workshop and spotted the broom leaning by the wall and the small scatter of wood chips near the pegs. While Papa kept polishing, Una took the broom and swept the chips into a neat pile, the soft bristles whispering across the floor. The temperature inside stayed pleasantly mild, neither hot nor chilly, and Una’s arms moved in easy rhythm. They emptied the chips into the little basket Papa kept for the garden path. Then Una wiped the lower pegs clean with a dry rag so each tool hung free of dust. The air felt settled and kind after the sweeping. Papa glanced over once but said nothing, letting Una finish the useful thing he had not asked for.

When the floor looked tidy Una set the broom back and stood beside Papa again. They reached out and touched the rabbit’s scarf, feeling the familiar wool that still held the shape of their own neck. A leaf had blown in from the fig tree outside, and Una picked it up to set it on the windowsill, brushing the leaf’s soft underside with one careful thumb. The texture felt cool and velvety against their skin. “I saw how you fixed the ear and made the scarf sit right,” Una said plainly. Papa paused and looked at them with warm eyes. The workshop held a peaceful hush broken only by a distant bird call. Una kept their voice steady and clear so the words would land soft and true.

Papa set the cloth down and rested one hand on Una’s shoulder. The light through the door had turned a deeper gold now, painting long gentle shapes across the floor. “I wanted the little rabbit ready for tonight’s story,” Papa answered. Una nodded and felt glad they had spoken what they noticed instead of letting it stay quiet inside. They both looked at the rabbit together, the scarf still bright around its neck. Outside the fig leaves kept their soft talking with the breeze. Una breathed in the mild air and felt their chest ease. The tools on the low peg hung straight and ready, each handle clean from Una’s earlier wiping.

Una wandered a few steps toward the open doorway where a single brick lay half in sun and half in shade. A small snail had begun to cross that brick, its shell the color of pale honey. Una crouched and watched the snail travel the whole length without hurry, leaving a thin shining trail that caught the light. The stone of the brick felt faintly warm under Una’s palm where they steadied themselves. Time moved slow and kind while the snail finished its journey and slipped into the grass beyond. Una stood again and returned to Papa’s side, the picture of the patient snail still clear in their mind. The workshop smelled clean and ready after the sweeping.

Papa lifted the wooden rabbit and held it out so Una could see every smooth place he had tended. The scarf fluttered once in a draft from the door and then settled. “Shall we set it on the shelf until evening?” Papa asked. Una answered, “Yes, right where the light will find it later.” Together they carried the story character to the low shelf near the window and placed it carefully so the scarf draped just so. Una straightened one loose end of wool with gentle fingers. The air around them felt still and friendly. Outside the fig tree swayed and the leaves answered with their quiet music.

They stood side by side for a moment longer, listening to the soft sounds of the late day. Una leaned their head briefly against Papa’s arm and felt the sturdy cotton of his sleeve. The temperature in the workshop had cooled a little as the sun slid lower, yet it remained comfortable. Papa put the beeswax tin away and hung the polishing cloth on its peg. Una checked that every tool sat straight. The rabbit on the shelf looked ready for stories, scarf bright under the fading gold light. Una felt the calm of the room settle around them both like a shared blanket.

Later when the sky turned the color of soft plums they closed the workshop door halfway and left the rabbit resting. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Una and Papa walked the short path toward home while the last warm air brushed their faces and the fig leaves whispered one more time above them.
