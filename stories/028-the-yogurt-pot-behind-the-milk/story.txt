The late sun touched the greenhouse gently through the south window, laying warm golden stripes across the wooden bench where seed pots sat in tidy rows. Noemi stepped inside and felt the mild heat settle on their bare arms like a soft blanket. The air held a quiet stillness broken only by the far-off tick of a tin clock somewhere on a shelf. Noemi walked closer to the bench, noticing how the light made the glass panes glow, and they breathed in the warm scent that rose from the damp soil in the pots. Auntie June stood near the milk jug with a gentle smile, her apron still dusted with a little flour from earlier baking.

Noemi spotted the small yogurt pot tucked carefully behind the milk jug and felt a quick wish rise up to lift the cover right away. The pot sat in the warmest spot on the bench, right where the south window let in the steadiest light. “Auntie June, may I look inside the yogurt pot now?” Noemi asked, their voice soft as they rested one hand on the cool wood of the bench. Auntie June shook her head kindly and kept her voice low. “Not quite yet, Noemi. It needs more time in the quiet warmth before it is ready.” Noemi nodded slowly, fingers brushing a seed pot that felt rough with soil grains.

Instead of lifting the cover, Noemi left the yogurt pot safely tucked behind the milk and chose a quiet task nearby. They settled onto the low stool beside the bench and began to watch the shadow of a hanging fern move the width of a thumb across the wood. The shadow slid so slowly that Noemi almost forgot to breathe while they waited. Priya had left a small folded quilt on the stool earlier, and Noemi traced one raised quilt stitch with a careful fingertip, feeling its tiny ridge of thread. The greenhouse stayed pleasantly warm, and the seed pots cast their own short shadows that barely shifted.

Auntie June poured a little water into a tray of waiting pots and spoke again in her calm way. “The yogurt will finish when it finishes, Noemi. We can sit with it and keep the cover closed.” Noemi answered, “I will wait with you, Auntie June,” and they kept their eyes on the slow shadow. The tin clock continued its steady soft ticking from the shelf, marking minutes that felt gentle rather than long. Noemi pressed their palm flat against the quilt and felt the even quilt stitch again, letting the texture ground them while the light from the south window stayed bright and kind.

Outside the glass the afternoon breeze stirred the lavender bushes that grew along the greenhouse wall, and a sweet lavender smell drifted in through a cracked pane. Noemi breathed it deeply and smiled, thinking how the flowers matched the quiet of the bench. They moved only to straighten one seed pot that had tipped a little, then returned to watching the fern shadow inch forward. Auntie June hummed a tiny tune without words while she wiped a spill of milk from the jug’s side. The yogurt pot stayed covered and still behind the milk, safe in its warm place.

Noemi shifted once to rest both elbows on the bench edge and whispered, “It smells nice in here with the lavender.” Auntie June replied, “Yes, Noemi, the warm air carries it just right.” They both stayed close to the south window where the light poured in steady and golden. Noemi felt the quilt stitch under their hand once more and counted three slow breaths. The seed pots waited patiently in their rows, some already showing tiny green tips that caught the sun. Time passed in small soft pieces marked only by the tin clock and the drifting shadow.

When the fern shadow had moved a full thumb-width and then a little more, Auntie June glanced at the yogurt pot and gave a quiet nod. Noemi felt ready too and stood slowly so their chair did not scrape. Together they slid the milk jug aside just enough to reach the covered pot. The air near the bench stayed warm, and the lavender smell lingered like a friendly hello. Noemi’s hands were steady as they waited for Auntie June’s signal. Nothing rushed, and the greenhouse held them both in its calm light.

Auntie June lifted the cover at last and showed Noemi the thick creamy yogurt that had set perfectly in its own time. “There it is, Noemi. All finished and smooth,” she said with a warm look. Noemi leaned close and answered, “It waited just right, Auntie June.” They both smiled at the simple success sitting on the greenhouse bench among the seed pots. The south window still poured soft light over everything, and the tin clock kept its gentle rhythm in the background. Noemi felt glad they had left the pot covered and chosen the quiet watching instead.

Noemi and Auntie June sat a little longer on the quilt-draped stool while the last of the afternoon light rested on the seed pots and the empty milk jug. The lavender smell faded softly as the breeze slowed outside. Noemi ran one finger along the quilt stitch one final time, feeling its neat raised line, then leaned their head against Auntie June’s shoulder. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Everything on the bench stayed peaceful, and Noemi felt the quiet warmth settle deep inside them as the day eased toward evening.
