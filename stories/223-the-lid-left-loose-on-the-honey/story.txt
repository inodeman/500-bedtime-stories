Miss Dalca stood already at the library table by the round window, smoothing a floured towel flat across one corner while the warm afternoon light spilled in and turned the wood a soft honey gold. Gita came close and watched the flour dust catch in the beams like tiny stars. The towel felt powdery and dry under Miss Dalca’s careful hands as she patted it ready for later snacks. Gita rested both elbows on the table edge and breathed in the quiet room. Miss Dalca kept working without hurry, lining up three picture books so their spines faced the same way. Gita liked the gentle scrape of covers sliding into place and the way the light stayed steady and kind on everything. The round window framed the garden leaves beyond, and Gita felt glad to sit so near Miss Dalca in that calm corner.

Outside the glass the wind in the fig leaves made a soft rustling that rose and fell like a slow song. Gita turned toward the sound and smiled as it whispered past the window. On the table sat a small glass jar of golden honey left from the morning biscuit tray, its lid resting loose and crooked on top. Miss Dalca had set it there earlier while she finished her other tasks. Gita reached out one careful finger and touched the cool metal rim. The jar sat near a stack of clean napkins and a little spoon. Gita’s eyes followed the way the light danced through the thick honey inside. Miss Dalca hummed again and Gita felt the peaceful air settle around their shoulders like a blanket.

Gita picked up the loose lid to set it straight, but the sticky honey had already crept along the threads and caught their fingers in a warm clingy tangle. A thin golden drip slid onto the table wood before Gita could stop it. The sticky patch felt thick and sweet against their skin. Gita’s heart gave a tiny jump and for one moment they thought of sliding a napkin over the drip to hide it quickly. The light from the round window stayed bright and steady on the mess. Gita held the lid in both hands and felt the honey pull gently between their fingers. The library stayed quiet except for the far rustle of pages being turned by someone else at another table.

Gita looked at the sticky shine on the wood and then at Miss Dalca who still arranged the last books with calm movements. The honey smell rose sweet and mild into the warm air. Gita almost tucked the lid under a book and wiped the drip away alone, but the idea felt heavy and wrong. Instead Gita took a slow breath and kept their hands open and honest. The temperature near the window stayed pleasantly mild. Gita waited until Miss Dalca glanced over with her kind eyes. The floured towel waited nearby ready for any small cleaning. Gita knew the plain truth would be easier than any hiding.

“Miss Dalca,” Gita said in a clear soft voice, “I touched the honey lid that was left loose and now my fingers are sticky and a little honey dripped on the table.” Miss Dalca turned fully toward Gita and the light caught the gentle smile that grew on her face. “Thank you for telling me right away, Gita,” Miss Dalca answered. She reached for the floured towel and offered one clean corner. “We can mend this together with no fuss at all.” Gita felt the warm relief wash through them like sunshine. The sticky honey still clung but no longer felt like trouble. Outside the fig leaves kept their quiet motion in the breeze.

Miss Dalca dampened a soft cloth with water that carried the fresh smell of lemon soap from the nearby sink. Gita held out their sticky fingers and Miss Dalca wiped them in slow gentle strokes until the honey melted away and the skin felt smooth again. “The lid just needed a firmer twist,” Miss Dalca said kindly. Gita watched the cloth move and smelled the clean lemon rising. “I can help wipe the table too,” Gita offered. Together they leaned over the wood. Miss Dalca showed Gita how to move the cloth in slow circles that erased every golden trace. The table felt cool and clean under the cloth. Gita worked carefully beside Miss Dalca and felt proud of the honest words they had spoken.

Tamsin wandered over from the story corner and peered at the shining table with curious eyes. “What happened here?” Tamsin asked. Gita answered without any worry, “The honey lid was loose and I told Miss Dalca so we could clean it.” Miss Dalca nodded and set the lid firmly back on the jar with a quiet click. Tamsin smiled and sat on the bench beside Gita. The three of them rested for a moment in the mild light. Gita ran one clean hand over the smooth wood and felt how nice it was when everything returned to right. The lemon scent faded gently into the air. Miss Dalca placed the floured towel back in its neat fold.

Gita and Miss Dalca finished wiping the table in slow circles until the surface gleamed under the round window light. The cloth made a soft whisper against the wood. Gita felt the calm settle deeper into their chest. Miss Dalca poured a tiny drop of fresh honey onto a biscuit for each of them as a quiet thank-you treat. “You chose well today, Gita,” Miss Dalca said. Gita bit into the sweet biscuit and tasted the warm gold on their tongue. Tamsin munched happily too. The library air stayed peaceful and the books waited patiently in their stacks. Gita leaned back and listened to the far soft sounds of the room.

Later when the afternoon faded toward evening Gita sat once more at the library table by the round window with hands folded and heart light. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Gita watched the gentle circle of light and felt the last warmth of the day on their cheeks. Miss Dalca closed the final book with a quiet thud. Outside the garden rested in soft shadow. Gita yawned a small peaceful yawn and knew the sticky honey moment had turned into something simple and mended. The table stayed clean and ready for tomorrow. Gita smiled at Miss Dalca and at Tamsin and felt ready for quiet dreams under the same calm light.
