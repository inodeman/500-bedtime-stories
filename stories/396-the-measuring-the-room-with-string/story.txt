Soft rain brushed the school hall windows like a cool fingertip, and the air inside held a gentle damp chill that made the wooden floor smell faintly of polish. Niko stepped through the wide doors with careful feet and felt the coolness settle on their cheeks while paper lanterns waited in neat stacks and chairs stood in quiet rows for the gentle fair. Niko’s coat sleeve brushed a table edge, and the light from the high windows fell in pale stripes across their hands as they looked around at the open space that still needed measuring. Grandpa Sol stood near a long coil of soft string, his own sweater carrying a mild outdoor scent, and he nodded when Niko came close. Walid arrived a moment later, shaking a few raindrops from his hair, and the three of them faced the empty middle of the hall together. Niko ran one finger along the smooth floorboard and listened to the hush that filled the big room.

Niko picked up one end of the string and felt its light roughness while Grandpa Sol held the coil steady. “We can mark the whole room in string lengths for the booths,” Niko said, voice low so it would not bounce too hard. Walid took the middle of the string and smiled. The pale afternoon light slipped across the floor in soft rectangles, and Niko noticed how the string would stretch from wall to wall if they stayed patient. Grandpa Sol only watched and kept one hand on the coil so it would not tangle. Niko breathed in the cool hall air and felt ready to begin the careful work of turning the open space into measured paths for the fair.

Niko and Walid began walking the string along the longest wall, but the coil slipped once when footsteps quickened. Niko felt the string tug and stopped at once. “If we rush, the lengths will go wrong and the booths will sit crooked,” Niko said. Walid nodded and slowed his steps until the string lay straight again. The temperature near the windows stayed cooler than the center of the hall, and Niko’s fingers kept a gentle hold so nothing pulled too tight. Grandpa Sol stayed nearby without taking over, simply watching the line grow true. Niko glanced at Walid and felt the calm return as they both chose slower steps.

Niko suggested they split the work into two slow jobs so nothing hurried. “You take the short walls and count the lengths there, and I will walk the long ones,” Niko told Walid. Walid agreed with a quiet “That feels right,” and they moved apart with the string still shared between them. The light from the side windows warmed Niko’s shoulders a little as they stretched the string carefully from corner to corner. Each length was marked with a small fold, and Niko paused often to keep every fold even. Grandpa Sol remained by the coil and offered only a soft count when asked, never more. Niko felt the floor cool under their shoes and kept the pace gentle.

Halfway along the second long wall Niko paused beside a stack of folded tablecloths that still held the smell of wet wool from earlier washing near an open door. Niko breathed the mild scent and then continued, calling softly so Walid could match the pace on his side. “Three lengths here already,” Niko said. Walid answered from across the hall, “Two on my short wall, and they sit straight.” The shared string stayed loose enough not to pull, and Niko felt its texture slide smoothly through their fingers. Grandpa Sol named the simple knot they would need later when the ends met, saying only “A reef knot will hold the join.” Niko nodded and kept measuring without hurry.

Niko reached the far corner and smoothed a paper edge on the instruction sheet that listed the fair booths so the corner would not catch the string. The thin paper felt crisp under their thumb, and they set it aside again. Walid finished his last short wall at the same moment and walked the free end of the string toward the center. The tin clock on the far wall gave a single clear tick that marked the quiet minute. Niko smiled at the sound and waited while Grandpa Sol held the meeting point steady. Together they laid the final stretch flat and checked that every fold matched. Niko’s hands stayed warm from the work even though the hall air stayed cool.

Niko and Walid brought both ends of the string to the middle and joined them with the knot Grandpa Sol had named. “It sits true all the way around,” Niko said, pressing the knot gently flat. Walid traced one more length with his finger and answered, “No part pulls longer than another.” Grandpa Sol offered one last quiet count of the total folds so they could write the number down. The soft overhead lights glowed a little warmer now as evening eased closer, and Niko felt the finished lines resting even under their palms. They stepped back together and saw the whole room held in neat string lengths ready for the gentle fair booths.

Niko helped roll the spare string while Walid set small paper markers at each fold. The floor still felt cool through their socks after they kicked off shoes for the last careful steps. Grandpa Sol only watched and smiled when the last marker sat straight. Niko breathed in the settled air of the hall and noticed how the rain outside had softened to a hush against the glass. Everything they had measured stayed true because no one had rushed, and the three of them stood in the calm center for a moment longer. Niko felt glad the slow jobs had fit together so well.

Niko sat on a low bench near the window while Walid and Grandpa Sol tidied the last scraps of string into a basket. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Warm light touched Niko’s hands and the quiet hall smelled only of clean wood and finished work. Niko rested their head against the cool wall and listened to the soft shuffle of chairs being edged into place for tomorrow. Outside the rain had nearly stopped, and the gentle fair space waited ready and even. Niko closed their eyes for a breath and felt the peaceful room hold them safe until it was time to walk home.
