Cousin Pio was already there in the schoolyard, mid-task, sweeping fine grit from the circle of stones with a small broom so each one looked smooth and inviting in the gentle evening light. A mild coolness rested on Ilaria's shoulders as they crossed the open space and stopped beside the low swing. "I can help with the stones if you like," Ilaria said. Cousin Pio smiled and handed them a soft cloth, and together they worked while the ropes of the swing made a tiny soft sound against the wood. Ilaria rubbed the top of a flat stone and felt how solid and sure it sat in the earth, happy that the yard felt peaceful and ready for quiet play.

Soon other children gathered near the circle, and Ilaria saw Opal standing just outside the fun, close enough to watch but not yet part of the joining hands. The game needed a handshake a new friend had not been taught, and Opal waited with quiet eyes while the others practiced the secret pattern of presses and taps. Ilaria felt the cool satin ribbon tied in their hair brush softly against their neck as a little wind moved it. They stepped nearer to Opal without calling attention. The low swing cast a long soft shadow across the stones, and the air around Ilaria held the quiet of shared space waiting to open.

The smell of rain on dust drifted up from the packed ground where earlier drops had darkened the earth in soft patches. Ilaria breathed it in and felt calm settle deeper. They decided to change the seating by gently shifting two stones outward so there was a clear open place right in the ring for Opal. "Come sit here beside me," Ilaria said in a voice meant only for Opal. Cousin Pio glanced over once and then returned to tidying the far edge, letting the children solve the small matter themselves. The temperature of the air stayed mild and kind as Opal settled onto the newly made space.

Ilaria slowed the pace of everything so the handshake could be shared without hurry or fuss. They showed Opal the first gentle palm press, then the soft finger curl, moving their own hands in clear open motions anyone could follow. "Like this, slow and easy," Ilaria murmured, and Opal copied with careful attention. The other children matched the new gentle rhythm, and no one made a loud prize of the change. Light from the setting sun warmed the tops of Ilaria's shoes as they sat, and the circle of stones felt friendlier with every quiet turn of hands.

A gate latch clicked softly somewhere along the fence line as a late breeze settled the wooden bars into place. Ilaria heard it and smiled, knowing the yard was safely closed for their calm time. They invited Opal to try the full handshake again, and this time Opal's fingers met theirs with growing certainty. Cousin Pio stayed nearby yet gave them room, polishing the low swing seat with slow strokes so it shone faintly. Ilaria felt the solid coolness of the stone under their legs and knew the change had made room for everyone without any big announcement at all.

Opal grinned as the handshake finally clicked into place between them, and Ilaria answered with a matching quiet smile. "We can swing after if you want," Opal said, voice light with ease. Ilaria nodded and the two of them rose together while the others kept a soft humming game going around the stones. The texture of the worn rope on the low swing felt familiar under Ilaria's palms as they held it steady for Opal to climb on first. Cool air brushed Ilaria's face when the swing moved in a small slow arc, and Cousin Pio watched with quiet approval from his place by the circle.

After a few gentle swings Ilaria and Opal returned to the stones and sat again, this time side by side as if they had always belonged there. Ilaria traced a small pattern on the dusty ground with one dry finger, making a faint circle that caught the last of the light. "Your turn to lead the next press," Ilaria told Opal, and Opal did so with bright careful hands. The children followed, and the whole group moved in easy time. A soft warmth still clung to the stones from the day, and Ilaria rested their palm there while the schoolyard held its peaceful hush.

Cousin Pio finally set his cloth aside and joined them at the outer edge, listening rather than directing. Ilaria leaned close so only Opal could hear and said, "I am glad you are here with us." The words floated gently without needing to be large. Opal answered, "Me too," and their hands found the new handshake one more easy time. The evening light turned soft blue across the low swing and the circle, and Ilaria felt the mild chill of coming night on their wrists. Everything felt settled and kind, the way good company does when no one is left standing apart.

Later Ilaria walked with Cousin Pio and Opal toward the school building at the edge of the yard where a familiar window waited. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Ilaria paused to look at the warm gold light spilling out onto the path and felt the quiet happiness of the shared afternoon still resting in their chest. The air smelled clean and the stones behind them kept their neat circle under the dimming sky. Ilaria took one last slow breath of the cool evening and knew the handshake and the open place in the ring would stay with them like a gentle secret of belonging.
