The soft tick of a tin clock came from the far desk while Bodhi sat in the library story circle and noticed the steady little sound at once. Warm light from the high windows lay in gentle stripes across the round rug, and Bodhi felt the mild temperature of the air against their cheeks as they settled deeper onto a thick floor cushion. Auntie June held a wide picture book and smiled at the group of children gathered close. Beside the circle stood a short wooden library ladder that usually helped reach the tallest shelves; today Auntie June had pulled it near so its lowest rung waited ready, explaining that anyone who wished could stand on that single rung for a moment during the story about reaching high for a favorite book, then step down whenever they chose. Bodhi watched the dust motes drift in the light and listened again to the quiet tick while their fingers brushed the edge of the cushion.

Bodhi felt a shy flutter in their chest at the sight of that one rung of a ladder and the clear permission to stop there. The wood of the rung looked smooth and steady under the library lamps, yet Bodhi kept their eyes on the book rather than speak up right away. Auntie June’s voice stayed soft as she turned a page that smelled faintly of old paper. Lale sat nearby and offered a small friendly glance without crowding. Bodhi shifted so their shoulder touched the cool side of a low shelf for a second, then returned to the circle, still deciding whether to pretend they did not care about climbing even one step.

Auntie June looked around the circle and said, “Anyone who wants may try just the first rung while I read. You may stop right there and come down whenever you like. No one has to go higher.” Bodhi heard the kind tone and felt the soft nap of the carpet under their palms. The light from the windows had shifted a little, warming the side of Bodhi’s face. Lale leaned closer and whispered, “It looks steady, Bodhi.” Bodhi nodded once but stayed seated a moment longer, breathing the quiet air of the room while Auntie June waited without any hurry.

Instead of pretending not to care, Bodhi lifted their chin and tried the smallest brave version in a voice that could be heard. “I would like to stand on the one rung, Auntie June,” Bodhi said clearly enough for the nearby children to hear. Auntie June answered at once, “Of course, Bodhi. I will hold the side, and you stop exactly where you wish.” Bodhi rose carefully, feeling the firm floor under their shoes, and walked the few steps to the ladder. The wood of the rung felt solid when Bodhi placed one foot, then the other, and stood only on that single step while Auntie June kept one gentle hand nearby but never pushed.

Bodhi stayed balanced on the one rung of a ladder and looked out over the story circle from that small height. The quilt stitch on the edge of the nearest floor cushion showed neat little ridges of thread that Bodhi could see clearly now. Warm light rested on Bodhi’s hair, and the air up there felt the same mild temperature as below. Auntie June continued the story in her calm voice about a child who reached for a high book and then climbed down again. Bodhi listened and felt proud of the quiet choice to try and then to stop. Lale smiled up from the rug and said, “You look tall up there, Bodhi.” Bodhi answered softly, “I am stopping on this rung.”

After a short while Bodhi stepped carefully back down to the carpet and returned to their cushion. Auntie June said, “Stopping there was perfect, Bodhi. Thank you for trying in your own way.” Bodhi settled again and noticed the faint smell of lavender drifting from a small cloth sachet that rested on the librarian’s side table near the open window. The scent was clean and gentle, mixing with the paper smell of the books. Bodhi’s shoulders relaxed as they watched Auntie June turn another page, and the stripes of light on the floor had moved only a little farther.

Bodhi rested both hands on their knees and watched a thin shadow from the ladder move the width of a thumb across the rug while Auntie June finished the short tale. The room stayed peaceful, and Bodhi felt the cushion give softly under their weight. Lale scooted a bit closer and shared a quiet look of friendship. Auntie June closed the book with a soft thump and spoke once more, “You used a brave voice today, Bodhi, and you knew when to stop. That matters.” Bodhi answered, “I liked standing on just one rung.” The mild air brushed Bodhi’s arms as they sat still and let the words settle.

The children began to stretch and gather their things while the library lights glowed evenly overhead. Bodhi helped smooth the edge of the story-circle rug and felt the woven fibers under their fingertips. Auntie June thanked everyone and gave Bodhi a warm nod that needed no extra show. Bodhi walked slowly past the short ladder, glancing once more at the single rung that had been enough, then joined Lale near the door where the late light fell in a calm rectangle. The whole room smelled of books and the lingering lavender, and Bodhi felt steady and ready for the walk home.

Later that evening Bodhi sat by their own window at home with a blanket over their legs and remembered the library ladder and the kind way Auntie June had stayed close without pushing. The quilt stitch along the blanket edge felt familiar under Bodhi’s fingers as they thought about speaking up and then stopping exactly where it felt right. Outside the sky had turned a deep soft blue, and the house was quiet except for distant ordinary sounds. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
