The caterpillar rested on a damp stone ledge of the harbor wall at low tide, its body the soft green of new leaves and its many feet taking steps more careful than fingers ever managed. Cool salty air drifted up from the exposed mud and wet pebbles below while the last gold light of evening lay gently across the stone. Golda leaned so near that their own breath warmed the air above the little creature, and they saw how each foot tested the rough surface before settling. The stone felt grainy under Golda’s palms as they balanced, and a thin ribbon of water still trickled somewhere far along the wall with a quiet drip. Golda stayed very still so the caterpillar would keep walking its slow path across a patch of dried seaweed that smelled faintly of the sea.

Golda felt the small wish rise inside them to scoop the caterpillar into a pocket or to touch it with one finger just to feel the soft fuzz, yet they kept their hands flat on the cool stone and only watched. The tide had pulled far out, leaving the wall dark and glistening in places, and the air carried a clean chill that made Golda’s cheeks tingle. Eshe arrived with soft steps and knelt beside Golda, their sleeves brushing the same grainy surface. Golda whispered, “See how carefully its feet choose each place,” and Eshe nodded without reaching forward. A faint mint leaves scent floated from a small bundle someone had left on a higher rock, mixing with the harbor smell in a way that felt fresh and green. Golda kept their voice low so the air stayed calm around the caterpillar.

“We can look without moving it at all,” Golda said, and Eshe answered softly, “I like watching the way it lifts each foot.” The two of them stayed side by side while the evening light turned a warmer yellow and the stone beneath them held a leftover warmth from the day. Golda pointed with their eyes alone to the tiny black dots along the caterpillar’s side and to the way the careful feet never slipped even when the wall slanted. A gentle breeze brought the distant clink of ropes against a boat mast, and Golda felt glad that the caterpillar seemed content right where it was. They breathed slowly and matched their stillness to the creature’s slow progress over a smooth bit of rock.

The ferryman walked along the top of the wall with quiet boots and paused when he saw the children crouched so intently. His coat carried the clean smell of river water and wood, and he lowered himself carefully so his shadow would not fall across the stone. “What have you found that holds you so still?” he asked in a low voice that blended with the soft lap of remaining water. Golda looked up and said, “A caterpillar whose feet are more careful than fingers, and we are only looking.” The ferryman smiled and rested his hands on his knees while the cool air moved around all three of them. Eshe added, “Golda showed me how to watch without touching,” and the ferryman nodded as if that was the best kind of finding.

Together they studied the tiny traveler as it crossed a crack filled with dried salt that sparkled under the fading light. Golda noticed a smooth glass bead caught in a crevice near the seaweed, its surface cool and rounded when their fingertip brushed it by accident, and they left the bead exactly where it lay. The ferryman murmured that he had never seen feet so patient on such a small traveler, and the three of them shared a quiet stretch of time while a cat’s collar bell jingled once from a boat deck farther down the harbor. The sound was brief and clear and then gone, leaving the air soft again. Golda felt the stone growing cooler under their hands and knew the light would soon thin into dusk.

Golda spoke again, “If we leave it here the careful feet can keep walking wherever they need to go,” and Eshe agreed with a small sound of yes. The ferryman said, “That is a fine choice,” and the words settled gently among them like another layer of calm air. Below the wall the low-tide mud shone in long ribbons of silver and brown, and a few shorebirds called once in the distance without startling anyone. Golda breathed in the mint leaves scent that still lingered nearby and felt their shoulders loosen. They watched the caterpillar reach a tuft of green that had rooted in a higher crack and begin to climb it with the same unhurried care.

As the light softened further Golda, Eshe, and the ferryman rose together and stepped back so the wall belonged again to the little green traveler. Golda brushed their palms free of stone grit and felt the evening coolness settle on their skin like a light blanket. They named three smells without hurry—the sharp mint leaves, the clean wet stone, and the faint wood scent from the ferryman’s coat—and each one made the moment feel complete. The caterpillar continued its path undistracted, and the harbor wall held its quiet stretch of low tide around them. Eshe’s sleeve brushed Golda’s arm with a soft cotton touch as they turned toward the path that led home.

They walked slowly along the upper edge where the stones were dry and warm from the day, and Golda looked back once to be sure the spot remained peaceful. A lantern someone had set near a shed threw a steady pool of yellow light across the path, and the air tasted faintly of salt mixed with the cool of coming night. The ferryman waved a quiet goodbye and returned to his boat while Golda and Eshe kept matching steps. Golda felt glad all the way through their chest that the caterpillar stayed free on the wall with its careful feet. The path home smelled of evening gardens and felt smooth under their shoes.

Later Golda sat by their own window with a blanket soft around their shoulders and the house quiet around them. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Outside the dark held only gentle night sounds, and inside the air was warm and still while Golda remembered the green traveler and the way everyone had chosen to look and then leave it in peace on the harbor wall at low tide.
