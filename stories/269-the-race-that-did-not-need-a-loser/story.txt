The warm smell of cinnamon drifted across the ferry deck and curled around Esme’s busy hands as they smoothed a bright paper flag flat against the cool metal rail. Esme pressed each corner with careful thumbs and felt the gentle rock of the boat under their sneakers while soft afternoon light lay in long gold bars on the wooden planks. The air felt kindly warm on Esme’s cheeks, and Neighbor Sami sat on a nearby bench with a thermos, watching the water without hurry. Esme’s fingers kept working until the flag looked ready, and they listened to the low steady engines humming below. With only two elbows of room between the rail and the benches, the space felt small yet friendly, and Esme pictured how a little race might begin right there if everyone stepped lightly and shared the path.

Esme turned and saw Bibi standing just outside the gathering fun, near the gate that led back toward the cabin stairs, hands tucked in pockets and eyes following the paper flag with quiet hope. The deck smelled faintly of river water mixed with that lingering cinnamon, and the sun warmed the rail under Esme’s palm. “We could have a race along the rail,” Esme said softly to Neighbor Sami, “but maybe it does not have to stay a race.” Neighbor Sami only nodded once and let the children work it out, the thermos warm between their own hands. Esme waved Bibi closer with the flag so the paper fluttered like a small bird. Bibi took one careful step forward and the wooden planks felt smooth and safe under every foot.

Esme shifted a low folding stool so there was a clear place for Bibi right beside the starting spot, changing the seating without any loud announcement of prizes. The light glanced off the water in gentle sparkles, and a soft breeze cooled Esme’s wrists. “Bibi, will you walk with us at the front?” Esme asked, holding out the flag. Bibi’s face brightened like a lamp turning on, and they answered, “I would like that very much.” Neighbor Sami smiled but stayed quiet, trusting the children. Esme felt the walnut-smooth spoon that Sami had lent earlier for stirring cocoa still resting in their pocket, cool and familiar against their side, and the texture reminded them that simple things could make room for everyone.

They decided together that the race could become a parade if they slowed the pace and walked side by side instead of rushing past one another. Esme explained the new gentle rule in a calm voice so no one felt left out, and the ferry deck seemed to grow roomier under the warm sun. “We will go slow enough that our feet match,” Esme told Bibi and the other small friends who had gathered. Bibi nodded and took the other edge of the flag so it stretched between them like a shared ribbon. The engines kept their low song, and the air tasted clean and slightly sweet. Neighbor Sami watched from the bench and let the plan unfold without stepping in.

Esme led the first careful steps along the rail, keeping the parade close so everyone stayed within those two elbows of space. Bibi walked right beside them, shoulder almost touching, and the paper flag whispered between their hands. A cool patch of shade from the upper deck drifted over Esme’s hair for a moment and felt pleasant. “Listen,” Esme whispered, and just then a gate latch clicked softly somewhere near the cabin, a neat little sound that marked the start without any shout. The children smiled at the quiet signal and began to move together in an easy line, no one racing ahead and no one falling behind.

As they paraded past the benches, Esme changed the pace once more so the steps became a slow happy march that left space for talking and looking at the water. Bibi pointed at a floating leaf and Esme paused so everyone could see it glint. The sunlight felt buttery on their arms, and the rail stayed cool and steady under trailing fingers. “This is better than any hurry,” Bibi said, and Esme answered, “Yes, because now we all fit.” Neighbor Sami poured a little warm cocoa into tiny cups for later and kept the peace by saying nothing more than a gentle hum of approval. The deck boards creaked kindly under the careful feet.

They circled once more along the short open stretch, Esme guiding the parade so Bibi stayed in the real middle of the fun rather than waiting at the edge for a prize that never needed naming. The cinnamon scent still floated faintly from Sami’s thermos, mixing with river air, and Esme’s hands felt the soft paper of the flag growing familiar. Light danced on the ripples beyond the rail, and the temperature stayed mild and friendly. Esme hummed four notes and then left a rest so the quiet could settle around them like a blanket. Bibi copied the little tune, and the other children joined with soft voices until the whole short parade felt like one shared breath.

When the walking slowed to a stop near the bench, Esme helped fold the flag and set the stool back so the deck stayed neat for anyone else who might come. Neighbor Sami handed around the small cups, and the cocoa tasted warm and kind after the gentle parade. Esme’s cheeks glowed from the soft light and the happy work of making room. “Thank you for changing the way we walked,” Bibi told Esme, and Esme squeezed their friend’s hand once. The engines hummed lower as the ferry prepared for the next quiet stretch of water, and every child found a place to sit without crowding. The afternoon felt complete in the simplest way.

Later, when the ferry docked and the children carried their quiet joy toward home, Esme kept the memory of the shared flag and the slow steps close. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Esme settled under a light blanket that felt smooth against their arms, still smelling a trace of cinnamon on their sleeves, and listened to the far-off peaceful sounds of the evening while Neighbor Sami’s kind watchfulness and Bibi’s bright place in the parade stayed warm in their thoughts until sleep arrived like a gentle tide.
