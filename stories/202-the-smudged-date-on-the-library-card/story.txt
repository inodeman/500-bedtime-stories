A soft little tinkling drifted through the warm kitchen, and Idris paused with one hand on the edge of the table to listen. The sound came from the cat's collar bell as the cat wandered past the open doorway, each step sending a gentle chime into the room. Idris felt the mild evening air brush their bare arms and watched the yellow lamp light pool across the wooden surface where a jar of jam, a dish of soft butter, and a shared pencil tin sat side by side. The pencil tin lid felt cool and slightly dented under Idris's fingertips. Idris breathed in the ordinary kitchen scents and settled more firmly onto the chair, glad the quiet sound had pulled their attention back to the present moment instead of the small worry tucked in their pocket.

Idris pulled the library card from their pocket and laid it flat beside the jam jar, where a thin smear of strawberry jam already glistened under the lamp. The date stamp on the card had smudged into a soft gray blur, almost as if rain had fallen on the ink, though no rain had touched it. Idris traced the blurred numbers with one careful finger and felt the paper's slight roughness. Warm light from the window mixed with the lamp glow and made the butter look golden. Idris almost slid the card under the pencil tin so no one would see, but the idea made their shoulders feel tight. The kitchen stayed peaceful, filled only with the low hum of the refrigerator and the faint sweetness rising from the open jam.

Ms. Okonkwo stepped into the kitchen carrying a small glass of water and sat across from Idris at the same table. She set the glass down gently so it would not tip the pencil tin. Idris watched the clear water catch the light and felt the solid chair beneath them. "I found something on my library card," Idris said quietly, voice steady though their fingers still rested near the smudged stamp. Ms. Okonkwo looked at the card without hurry and answered, "Show me, Idris. We can look together." The air between them felt calm and ordinary. Idris slid the card closer so the blurred date sat in plain sight, and the shared pencil tin gave a tiny metallic click when Idris's sleeve brushed its side.

Idris took a slow breath and told the plain truth without looking away. "I almost hid it under the tin because the stamp got smudged and I felt unsure," Idris said. Ms. Okonkwo nodded once and replied, "Thank you for saying it straight, Idris. Cards get smudged sometimes, and we can mend what can be mended." She reached into the pencil tin and chose a soft eraser and a fine-tipped pencil, placing them beside the jam jar so everything stayed neat. Idris felt relief loosen their hands. The butter dish gave off a mild creamy scent that mixed with the jam, and the lamp light stayed steady and kind on both their faces while they began the quiet work of fixing the card.

Together they smoothed the edges of the smudged area with the eraser, working slowly so the paper would not tear. Idris held the card steady while Ms. Okonkwo guided the pencil to rewrite the clear date in the clean space beside the blur. "Does that look right to you, Idris?" she asked. Idris studied the fresh numbers and answered, "Yes, that looks right now." The pencil made a soft scratching sound against the card. Idris noticed a small glass bead that had rolled from the pencil tin onto the table; it felt smooth and cool when they rolled it once under one fingertip before setting it safely back inside the tin. The kitchen air stayed comfortably warm, and the whole task felt simple and shared.

While they finished, Felix appeared in the doorway holding a small bunch of fresh mint leaves from the windowsill pot and set them near the butter dish so the bright green smell of mint leaves drifted across the table. Idris smiled at the friendly arrival and kept one hand resting lightly on the mended library card. Felix pulled out a chair with a quiet scrape and sat without crowding the work. The three of them stayed close to the jam, the butter, and the open pencil tin. Idris felt the card's paper grow familiar again under their palm. Soft light from the overhead lamp and the cooler light from the window blended across the wood, making every jam spoon and butter knife shine gently.

Idris picked up the card once more and turned it slowly so both sides caught the light. The new date stood clear beside the soft old smudge, and nothing about the card felt hidden anymore. Ms. Okonkwo wiped a tiny spot of jam from the table edge with a clean cloth while Felix arranged the mint leaves in a neat little row. Idris listened to the quiet house sounds and felt the smooth grain of the wooden table under both elbows. "I like how we fixed it together," Idris said. The others simply smiled, and the kitchen remained peaceful, filled with the mild temperature of early evening and the easy company of people who had chosen kindness over teasing.

Idris carefully returned the pencil and eraser to the shared tin, closed the lid with a soft click, and rested both hands flat on the table for a moment of stillness. The jam jar lid felt cool when Idris twisted it shut, and the butter dish lid settled into place with a small final sound. Felix offered Idris a single mint leaf to smell more closely, and Idris did so without hurry. Warm light continued to rest on the repaired library card that now lay safely in the middle of the table. Everything in the room felt settled and ordinary again, the kind of quiet that invites slow breathing and unhurried thoughts before bedtime.

Idris slipped the library card into a safe pocket, then stood and stretched both arms toward the ceiling while the cat settled nearby. The kitchen table still held its jam, butter, and pencil tin in friendly order, and the mild air carried only calm scents. Idris looked once more at Ms. Okonkwo and Felix, feeling grateful for the steady company and the mended card. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Idris took one last slow breath of the peaceful room and turned toward the hallway with quiet steps, ready for sleep.
