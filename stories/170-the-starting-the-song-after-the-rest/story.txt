A faint ringing sound slipped into the music corner from the hallway, and Ines lifted their chin to notice the gentle chime of a cat's collar bell on the still air. Ines sat on the long bench beside Auntie Luz and breathed the fresh scent of tomato vines wafting through the open window from garden pots. Soft evening light painted pale gold across the drum's round top and the smooth recorder nearby. Ines felt the cool temperature of the room wrap around their shoulders. Auntie Luz rested her hands in her lap without hurrying while Ines watched dust motes dance in a thin beam. The rest in their shared song had lasted so the quiet itself seemed to lean closer. Ines thought about the notes waiting to begin again as the bell faded into silence.

Ines placed both palms flat on the bench and felt the fine grain of the wood under their skin, rough in tiny lines like little rivers. The drum waited ready and the recorder gleamed under the lamp's mild glow. Auntie Luz turned toward Ines with a patient look that asked nothing hurried. Ines could hear their own quiet breathing mix with a distant murmur from outside. Warmth from the floorboards rose around their ankles. Ines remembered how the song had paused before this rest and now the whole corner waited with open ears. They wanted to start again but the listening quiet made their chest feel tight in a shy way. Auntie Luz hummed a tiny fragment under her breath to keep the air friendly. Ines shifted closer to the instruments.

"The room is listening hard right now," Ines said in a small voice that carried clear to the waiting drum. Auntie Luz nodded and answered, "We have all the time we need, Ines, and no one is asking for a big show." The light from the window grew softer and bluer as day leaned into evening. Ines felt a cool draft drift across their wrists. They tapped one finger on their knee and heard the faint thump against soft fabric. The drum skin looked smooth and inviting. Ines pictured starting the song after the rest with one careful breath into the recorder or one soft palm on the drum. Auntie Luz stayed still and kind without pushing. Ines drew a longer breath and let it out slowly.

Ines reached for the recorder and held its light wooden body smooth and cool against their fingers. Mild warmth still clung to it from earlier play. Auntie Luz watched with soft eyes and said nothing for a stretch of heartbeats. Ines brought the mouthpiece near but stopped as the listening room leaned closer. Soft lamp light reflected along the recorder's length. They lowered it and glanced at the drum, feeling the shy flutter return. Outside a leaf brushed the sill with a dry whisper. Auntie Luz smiled and rested one hand on the bench as quiet company. Ines decided the smallest brave version would be enough after the rest.

"I can try just one soft sound to begin again," Ines whispered. Auntie Luz replied with kindness, "That sounds perfect, Ines. Your note can be small and still be real music." The temperature stayed pleasantly mild against Ines's cheeks. They lifted the recorder and felt its familiar weight settle like an old friend. A pale shaft of daylight crossed the table and lit the drum's edge. Ines closed their eyes and heard the hush of the space waiting without demand. They remembered the place in the song after the rest and pictured the next phrase starting gently. Auntie Luz kept her breathing even and slow. Ines opened their eyes and prepared the tiniest start.

The first note came out as a thin clear thread from the recorder, soft yet firm enough that the room could hear it. Ines felt a small rush of warmth in their face that faded into quiet pride. Auntie Luz beamed with a single nod of deep appreciation. The light on the wall shifted as a cloud passed and softened the glow. Ines played a second note, joining it so the song after the rest began to walk forward gently. They heard the wood vibrate faintly under their fingers. The drum waited its turn without feeling forgotten. Ines let the melody grow one careful step at a time. Auntie Luz joined with a soft hum that matched without taking over.

"Would you like to add the drum after a few more notes, Ines?" Auntie Luz asked in a low voice. Ines spoke clearly, "Yes, after this phrase I will touch it once and keep the song going." The air seemed lighter now that music filled it. Ines finished the phrase on the recorder and set it down with care. They rested their palm against the taut drum skin which felt firm and springy. A single soft beat followed, deep and round, joining the song after the rest. The lamp light glowed steady on their hands. Ines smiled as the shy feeling eased into something warmer. Auntie Luz tapped her knee in quiet time.

Together they kept the song moving while the drum and recorder took turns in small friendly phrases. Ines felt the mild cool of the evening deepen around their legs as night grew closer. They played another soft pattern and listened to how the room held the music instead of empty waiting. Auntie Luz stayed with kind attention and never asked for louder sounds. Ines noticed how starting after the rest had been possible with the smallest brave version that could still be heard. The bench supported them through each measure. A gentle hush returned between phrases. Ines's breathing matched the slow pace. The whole corner felt peaceful and complete.

Ines set both instruments back after one last soft exchange and leaned against the wall with an easy breath. Auntie Luz said, "I liked the way you began again, Ines. It was just right for us." Ines answered, "Thank you for waiting with me and not pushing." The lantern by the window glowed softly. Warm light from it mixed with the last blue of dusk and made the drum and recorder look cozy. Ines felt the smooth cool of the wall behind their shoulders. They listened for a moment to the far clock ticking down the hall rather than any nearer sound. Auntie Luz rose and offered a hand. Ines took it and stood, carrying the quiet feeling of the small start inside them. The music corner rested ready for another day.
