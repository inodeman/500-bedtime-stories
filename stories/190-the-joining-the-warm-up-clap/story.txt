The soft evening air drifted in through the open window and touched the music corner with a cool gentle brush, stirring the curtain so that pale gold light slid across the floorboards and rested on the drum. Kei walked in on quiet sock feet and felt that same cool air settle on their arms and the back of their neck like a friendly shawl. They paused near the low shelf where the drum and the recorder waited side by side, their eyes taking in the round skin of the drum and the slender wooden body of the recorder while the temperature of the room stayed mild and kind. Kei stayed close to the doorway a moment longer, listening to the hush of the breeze and smelling nothing yet but clean wood, then stepped fully inside.

Auntie Luz sat already on the long bench, her smile easy and her hands open on her knees. Kei slid onto the bench beside her and let their fingers travel along the wood until they felt the fine grain of the bench, tiny raised lines that caught lightly under their fingertips and felt steady and real. The grain of the bench helped Kei sit still while the last daylight warmed the wall in soft patches. “We can begin whenever you like, Kei,” Auntie Luz said in a voice no louder than the breeze. Kei nodded once, the texture still under their hand, and kept their shoulder near Auntie Luz so the space felt safe and shared.

Kei looked at the drum and thought about the warm-up clap that felt loud, the kind that might fill the whole corner at once and make their cheeks grow warm. The idea made them shy, so they pressed their palms together without sound and watched the way the lamp light glowed on the recorder’s smooth side. The air stayed cool against Kei’s wrists. They did not pretend the clap did not matter; they simply held the thought gently and stayed right there on the bench with Auntie Luz, who waited without hurrying.

“A tiny clap is still a real clap,” Auntie Luz said, her tone warm as tea. Kei breathed in and out and answered in a voice that could be heard, though it was soft. “I can try the smallest brave version,” Kei said. The words left their mouth clearly enough for Auntie Luz to catch every one. Light from the window lay in a quiet stripe across Kei’s knees, and they felt the cool floor through their socks while they gathered the courage that lived in a small space inside them.

Kei lifted their hands just a little way apart and brought them together once in a clap that stayed close to their body, a sound no bigger than a single raindrop landing on a leaf. The clap was brave because it was true and because Kei let it be heard instead of hiding it. Auntie Luz smiled and answered with a matching soft clap of her own. “That was perfect, Kei,” she said. The drum waited nearby without asking for more, and the recorder rested quiet, while the mild air moved once more across Kei’s hair.

A faint green scent drifted through the same open window, the smell of tomato vines growing in the garden boxes just outside, and Kei noticed it like a friendly hello from the evening. They stayed seated and let the smell mingle with the cool air on their face. Jasper the cat padded in from the hallway then, and the small bright sound of a cat’s collar bell tagged along with each careful step. Kei heard the bell clearly and smiled a little, still holding the memory of their own small clap. Auntie Luz did not ask for a louder version or a show; she simply sat kind and near.

Kei rested both hands on their lap and chose to listen for the far clock ticking somewhere deeper in the house rather than any nearer sound, letting that distant steady beat settle their breathing. The light in the corner had turned softer now, the color of warm honey on the drum head. “Would you like to tap the drum once the same small way?” Auntie Luz asked. Kei thought, then said, “Yes, just once, the quiet way.” Their finger met the drum skin in a single gentle touch that made a low round note, no bigger than the clap had been, and the note floated up and away without fuss.

Auntie Luz kept her voice easy. “We can put the recorder away for tonight if you want,” she said. Kei answered, “I like leaving it here where I can see it.” They leaned a little against Auntie Luz’s side and felt the mild temperature of the room wrap around both of them while the tomato-vine smell lingered faintly and the cat’s collar bell chimed one last soft time from under the bench. Kei stayed close, proud in a quiet way of the voice they had used and the clap they had offered.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Kei watched the gentle circle of light it made on the wall and felt their eyelids grow heavier in the calm air. Auntie Luz rested a hand lightly near Kei’s on the bench so the grain still whispered under their shared touch. Outside the tomato vines kept their green scent, and somewhere the far clock ticked on. Kei let their shoulders drop and their breathing slow, the drum and recorder safe nearby, the small brave clap still warm inside them like a pocket of light that needed no bigger stage.
