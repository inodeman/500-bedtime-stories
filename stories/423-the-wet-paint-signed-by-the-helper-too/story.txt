Mr. Ade stood already at the low table in the library corner, easing a bright paper ribbon between the pages of a heavy picture book so it lay flat and neat. Warm golden light from the tall window settled across the quiet shelves and touched Gita’s cheeks as they walked nearer on soft socks. The wood under their feet felt smooth and cool. Mr. Ade hummed a tiny tune while he smoothed the ribbon’s edge with one careful finger, and Gita smelled the clean lemon soap still lingering on his washed hands from earlier. Outside the open window the wind in the fig leaves made a low shushing sound that drifted in like a friendly whisper.

Gita leaned on the table edge and looked down at the small wooden block resting beside the open book. Earlier that afternoon they had painted a cheerful sun and three blue birds across its front face, and the paint still shone wet and bright under the slanting light. Gita remembered pressing their own tiny thumb mark on the underside before the paint dried. Now they turned the block over gently and saw a second tiny mark sitting politely beside theirs, no bigger than a seed, not touching the pretty front at all. The underside felt slightly tacky under Gita’s careful fingertip. Mr. Ade kept working on the paper ribbon without looking up, and the library corner stayed peaceful and close.

Gita understood at once that Mr. Ade had added the helper’s tiny mark himself while they had stepped away to wash brushes. The two marks rested side by side like quiet friends sharing a secret space. Cool air from the window brushed Gita’s bare arms and made the painted surface gleam. Gita held the block steady so no smudge would spoil anything. Mr. Ade finished settling the paper ribbon and closed the book with a soft thump that sounded kind. Gita set the wooden block down again, paint side up, and felt glad the front stayed only theirs while the underside held both marks together.

A quick thank-you rose in Gita’s mind, yet it felt too thin for the careful way Mr. Ade had matched the marks without taking any space on the sunny front. The late light turned the edges of the books amber and gentle. Gita glanced around the library corner and noticed a thin scatter of paper scraps and a faint chalky dust left from the painting. Nearby lay a floured towel someone had brought from the morning baking table in the library kitchen. Gita picked up the towel; its dry powdery texture brushed softly against their palms. They decided to do one useful thing Mr. Ade had never asked for.

Gita began to wipe the table in slow circles, gathering the scraps into a neat pile and brushing away the dust until the wood shone again under the warm window light. The floured towel left the surface smooth and ready. Mr. Ade watched for a moment and then smiled. “You found the little mark I added underneath,” he said in a low voice that matched the quiet shelves. Gita kept wiping in steady circles and answered, “I saw it right beside mine.” The air stayed mild and kind while the last scraps disappeared into Gita’s hand.

Mr. Ade took the closed book with its paper ribbon and slid it onto the special display shelf that faced the reading pillows. Soft fabric of the pillows looked inviting in the golden light. Gita finished the last slow circle and folded the floured towel into a tidy square. “I wanted the front to stay all yours,” Mr. Ade said. Gita nodded and placed the wooden block carefully next to the book so the wet paint could finish drying in peace. The library corner felt settled and bright. Gita spoke again, plainly, “I noticed what you did, and I wiped the table because it needed it.”

Mr. Ade’s eyes crinkled. “That was thoughtful, Gita,” he replied. The two of them stood side by side for a calm minute, listening to the far-off hush of turning pages elsewhere in the library. Gita felt the cool table edge under their resting hands and watched dust motes drift like tiny stars in the beam of light. Tamsin, who had been choosing a puzzle at the next shelf, wandered over and whispered, “The sun you painted looks happy.” Gita smiled at their friend and answered, “Mr. Ade helped underneath where it stays secret.” Tamsin nodded and tiptoed back to the puzzles, leaving the corner peaceful once more.

Gita and Mr. Ade straightened the last cushion and made sure the paper ribbon peeped just enough from the book so the next child would find the marked page easily. The wooden block sat proudly with its wet paint catching the fading afternoon glow. Gita rubbed their palms together and felt how clean they were after the wiping. Mr. Ade rested one hand lightly on the shelf and said, “We make a good team when the work is quiet.” Gita looked at the two tiny marks in their mind and felt warm inside, though the air itself stayed pleasantly cool. Everything in the library corner seemed to breathe slowly and kindly around them.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Gita settled onto a reading pillow with a light blanket across their lap while Mr. Ade turned the lamp lower so only that gentle circle of light remained. The painted block and the ribbon book rested safe together on the shelf, and the whole corner smelled faintly of old paper and calm. Outside the fig leaves had gone still, and inside Gita’s eyes grew heavy in the kind yellow shine. Mr. Ade sat nearby in the wooden chair, and together they watched the lantern keep its steady quiet watch until sleep came near.
