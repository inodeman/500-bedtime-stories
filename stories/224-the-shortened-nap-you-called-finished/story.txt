The folded blanket still kept the shallow dent of Kira’s shoulder and the faint heat of the shortened nap they had called finished, the cloth pale and slightly damp where their cheek had rested only a short while. Late light poured through the high window and made the wood of the workshop bench glow honey-gold, and the sweet sharp scent of glue rose from open pots lined beside neat rows of paper labels. Kira stood very close to that warm dent, one hand hovering as if they might tug the blanket higher and hide the whole quiet truth beneath it. The air near the bench felt mild against their wrists. They could still hear the soft hush of their own breathing from the nap that had ended early.

Kira’s free hand rested on a smooth scrap of pine while they almost turned away, the grain cool and satiny under their palm. Uncle Idris’s footsteps sounded gentle on the floorboards, and the tin clock on the shelf began its steady soft ticking, each small click measuring the ordinary afternoon. Kira swallowed and kept their eyes on the labels waiting in their shallow tray. The workshop smelled of fresh wood shavings mixed with something calm and flower-like that drifted from a mug nearby. They straightened a crooked stack of tags so the letters lined up perfectly, as if perfect edges could keep the shortened nap from mattering.

“Kira, I brought you chamomile tea while the glue dries,” Uncle Idris said, setting the warm mug down so the steam curled upward in the dusty light. Kira’s shoulders lifted once. The steam carried the gentle chamomile smell across the bench and wrapped around them like another soft blanket. They touched the tin lid of the tea tin that sat open beside the mug; the metal felt cool and faintly ridged under their fingertip, a quiet texture that steadied them. Hiro, who had been sorting wooden pegs at the far end, looked up with a small smile but said nothing yet. The tin clock kept time without hurry.

Kira drew a slow breath that tasted of wood and tea. “Uncle Idris,” they said, voice quiet but clear, “I shortened my nap and then I called it finished. I almost hid it under the blanket.” The words left their mouth and seemed to settle gently among the labels and the pots of glue. Uncle Idris nodded once, the light catching the kind lines at the corners of his eyes. No teasing came. He simply reached for a clean cloth and wiped a smear of glue from the bench edge so the surface shone again. Kira felt the mild warmth of the room on their bare arms and the solid floor steady under their feet.

“Thank you for telling me the plain truth, Kira,” Uncle Idris answered, his voice low and even. “We can mend what can be mended.” He guided their hands toward a half-finished wooden frame that needed fresh labels pressed straight. The glue smelled mild now that it had begun to set, and the paper labels felt dry and slightly fuzzy at the edges. Kira pressed one label carefully into place, feeling the slight give of the wood beneath. Hiro slid two matching pegs across the bench so they rested side by side, a small calm motion that fit the quiet room. The tin clock ticked on, soft as a heartbeat.

Together they worked without rush. Kira held the frame steady while Uncle Idris dabbed fresh glue along a loose corner, the brush making a tiny wet sound against the grain. Light from the window warmed the top of Kira’s hair and made the drying glue glisten like clear water. “The corner will hold now,” Uncle Idris said. Kira answered, “It feels stronger already.” Hiro added a third peg so the three lined up in a neat row, and the air stayed gentle around them. Every careful press of paper and wood seemed to smooth the earlier hurry of the shortened nap into something peaceful again.

Kira noticed how the labels caught the light differently once they sat flat and true. They ran one fingertip along a finished edge and felt the cool smoothness of sealed wood. Uncle Idris poured a little more tea, and the chamomile scent rose once more, softer now. No one spoke of mistakes or missing minutes. Instead they simply kept mending: a label realigned, a drop of glue wiped clean, a wooden joint held until it set. The workshop held only the quiet sounds of careful hands and the distant tin clock. Kira’s breathing matched that steady rhythm.

When the last label sat straight, Kira stepped back and felt the mild air move across their cheeks. Uncle Idris rested a hand lightly on their shoulder for a moment, warm and sure. “We finished the frame together,” he said. Kira smiled and answered, “It looks ready for whatever comes next.” Hiro gave a small contented nod and began to gather the unused pegs into their box. The light in the room had grown softer, and the blanket on the nearby stool no longer looked like something to hide. Everything that needed mending had been tended with quiet care.

Kira and Uncle Idris washed the brushes in a shallow pan of warm water that smelled faintly of soap and wood. The water felt silky against Kira’s fingers. They dried their hands on a soft towel and looked once more at the finished frame resting in the center of the bench. Hiro closed the peg box with a gentle click. Outside the window the sky held the last pale gold of day. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Kira felt the calm settle all the way through them as the tin clock marked one more peaceful minute and the workshop breathed its quiet scents of wood and tea.
