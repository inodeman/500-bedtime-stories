The uneven pot rested on the kitchen table in the soft yellow light from the lamp, its sides sloping a little more on one side than the other like a gentle hill, and Delia ran a finger along the cool rim where the clay had dried in soft waves that caught every bit of glow. Delia had shaped it that afternoon while sitting close to Nana Ruth, pressing thumbs into the wet earth and turning the wheel slowly until the form stood ready on its own. Now the pot held a folded cloth inside to cushion it, and Delia smoothed one wrinkle out of the cloth with careful palms that felt the fabric grow flat again. The air in the room stayed mild and still as evening settled, and Delia looked at the pot for a long quiet minute before the walk began.

They packed the pot carefully into a basket whose wicker handle Delia gripped tightly, feeling the rough weave press into their fingers as they stepped out onto the evening lane with Nana Ruth. Lanterns already glowed ahead along the path, casting warm pools of light that made the gravel sparkle faintly underfoot, and Delia walked close enough that Nana Ruth’s scarf brushed their sleeve now and then. Families moved in slow pairs and small groups, their own lanterns swinging gently, and the whole lane smelled briefly of tomato soup drifting from an open kitchen window where someone stirred a pot for supper. Delia kept the basket steady against their side and matched Nana Ruth’s unhurried pace under the soft sky.

Delia held the basket a little tighter as other walkers passed with bright lanterns, the light flickering across coats and smiling faces, and they felt shy about the way the pot leaned just enough to notice. Nana Ruth stayed kind beside them and never asked Delia to hurry or speak louder, simply walking at the same gentle speed while the mild night air cooled their cheeks. Somewhere in a nearby garden a cat’s collar bell gave one clear jingle as the animal slipped between flower stems, and Delia listened to that small sound before looking down again at the covered pot. The path felt safe and familiar under their shoes.

“What if they notice how it leans and think it is not finished?” Delia whispered, their voice nearly lost in the soft shuffle of many feet on the lane. Nana Ruth answered in the same quiet tone, “You can show it if you choose, Delia, or keep it just for us. I think it is lovely exactly as it stands.” Warm lantern light painted golden edges on the leaves that hung low overhead, and Delia breathed the fresh evening air while staying near Nana Ruth’s side. They took three more slow steps together before the path widened a little near an old stone wall.

Further along they met Willa who carried a lantern of her own, its glass sides still faintly warm when Delia glanced close enough to feel the leftover heat on the air, and the two walked side by side for a stretch of the path. Delia felt the shy feeling rise again as the pot shifted once inside the basket, yet Nana Ruth stayed right there without pushing them forward. Willa pointed toward a bright open space under an oak where a few neighbors had paused to look at handmade things resting on a low bench. The light from many lanterns made the leaves glow green-gold above them.

At that quiet gathering spot Delia paused and lifted the pot a little so the warm lantern glow could touch its uneven sides and show the soft waves in the clay. In a voice that could be heard over the gentle talk around them, Delia said, “This is my pot. I made it today with Nana Ruth, and it came out a little uneven, but I still wanted to show it.” Nana Ruth nodded kindly without any push for a bigger speech, her hand resting light and steady on Delia’s shoulder while the night temperature settled cooler around their ankles. Willa answered right away, “I like the waves on the side, Delia. They make it look like it belongs to the evening.”

Others nearby smiled softly at the pot without any fuss or long stares, and Delia felt a small ease settle through their shoulders as they set it down briefly on the low wall that still held a trace of the day’s earlier warmth. Quiet conversations mixed with the distant rustle of leaves high in the oak, and Delia watched the lanterns bobbing like friendly low stars farther along the continuing lane. Nana Ruth stayed close the whole time and offered only a shared glance that felt calm, never turning the moment into anything larger than the pot itself. Delia lifted the basket again when they were ready.

They walked on with the basket held comfortably now, Delia feeling the evening deepen into deeper blues under the wide sky while the cool air stayed pleasant on their bare arms as more families joined with soft greetings. Delia talked a little longer with Willa about how the clay had felt cool and smooth when it was still wet and how the sides had decided on their own gentle lean, and Nana Ruth listened without any hurry or added words. The lane curved past quiet gardens where soft window lights shone out and the gravel crunched steadily under every careful step they took together.

Back through their own gate Delia carried the pot inside and set it carefully near the sill where the cloth could cover it once more and keep the dust away. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Nana Ruth poured warm mugs of cocoa as the room’s cozy temperature wrapped around them both, and Delia rested a hand on the pot’s rim one last time before bedtime, glad they had spoken the small true words while the uneven clay stayed exactly itself under the gentle indoor light.
