Ms. Okonkwo stood at the kitchen table already busy with her task, spreading pale butter in even layers across several squares of toast while the open jam jar waited close by and the shared pencil tin sat in the middle of the wood. Soft afternoon light poured through the window and warmed the surface until the butter gleamed. Golda entered the room with slow steps and climbed into the chair across from Ms. Okonkwo, resting their arms on the cool table edge that felt smooth under their sleeves. The air felt gentle and still around Golda's shoulders as they watched the knife move back and forth. Ms. Okonkwo glanced up once with a quiet smile and returned to her careful work without rushing.

Golda kept both hands below the table edge at first because a long blue ribbon from the gift drawer wound tightly around their fingers and wrists in a messy tangle they had made without asking. The ribbon felt cool and silky wherever it touched Golda's skin, and each small movement pulled a little more against their palms. Golda considered sliding the whole knotted length deeper into their lap so no one would see it beside the jam and butter. Warmth from the nearby toast plate reached Golda's knees in a pleasant wave. They sat very still and listened to their own quiet breathing for a calm moment while the ribbon stayed hidden.

Ms. Okonkwo finished one toast square and reached for a small pot that sat near the jam, lifting the lid so the sweet smell of honey rose into the kitchen air. Golda watched the golden threads stretch from the spoon and felt a gentle heat on their face from the open pot. Outside in the yard the sound of a gate latch clicked once and then everything fell quiet again. Golda shifted in the chair and the ribbon tightened around one thumb with a tiny squeeze. Ms. Okonkwo set the honey pot down carefully and looked across the table with kind eyes that asked no hard questions yet and simply waited.

"Golda, your hands seem busy with something under there," Ms. Okonkwo said in a calm low voice that matched the peaceful kitchen. Golda felt the wooden chair solid beneath them and the mild temperature of the room wrapping around their arms. The light from the window brightened a little as a cloud moved past and left a brighter patch on the jam jar. Golda brought both hands up slowly into view so the blue tangle showed clearly against the shared pencil tin. Their heart beat steady and plain while they kept their gaze on Ms. Okonkwo's face.

"I used the ribbon from the gift drawer without asking," Golda said. "I wanted to make a border for a drawing I did with the pencils from the tin, but it twisted all around me, and I almost hid it under the table so you would not notice." Ms. Okonkwo listened without any change in her gentle expression and without a single teasing word. She reached across with a chalky hand, the dry powdery texture left from earlier writing on the board in the next room, and rested it lightly near Golda's wrists. Golda felt that dry softness against their own skin and did not pull away. The jam jar glowed ruby in the light.

"Thank you for telling me the plain truth, Golda," Ms. Okonkwo said. "We can sit right here and loosen every loop together." Golda nodded and they began the slow work of finding each crossing place in the blue ribbon with careful fingers. The silky fabric slid free bit by bit under their joined care. No hurry touched the quiet table or the space between the toast and the pencil tin. Golda breathed the faint scent of butter and jam while they worked side by side and felt their shoulders drop lower in easy relief as the tangle grew smaller.

One by one the knots gave way until the long blue ribbon lay smooth and nearly whole between the toast plate and the shared pencil tin. Golda ran a careful finger along the full length and found only a small fray near one end. "We can fold that part under and press it flat so it is tidy again," Ms. Okonkwo said. Golda helped make the neat fold with both hands and held it firmly while the gentle press set the mend. Soft light from the window made the ribbon shimmer like quiet water beside the jam. The kitchen stayed warm and safe around them both as they finished the last tuck.

They slipped the mended ribbon into a clean paper sleeve from near the pencil tin so it could return to the gift drawer later in proper order. Golda wiped a small crumb from the table with a napkin and felt the smooth wood under their palm. "May I help you finish the toast now?" Golda asked. Ms. Okonkwo smiled and passed the butter knife handle first. Together they set the last toast squares neatly beside the jam jar. The mild air moved once when the curtain lifted on a tiny breeze and then settled back.

When the toast was ready Golda sat back with a small cup of warm milk and watched the blue sleeve rest safely by the pencil tin. Evening gathered slowly outside and the kitchen grew quieter. Golda felt the cup's gentle heat against their fingers while they listened to the house settle. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Golda closed their eyes for a peaceful breath and knew the ribbon rested easy now.
