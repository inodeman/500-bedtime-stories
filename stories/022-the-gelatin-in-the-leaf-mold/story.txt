The mild evening air drifted through the open cottage window and brushed the windowsill with a soft coolness that touched the glass jars, the wooden trays, and the hanging lengths of drying wool. Cosima stood on the low stool beside that sill and felt the gentle temperature change on their bare arms as the light faded to a warm honey color across the shelves. Cosima leaned closer to the cloth-covered mold and saw the faint outline of the leaf-shaped gelatin resting inside. The ferryman sat in the wooden chair a few steps away, mending a small coil of rope with steady hands, and the cottage smelled faintly of clean soap and wood. Cosima wanted to lift the cloth right then to see if the gelatin had set, yet they stayed still for a moment and listened to the quiet creak of the floorboards under their own shifting feet.

Cosima reached one careful finger toward the edge of the cloth that hid the leaf-shaped gelatin cooling in its mold, then stopped and looked at the ferryman. The warm light from the last of the day made the jars glow like little moons along the sill. “May I peek just once?” Cosima asked in a soft voice. The ferryman smiled without looking up from the rope and answered, “It needs its own slow time, Cosima.” Cosima nodded and drew their hand back, feeling the smooth wood of the stool under their palms. Elif sat cross-legged on a cushion nearby, winding a bit of wool, and the air between them all felt peaceful and unhurried. Cosima kept their eyes on the covered mold and breathed in deeply, catching the fresh scent of mint leaves that someone had crushed earlier for tea.

Cosima turned away from the mold on purpose and picked up a small tray of buttons and beads that waited near the drying wool. The ferryman set the rope aside and said, “Shall we sort these while we wait, Cosima?” Cosima answered, “Yes, that sounds good,” and their fingers found a cool round glass bead among the others. The bead felt smooth and solid against Cosima’s skin as they rolled it gently between thumb and finger. Elif held out an empty jar and Cosima dropped the glass bead inside with a tiny click. Outside the window a soft dusk settled, and the temperature of the room stayed pleasantly mild so that no one needed an extra shawl yet. Cosima kept sorting, naming the colors quietly to themself while the leaf-shaped gelatin stayed covered and finished cooling in its own way.

Cosima chose three small piles of wool fluff and brought them near their nose one by one without speaking any worry aloud. First came the clean grassy smell of the undyed wool, then the faint flower scent of a pale pink strand, and last the warm dusty smell of the brown bundle that had dried longest on the sill. The ferryman watched and said, “Those are fine things to notice, Cosima.” Cosima smiled and answered, “I like how each one is different.” A small cat wandered in from the kitchen, and the clear sound of a cat’s collar bell rang once as it jumped onto the cushion beside Elif. Cosima listened to that gentle jingle and kept their hands busy with the wool so the gelatin could stay undisturbed under its cloth.

Cosima carried a shallow tray of empty jars over to the windowsill and began wiping each one with a soft cloth while the ferryman held the larger ones steady. The last daylight thinned into a cooler blue that touched Cosima’s hair and made the drying wool look softer still. Cosima worked slowly, feeling the cool glass under the cloth and hearing only the quiet swish of fabric. Elif hummed a short tune without words, and Cosima joined in for a few notes before stopping to listen again to the room. The leaf-shaped gelatin cooling in its mold remained covered the whole time, and Cosima did not lift the edge even once. The ferryman nodded approval when Cosima set the clean jars back in a neat row and said nothing more about waiting.

Cosima sat down on the cushion next to Elif and ran both hands along a long strip of drying wool that felt springy and light. The room held a gentle warmth from the stove that had burned low earlier, and Cosima’s shoulders relaxed into that comfort. “Will it be ready soon?” Cosima asked the ferryman in a voice barely above a whisper. The ferryman replied, “Soon enough, Cosima. Keep your hands busy a little longer.” Cosima nodded and began folding the wool into soft loops, stacking them carefully so air could still move between the folds. Outside, the garden rested in the growing dark, and no wind troubled the quiet. Cosima felt proud of leaving the mold alone and stayed close to the windowsill without touching the cloth.

Cosima stood once more and arranged the trays so that none blocked the view of the covered mold, then stepped back to admire the neat order. The air near the window had cooled another degree, and Cosima rubbed their arms lightly to keep the pleasant chill from settling too deep. Elif offered a small cup of water, and Cosima drank while watching the cloth stay perfectly still. The ferryman rose and lit a single candle on the table, casting a calm circle of light that reached just to the edge of the sill. Cosima breathed evenly and counted the jars again for the simple pleasure of knowing where everything belonged. The leaf-shaped gelatin cooling in its mold continued its slow work underneath the cloth, and Cosima felt no need to hurry it.

Cosima returned to the stool and rested both elbows on the windowsill among the jars and the drying wool, looking out at the dark garden for a long quiet stretch. The temperature of the glass under their arms felt cool and steady. Elif came to stand beside them, and together they watched a moth flutter past without landing. The ferryman spoke once more in his calm tone, “You chose well to wait, Cosima.” Cosima answered, “It feels nicer this way,” and meant every word. The cat’s soft presence remained nearby, and the whole cottage seemed to breathe with them. Cosima kept their hands folded and let the final minutes pass without a single peek.

When Cosima finally lifted the cloth, the leaf-shaped gelatin had set into a perfect green shape that caught the candlelight. Cosima smiled and showed it to the ferryman and to Elif, who both leaned close to see. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Cosima carried the mold to the table with careful steps, feeling the smooth finish of the gelatin edge against the air, and everyone sat together in the gentle light while the cottage rested around them.
