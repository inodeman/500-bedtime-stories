The quilt squares lay in a careful pile on the low wooden bench, each cotton edge catching the pale gold light that slipped between the masts. Delia bent so close that they could see the tiny stitched boat on the blue square and the way its white sail only pointed the right way when the green wave square sat beside it. Delia traced the lighthouse tower on the red square with one slow fingertip and noticed how the yellow square of sunny cliff had to come next or the whole harbor picture would tilt sideways. The cloth felt cool and dry under Delia’s hand, and a faint breeze lifted the top corner so that it fluttered like a quiet wing. Delia smiled and kept looking, counting the squares once more without touching them yet.

Out on the open boards of the harbor dock the air held the low creak of ropes and the soft splash of water against the pilings. Delia stood up and watched baskets being passed from hand to hand up toward the waiting boat, their shapes dark against the bright sky. Uncle Idris stood near the edge with his sleeves rolled, and Willa waited beside a stack of empty baskets, her shoes quiet on the planks. Delia carried the quilt squares over and set them down gently so that every piece stayed in the order they had practiced at home. The boards felt warm under Delia’s bare feet where the sun had rested all afternoon.

“If we hurry the order the picture will come out wrong,” Delia said, holding the first blue square steady. Willa nodded and answered, “Then we can split the work into two slow jobs.” Uncle Idris only smiled and kept one hand ready on the rope. Delia felt the mild heat of the afternoon sun on their shoulders while they and Willa decided that Delia would place the left-hand squares one by one and Willa would place the right-hand squares the same careful way. The cat that lived near the fish shed wandered past, and the small sound of a cat’s collar bell rang once like a tiny silver note before the animal settled in a sunny spot.

Delia began their slow job, laying the blue boat square exactly where the big background cloth waited, then smoothing one wrinkle out of the cloth with the flat of their palm so the surface stayed flat and true. Willa matched the pace on her side, setting the green wave square down only after Delia’s piece had settled. The cloth drank the light and turned soft colors toward the open water. Delia breathed evenly and named each square under their breath so the order stayed clear. Between them the picture of the safe little harbor began to grow row by row without any piece sliding out of place.

Uncle Idris watched the baskets waiting to be filled and spoke only when Delia glanced up. “When you need the knot for the lid cord, call for a square knot,” he said in his calm low voice. Delia nodded and kept placing the red lighthouse square next to the yellow cliff square, feeling how the cotton edges kissed each other just right. Willa whispered the names of her own squares so the two jobs stayed even. The dock smelled briefly of tomato soup drifting from the small cook shack up the hill, a warm friendly scent that mixed with the salt air and made Delia’s shoulders loosen.

Delia reached for the next square and felt the smooth coolness of its face before setting it down. Willa did the same on her side, never rushing past Delia’s careful count. “Three more on my side,” Delia said softly. Willa answered, “Two more on mine, and then the sky strip.” Uncle Idris offered nothing but the quiet name again when the cord appeared: “Square knot when you are ready.” Delia and Willa worked on, the picture growing complete under their matched slow hands while the water lapped in steady rhythm against the dock.

When the last square settled into place the whole harbor scene shone clear across the cloth, boat and waves and lighthouse and cliff joined in perfect order. Delia and Willa lifted the finished top together and folded it with long gentle strokes so no corner folded crooked. Uncle Idris held open the nearest basket. Delia curled their fingers around the rough woven curve of a wicker handle and steadied the basket while Willa eased the folded quilt inside. The handle pressed firm and familiar against Delia’s palm as they guided the basket upward into waiting hands on the boat.

Together they passed two more baskets the same unhurried way, each one packed with the ordered squares now safely rolled and knotted. Delia stayed close beside Willa and Uncle Idris until every basket had risen out of reach and the boat’s deck held them all. The evening air cooled against Delia’s cheeks and the light turned the color of soft honey across the water. Delia leaned against the warm rail for a moment and felt the quiet pride of work finished side by side.

Later they stepped into the little harbor room that looked out over the quiet dock, and Delia sat with Willa and Uncle Idris on the wide bench. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Delia rested their head against the cool glass for a breath and watched the last pale light fade while the room held the gentle hush of settled work and the easy company of the two people who had shared every slow step.
