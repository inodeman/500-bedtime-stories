The drum sat plain and round on the ferry bench, its pale top stretched smooth under the mild afternoon light that slipped through the tall windows and laid soft gold across every curve. Right beside it the wooden spoon lay ready, handle long and even, bowl curved so it could serve as a second beater if lifted. Zola leaned close enough to see the fine wood grain swirling like quiet rivers on the spoon and the faint circles pressed into the drum’s side from earlier careful hands. The light warmed the air just enough that Zola felt a gentle temperature on their cheeks while they studied both objects without lifting them yet. The ferry moved in an easy sway, and the folded blanket waited nearby like a soft square of calm.

Zola settled onto the ferry bench and drew the folded blanket a little closer, fingertips finding a quilt stitch that rose in one small firm ridge along the edge. That texture felt steady and kind under their thumb as they sat still. Maude scooted near so their knees almost touched. “The drum and the spoon look ready for soft songs,” Maude said in a voice barely louder than the water outside. Miss Dalca rested her hands in her lap and watched the light change on the bench. Zola kept one palm on the blanket and breathed in the quiet of the moving ferry while the wooden objects stayed side by side in easy reach.

Zola lifted the wooden spoon for a moment, felt its solid cool weight, then set it back beside the drum and thought carefully. The notion that only one person might take both things first did not sit well inside them, because Maude sat right there wanting a share too. “If only one of us is served the drum and the spoon first, it will not feel fair,” Zola said so the words carried gently across the bench. A faint lavender smell rose from the folded blanket, as though the cloth had rested near garden flowers before the crossing began. Miss Dalca simply nodded once and kept her peaceful silence while the ferry light stayed warm on everyone’s shoulders.

Maude looked at the drum and then at Zola with open, waiting eyes. “How can we make it even then?” Maude asked, voice soft as the slow rock of the boat. Zola considered the single drum and the one wooden spoon that could act as second beater, turning the problem over like a smooth stone in their mind. The air held a mild coolness near the window glass that brushed Zola’s wrist whenever the ferry turned a little. Miss Dalca stayed quiet, offering no extra words, only the calm of her presence on the shared bench.

Zola decided the turns themselves could become smaller and more even so no one waited long and Maude stayed fully included from the start. “We can each tap just a few quiet beats, then pass both things right away,” Zola said, already sliding the drum a short distance toward the middle of the blanket. The wooden spoon followed so both objects rested in easy reach of every hand. A low tin clock ticked somewhere near the ferry door, its steady tiny sound marking the gentle minutes without hurry. Miss Dalca watched the choice take shape and simply let it stand.

Zola began with three soft taps of the wooden spoon against the drum, the sound round and low like a distant heartbeat under the ferry roof. Then they passed both objects straight to Maude, who answered with three taps of their own before sliding everything onward. Miss Dalca received the drum and spoon next and added three careful beats that blended into the same calm pattern. The light through the windows had grown softer and more golden, laying long gentle shapes across the bench and the folded blanket. Zola felt the pleasant temperature of the wood each time it returned to their hands.

They kept the small even shares going, round after round, so the drum and the wooden spoon traveled in a quiet circle that never left anyone out. Zola noticed how the spoon handle warmed slightly from their shared palms and how the drum top answered every light touch with a muted thump. “This way everyone hears the song together,” Zola said once while passing the pair again. Maude smiled and tapped their three beats in answer. Outside the water made a hushed rushing sound against the ferry hull, and inside the bench felt steady under them all.

After many slow passes Zola rested both hands on the folded blanket and watched a thin shadow from the window frame move the width of a thumb across the wood of the bench. The movement was so small and peaceful it matched the easy breathing of the three of them sitting side by side. Miss Dalca held the drum and spoon for one last short turn, then returned them to the center without speaking any extra words. The air near the blanket stayed mild, and Zola’s shoulders felt loose and ready for rest while the ferry carried them onward under the fading day light.

Zola tucked the drum and the wooden spoon safely against the folded blanket so they would not roll, then leaned back and let their eyes half close. Maude rested a head lightly near Zola’s shoulder, and Miss Dalca kept her quiet watch over the calm bench. The lantern by the window glowed softly. The ferry rocked like a cradle, the last warmth of daylight slipped away, and Zola felt the whole gentle scene settle around them like another soft layer of the blanket itself while the water whispered on and on.
