A small soft tick drifted through the craft corner, no louder than a drop of paste landing on paper. Pilar noticed it at once and held still on the wooden bench while the sound came again, slow and steady from somewhere deeper in the house. Warm evening air brushed Pilar’s arms. They looked toward the wooden rack where the leather boots rested under a light cloth, each boot wearing its thin coat of cream that still needed quiet time. Papers waited in tidy stacks, and the paste jar sat open with a mild sweet scent rising. Soft lamp light pooled across the floorboards. Pilar rested both palms flat and felt the cool temperature of the seat beneath them. They breathed in and out, listening, and the same gentle tick answered once more.

Pilar leaned forward a little and watched the covered boots with careful eyes. The cloth looked almost still, yet a faint shine showed at one edge where the cream met the leather. Auntie Luz sat nearby, her chair creaking once as she shifted. Pilar smelled the clean paper stacked beside them and heard the quiet hush of the room. “Auntie Luz,” Pilar said softly, “may I lift the cloth and check the boots now?” The words floated out like feathers. Cool air touched the back of Pilar’s neck while they waited. Dust motes drifted in the golden light above the paste jar. Pilar kept their hands folded so they would not reach too soon.

Auntie Luz smiled and set down her own sheet of paper. The grain of the bench pressed lightly under Pilar’s fingertips when they slid one hand along the edge. “Not yet, Pilar,” Auntie Luz answered in a warm low voice. “The cream is still settling into the leather. It finishes in its own time.” Pilar nodded and felt the smooth wood under their skin. A thin ribbon of cooler air moved past their ankles. They looked again at the wooden rack and the quiet shape of the boots. The lamp cast a steady circle of light that did not flicker. Pilar drew a slow breath and decided to leave the cloth exactly where it was.

Pilar chose a quiet task instead and reached for a square of soft blue paper. They began to fold it into a small star, pressing each crease with the side of one finger. The paper felt dry and slightly rough against their skin. Auntie Luz watched with kind eyes. “I will make this star while I wait,” Pilar said. “That will keep my hands busy.” The paste jar gave off its mild scent again when they shifted a scrap. Warm light from the lamp rested on Pilar’s hair. They folded another corner and heard only the soft rustle of paper. Outside the open window the night stayed gentle and still.

While Pilar worked, a faint smell of tomato vines drifted in on the evening breeze, green and earthy and familiar from the garden pots just beyond the sill. Pilar paused and breathed it in deeply, then returned to the blue star. Auntie Luz hummed a tiny tune without words. The wooden rack held the boots steady under their cloth. Pilar felt the temperature of the room stay pleasantly mild. They pressed the final fold and smoothed the star flat against the bench. Soft shadows stretched longer across the floor. Pilar kept their eyes on the paper rather than the rack so the cream could keep working alone.

A far-off clock began to sound its slow hours, and Pilar chose to listen for that distant voice instead of any nearer tick. The notes arrived muffled and calm. “Do you hear the far clock, Auntie Luz?” Pilar asked. “I am listening only to that one.” Auntie Luz nodded and answered, “Yes, Pilar, it keeps good company while things finish.” The paste lid clicked softly when Auntie Luz closed it for the night. Cooler air slipped across Pilar’s wrists. They set the finished paper star beside the stacks and rested both hands in their lap. The lamp light stayed golden and even across the craft corner.

Then a small clear sound reached them: a cat’s collar bell rang once from the hallway, bright and tiny as a raindrop on glass. Pilar smiled without turning. The boots remained covered and quiet on the wooden rack. Auntie Luz rose and stretched, her dress brushing the floor with a whisper. Pilar felt the mild warmth of the room settle around their shoulders again. They picked up a second scrap of paper and began a slow fold just to keep company with the waiting. Soft light touched the edge of the cloth. Pilar breathed evenly and let the moments pass without hurry.

After a longer stretch of hush, Auntie Luz glanced toward the rack and then back to Pilar. “I think the cream has finished its work now,” she said. Pilar stood carefully and walked the few steps to the wooden rack. The leather boots looked ready beneath the cloth. Pilar lifted the cover with both hands and felt the smooth cool surface of the boots. A soft shine answered the lamp light. They ran one finger gently along the side and found the cream gone soft and even. Auntie Luz stood close. Pilar’s cheeks felt pleasantly warm from the quiet joy of waiting well.

Pilar carried the boots to the bench and set them side by side so they could rest upright. The paper star sat nearby like a small blue friend. Auntie Luz placed one hand on Pilar’s shoulder. The whole craft corner smelled clean and calm. Outside the window the garden air stayed mild. Pilar leaned against Auntie Luz and watched the boots gleam under the steady lamp. They felt sleepy and content. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Pilar closed their eyes for a moment and listened to the far clock once more while the room held them in gentle quiet.
