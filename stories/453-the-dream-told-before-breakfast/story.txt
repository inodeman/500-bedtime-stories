"Yelena, come sit by the warm hearth with me before the day grows busy," Auntie June said, her voice low and even while the unhurried clock ticked once and then seemed to rest.

Yelena settled onto the low stool nearest the cottage hearth, feeling the mild heat of the morning fire touch their cheeks and the backs of their hands as pale light spilled across the stone floor in quiet bands. The air held a faint, pleasant scent of toasted sesame from the seeds Auntie June had warmed earlier for the breakfast bread, and Yelena breathed it in while watching the slow orange glow of the embers. Auntie June wore her familiar cotton apron, its soft worn fabric brushing against the wooden table edge each time she moved, and Yelena noticed how the cloth caught the light like a gentle promise of ordinary care. Outside, a gate latch clicked once with a small clean sound as someone far down the path closed it carefully, yet the cottage stayed peaceful and still. Yelena felt a bubble of bigger news rising inside their chest, something bright they wanted to share at once, but the steady calm of the room and Auntie June’s patient face made them press their lips together and wait. The clock on the mantel kept its unhurried pace, each soft tick reminding Yelena there was no rush at all. Auntie June poured warm milk into two cups, the steam rising in thin curls that smelled faintly sweet, and Yelena cupped their hands around one mug just to feel the solid heat settle into their palms.

Auntie June began the dream told slowly before breakfast, speaking as if each word needed its own small space to rest. “I dreamed I walked through a meadow where the grass was the color of soft butter and every flower leaned toward me as if it already knew my name,” she said, and Yelena listened while the fire gave a quiet crackle that sounded almost like a friendly greeting. The dream unrolled without hurry: Auntie June described a wooden bridge painted pale blue that crossed a shallow stream whose water sounded like distant bells when the pebbles shifted underneath. Yelena’s toes curled inside their slippers against the cool stone, yet they kept their eyes on Auntie June’s face and did not speak the bigger news waiting behind their teeth. The cotton apron hung smooth against Auntie June’s skirt, and Yelena could almost feel the soft weave of it in their mind whenever Auntie June’s hand rested there. Outside light grew a little brighter through the windows, warm and honey-colored, and the cottage hearth sent up tiny sparks that winked out before they rose far. Yelena thought of Cosima, who lived two cottages away and often brought smooth river stones to trade, yet even that friendly thought stayed quiet while the dream continued its gentle path. Auntie June paused only long enough to stir the pot of oats once, the spoon making a soft circle against the metal, then went on describing a path of flat gray stones that led to a small wooden door carved with leaves.

Yelena felt the urge to interrupt grow stronger for a moment, their shoulders lifting with the weight of the bigger news, but they pressed their hands flat on their knees and kept listening all the way through. Auntie June’s dream moved next into a kitchen that looked almost like their own, except the shelves held jars of glowing jam that smelled of summer berries even though it was still early morning in the real cottage. “In the dream I opened one jar and the jam tasted like sunlight mixed with rain,” Auntie June said with a small smile, and Yelena nodded without speaking, noticing how the hearth light made the edges of the table gleam. The unhurried clock ticked again, slower than any clock Yelena had ever known, and the sound seemed to wrap around the words like a soft blanket. Yelena’s bigger news fluttered again, something about a nest of tiny birds Cosima had shown them yesterday near the garden wall, yet they held it back and watched the steam from the milk cups rise and fade. The temperature near the fire stayed kindly warm, never too hot, and Yelena leaned a little closer so the heat could reach their elbows. Auntie June described how the dream kitchen window looked out onto a hill covered in low white flowers that nodded even when no wind blew, and Yelena could almost see the gentle motion.

The dream told slowly before breakfast continued as Auntie June spoke of sitting at that dream table with a loaf still warm from an unseen oven, the crust crackling softly under her fingers when she broke it. Yelena’s own fingers traced the edge of their stool, feeling the smooth worn wood, while they waited for every last detail. “Then a small bird with silver feathers landed on the windowsill and sang three clear notes before flying off toward the hill,” Auntie June said, her voice barely louder than the quiet hearth. Yelena breathed in the lingering smell of toasted sesame again and felt glad the kitchen held that quiet comfort. The bigger news pressed once more against Yelena’s tongue, yet they remembered the clock’s unhurried face and stayed silent, listening so carefully that they noticed the tiny shift of ash in the grate. Auntie June’s cotton apron moved when she reached for the butter dish, the fabric whispering against itself, and Yelena watched the motion as if it were part of the dream itself. Light from the window now reached the far wall in a soft rectangle, and the temperature of the room felt even and kind, like a held breath that never grew sharp. Yelena’s shoulders eased down as the dream kept its slow pace, and they realized the waiting itself felt almost as gentle as the story.

Auntie June described walking back across the blue bridge in the dream, the wooden planks warm under her bare feet even though morning mist still hung low over the stream. Yelena stayed close to the hearth, the heat painting a mild flush across their nose, and they did not rush a single word of their own. The gate latch sounded one more distant time somewhere beyond the garden, a small clean click that faded quickly into the quiet, yet inside the cottage everything remained still and safe. Yelena thought again of the bigger news and felt it settle lower in their chest, no longer so urgent, while Auntie June’s voice carried the dream toward its closing moments. “I woke just as the bird’s three notes came back to me on the breeze, and the kitchen around me smelled exactly like ours does now,” Auntie June finished, her last sentence landing soft as a feather on still water. Yelena waited one full heartbeat after that final word, making sure the dream had truly ended, then asked the one real question that had grown quietly inside them while they listened. “Auntie June, did the silver bird look happy when it sang?” Yelena said, their voice careful and clear in the warm air.

Auntie June smiled and answered that yes, the bird had looked perfectly content, its feathers catching imaginary sunlight before it flew. Only then did Yelena share something small of their own, speaking of the three smooth stones Cosima had left on the garden step the day before, stones that felt cool and round in the hand and seemed to hold tiny flecks of green. Yelena set the chair square to the table with a gentle push so it lined up neatly beside Auntie June’s place, the wooden legs making a soft scrape against the floor that ended almost at once. The hearth glowed with steady orange light, and the unhurried clock ticked once more as if approving the careful order of the morning. Yelena’s bigger news about the bird nest stayed quiet for another time, folded away like a letter not yet ready to open, and instead they spoke only of how the stones had felt smooth as river water under their fingertips. Auntie June listened in return, nodding slowly, and the cottage filled with the simple comfort of two people taking turns without hurry. The smell of toasted sesame drifted once more from the cooling pan, mixing with the warm milk steam, and Yelena felt the morning stretch itself like a contented cat.

They sat together a little longer while the fire settled into lower coals that gave off a soft dry heat against Yelena’s shins. Auntie June reached across and smoothed a small crease in Yelena’s sleeve, the touch light and brief, and Yelena smiled at the ordinary kindness of it. Outside the light had grown fuller yet still gentle, painting the walls in calm shades of cream and gold, and the temperature near the hearth remained pleasantly even. Yelena thought of the dream’s blue bridge and the real stones Cosima had shared, and both felt equally quiet and good inside their mind. The cotton apron hung neatly now that Auntie June had finished moving about, its soft texture visible in every careful fold. Yelena rested their chin in their hands and watched the last thin ribbons of steam leave the milk cups, content that they had listened all the way through before speaking. The unhurried clock kept its slow company, and the cottage hearth held them both in its steady circle of warmth without asking for anything more.

The morning continued its gentle unfolding as Yelena helped clear the two empty cups, the ceramic still faintly warm under their fingers, and Auntie June hummed a tune without words that matched the quiet tick of the clock. Yelena felt the bigger news still waiting somewhere safe inside them, ready for another morning when the time felt right, yet today the small shared stones and the fully heard dream were enough. Light slipped across the table in a final soft band, and the air near the window stayed cool enough to feel fresh against Yelena’s wrist when they reached toward it. Auntie June set the butter dish back in its place with a soft click of pottery on wood, and Yelena watched the simple motion with the same careful attention they had given the dream. The hearth coals glowed lower now, their heat a mild steady presence rather than a bright flame, and the whole room smelled clean and calm. Yelena stood close beside Auntie June for one more quiet moment, grateful for the space the listening had made between them.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Yelena watched its gentle light mingle with the morning sun across the sill while the cottage hearth kept its low warm company and the unhurried clock marked another peaceful minute, and they felt the quiet settle around their shoulders like a well-loved blanket ready for the rest of the slow bright day.
