"Come under the roof where the wind cannot push," Neighbor Sami said, and Paz walked into the harbor shelter on the lee side of the wind, feeling the sudden coolness of the still air settle over their arms like a soft blanket.

Inside the shelter the wooden boards smelled faintly of old rope and dry salt, yet a warm thread of cinnamon drifted from a small tin pot resting on the low shelf, and Paz breathed it in while they looked around at the neat stacks of folded blankets and the careful way everything had been set against the damp. Neighbor Sami had already taken care of the letter someone had read aloud twice because Paz liked the middle; the paper lay smooth and dry on a flat crate, its edges no longer curled, and a smooth stone held one corner so the harbor breeze that tried to sneak around the posts could not lift it. Paz stood close to Neighbor Sami and watched the water beyond the open side of the shelter, where late light lay in long silver lines and little waves made quiet tapping sounds against the pilings.

Paz ran one finger along the edge of the crate and felt how solid it was, then they noticed the walnut-smooth spoon resting beside two blue cups, its handle polished by many careful hands so that it felt almost like silk under their palm. "I brought the cinnamon drink while it was still hot," Neighbor Sami said in a low voice that matched the hush of the sheltered place, and Paz nodded, grateful for the warmth that rose in gentle curls. They remembered how the letter had sounded the first time and the second time, how the middle part held a description of a green hill and a path of flat stones that Paz had asked to hear again, and how Neighbor Sami had read those sentences slowly so every word stayed clear.

The light inside the shelter was soft gold, filtered through the slats above, and it rested on Paz’s shoulders while they thought that a quick thank-you would feel too thin for the care already given to the letter. Paz looked at the open gate that led from the path down to the shelter and saw that it hung slightly ajar, so they stepped over, pressed it shut with both hands, and heard the solid click of a gate latch settling into place. That small useful thing had not been asked for, yet it kept the space even quieter and safer from stray gusts, and Paz returned to the crate feeling the cool boards under their shoes.

"The middle of the letter still makes me picture the green hill," Paz said, and Neighbor Sami smiled and poured a little of the warm drink into one cup, the steam carrying more of that gentle spice into the calm air. Paz lifted the walnut-smooth spoon and stirred once, feeling the even glide of the wood against the side of the cup, then set the spoon down again without spilling a drop. Outside, gulls called far away, but inside the lee of the wind the only near sound was the soft settle of the latch and the tiny tick of cooling wood.

Paz sat on the bench beside Neighbor Sami and watched the letter stay perfectly still under its stone, the paper looking almost bright in the fading light that slipped through the open side of the shelter. They thought again of how Neighbor Sami had smoothed every crease and placed the letter where no spray could reach it, and the care felt large and kind inside Paz’s chest. "I noticed you kept the letter dry and flat for me," Paz said plainly, looking at Neighbor Sami’s face in the quiet gold light, and the words sat steady between them like another solid board in the floor. Neighbor Sami answered, "I knew the middle mattered to you," and the simple sentence wrapped around Paz like the still air of the sheltered place.

A few minutes passed while Paz held the warm cup with both hands and felt the heat soak slowly into their fingers, and the cinnamon scent stayed mild and pleasant without ever growing sharp. They hummed four soft notes under their breath and then left a rest so the quiet could fill the space again, and the sound of their own hum faded into the gentle slap of water against the posts. Neighbor Sami reached over and touched the corner of the letter once more, making sure the stone still held it, and Paz watched the careful motion with calm eyes. The harbor beyond the shelter glowed with the last low light of day, turning the water the color of pale honey, and the air inside remained cool and kind on Paz’s cheeks.

Paz leaned a little closer to Neighbor Sami and felt the solid warmth of the shared bench, then they set their empty cup aside and folded their hands in their lap while the sky outside deepened. "Thank you for reading the middle twice and for keeping the letter safe," Paz added, their voice as even as the boards underfoot, and Neighbor Sami only nodded once in the soft light. The useful click of the gate latch still seemed to linger in the quiet, a small proof that Paz had given something back without being asked, and the letter rested exactly where it belonged. Everything in the harbor shelter felt settled and enough, from the dry blankets to the blue cups to the stone that guarded the paper.

Paz rested their head for a moment against the post beside them and listened to the far-off water sounds that never grew loud, and the cool air brushed their hair like a slow breath. Neighbor Sami began to gather the cups with slow hands, and Paz helped by placing the walnut-smooth spoon back on the shelf where it belonged, feeling once more the even polish of the wood. No hurry touched the shelter on the lee side of the wind, only the steady presence of the letter, the friend who had cared for it, and the child who had noticed.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Paz watched the small steady flame throw a circle of warm light across the floorboards and across the edge of the letter, and the cinnamon scent thinned to a memory while the air stayed cool and kind, and they closed their eyes for a peaceful breath inside the quiet harbor shelter.
