Soft afternoon light drifted in through the round window and settled gently across the library rug like a quiet golden shawl after the mild breeze had brushed the trees outside. Sora walked onto the thick blue and cream fibers and felt the cool air of the room touch their bare arms as they settled cross-legged near the center. The soft pile of the rug pressed warmly against Sora’s knees while faint dust motes floated in the beam of light. Sora breathed in the calm space and listened to the hush of turning pages from far shelves. Grandma Nia sat in her low chair just beyond the edge of the rug, her hands folded loosely, watching Sora with kind eyes that matched the peaceful temperature of the late day.

Sora looked toward the round window and noticed Lumen standing a little apart near the wall, close enough to hear but not quite on the rug itself. The air carried a fresh scent of basil from a small pot of leaves Grandma Nia kept on the windowsill, mixing lightly with the paper smell of the books. Sora scooted sideways so there would be more room and patted the spot beside them. “Lumen, come sit here with me,” Sora said softly. Lumen stepped closer, still hovering at the border of the fun, and Sora felt the smooth warmth of the rug under their palms as they waited. A tin clock on the nearby shelf ticked with a steady gentle note that marked the quiet minutes without hurry.

Grandma Nia lifted a thin book from the side table and held it out so both children could see the cover painted with stars and a winding path. Sora took the book carefully and opened it on their lap, feeling the cool pages. “This is a story we could retell so a friend was the hero too,” Sora said, glancing at Lumen who still stood half a step outside the circle of light. The temperature near the window felt softer than the rest of the room, and Sora wanted Lumen right in the middle of it. Grandma Nia only smiled and leaned back, letting the children decide what came next without saying a word of direction.

Sora shifted the cushions that formed a loose ring on the rug, sliding one farther out so the seating opened toward Lumen. The fabric of the cushions felt slightly fuzzy under Sora’s fingers as they rearranged the space at a slower pace than usual. “We can make the circle bigger,” Sora told Lumen, “and start the story whenever you are ready.” Lumen nodded and finally sat fully on the rug, knees touching the edge of Sora’s own. The polished pebble that Sora kept in their pocket for quiet moments pressed smooth and cool against their side when they leaned. Light from the round window glowed across Lumen’s face now that they shared the same soft patch of floor.

Grandma Nia watched without interrupting while Sora slowed the opening of the book even more, turning each page with care so no one felt rushed. “You can choose which animal speaks first if you like,” Sora said to Lumen, holding the book between them so the pictures faced both ways. Lumen’s voice came quiet but clear as they pointed to a small fox drawn under a tree. The air stayed pleasantly warm around their shoulders, and the rug fibers brushed lightly against Sora’s ankles whenever they adjusted their seat. Sora waited until Lumen had settled completely before beginning the first words of the tale out loud.

Sora began to retell the adventure, changing the hero into a kind traveler who looked a little like Lumen, brave in a gentle way and always ready to share the path. The sound of their own voice stayed low and even while the tin clock continued its calm ticking from the shelf. Lumen listened with bright eyes and added a small detail about a bridge made of moss. Sora smiled and wove that idea straight into the next sentence so Lumen’s thought became part of the heart of the story. The basil scent drifted a little stronger when a draft from the window stirred the leaves, wrapping the three of them in the same peaceful smell.

Grandma Nia remained quiet in her chair, hands resting still, while Sora and Lumen took turns choosing the next turn of the path. Sora kept the pace slow and the seating open so Lumen never felt like a guest who might be called forward as a prize. “The traveler found a lantern that shone just bright enough for two,” Sora continued, and Lumen finished the thought by saying the light helped them both find the meadow. Warmth from the afternoon sun still lingered on the wooden floorboards beyond the rug, and Sora felt it when their hand brushed the border. Every page turned with a soft whisper of paper that matched the gentle mood of the room.

When the story reached its quiet ending, Sora closed the book and rested it between them so Lumen could keep one hand on the cover. The light through the round window had grown softer and more golden, casting long calm shapes across the library rug. Sora and Lumen sat side by side without any need to hurry, simply enjoying the shared silence after the words. Grandma Nia finally spoke in her warm low voice, saying only, “That was a fine telling,” and then she rose to dim the other lamps. Sora felt the cool polished pebble once more as they shifted, a small steady reminder of the peaceful afternoon.

Sora and Lumen remained on the library rug under the round window while the last of the daylight faded into evening blue. Grandma Nia brought a small blanket that smelled clean and cotton-soft and draped it over both children’s shoulders without a word of fuss. Sora leaned a little closer to Lumen so the blanket covered them evenly and the story they had made together stayed bright in their minds. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Outside the glass the trees stood still in the mild air, and inside everything felt safe and finished for the night as Sora’s eyes grew heavy with quiet happiness.
