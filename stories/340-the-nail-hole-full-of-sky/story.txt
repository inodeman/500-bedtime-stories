The late sun laid a gentle golden light across the meadow grasses and warmed the old wooden fence posts with a mild heat that felt kind against bare arms. Violeta walked slowly beside Auntie Luz toward the fence where a metal bucket of clear water waited in the shade of a low bush. Ciro trailed a step behind them carrying a small basket. Violeta noticed how the air smelled faintly of cut grass and how the soft breeze lifted the edges of their shirt. They stopped near the bucket and let their fingertips brush the cool side of the metal while Auntie Luz set down a folded cloth. Violeta looked along the fence boards that ran between the open meadow and the quiet path home.

Violeta dipped one hand into the bucket and felt the water slide cool and smooth over their skin before they shook the drops back with a quiet splash. The surface of the water caught a bright patch of sky and wobbled. Auntie Luz handed Violeta a linen napkin so they could dry their fingers, and the cloth felt crisp and dry with a fine woven texture under their palms. Ciro peered into the bucket too. Violeta wiped carefully and then turned toward the fence again, curious about the spaces between the boards. A soft ticking reached them from the basket where a small tin clock sat wrapped in a towel, its steady sound marking the calm afternoon without hurry.

Violeta’s eyes found a small round nail hole in one weathered board, and through that tiny opening the sky poured a pure bright blue that seemed to fill the hole completely. They leaned closer and the blue stayed perfect and still. For a moment Violeta wanted to poke a finger into the hole or somehow pocket that circle of blue and keep it, yet they held their hands still against the warm wood. “Ciro, come look through this little hole,” Violeta said in a low voice. Ciro stepped nearer and the grass underfoot made a dry whisper. Violeta kept their own face close so they could guide the looking without anyone touching the board.

Ciro bent carefully and peered. “It looks like someone put a piece of sky right inside the wood,” Ciro said softly. Violeta nodded and smiled. They showed Ciro how to place both hands on their own knees and simply lean the head just enough to see without pressing or poking. The blue remained calm and full. Auntie Luz watched from a few steps away and the sunlight made the water in the bucket gleam like liquid glass. Violeta felt proud of the careful way they all stood. “We can look as long as we want if we leave it just the way it is,” Violeta told Ciro. The air stayed warm and easy around their shoulders.

Auntie Luz walked closer and knelt so her eyes met the same small hole. She breathed out a quiet sound of delight. Violeta stood beside her and watched the blue glow steadily inside the dark wood. From the basket Auntie Luz drew an orange and began to peel it; the bright smell of orange peel rose fresh and sweet into the mild air and mixed with the meadow scent. Violeta inhaled deeply and felt happy that the hole still held its blue. “It is like a tiny window that stays put,” Auntie Luz said. Violeta agreed with a small nod and kept their hands gently folded so no one would disturb the fence.

They stayed that way for a long quiet stretch while the tin clock continued its soft ticking from inside the basket. Violeta counted their own slow breaths and watched a single cloud drift past the blue circle. Ciro did the same. No one reached toward the hole. Violeta felt the sun’s warmth ease a little as the light turned softer and more golden across the meadow. Auntie Luz shared the peeled orange sections and the juice tasted bright on Violeta’s tongue. They all looked once more through the nail hole together, taking turns so carefully that the board never shifted.

After the last bit of orange was finished Violeta suggested they sit on the grass for a few more moments simply noticing the blue without needing to move closer. Ciro sat cross-legged and Auntie Luz rested the linen napkin across her lap again. Violeta closed their eyes for a short count and opened them to find the hole still full of the same steady blue. The bucket water had grown still and mirrored a quiet corner of sky of its own. “I like knowing it will be here if we come back another day,” Violeta said. Ciro smiled and answered, “Me too.” The meadow felt peaceful around them and the fence stood solid and kind.

When the light began to lean longer across the grass Auntie Luz packed the basket and lifted the bucket so they could carry the water back toward the house for the garden. Violeta gave the nail hole one last long look, then turned and walked between Ciro and Auntie Luz along the path. The air cooled just enough to feel fresh on their faces. They talked lightly about the shape of the cloud they had seen and about how the blue had stayed perfect. Violeta’s steps felt easy and content. By the time they reached the back steps the sky above the roof had deepened toward evening and a first star waited high up.

Inside the house Violeta washed their hands at the sink and dried them once more while Auntie Luz set out cups of warm milk. Ciro waved good-night and headed next door. Violeta changed into soft pajamas and climbed into bed where the sheets felt smooth and cool. Auntie Luz sat for a minute on the edge of the mattress and smoothed Violeta’s hair. The room held a gentle hush. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Violeta closed their eyes and remembered the small circle of blue resting safely in the fence, still full and undisturbed under the wide evening sky.
