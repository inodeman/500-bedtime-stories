A soft tick drifted through the quiet air of the school cloakroom, steady and gentle like a tiny footstep on metal. Farah lifted their head from the warm heap of coats and listened closely. The sound came from a tin clock resting on the low shelf above the pegs, and Farah smiled because the little ticks made the room feel friendly and awake. Cool air brushed Farah’s cheeks as they stood near the open doorway where the lunch mats began, soft blue squares waiting just beyond the neat rows of shoes. Farah’s fingers brushed a mitten that still held the faint heat of someone’s hand, and the clean wool felt cozy against their skin. Farah liked this corner after the busy day, when everything slowed down and the pale gold light from the high window rested on the floorboards like a quiet blanket.

Farah stepped onto the first lunch mat and felt the smooth rubber give a little under their socks. Uncle Idris had set a small tray on a low table right at the edge of the mats, and Farah saw a plate of pear slices cut thick, pale and shiny in the afternoon glow. The fruit looked juicy enough to cool a thirsty tongue, and Farah could almost taste the sweet crispness already. Juno sat cross-legged on a neighboring mat, swinging one foot and humming a soft tune that floated like a feather. Farah’s name hung on a blue tag by their peg, and Farah touched the tag lightly before walking over. The room held a gentle hush broken only by distant hallway footsteps, and Farah felt calm all the way to their toes as Uncle Idris wiped his hands on a soft cloth and nodded toward the plate with a kind look.

“Farah,” Uncle Idris said softly, “these pears are ready whenever you are.” Farah looked at the thick slices and then at Juno. Farah saw at once that a plate of pear slices cut thick would not feel fair if only one person was served first. Farah wanted Juno included right from the beginning so both could smile at the same moment. The light from the window warmed Farah’s hair as they knelt by the table. Farah’s sleeve brushed the side of a small tin lid that covered a cup of warm drink, and the tin lid felt smooth and cool under Farah’s fingertips. A gentle steam rose in a thin curl, carrying the smell of chamomile that made Farah’s shoulders loosen and rest.

“Can we share them so we both get some at the same time?” Farah asked, looking up at Uncle Idris with steady eyes. Juno leaned forward on the mat and whispered, “I like pears a lot.” Farah smiled at Juno and carefully slid the plate nearer until the sweet fruit scent rose clearly. Farah counted the thick slices with careful attention. There were six big pieces gleaming like little moons, and Farah decided to make them into smaller, even pieces so both friends could begin together. Farah’s hands moved slowly and kindly, and the cool juice left a silky feeling on their fingers while soft shadows stretched across the lunch mats as the light shifted toward evening pink.

Uncle Idris watched without saying much at all. He simply set two little napkins side by side on the table and waited with quiet patience. Farah broke each thick slice into neat halves with gentle pressure, making twelve smaller pieces that looked even and ready for sharing. Farah arranged six pieces on one napkin and six on the other, lining them up so they looked like twin rows of pale petals. The fruit still gleamed under the softening light. Farah could hear the quiet breathing of Juno beside them and the far-off hush of the hallway beyond the cloakroom door. The mats felt springy and kind under Farah’s knees, and a warm patch of sun rested on Farah’s back like a soft, familiar hand.

“Here, Juno,” Farah said, holding out one napkin with both hands. “We can start together.” Juno took the napkin carefully and grinned so wide their eyes nearly closed. “Thank you, Farah. They look perfect.” Farah and Juno sat on neighboring mats and took small, happy bites. The pear was cool and sweet, and a tiny drop of juice dotted Farah’s chin in a friendly way that made them giggle under their breath. Uncle Idris smiled and let the choice stand without a speech of any kind. He simply poured a little more of the warm drink into two cups and set them down within easy reach. Farah felt the pleasant heat of the cup through their palms and sipped slowly while the quiet room seemed to hold them both like a gentle nest.

After the last sweet bite, Farah walked back toward the warm heap of coats and scarves near the pegs. Farah found two soft socks that matched, each with a tiny blue star stitched near the cuff, and pressed them together until they made a neat pair. The socks felt fleecy and still held gentle warmth from the radiator that hummed low against the wall. Farah set the pair neatly by their shoes so nothing would be left behind. Juno laughed lightly when a loose coat sleeve slipped down like a waving arm, and Farah laughed too, a quiet sound that mixed with the soft rustle of fabric. The light on the floor had grown softer and rosier as the afternoon leaned kindly toward evening, and Farah felt the peaceful stretch of the moment settle in their chest.

Uncle Idris gathered the empty plate and rinsed it in the little sink by the wall while water tinkled gently into the basin like tiny bells. Farah wiped their hands on a towel that felt thick and cotton-dry against their skin. Farah helped fold the used napkins into small neat squares and stacked them on the tray. Juno stood and stretched both arms high, then linked elbows with Farah for a slow walk around the edge of the lunch mats. Farah felt peaceful and full of quiet gladness. The cloakroom pegs held coats like quiet friends waiting for the walk home. Farah’s own coat was red and puffy, and Farah ran a hand over the smooth zipper that would keep out the evening chill. “Ready to head home soon?” Uncle Idris asked in his easy voice. “Yes,” Farah answered. “The pears were nice.”

Farah and Juno slipped their arms into their coats while Uncle Idris held the sleeves steady. Farah zipped the red coat all the way to the chin and felt the snug fabric settle around their shoulders like a hug. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Farah watched the warm circle of light rest on the wall and felt ready for the quiet walk home under the early stars. Cool evening air touched Farah’s face as the door opened, carrying the last sweetness of shared pears in memory. Juno waved from the first step, and Farah waved back with a calm smile, stepping out beside Uncle Idris with light feet and a heart as steady as the little ticks that had begun the afternoon.
