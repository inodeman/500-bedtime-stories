Soft evening mist brushed the cottage panes and left cool beads that caught the last pale light of day. Eko stepped into the cottage room and felt the quiet wooden boards under their socks while they looked along the long tablecloth that covered the big table and the paper stars that hung still from the ceiling beams. A gentle warmth rose from the floorboards as Eko moved closer and saw the choir folders resting on the low shelf, colors tumbled together instead of neat rows. Eko ran one careful finger along a blue edge that sat against a red spine and whispered the names of the colors they could see. The air held a soft hush broken only by the faint creak of the old house settling.

Eko turned when Casper appeared in the doorway with a quiet smile and the soft scuff of slippers. “The folders got shelved by the wrong color,” Eko said, pointing so Casper could see the mix of greens tucked among yellows. Casper nodded and touched a paper star that drifted just above their head. “We can fix them if we take our time,” Casper answered. Eko felt the cool draft from the window settle against their wrists as they both studied the shelf. Together they decided that rushing would only tangle the colors worse, so Eko suggested they split the work into two slow jobs. The lamp light glowed steady and honey-colored across the long tablecloth while they planned.

Eko chose the job of lifting each folder one by one and sorting it onto clear color piles on the table, and Casper chose the job of checking the labels inside before placing each pile back in order on the shelf. “I’ll go left to right and you can go slow behind me,” Eko said. Casper agreed with a small nod. The paper stars cast tiny pointed shadows that shifted only when someone breathed near them. Eko felt the slight roughness of the folder covers under their fingertips and moved without hurry, setting blue with blue and red with red. The room stayed pleasantly warm near the table where the lamp shone.

The baker stepped in from the kitchen doorway carrying a small basket and the clean scent of orange peel drifted with them across the long tablecloth. “I can count the stacks for you when you are ready or name the knot if you need one for the ribbon ties,” the baker offered in a calm voice. Eko looked up and smiled. Casper kept sorting while the baker set the basket down. Soft steam rose from fresh rolls under a cloth and the orange smell mixed gently with the warm air. Eko placed three green folders side by side and waited for Casper to check the names written inside before moving on.

Eko and Casper kept their two slow jobs going side by side without any hurry. Eko lined the yellow folders into a neat row that felt smooth under their palm, and Casper double-checked each label by the soft lamp light. “This yellow stack is ready for the shelf,” Eko said. Casper answered, “I see the matching names, so we can put them up together.” The paper stars hung motionless above them like quiet friends. A faint coolness touched Eko’s cheek whenever the door to the kitchen opened a little, yet the cottage room itself stayed cozy.

When the piles grew tall enough to need tying, the baker came close again and said the knot they needed could simply be called the baker’s loop. Eko watched while the baker showed one easy twist of the soft ribbon. Casper held the folders steady and Eko guided the ribbon ends through the loop just as shown. The linen napkin that covered the bread basket felt dry and crisp when Eko’s hand brushed it by accident. They tied each color group with the same calm knot and set the bundles back on the shelf one careful stack at a time. The long tablecloth stayed smooth under their elbows as they worked.

A tin clock ticked from the mantel in a steady gentle rhythm that matched the slow pace Eko and Casper had chosen. Eko counted the finished bundles under their breath and felt glad that none of the colors had mixed again. Casper placed the last red bundle beside the others and the shelf finally looked right. The baker smiled from the side of the room and offered no more than the earlier count of twelve blue folders and the simple name for the knot. Warm light from the lamp pooled brighter now that evening had settled fully outside. Eko rested both hands on the long tablecloth for a moment and listened to the quiet house.

Eko and Casper stood back together and looked at the neat rows of choir folders under the paper stars. “We finished it side by side,” Eko said softly. Casper nodded and brushed a speck of ribbon fluff from the table. The baker packed the empty basket and left the orange scent fading kindly in the air. Cool night air slipped through a cracked window and touched Eko’s forehead like a friendly hand. Everything in the cottage room felt settled and ready, the long tablecloth still smooth and the stars hanging peaceful overhead.

Eko sat on the edge of a low chair and watched the paper stars while Casper folded the leftover ribbons. The baker waved good night from the doorway and the cottage grew even quieter. Eko felt the soft warmth of the room wrap around them as their breathing slowed. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
