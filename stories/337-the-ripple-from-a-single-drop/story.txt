The sweet smell of honey floated from an open jar on a nearby crate as Femi’s hands already moved slowly along the cool damp stones of the harbor wall at low tide. Femi pressed their palms against each smooth ridge and then lifted them to feel the cool wet film left by the water. Soft gold light spilled across the stones while the air stayed mild and quiet. Femi shifted a little closer to the edge where small still pools sat like clear glass. Their fingers traced gentle circles without hurry. The stones held a leftover warmth from the afternoon sun. Femi kept their hands busy exploring every little hollow and kept breathing in the calm evening air that carried that faint honey sweetness.

A single clear drop slipped from the edge of the wooden roof that sheltered the waiting bench right beside the wall and landed in the center of one perfect pool. Femi watched the ripple spread outward in soft perfect rings across the still water. The light caught each tiny wave and made it sparkle for a moment before it faded. Femi leaned nearer and felt the cool air rising from the pool against their cheeks. Their hands rested quietly on the stone now. The water looked so smooth and round that Femi almost wanted to scoop the whole ripple into a pocket or poke it just once with one careful finger. The wish sat there for a breath and then Femi simply stayed still and watched the last ring disappear into the edges of the pool.

Femi turned and saw Noemi walking along the wall with quiet steps. The light on the water made soft shifting patterns that danced across both their faces. Femi felt the mild evening temperature on their skin and smiled. “Noemi come look at this,” Femi said in a low voice. Noemi stepped closer and the stones underfoot felt firm and cool. Femi kept their hands still on the wall so nothing would shake the pool. The ripple was gone but the memory of its perfect circle stayed in the water’s calm surface. Femi pointed with one steady finger without touching anything. The air smelled clean and the light grew a little softer as clouds drifted high above the harbor.

“See how the drop made that circle all by itself,” Femi whispered. Noemi nodded and leaned in until their shoulder brushed Femi’s. The two friends stayed very still so the pool would not tremble. Femi said “We can look and look and never need to move it.” Noemi answered “It is like a little moon on the water.” Their voices stayed soft as the gentle splash of distant oars. Femi felt glad they had chosen to show the quiet way instead of poking. The stones under their knees held a pleasant coolness. Evening light turned the whole harbor wall the color of pale honey though the jar itself was far behind them now.

The ferryman came along the wall with slow steady steps and stopped when he saw the two children kneeling. His boots made almost no sound on the dry upper stones. He looked down at the same still pool and smiled a quiet smile. “What have you found here,” the ferryman asked. Femi answered “A ripple from one drop off the roof. We are only looking.” The ferryman nodded and the three of them watched together while the water stayed glass-smooth. A faint warmth still rose from the stones near their hands. Soft light reflected up into their faces and made everything gentle. Noemi shifted a little and Femi felt the calm of leaving the pool exactly as it was.

Femi reached out and took Noemi’s chalky hand for a moment so they could both keep perfect balance on the wall. The chalky texture felt dry and fine against Femi’s own skin. They stayed that way while the ferryman rested one hand on the wooden post of the roof. “Best to leave such a quiet thing right where it belongs,” the ferryman said. Femi nodded and let go of the hand so nothing would disturb the pool. The light had turned lavender at the edges of the sky. The air grew cooler by one soft degree. All three kept watching the empty circle that still seemed to hold the shape of the ripple even after it had gone.

They waited without speaking while a single clear chime sounded from the ferry bell far along the dock. The note hung in the air and then faded into the hush of low tide. Femi listened until the last vibration disappeared. The stones felt steady under their knees. Soft purple light now lay across the water and the wooden roof. Noemi breathed out a long quiet breath. The ferryman straightened and looked once more at the pool. Femi felt peaceful all the way through because the ripple had been seen and left alone. The evening stayed kind and the temperature stayed just cool enough to feel fresh.

A gate latch clicked softly somewhere behind the waiting bench and the sound drifted over them like a small goodbye. Femi stood up carefully so no stone would shift. Noemi stood too and the ferryman gave them both a gentle nod. They walked a few steps along the wall together and then paused to look back one last time at the still pool under the roof. The light had grown dimmer yet stayed kind. Femi’s hands hung loose and calm at their sides. The air moved just enough to cool their cheeks. Everything felt finished and right.

They turned toward the path that led home while the harbor stayed quiet behind them. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Femi walked between Noemi and the ferryman and felt the mild night air on their face and the solid ground under their feet and knew the little ripple would stay safe in its own pool all through the dark.
