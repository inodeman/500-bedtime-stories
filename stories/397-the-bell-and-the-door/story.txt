Ms. Okonkwo was already standing at the long wooden counter in the bakery kitchen before the early table, her chalky hand pressing slow circles into a cool mound of dough that left pale dust on her skin. Pale morning light spilled through the shutters in quiet stripes across the warm tile floor. Sora stepped inside beside Kavi and felt the gentle oven heat wrap around their arms like a soft blanket. The dough made a low thump each time Ms. Okonkwo folded it. “Good morning, Sora and Kavi,” Ms. Okonkwo said in her steady voice. “I am glad you are here for the careful work ahead.”

Sora looked around the quiet bakery kitchen and saw the small brass bell hanging beside the wide door that opened toward the yard. A faint sweetness of honey rose from a glaze pot resting on the back shelf and mixed with the yeasty air. Kavi stood close to Sora while Ms. Okonkwo explained that one person must stay at the bell and one at the door, yet everything would go wrong if anyone rushed and let a hard gust spoil the rising loaves. Sora rested their fingers on the smooth counter edge and listened. The room felt snug and ready.

Sora and Kavi decided together to split the task into two slow jobs so nothing hurried. Sora walked to the heavy door while Kavi took a place near the bell string. Cool floor tiles pressed against the bottoms of Sora’s socks. “I will open the door only a little at a time,” Sora said. Kavi answered, “And I will wait for your nod before I touch the bell.” Ms. Okonkwo kept shaping loaves at the counter, the dough cool and stretchy under her palms, and the children felt safe in the calm pace.

Sora placed both hands on the door and eased it open just a finger’s width. A thin ribbon of fresh air brushed Sora’s cheeks and stirred the flour dust into tiny sparkles in the light. Kavi held the bell string loosely and stayed still. Ms. Okonkwo spoke gently from across the room. “For the napkin knot on the early table, name it the bakery bow and count three slow loops.” Sora paused so the breeze stayed soft and kind. The kitchen light brightened a little as the door gap widened by another careful inch.

Kavi watched Sora and kept the same unhurried rhythm. Sora pushed the door open another small measure and heard the wood creak once like a sleepy whisper. Then the quiet click of a gate latch sounded from the yard beyond, a gentle metal note that settled into the morning without alarm. Sora smiled and waited through a single soft chime that drifted from a hanging spoon near the window. The honey scent floated more clearly on the new air and filled Sora’s nose with its warm golden smell while everything remained peaceful.

Sora opened the door the rest of the way with steady hands so the breeze flowed evenly across the bakery kitchen and cooled the space just enough. Kavi then drew the bell string once in a slow arc, and one clear silver ring sang out and faded like a kind goodbye. The sound left the room quieter than before. Ms. Okonkwo nodded while she wiped her hands on a clean towel. The temperature stayed perfect, warm from the resting ovens yet freshened by the yard air that now touched Sora’s hair.

Sora and Kavi left the door and bell and walked side by side to the neat stack of cloth napkins waiting for the early table. Sora held the corners steady while Kavi followed the name Ms. Okonkwo had given. They counted three slow loops aloud in soft voices and pulled the soft fabric into the bakery bow knot. The cloth felt nubby and kind between Sora’s fingers. Light from the open door danced across the folds in gentle patches. “We made the loops just right,” Kavi said. Sora answered, “Yes, the slow jobs are done.”

Ms. Okonkwo set her last loaf to rest under a clean cloth and crossed the room to stand with the children. She rested one warm hand lightly on Sora’s shoulder. The bakery kitchen now held a perfect balance of oven heat and outdoor freshness that smelled clean and sweet. Sora carried the knotted napkins to the early table in the corner while Kavi brought the smooth plates that made soft clinks against one another. They arranged everything together until the table looked neat and welcoming under the quiet light, and Sora felt the calm pride of work shared without hurry.

Sora stood back with Kavi and Ms. Okonkwo to look at the finished kitchen. A last soft breeze from the open door cooled Sora’s forehead and made the flour motes drift like slow snow. The brass bell hung still and silent once more. All three smiled in the peaceful space they had prepared. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Sora breathed in the gentle mix of honey and bread and knew the morning would begin with care because they had moved together one slow step at a time.
