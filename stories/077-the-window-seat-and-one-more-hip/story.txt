"Would you like the honey roll or the plain one first, Tamsin?" Ms. Okonkwo asked, her voice warm above the soft clatter of trays at the bakery counter on that gentle busy morning.

Tamsin stood close to the polished wood and felt the cool edge under their palms while pale sunlight poured through the front glass and made little gold squares on the floor. The bakery smelled clean and kind, and people moved slowly with baskets and paper bags, never rushing. Lior waited right beside Tamsin, shoulder nearly touching, and both of them looked toward the wide window seat that held thick cushions and just enough space for one more hip to settle in. Tamsin could hear the low murmur of quiet talk and the soft scrape of a chair leg, and the air felt pleasantly warm against their bare arms. Ms. Okonkwo wiped her hands on her apron and smiled down at them both without hurry.

Tamsin watched a tray of golden rolls slide onto the counter and noticed how the light caught the sugar crystals so they sparkled like tiny stars. The window seat looked so inviting with its faded blue fabric and the way it faced the street where leaves drifted past in slow circles. Tamsin thought about sitting there with a treat and then paused, because the seat had room for one more hip yet the first serving might go only to one person, and that did not feel even. Lior shifted from foot to foot, the soft leather of their shoes making a tiny whisper on the floorboards. Tamsin breathed in the gentle bakery air and felt the steady heat coming from the ovens somewhere behind the counter.

"I think we should make two smaller pieces so we both can sit," Tamsin said, looking up at Ms. Okonkwo and then sideways at Lior. The idea settled calmly in Tamsin's mind like a feather landing. Ms. Okonkwo simply nodded once and reached for the knife, the metal flashing briefly in the morning light. Tamsin smelled the rich honey that rose when the roll was cut carefully into two even halves, each one still warm and soft. Lior's eyes brightened, and Tamsin felt glad that the share would fit them both. The counter stayed busy in its soft way, with other customers choosing breads and speaking in low happy tones, yet no one pressed forward.

Tamsin held out both hands, palms open, and received one small piece while Lior received the other, the bread still giving off a gentle warmth that seeped into their fingers. The texture of the crust felt pleasantly rough against Tamsin's skin, and a thin line of honey glistened along the cut edge. They turned together toward the window seat that waited with room for one more hip, the cushions looking plump and ready. Outside, a faint metallic click sounded as someone closed a gate latch along the little side path, the noise clear and brief in the soft morning. Tamsin walked carefully so crumbs would not fall, and Lior matched their slow steps across the sun-patched floor.

"May we sit together here?" Tamsin asked, already easing onto the left side of the cushion so the space for one more hip stayed open and fair. Lior slid in beside them without bumping, and the seat held them both just right. The fabric felt smooth and slightly cool at first, then warmer where their legs rested. Ms. Okonkwo came around the counter carrying two small napkins and placed them nearby, her chalky hand brushing the edge of the table as she set them down; the dry soft powder came from the board where she wrote the morning specials. Tamsin noticed the quiet kindness in the gesture and said nothing more, simply glad the choice stood.

"Thank you for cutting them the same," Lior whispered, and Tamsin answered, "We can both taste the honey this way." They sat shoulder to shoulder while the bakery continued its gentle busy rhythm, cups being filled and paper being folded with soft rustles. Tamsin took a small bite and felt the sweetness spread, the bread tender inside. Light from the window lay across their knees in a calm stripe, and the air held a steady comfortable temperature that made eyelids feel a little heavier in a pleasant way. Somewhere near the back a single chime sounded once from the oven timer, and Tamsin and Lior waited through it without moving, just chewing slowly and watching dust motes drift in the sun.

Ms. Okonkwo returned to her place behind the counter and let the morning go on, offering no speech about what Tamsin had chosen, only a quiet smile that reached her eyes. Tamsin leaned a little into the cushion and felt Lior's sleeve brush theirs, the cloth light and familiar. Outside the window a bicycle rolled past with a soft whir of tires, and the leaves kept turning slowly on the trees across the street. Tamsin finished the last bite and brushed a few crumbs onto the napkin, the motion small and careful. The window seat held them both with room to spare for one more hip if anyone else had needed it, yet for now it was perfect just as it was, warm and steady under them.

The bakery sounds grew even softer as the early rush eased, and Tamsin rested their head briefly against the cool glass, watching the street grow quieter. Lior hummed a tiny tuneless note that barely reached past the seat. Tamsin felt full of the gentle morning and the even share they had made. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Tamsin closed their eyes for a moment and breathed the last faint sweetness still hanging in the air, knowing the cushions would keep them safe and close until it was time to go home.
