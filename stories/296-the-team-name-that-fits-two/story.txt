The afternoon light rested soft and warm on the schoolyard, brushing the low swing and the circle of stones with a gentle glow that made the packed earth smell faintly of sun-warmed dust. Niko walked across the yard with careful steps, feeling the mild air on their cheeks, and Niko paused near the swing where the chains hung quiet and cool to the touch. Cousin Pio sat on a low bench at the edge, watching without rushing anyone, and the light touched Niko's hair as they looked around the quiet ring of stones. Niko liked the way those stones made a neat circle, smooth and pale under the sky, and Niko breathed in the open air while a soft breeze moved past their sleeves. The weather held steady and kind over the yard, and Niko felt ready for a gentle game with friends who might arrive soon.

Niko found a folded scrap of paper near the swing, its surface cool, and when Niko ran a thumb along a paper edge the thin line felt slightly rough yet safe. On that scrap someone had begun writing a team name that was not only Niko's own name, half the letters looping in pencil, waiting for more hands to finish it. Niko held the scrap carefully and looked toward the circle of stones where two children already sat, and Niko heard Cousin Pio hum a quiet tune from the bench. The swing creaked once in a tiny friendly way when the breeze nudged it, and Niko smiled at the sound. Niko tucked the paper into a pocket for a moment, feeling the yard settle around them, and Niko decided the game should leave room for every friend who wanted a place in the circle.

Walid stood just outside the fun, close enough to see the stones and the low swing, hands in pockets, shoes scuffing softly against the warm ground. Niko noticed Walid's quiet stance and how Walid leaned a little toward the circle without stepping in, as if waiting for a clear welcome that did not shine like a prize. The air near Walid carried a faint smell of wet wool from a damp sweater draped over the fence, and Niko breathed that clean damp scent while thinking. Niko walked nearer, still holding the idea of the team name that was not only one person's name, and Niko felt the soft heat of the afternoon on their shoulders. Cousin Pio stayed on the bench and only nodded once, letting the children find their own way, and Niko understood that the solving belonged to them.

"We can make the circle bigger," Niko said, voice calm as they looked at the stones. Niko knelt and shifted two smooth stones outward so a wider space opened beside the swing, the grit under Niko's knees cool and grainy. Walid watched the change with bright eyes, and Niko patted the new empty spot between two stones as if it had always been meant for another friend. The light slanted lower and painted long mild shadows across the yard, and Niko felt the temperature slip a little cooler on their arms. Niko pulled the scrap of paper free again and smoothed it on a flat stone, inviting quiet help rather than calling attention like a trumpet.

"What if the name holds all of us," Niko said gently, tapping the unfinished letters. Pio watched from the bench without speaking, and the children gathered closer while Niko waited. Walid stepped into the widened circle and sat where Niko had made room, and the low swing hung nearby like a quiet neighbor. Niko offered the pencil, and together they added loops and lines until the team name on the paper belonged to more than one child at once. The paper felt steady under Niko's palm, and Niko heard a tin clock tick from the open classroom window across the yard, steady and small, marking the slow afternoon without hurry. Niko smiled as the shared name took shape, and the stones held everyone in a soft ring.

"I like how it sounds when we say it together," Walid said, reading the finished letters in a low voice. Niko nodded and repeated the name once, and the other children joined in a soft chorus that stayed kind. Niko changed the pace of the game then, slowing the turns so each child could step to the low swing, push gently, and return to the circle without rush. The chains of the swing made a mild metallic whisper under careful hands, and Niko felt the warm wood of the seat when it was their turn. Cousin Pio smiled from the bench and still did not decide for them, trusting Niko and Walid and the others to keep the space fair. Niko settled back onto a cool stone after swinging and listened to the quiet laughter that rose and fell like a friendly breeze.

Niko suggested they pass a smooth pebble around the circle as a talking token, so no one needed to shout or wait too long, and everyone agreed with small nods. When the pebble reached Walid, Walid spoke about a cloud shaped like a boat, and Niko listened with full attention while the late light turned honey-gold on the stones. Niko felt the pebble's cool weight later when it came around again, and Niko described the way the swing shadow stretched long and thin. The damp sweater on the fence still sent its mild wet-wool scent when the breeze turned, and Niko noticed how Walid's shoulders had eased now that the place was truly shared. Cousin Pio rose only to hand around cups of water from a jug, then sat again, letting the children keep solving the small moments themselves.

As the sun slid lower, Niko gathered the scrap with the team name that was not only one name and folded it once more, tucking it safe. "We can bring this tomorrow and add drawings if we want," Niko said, and Walid answered, "That would feel nice," with a quiet grin. Niko walked with Walid and Cousin Pio toward the gate while the schoolyard cooled, the stones still holding the day's warmth under their shoes. Niko felt content that Walid had sat inside the circle as a real part of the game, not as something pointed out loud like a trophy. The air grew softer on Niko's face, and the low swing rested empty and peaceful behind them as they left the yard together.

At home the rooms smelled faintly of supper, and Niko washed hands in water that felt silky and warm before settling near the window with a blanket over their knees. Cousin Pio read a short quiet page nearby while Niko thought again of the widened stone circle and the shared name on paper. Niko breathed slowly and watched the last pale light leave the sky, feeling safe and full of the gentle day. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Niko closed their eyes to the mild hush of the house, holding the memory of friends inside the circle, and sleep came easy and kind.
