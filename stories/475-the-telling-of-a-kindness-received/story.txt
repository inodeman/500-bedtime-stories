The sweet smell of beeswax drifted gently from the small candle on the nightstand while Jorie's hands were already busy smoothing the edges of their quilt into perfect straight lines and tucking a corner under the mattress with focused care. Golden lamp light poured across the bed and made the room feel wrapped in a soft afternoon even though night had come, warming Jorie's arms where they rested above the covers. Auntie Luz sat in the chair pulled close, her presence steady and kind as she watched Jorie finish the little task. The air held a pleasant warmth that invited stillness, and Jorie knew the story time had nearly begun though nothing had been said yet. Bigger news about a special drawing waited inside Jorie, but for this moment their hands kept moving slowly over the familiar fabric.

"I have been thinking of the telling of a kindness I once received, and I want to share it with you now along with the feeling that came after," Auntie Luz said in a voice as calm as still water. Jorie's hands slowed their work on the quilt and rested in their lap, the desire to speak of the drawing rising fast yet set aside for listening. Soft shadows stretched along the walls from the lamp's glow, giving the bedroom a deeper cozy look that matched the hour. Auntie Luz leaned forward a little, and Jorie felt the familiar comfort of being the only listener in the quiet space. They waited with open ears, toes curling slightly under the sheet in anticipation without rushing ahead.

Auntie Luz described a day when her arms ached from carrying heavy baskets of apples from the orchard down the long path home. "Ines saw me from her garden and came right over to take the biggest basket," Auntie Luz said. "She walked all the way beside me and talked about the way the clouds looked like sailing ships that afternoon." Jorie listened without a single interruption, though the bigger news pressed like a bubble wanting to pop, and they kept their eyes on Auntie Luz's gentle face. The sound of those spoken memories filled the air between them, clear and unhurried, while Jorie noticed how the pillow under their head felt plump and supportive against their neck. Every detail of the kindness settled into place as the story moved forward slowly.

The help continued at the house where Ines stayed to sort the apples into baskets by size and color, making neat rows on the porch table. Auntie Luz spoke of how Ines never hurried and shared a joke about a squirrel that had tried to steal one apple earlier. Jorie held back their own words completely, breathing evenly as they took in the whole scene of shared work and easy talk. A mild coolness from the window seeped into the room now and then, brushing Jorie's forehead like a reminder of the open world beyond the glass. They imagined the red apples and the porch clearly, staying right with Auntie Luz through each part of the telling without jumping ahead to anything else.

"When Ines finally waved goodbye and went home, I felt the kindness settle deep inside me," Auntie Luz continued with a soft smile. "The ache in my arms faded, and a bright gladness took its place, as if someone had lit a tiny lamp right in my heart that stayed shining even into the next morning." Jorie absorbed the words about the feeling after, their bigger news still tucked away safely while they attended to every sentence. Lamp light caught the edge of a glass of water on the table and made it sparkle briefly, drawing Jorie's glance for only a second before returning fully to the story. The gladness Auntie Luz described seemed almost real enough to touch in the quiet bedroom air.

Auntie Luz brought the telling to its close by explaining how that inner light from the kindness helped her notice other good moments all week long. "And that is the last sentence of it, the way the feeling after stayed and grew in ordinary days," Auntie Luz said, folding her hands. Jorie had listened all the way through without breaking in even once, true to the unfolding tale until its very end. Just then the sound of wind in the fig leaves reached them from the yard, a soft shushing rhythm against the window that rose and fell like slow breathing. Jorie turned their head slightly toward it but stayed settled in bed, ready at last for a question of their own.

After a small silence Jorie asked one real question that had formed while listening. "Auntie Luz, does the feeling after a kindness ever come back when you remember it later?" they said carefully. Auntie Luz answered, "Yes it does, often when least expected, like a welcome visitor." Jorie then stretched out one hand to the windowsill and touched a leaf's soft underside where a fig leaf had come to rest after a daytime breeze, tracing the fuzzy gentle surface with wonder. The texture felt living and quiet under their fingertip, connecting the outside tree to this indoor moment as they gathered a small share to offer next.

Only then did Jorie share something small of their own from the day. "I watched the way sunlight made dust float in a beam near the bookshelf this afternoon, and it looked like tiny stars dancing," Jorie told Auntie Luz in a near whisper. Auntie Luz nodded with full interest and said, "It sounds lovely and peaceful." The temperature in the bedroom dipped just enough to make the quilt's weight feel perfect and reassuring over Jorie's legs. They both rested in the shared smallness of the dust stars and the remembered kindness, no need for anything larger right then as the night deepened gently around them.

Auntie Luz rose quietly to lower the main lamp a bit and check that Jorie had enough blanket for the night ahead. Far sounds of the settling house created a background hush that invited eyes to close while Jorie's hands lay finally still and relaxed. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Jorie felt sleep approaching on gentle feet, filled with apples and glad hearts and floating dust, as the whole space held them safe with warm toes under the covers until morning.
