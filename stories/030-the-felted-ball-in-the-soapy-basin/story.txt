In the quiet craft corner the soapy basin rested on the low table and the wool ball floated half-sunk beneath a shimmering skin of bubbles that caught the soft lamp light and broke it into tiny trembling stars. Warm steam rose in slow curls and brushed Ximena’s cheeks with gentle heat while the fibers of the ball drew closer together under the milky water. Ximena leaned so near that the damp air tickled their nose and they could see every slow twist of the white wool as it worked at becoming round and firm. Paper scraps waited on the side and a wooden rack stood ready with empty pegs yet all of Ximena’s attention stayed fixed on the quiet motion inside the basin. The light slid across the soap film and made the whole corner feel hushed and careful.

Ximena stretched one finger toward the water and whispered, “I want to feel if it is ready yet.” Auntie Luz sat on the bench nearby and answered in a calm voice, “It still needs its own quiet time under the cloth, Ximena.” The air carried a faint warmth from the basin and Ximena pulled their hand back while the soft glow of the lamp rested on their hair. They shifted on the seat and the room stayed peaceful around them. Auntie Luz smiled and the steam continued its slow rise without hurry.

Ximena looked at the wooden rack and the stacks of colored paper and the little pot of paste and said, “Maybe I can just peek once more.” Auntie Luz shook her head gently and replied, “Leave the cover on and the wool will finish when it is ready.” A cool draft from the open window mixed with the warm steam and brushed across Ximena’s arms like a soft scarf. They nodded and reached for the clean cloth that waited beside the basin. The light stayed steady and kind while they settled the cloth over the soapy water so the ball could rest unseen.

Ximena smoothed the cloth edges and turned toward the paper and paste. They chose a sheet of soft blue paper and began to tear it into careful strips that made almost no sound. The wooden rack held the finished pieces and Ximena placed each new strip on a peg with slow fingers. Cool evening air slipped through the window and touched the back of their neck while they worked. Auntie Luz hummed a tiny tune that blended with the quiet room and Ximena felt the calm settle deeper around them both.

While Ximena arranged more paper strips they caught the distant smell of tomato vines drifting in from the garden outside the window. The green scent mixed with the mild soap air and made the craft corner feel like a gentle bridge between inside and out. Ximena breathed it in and kept tearing the paper into even smaller pieces that would dry on the rack later. Soft shadows stretched across the table as the lamp light stayed warm and steady. They worked without rush and the minutes passed like slow breath.

A soft chime of a cat’s collar bell sounded from the hallway and then faded again as the cat wandered past the open doorway. Ximena smiled at the familiar jingle and kept pressing paste onto the edges of two paper circles so they could hang together on the rack. The paste felt cool and smooth under their fingertips and the wooden pegs waited patiently. Auntie Luz watched with quiet eyes and the room held its peaceful temperature. Ximena placed the joined circles carefully and listened to the last echo of the bell disappear.

Ximena rested one hand on the bench and felt the fine grain of the bench run under their palm like tiny calm rivers of wood. They said, “The ball must be almost finished now.” Auntie Luz answered, “Soon, Ximena, and we can look together when the time is right.” The lamp light pooled golden across the table and warmed the side of Ximena’s face while they sat still. Cool air from the window brushed their ankles and they stayed close to the covered basin without lifting the cloth. The grain under their hand felt smooth and steady and helped them wait.

Ximena picked up another scrap of paper and folded it into a small quiet shape while Auntie Luz spoke once more. “You are doing well to let it rest,” she said, and Ximena replied, “I can wait a little longer.” The temperature in the corner stayed mild and pleasant and the soft light never flickered. They listened for the far clock ticking its slow notes instead of any nearer sound and the whole craft space seemed to breathe with them. Paper edges rustled faintly under careful fingers and the wooden rack held everything in neat rows. Ximena felt the peaceful stretch of time like a warm blanket and kept their hands busy with the gentle folding.

When the far clock marked another slow hour Ximena and Auntie Luz lifted the cloth together and found the wool ball firm and round and ready in the cooling soapy water. Ximena cupped it in both hands and felt its soft solid shape while the last steam rose in the mild air. They set the finished ball on a clean cloth beside the paper and paste and the wooden rack held its new decorations proudly. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Ximena leaned against Auntie Luz and the quiet craft corner wrapped them both in warm still light as the evening settled deeper and kind.
