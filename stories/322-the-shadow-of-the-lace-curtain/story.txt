The late afternoon sun rested warm and golden on the harbor wall at low tide, laying a gentle heat across the old stones so they felt almost sleepy under Cosima’s bare feet. Soft light slid over the wet patches and made them shine like quiet coins. Cosima walked slowly along the top of the wall, feeling the cool sea air brush their cheeks and ruffle the ends of their hair. Far below, the water had pulled back and left smooth ribbons of sand and little pools that held pieces of sky. Cosima paused and listened to the hush of the tide, then kept walking, glad for the peaceful stretch of harbor that belonged only to this hour.

Near a low post where someone had once tied a boat, Cosima stopped short. A lace curtain hung from the post and drifted in the light breeze, and the sun shone straight through its openwork so that pale flower shapes printed themselves across the stone floor of the wall. Cosima crouched and watched the patterns move when the curtain swayed. The wish to pocket a corner of the lace or poke one of the printed flowers was real and strong, yet Cosima stayed still and only looked. They noticed how the flowers seemed to bloom and fade with every small shift of air, and the sight made Cosima’s eyes feel wide and quiet.

Elif came along the wall a moment later, sandals soft on the warm stone, and Cosima raised a hand in greeting. “Look at the flowers the lace is making,” Cosima said, voice low so the patterns would not scatter. Elif knelt beside them and watched the shifting petals of light. “They look like they could float away if we breathed too hard,” Elif whispered. Cosima nodded and showed Elif how to lean close without touching, how to let their shadow fall carefully to the side so the printed flowers stayed bright. Together they counted the open blossoms and the tiny leaves of light that appeared and vanished.

A faint jingle reached them then, the clear ring of a cat’s collar bell from somewhere along the shore path, and both children turned their heads for a second before returning to the lace. Cosima pointed to a cluster of shapes near the hem. “See how this one looks like a daisy and that one like a little star?” Cosima said. Elif smiled and answered, “I see them. They keep changing when the wind does.” The air around the wall smelled clean and salty, and Cosima felt the rough grain of the stone under their fingertips while they kept their hands carefully away from the curtain itself.

The ferryman stepped up onto the wall from the sandy side, his boots quiet, a coil of rope over one shoulder. He stopped when he saw the children and the floating curtain. Cosima looked up and said, “We are only looking. The lace is printing flowers on the floor, and we do not want to disturb them.” The ferryman set the rope down gently and crouched so his eyes were level with the patterns. “They are fine flowers,” he said in a calm voice. “Best left right where the light puts them.” He stayed still as the curtain drifted and the flower shadows danced across the stone between their knees.

Cosima reached into a small pocket and found a smooth glass bead they had carried from home, rolling it once between thumb and finger to feel its cool round surface before putting it safely back. The bead’s quiet weight helped Cosima remember to keep hands gentle. Elif leaned a little closer and breathed out slowly so the lace would not stir more than the breeze already allowed. The three of them watched in shared silence while a gull called far out over the water and the sun lowered another notch, turning the printed flowers a warmer gold.

After a long looking, Cosima stood and brushed the grit from their knees. “We should leave it here for the next person who walks this wall,” Cosima said. Elif rose too and nodded. The ferryman smiled and lifted his rope again. “That is a good choice,” he answered. “The tide will come in later, and the light will change, but the lace can keep making flowers until then.” Cosima felt proud of the quiet decision and gave the curtain one last careful glance, memorizing the way the largest bloom sat right in the middle of a sunlit stone.

They walked together along the wall toward the path that led inland, the ferryman matching their slow pace. Cosima named three smells that floated on the evening air: the sharp green scent of mint leaves crushed underfoot near a garden fence, the warm smell of sun-baked wood from the old boathouse, and the soft sweet smell of drying seaweed on the sand. Naming them felt like a small calm game, and Elif joined in with a smile. The light grew softer and pinker across the harbor, and Cosima’s bare feet found the cooler earth of the path as they left the wall behind.

At home the rooms held the last of the day’s warmth, and Cosima sat by the wide sill while Elif rested nearby and the ferryman’s footsteps faded down the lane. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Cosima watched its steady circle of light and thought again of the lace flowers left safe on the harbor stones, still printing their quiet shapes for anyone who might pass before full dark.
