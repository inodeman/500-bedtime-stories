The little brass clock rested unevenly on the library table by the round window, its hands twisted the wrong way so the minutes seemed to drift backward through the warm honey-colored light that poured across the smooth wood. Kavi bent near enough to see the winding key jammed halfway around against its usual path, and they brushed one careful finger along the cool metal casing while tiny dust sparkles floated in the glow. Outside the round glass the garden lay still and green, yet Kavi’s whole attention stayed on those stubborn backward hands that gave a faint wrong-way tick. They felt the table’s solid edge under their elbows and listened to the quiet hush of the room, wondering how the clock had ended up wound that way in the first place.

Kavi’s fingers tangled with the chain and the little key, and for a moment they thought of sliding the whole clock under a tall stack of picture books so no one would notice the mistake. The air near the window felt pleasantly cool against their wrists as they hesitated, the light shifting gently across the brass face. They could almost hear their own heartbeat in the silence between the soft ticks. Kavi glanced once toward the shelves and once back at the backward hands, still unsure, still holding the chain lightly so it would not rattle. The round window glass felt smooth when they rested their other hand against it for a second, and the thought of hiding the clock lingered like a small cloud before it began to drift away.

A faint jingle of a cat's collar bell drifted from somewhere among the tall shelves, light and friendly, and Kavi sat up a little straighter at the sound. They kept one hand on the clock and breathed in the calm of the library, noticing how the afternoon light made the brass shine in tiny circles. The chair beneath them was steady and kind, and Kavi rocked once in the chair and let it stop by itself while they decided what to do next. Their eyes stayed on the wrong-way hands, and they practiced the plain words they might say. Outside, a leaf brushed the glass with the softest scrape, and inside the room felt safe and waiting.

Miss Dalca stepped quietly toward the library table by the round window, her steps soft on the carpet, and the light caught the kind look on her face. Kavi held the clock carefully so the chain would not swing and felt the cool brass settle into their palm. “I wound it the wrong way,” Kavi said in a clear quiet voice, “and the hands went backward.” Miss Dalca’s wool sleeve brushed Kavi’s arm as she leaned closer to look, the soft knit warm and familiar against their skin. She did not laugh or frown; she simply nodded and rested one hand gently on the table edge. The round window kept pouring its gentle light over both of them while Kavi waited, feeling lighter already for having spoken.

“Thank you for telling me straightaway,” Miss Dalca said, her voice calm as still water. Together they turned the little key the proper direction, listening to the tiny gears catch and settle inside the brass case. Kavi watched the hands begin to move forward again under the warm light, and they smelled the faint sweet scent of cocoa drifting from a mug Miss Dalca had set nearby on a side shelf. The steam rose in slow curls, and Kavi breathed it in while their fingers helped guide the chain free of its tangle. “We can mend what can be mended,” Miss Dalca added softly, and Kavi nodded, feeling the table solid beneath their hands.

Kavi and Miss Dalca worked side by side, easing the key all the way around until the clock’s face looked right once more. The light from the round window made everything glow like late-afternoon gold, and Kavi felt the pleasant coolness of the metal give way to a gentle warmth from their own hands. “It sounds happier now,” Kavi said, tilting their head to hear the steady forward tick. Miss Dalca smiled and answered, “Yes, it does,” while she polished the glass with a soft cloth. No teasing came, only the quiet satisfaction of fixing what had gone sideways, and Kavi stayed close, watching every careful turn.

They set the mended clock in the center of the library table where the round window’s light could reach it fully, and Kavi rested both hands flat on the wood to feel its smooth coolness. The gears now ticked in their proper rhythm, a soft trustworthy sound that filled the peaceful space. Miss Dalca poured a little more cocoa into two small cups, and the warm chocolate smell rose again, comforting and sweet. Kavi sipped carefully, feeling the heat travel down, and listened to the clock keep good time. Outside the garden stayed quiet, and inside everything felt settled and kind.

Cleo, who had been reading nearby, wandered over with quiet steps and peered at the clock with friendly interest, the afternoon light catching the edges of their hair. “It looks just right now,” Cleo whispered, and Kavi answered, “We fixed it together.” Miss Dalca rested a gentle hand on Kavi’s shoulder while the three of them watched the hands move forward in the golden glow. The chair under Kavi felt steady, and the whole room seemed to breathe slowly with them. Kavi kept the plain truth close and felt glad they had spoken it aloud instead of hiding anything away.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Kavi sat back in the chair at the library table by the round window, listening to the mended clock tick its calm forward song while the last warm light of day rested on the brass. Miss Dalca and Cleo stayed near, and the air held the quiet comfort of a room that had been set right again. Kavi’s hands rested open on the table, free of any tangle, and they felt the gentle coolness of evening beginning to gather at the edges of the glass. Everything was peaceful, and the clock kept its steady time as the soft glow wrapped around them all.
