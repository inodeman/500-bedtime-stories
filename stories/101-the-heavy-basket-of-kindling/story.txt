The smell of rain on dust drifted up from the stone path while Dahlia’s small hands stayed busy straightening the woven mats on the cottage porch, brushing away loose bits of bark with careful fingers. Warm late-day light spilled across the boards and made the wood feel smooth under their palms. Dahlia hummed a soft tune and patted the last mat flat, listening to the quiet creak of the porch rails in the gentle breeze. Their sleeves brushed against the cool railing as they worked, and the air held a mild chill that made the porch feel snug rather than sharp. Cousin Pio appeared around the corner carrying a wide basket of kindling that kept tipping sideways no matter how he shifted his grip.

The basket wobbled again and a few sticks slid toward the edge while Dahlia watched the way the porch boards caught the fading gold light. “Pio, that basket wants to dance right out of your arms,” Dahlia said, stepping closer so the warm air from the open doorway touched their cheeks. Pio laughed under his breath and tried once more to steady the load, the dry twigs clicking softly together. Dahlia reached out without waiting and took one sturdy handle, feeling the rough weave press against their skin. Together they kept the basket level as they crossed the last few steps of the porch, the temperature dropping just a little in the shade of the roof overhang.

Inside the hall just beyond the threshold the light turned cooler and softer, painting pale rectangles on the floorboards. Dahlia and Pio set the basket down for a moment so they could catch their breath, and the kindling settled with a gentle rustle. “If we each carry half, it will stay quiet and still,” Dahlia said, already lifting a neat armful of the shorter sticks. Pio nodded and gathered the longer pieces into his own arms, the wood smelling faintly of the forest. Dahlia noticed a thin satin ribbon tied around one bundle of twigs; the ribbon felt cool and slick against their wrist as they adjusted their hold. They walked slowly down the short hall, the floor smooth and cool under their socks, carrying the kindling toward the little alcove near the hearth where it belonged.

A gate latch clicked once in the yard outside, a clear metallic note that floated through the open door and into the hall. Dahlia paused to listen, then continued placing their half of the kindling into the wooden box against the wall, stacking each stick so it lay flat and safe. The air in the hall held a pleasant coolness that brushed their arms while they worked side by side with Pio. “Thank you for the help, Dahlia,” Pio said quietly, his voice warm in the quiet space. Dahlia only smiled and kept arranging the sticks, glad the basket no longer tipped and spilled. Soft afternoon light filtered through the side window and lay in calm stripes across their hands and the neat piles of wood.

When the box was full they stood back and looked at the tidy result, the hall smelling faintly of clean wood and the earlier rain. Dahlia rubbed their palms together to brush away a few tiny splinters of bark, feeling the mild warmth returning to their fingers. Pio closed the lid of the kindling box with a soft thud that sounded friendly rather than loud. “The porch will look neat again once we bring the empty basket back out,” Dahlia said, lifting the light basket by both handles now that it held nothing at all. They walked together toward the doorway, the cooler hall air giving way to the milder porch air as they stepped outside once more. The empty basket swung gently between them without any tipping at all.

Back on the porch Dahlia set the basket in its usual corner beside the mats they had straightened earlier, the boards still holding a trace of day’s warmth under their feet. A thin mist of fog clung to the lower corner of the porch window where the cooler air met the last of the sun. Dahlia reached up with one dry finger and traced a slow careful circle through the fog, watching the clear line appear in the glass. The circle caught a little of the remaining light and glowed for a moment before the fog began to fill it again. Pio watched and smiled, then sat on the porch step to rest while Dahlia finished the small circle. The air felt gentle against their faces as evening settled closer around the cottage.

They went inside again and stood for a while in the hall where the kindling now rested safely, the light growing softer and more golden through the window. Dahlia ran one hand along the smooth wall, feeling how the plaster held a quiet coolness that soothed their skin. “We make a good team with heavy baskets,” Pio said, his words calm and pleased. Dahlia nodded and leaned lightly against the wall, listening to the far-off hush of leaves moving outside. The hall felt peaceful and settled now that the tipping basket was no longer a worry. Their breathing slowed to match the quiet house, and the last bits of outdoor chill faded from their clothes.

Dahlia wandered a few steps farther into the hall and sat on the low bench beneath the window, the wood of the seat cool through their clothes. Outside the sky turned a deeper blue and the porch beyond the door grew dimmer by degrees. They thought of the satin ribbon still tied around one bundle inside the kindling box and how smoothly it had slipped under their fingers earlier. Pio joined them on the bench and the two sat without hurry, shoulders nearly touching in the friendly silence. The temperature in the hall stayed mild and even, wrapping around them like a soft blanket. Dahlia rested their hands in their lap and felt completely at ease after the small shared work.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Dahlia watched the gentle light settle over the hall floor and the now-quiet porch beyond the open door, the air holding only the calm of a finished afternoon. Their eyelids grew heavier as the warmth of the lantern mixed with the cooler evening drafts drifting in. Pio spoke one last quiet sentence—“Sleep well when you are ready, Dahlia”—and Dahlia answered with a sleepy nod, already feeling the peaceful weight of the day. The light from the lantern painted slow circles on the walls while the cottage settled around them both, safe and still.
