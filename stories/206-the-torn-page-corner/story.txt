The torn page corner lifted in a thin curl where Chioma’s thumb had caught it during a too-quick flip, its edge frayed into pale threads that caught the honey-colored light pooling on the ceramic studio sill. Chioma stared at the small damage, the paper still warm from their hands, while the rest of the open book lay smooth and waiting. Below the sill the quiet plaza held its soft evening hush, and the sill itself felt cool and smooth under Chioma’s elbows. Chioma almost slid the book shut to hide the tear, yet the torn corner stayed stubbornly visible, whispering of the careless turn that had made it.

Chioma sat very still on the wide sill above the quiet plaza, the ceramic studio around them filled with the faint chalky scent of clay dust and the distant clink of finished bowls. Grandpa Sol’s workbench waited nearby with its neat rows of tools, and Chioma’s own sketchbook of patterns rested open beside the torn page. Warm late light brushed Chioma’s bare arms as they turned the book a little, hoping the corner might look whole again. A soft draft from the open window stirred the air, cool and gentle against Chioma’s neck, and they pressed the torn bit flat without success.

“Grandpa Sol,” Chioma said at last, voice quiet as the plaza below, “I tore the corner when I turned the page too fast.” Grandpa Sol stepped closer, the soles of his shoes making a slow scrape on the studio floor, and the air near him carried a clean smell of soapy wool from the sweater he had rinsed earlier. Chioma held the book out so the ragged white edge showed clearly in the slanting light. “I almost hid it under the other pages,” Chioma added, cheeks warm, “but that felt heavier than telling you.”

Grandpa Sol took the book with careful hands and studied the tear without a single frown. “Thank you for the plain truth, Chioma,” he said, his voice low and even like water poured slowly. Chioma watched his fingers smooth the paper, feeling the studio air cool against their own palms now that the book was no longer theirs alone. Outside a cat’s collar bell gave one clear tiny ring from the plaza stones, bright and brief, and Chioma’s shoulders eased at the familiar sound. “We can mend what can be mended,” Grandpa Sol continued, already reaching for the small drawer of papers and pastes.

Together they worked on the sill itself, Chioma holding the page steady while Grandpa Sol cut a matching scrap from spare sketch paper that felt almost the same under Chioma’s exploring fingertip. The torn corner accepted the thin paste with a quiet stickiness, and Chioma pressed the new piece into place, feeling the join grow firm. “Does it look all right now?” Chioma asked, leaning so close their breath fogged a little circle on the page. Grandpa Sol nodded and answered, “It holds, Chioma, and the story still turns,” his words warm in the cooling air of the studio.

Chioma’s friend Joss appeared in the doorway with a basket of clean spoons from the drying rack, the metal catching soft glints of the lowering light. “May I help set things ready?” Joss asked, and Chioma smiled and made room on the sill. While the paste dried, Chioma and Joss lined the spoons up by size along the cool ceramic edge, smallest to largest, each handle cool and smooth. Grandpa Sol watched with quiet approval as Chioma adjusted the last spoon so its curve nestled against the next. The plaza below stayed still and peaceful, and the light inside the studio turned a deeper gold.

Chioma ran one finger along the leather lace that bound Grandpa Sol’s old tool roll, the texture firm and slightly pebbled under their touch, then returned to the mended page. The join no longer pulled or folded, and Chioma turned the leaf slowly, carefully, listening for any catch that never came. “It feels whole again,” Chioma said, and Grandpa Sol rested a gentle hand on Chioma’s shoulder, the fabric of his sleeve soft against their skin. Joss stacked the lined spoons into a neat nest and the three of them shared the quiet satisfaction of work finished without hurry or scolding.

As dusk settled over the quiet plaza the ceramic studio filled with milder shadows, and Chioma closed the book with both hands, the mended corner tucked safely inside. Grandpa Sol wiped the sill clean of paste crumbs while Chioma and Joss carried the spoons to the low shelf, their footsteps soft on the wooden boards. The air grew cooler still, brushing Chioma’s wrists like a kind farewell to the day’s light, and Chioma felt the earlier tangle of worry loosen completely. Everything that needed mending had been mended, and the plain truth had made the work simple and shared.

Later Chioma curled on the studio daybed near the same sill, the book resting on the blanket within easy reach, while Grandpa Sol dimmed the main lamps one by one. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Chioma breathed the calm evening air, still holding the memory of the cool sill and the careful hands that had helped, and sleep arrived like a slow warm tide that needed no more words.
