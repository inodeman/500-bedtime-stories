"Let's take turns with the heavy pot so nothing spills," Vera said as the last light of day painted the garden path a soft gold. Vera stood close to Uma beside a neat garden row where bean plants climbed their poles, green string waited in tidy loops on the ground, and two watering cans sat side by side with their spouts pointing the same way. The evening air felt pleasantly cool on Vera's bare arms, and a few small moths drifted in the fading brightness near the fence. Miss Dalca walked toward them carrying a heavy pot with both hands, her steps slow and even on the packed dirt path.

Miss Dalca lowered the heavy pot onto a broad flat stone at the head of the row. Warm steam curled upward and brushed Vera's cheeks with gentle heat when they bent closer to look inside. "The soup is ready for our supper, but this pot is quite weighty to manage alone," Miss Dalca said in her calm voice. Vera placed one palm near the side and felt how solid and warm the metal stayed through the air. The two watering cans reflected a little of the pale sky, and the unused string lay ready for the last few plants that still leaned slightly to one side.

Uma lifted a length of the green string and glanced at the tall bean stems that needed one final tie before night. Vera paused and listened to the quiet wind in the fig leaves that grew just beyond the garden fence, a soft continuous shushing that settled over the whole space like a blanket. "If we rush while we ladle, the heavy pot might tip and make a mess on the ground," Vera said, keeping their words steady and kind. Vera looked at Uma and knew they could choose two careful jobs instead of one hurried try. The soil under Vera's shoes felt firm and slightly damp from the watering they had done earlier.

Vera and Uma decided together to split the work into two slow jobs that would keep every drop of soup safe inside the bowls. Vera would grip the handles of the heavy pot and hold it perfectly steady while Uma dipped the ladle and filled each bowl without any hurry at all. Miss Dalca only offered a simple count they could speak aloud together, and she also gave the proper name for the knot the bean plants still required. "You may count slowly to three between every ladle," Miss Dalca told them with a small smile. The empty bowls waited on a low wooden board whose surface felt smooth and cool when Vera ran their fingers along the edge to check it.

Before the ladling began, Vera and Uma finished the string work so every plant would stand straight through the coming night. Miss Dalca named the knot once more and called it a simple loop knot while Vera practiced the motions on a spare piece of string. Vera's fingers moved carefully over the rough green strands, feeling each dry fiber as they formed the loops and pulled them snug. Uma held each bean stem gentle and still, and together they completed every remaining tie without any rush. When Vera brushed past one of the watering cans, the metal felt cool and slightly wet against their knuckle.

After the plants stood secure, Vera and Uma washed their hands at the little outdoor spigot near the row. They used lemon soap that foamed white and left a bright fresh scent rising from their clean palms and wrists. Vera rubbed until every bubble rinsed away down the drain and the smell of lemon soap hung lightly in the cooling air around them. Then Vera took firm hold of both handles on the heavy pot and set their feet wide apart on the earth of the garden row. Uma raised the long ladle, waited for Vera's quiet nod, and prepared to work in the same unhurried rhythm they had chosen.

"One," Vera said softly while Uma lowered the ladle into the soup. "Two," Uma replied, holding the ladle motionless so the liquid settled inside the bowl of the ladle. "Three," they spoke together before Uma poured the warm soup carefully into the first waiting bowl. A soft cloud of steam rose and warmed Vera's face for a moment before it drifted off into the evening. They repeated the same slow count for every single bowl, and the heavy pot never tilted even a little under Vera's steady hands. Miss Dalca stood nearby and watched without taking the ladle or the handles, only nodding once when the last bowl filled completely.

When every bowl held its share, Vera lowered the heavy pot back onto the flat stone with both hands still steady and sure. Uma passed Vera a floured towel so they could dab a few stray drops from the rim without haste. The towel felt soft and powdery against Vera's skin, dusted lightly with flour from the bread Miss Dalca had baked that afternoon for their meal. Together they carried the bowls toward the small table by the kitchen door, and Vera wiped the table in slow circles with a clean dry cloth until the wood looked neat and ready under the fading sky. The evening air brushed cool against their faces as they moved side by side without speaking much at all.

Inside the warm kitchen Vera sat down with Uma and Miss Dalca to taste the soup that had come safely from the garden row with the string and the two watering cans. The wooden chairs felt solid and welcoming under them, and the room held a gentle indoor warmth that wrapped around their shoulders like a soft shawl. Vera listened to the quiet clink of spoons against bowls and felt peaceful because they and Uma had chosen two slow jobs and finished everything together. Miss Dalca offered slices of bread, and Vera smiled across at Uma while the last daylight faded outside the glass. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Vera rested their hands quietly in their lap and watched that mild light while the whole house settled into the calm of night.
