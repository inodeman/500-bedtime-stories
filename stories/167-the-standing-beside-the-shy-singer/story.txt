The ferryman polished the bakery counter with steady strokes of a damp cloth, already midway through clearing flour dust while golden afternoon light poured across the wood and made every surface feel gently warm to the touch. Uma watched from a few steps away and smelled the sweet air that drifted from the ovens, and they noticed how the light caught on the ferryman’s calm smile as he worked without hurry. Outside the wide front window the street hummed with soft footsteps, and Uma stayed close enough to see the careful way the cloth glided back and forth. The ferryman glanced up once and nodded in quiet greeting before returning to his task, leaving the space peaceful and open.

Uma’s eyes found the open place beside a singer who stood near the counter facing the street, the singer’s shoulders relaxed as they waited for one more voice nearby to join a quiet evening song. The wooden floor felt smooth under Uma’s shoes, and a soft breeze carried the distant sound of wind in the fig leaves through the open doorway. Uma’s fingers curled lightly at their sides while they looked at that empty spot, and the light from the street lamps beginning to glow outside painted pale stripes across the floorboards. The singer swayed a little in place, humming so low it almost blended with the room, and Uma felt the quiet invitation of the space without anyone speaking yet.

Warm bread scent rose stronger from the baskets lined along the counter, wrapping around Uma like a soft blanket as they took one small step closer to the place beside the singer. The air near the ovens stayed pleasantly heated, and Uma could see steam curling from a fresh loaf that the ferryman had just set down. Uma’s heart beat a little faster with the shy feeling that settled in their chest, yet the room stayed bright and kind under the hanging lamps. The singer turned slightly and offered a gentle smile, and Uma stayed right there without backing away.

“There is room right here if you would like it,” the singer said in a voice as soft as falling flour. Uma looked at the open place and felt the cool ceramic rim of a nearby mug that rested on the counter when their hand brushed it by chance. The mug held warm milk that steamed faintly, and Uma kept their fingers there for a moment to steady themselves. Light from the street filtered in and touched Uma’s cheek with a mild glow while they considered the spot. The ferryman continued his slow polishing nearby and did not rush anyone.

Uma wanted to pretend the place did not matter, yet they chose instead the smallest brave version and spoke so their words could be heard clearly across the short distance. “I can stand beside you and add a quiet voice,” Uma said, their tone steady even though their shoulders felt a little tight. The singer nodded with warm eyes, and the air between them held the gentle heat from the nearby ovens. Uma moved into the place and felt the solid counter edge under one hand while the street lights outside brightened another degree. The ferryman looked over with a kind expression that asked for nothing more than whatever Uma offered.

“That is more than enough,” the ferryman said softly, his voice carrying only calm encouragement as he set the cloth aside and folded it neatly. Uma stood in the place beside the singer and let out a low hum that matched the simple tune already floating in the room. Yael, who had come along earlier and now sat on a low stool near the window, smiled at Uma and whispered, “Your voice fits just right.” The wooden stool creaked once under Yael in a friendly way, and Uma felt the cool evening air seeping in from the street mix with the bakery warmth around their ankles. The singer began a soft melody, and Uma added one careful note after another without any need for a grand performance.

The four of them stayed near the counter facing the street while the song remained small and easy, the notes drifting out toward the passersby who paused only long enough to smile. Uma felt the texture of the counter’s smooth grain under their palm and kept their voice gentle yet present. “We can keep it this quiet,” Uma said once the first verse ended, and the singer answered, “Yes, just like this is perfect.” Light from inside the bakery spilled onto the sidewalk in a golden rectangle, and the temperature near the door stayed mild and comfortable. The ferryman poured fresh milk into clean cups and handed one to Uma without a word of pressure, simply sharing the moment.

Uma carried the empty bowl that had held a few bread crumbs over to the sink, rinsed it under cool water that sparkled in the light, and came back to the place beside the singer with easy steps. The water left a clean fresh feeling on Uma’s hands, and they dried them on a soft towel before joining the last soft notes of the song. Yael clapped once very quietly, and the singer rested a hand lightly on the counter in thanks. Outside the window the street grew quieter as evening settled, and Uma felt the bakery’s steady warmth wrap around their shoulders like a familiar scarf. The ferryman stacked the clean cups with careful clinks that sounded almost like tiny bells.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Uma stood in the place beside the singer a little longer while the last of the song faded into comfortable silence, and they listened to the distant hush of the street as the air inside cooled just enough to feel restful. The ferryman dimmed one overhead lamp so the room settled into a gentle dimness, and Uma’s breathing slowed to match the peaceful pace of everyone nearby. Yael leaned against the counter edge with a contented sigh, and the singer closed their eyes for a moment of quiet. Uma felt the smooth floor steady under their feet and knew the small brave choice had fit the evening perfectly, leaving the bakery calm and ready for rest as night deepened outside.
