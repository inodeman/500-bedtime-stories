Papa was already bent over the low harbor step where parcels changed hands, his sleeves rolled high as he eased a long wooden crate open with careful fingers. Cool evening air brushed Ximena’s face while they walked nearer, and the gray stone underfoot still held a faint daytime warmth. Inside the crate sat a neat row of flower jars that needed water carried in before the bright blooms wilted on the way home to the table. Papa tried to cradle two jars at once while reaching for a nearby bucket, but the awkward weight made the glass clink and shift. Ximena stood close enough to see the soft petals and the pale water rings already forming on the jar bottoms. “These came on the afternoon boat,” Papa said quietly, “and they will look lovely once they drink.” The harbor smelled of salt and wet rope, yet Ximena kept their eyes on the tipping jars and the way Papa’s shoulders worked.

Ximena stepped onto the same wide step and felt the solid stone press against the soles of their shoes. Afternoon light slanted low across the water and turned every ripple into a thin silver line. Papa lifted one jar higher, then set it down again because he could not manage both the heavy water bucket and the fragile row at the same moment. Without a word Ximena picked up the smaller pail that sat waiting near a coil of rope. The metal handle felt smooth and cool in their grip. “I can bring the water while you steady the jars,” Ximena said. Papa glanced over and gave a small nod. Nearby parcels wrapped in layers of fresh paper waited their turn to be claimed, and the clean crisp scent rose each time a breeze lifted a loose corner. Ximena dipped the pail carefully into the calm harbor edge and felt the gentle pull of the water filling it.

They carried the pail back together, Ximena taking the front handle and Papa guiding the rear so nothing spilled. The surface of the water inside the pail mirrored the pale sky for a moment before tiny waves broke the picture. Ximena poured a slow stream into the first jar, watching the stems darken and the blossoms lift a little. “Just enough for each one,” Papa murmured. Ximena answered, “We can do half and half so none tip over.” The next jar received its share with the same quiet care. A damp clay wall rose beside the step where the old harbor building met the water, and when Ximena rested one hand against it for balance the cool rough surface pressed gently into their palm. The texture stayed with them as they moved to the third jar, steady and sure.

One by one the row of flower jars that needed water carried in grew heavier and brighter. Ximena noticed how Papa’s arms no longer strained once the water work was shared. Soft lantern light from a nearby boat reflected on the glass and made little golden stars dance inside each jar. “The blue ones look happy now,” Ximena said, pointing to a cluster of cornflowers. Papa smiled and replied, “They do, and the table will thank us tonight.” They worked side by side without hurry, pouring and steadying until every jar stood upright and full. The harbor step felt spacious again once the awkward balance was gone. Ximena’s sleeves had caught a few cool drops, but the drops only made the cotton cling lightly and pleasantly.

When the last jar had drunk its fill, Ximena and Papa lifted the whole row into a shallow carrying tray lined with soft cloth. The tray smelled faintly of the fresh paper that had wrapped the other parcels earlier, a clean dry scent that mixed with the flower sweetness. Ximena held one end of the tray while Papa held the other, and together they stepped away from the water’s edge. A distant gate latch clicked shut somewhere along the quay, the brief metal sound clear and friendly in the quiet air. No one else needed to hurry them. They walked the short path toward home with even steps, the jars nestled safely between them. The evening temperature cooled another degree, and Ximena felt the change on their bare forearms like a soft blanket settling.

At the house Ximena helped set the tray on the kitchen counter while Papa found the best spots on the wooden table. Warm yellow light from the ceiling lamp fell across the blooms and made their colors deeper. Ximena arranged three jars near the center and two more near the edges so every seat would see them. “They fit just right,” Ximena said. Papa answered, “They do, thanks to the water we carried.” Ximena did not wait for more words; they simply wiped a stray drop from the table with a clean cloth and felt the smooth wood under their fingers. Outside the window the sky turned the soft color of lavender, and the room grew stiller. Ximena rested both palms on their knees for a moment while sitting on the low stool, feeling the calm settle through their hands and legs.

Papa brought two cups of warm milk and set one beside Ximena. The ceramic held a gentle heat that traveled into Ximena’s palms when they wrapped their fingers around it. They sipped slowly and listened to the quiet house settle for night. The flower jars on the table caught every bit of indoor light and threw tiny colored shadows onto the walls. Ximena watched those shadows move whenever a small breeze came through the open window. No one spoke of the harbor work again; the jars simply stood there full and bright, proof of the shared carrying. Ximena’s milk tasted mild and sweet, and the warmth spread through them until their shoulders loosened completely.

Later Ximena helped Papa draw the curtains partway so the room stayed gentle. The fabric felt thick and soft against their hands as they pulled it across. Outside, the last of the harbor sounds faded into ordinary evening hush. Ximena sat once more near the table and looked at the row of jars, remembering the cool pail and the careful pouring. Papa sat across from them and smiled without saying anything extra. The whole house seemed to breathe more slowly. Ximena’s eyelids grew heavier in the kindest way, and they leaned their cheek against the smooth table edge for just a second, feeling its solid coolness.

Ximena climbed into bed while Papa tucked the blanket up to their chin. The sheets smelled clean and the pillow held a mild fluffiness under their head. From the bed Ximena could still see the edge of the table and the soft outlines of the flower jars waiting for morning. Papa dimmed the main light and left only one small glow. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Ximena closed their eyes and felt the quiet of the room wrap around them like another blanket, warm and steady, while the memory of shared water and careful hands stayed gentle in their mind until sleep arrived without a single rush.
