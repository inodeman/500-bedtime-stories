The soft tick reached Opal first, a tiny steady sound from deeper in the room. Opal paused in the library aisle where a bright slice of sun lay across the carpet like a warm golden path. The light felt gentle on Opal’s bare ankles and made the dust motes dance slowly in the air. Opal’s fingers brushed the cool edge of a wooden shelf as they leaned forward to listen harder. Nana Ruth stood a few steps behind with Dina, both quiet so Opal could notice everything. The tick came again, clear and small.

Opal stepped into the sun slice and felt its mild heat on their cheeks. Beside a low table sat a round tin clock whose face caught the light. Right then the clock gave one clear chime, and at the very same moment a single bark floated in from far outside the tall window. Opal’s eyes widened. “Dina, did you hear them together?” Opal asked in a hushed voice. The bark was distant and low, the chime bright and near, and they arrived like two friends holding hands. Opal’s heart felt full of quiet surprise.

Dina moved closer so the sun warmed both their shoulders. “I heard them,” Dina said softly. “The bark and the chime at once.” Opal nodded and pointed without touching. The wish to pocket the moment or poke the clock was real inside Opal, yet they kept both hands open and still. The air near the window carried a light chamomile smell from the little pot Nana Ruth had set on the sill earlier. Opal breathed it in and smiled at how calm everything felt.

Nana Ruth came to stand with them in the golden light. “What do you see, Opal?” she asked. Opal answered, “The clock chimed exactly when the far dog barked, Nana Ruth. They met right here.” The three of them looked together at the tin clock and the open window. Outside leaves stirred with a soft rustle. Opal showed Dina how to lean just close enough to watch the second hand move without bumping the table. The wood under Opal’s palm felt smooth and steady.

They stayed very still while the slice of sun drifted a little farther along the carpet. Opal whispered, “We can look without moving it.” Dina copied the careful way Opal held still, eyes bright. A tin lid from a small round box of bookmarks rested near the clock; Opal brushed one fingertip across its cool ridged surface just once and then drew the hand back. The far dog did not bark again, and the clock waited quietly for its next soft mark of time. Warmth from the sun made Opal’s sweater feel cozy against their arms.

Nana Ruth rested a gentle hand on Opal’s shoulder. The fabric of her sleeve smelled faintly of the same calm air. “Shall we leave the chime and the bark right where they met?” Nana Ruth asked. Opal looked at Dina and then at the clock. “Yes,” Opal said. “We looked, and that is enough.” Dina smiled and tucked both hands into pockets so nothing would be disturbed. The library aisle stayed peaceful, filled only with the slow drift of light and the far-away hush of the street.

Opal took one last slow breath of the warm air and stepped back onto the softer part of the carpet. The three of them turned together and walked toward the reading chairs near the window. Opal’s shoes made almost no sound on the rug. Dina matched Opal’s quiet steps. Nana Ruth carried the little bookmark box with its tin lid closed tight again. The slice of sun still glowed behind them like a friendly path they had visited and left whole.

In the chair corner Opal curled their legs up and felt the cushion’s gentle give. Dina sat close enough that their elbows almost touched. Nana Ruth settled opposite and opened a picture book whose pages made a soft papery whisper. Opal listened to the turning leaves and the quiet room. No one needed to speak for a while. The afternoon light grew a little softer and turned the walls the color of honey. Opal felt safe and full of the small wonder they had chosen to keep only by looking.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Opal watched its steady circle of light while Nana Ruth’s voice rose and fell in the calm story. Dina’s breathing slowed to match Opal’s. The tin clock chimed once more from the aisle, farther away now and gentle. Outside the far dog was silent. Opal closed their eyes for a moment and felt the chair’s warm fabric under their cheek, knowing the meeting of chime and bark still rested exactly where they had found it, safe and undisturbed.
