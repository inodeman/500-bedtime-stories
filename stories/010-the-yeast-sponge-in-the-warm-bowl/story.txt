Soft dusk light slipped across the quiet craft corner and touched the stacked papers with a gentle silver edge while a mild breeze from the garden cooled the paste jars. Una walked in slowly and felt the smooth warmth of the floorboards under their socks. Auntie Luz sat nearby humming, and the wooden rack held drying cutouts that rustled faintly when the air moved. Una breathed in the green scent of tomato vines drifting through the open side door and smiled at the calm room. They set their hands on the edge of the bench and noticed how still everything felt in the fading day.

Una’s eyes found the yeast sponge bubbling in a warm bowl that Auntie Luz had placed on a folded towel near the papers because the craft corner stayed evenly heated by the late sun and kept dough happy while they worked. Tiny bubbles rose and popped with soft clicks. Una leaned closer and wanted to lift the cloth right away to see if it was ready. “Auntie Luz, may I check the sponge now?” Una asked in a low voice. The air near the bowl felt pleasantly steamy against their cheeks. Auntie Luz looked up from sorting colored squares and shook her head kindly.

“Not yet, Una,” Auntie Luz said. “It needs more quiet time under the cloth.” Una nodded but their fingers still itched to peek. They traced a slow circle on the bench and felt the grain of the bench under one fingertip, rough and steady like tiny rivers of wood. A soft jingle sounded as the house cat walked past wearing its collar bell, then settled under the rack. Una pulled their hand back and decided the cloth should stay right where it was. The paste jar gave off a mild clean smell that mixed with the warmer air rising from the bowl.

Una chose a quiet task instead and sat on the low stool to tear long strips of soft paper for a hanging mobile. “I can make the strips while we wait,” Una said. Auntie Luz smiled and answered, “That sounds perfect, Una. Tear them slowly so the edges stay nice.” The paper felt dry and whispery between Una’s fingers, and the light from the window turned the strips pale gold. They worked without hurry, laying each piece across their lap. The temperature in the corner stayed gently warm, as if the walls themselves held the day’s last heat.

While Una tore another strip they listened for the far clock in the hall rather than the nearer kitchen timer, just as Auntie Luz had once shown them, letting the distant soft ticks fill the quiet. The wooden rack above them cast thin striped shadows that moved only when the cat shifted. Una stacked the finished strips neatly and felt proud of the even lengths. “These will look pretty when we hang them,” Una whispered. Auntie Luz replied, “Yes, Una, and the sponge will finish when it is ready too.” A cool draft brushed Una’s ankles for a moment before the air settled again.

Una pasted two strips together with careful dabs, pressing the edges until they held. The paste felt cool and slippery at first then grew sticky under their thumbs. They wiped their hands on a soft cloth and glanced once more at the covered bowl but left the cloth alone. Outside the last light faded to a deeper blue, and the papers on the table glowed faintly. Una hummed the same tune Auntie Luz had hummed earlier, matching the calm pace of their work. The bench supported their elbows firmly while they shaped a small paper star.

Time stretched in the pleasant way that happens when hands stay busy. Una cut a circle from thicker paper and held it up to catch the leftover window light, watching it shine like a little moon. Auntie Luz brought a cup of warm water with lemon for them both and set it on a coaster. “Sip slowly, Una,” she said. Una did, feeling the warm steam rise toward their nose. They returned to the rack and clipped three finished pieces there so they could dry without touching. The yeast sponge stayed covered and quiet, doing its own unseen work in the warm bowl.

At last Auntie Luz lifted one corner of the cloth and nodded. Una came close and saw the sponge had risen high and full of tiny tunnels. “It finished in its own time,” Una said with a happy breath. They helped carry the bowl carefully toward the kitchen door while the craft corner settled into evening. The papers and paste waited ready for tomorrow, and the wooden rack held everything in neat rows. Una felt the last warmth of the day still clinging to the air around their shoulders as they walked.

They returned to the craft corner for one more look at the finished mobile pieces hanging ready. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Una sat once more on the stool and rested their hands in their lap, listening to the house grow quieter. Auntie Luz dimmed the brighter lamp so only the gentle lantern light remained, and the cat’s soft breathing joined the stillness. Una closed their eyes for a moment and felt perfectly ready for sleep, knowing the sponge had risen well and the paper work waited safely for morning light.
