"Niko, I want to tell you my wish for next year's garden, and I will say every part in the order of what matters most to me," Mama said as they sat side by side at the kitchen table before breakfast with their chairs pulled close together.

The early light slipped through the window in long pale bands that warmed the backs of Niko's hands and made the rims of the cups gleam like tiny moons on the wooden surface. Niko felt a quick bubble of bigger news rising inside them, news about a discovery from yesterday that seemed almost too large to hold, yet they kept their mouth closed and watched Mama's calm face instead. The air still carried a mild coolness left from the night, soft against Niko's bare arms, and the faint smell of rising bread drifted from the counter while the table felt solid and familiar under their elbows. Niko heard Mama take one slow breath before she began, and that small sound helped them decide to stay quiet and listen all the way through to the final sentence.

Mama started with the first and most important piece of her wish and spoke so gently that each word had space to settle. "First comes deep dark soil full of tiny living helpers that can hold rain and feed every root," she said, and Niko nodded once without speaking although their own story pressed forward like a bright bird. Outside the open window the wind in the fig leaves made a soft continuous rustling that rose and fell like quiet breathing, and Niko let that sound join Mama's voice inside their ears. The temperature near their feet stayed pleasantly cool on the floorboards, and they shifted their chair another inch closer so the two seats nearly touched. Niko's fingers traced the smooth edge of the table while they waited, holding the bigger news carefully inside because Mama was only beginning the list.

"Second in importance is clean water we can give whenever the sky stays dry for too many days," Mama continued in the same even tone, and Niko watched her hands rest quietly beside her cup without hurrying. A straw mat lay beneath the blue bowl of apples in the middle of the table, and when Niko's wrist brushed it they felt its dry crossed texture like tiny woven twigs against their skin. The light softened for a moment as a thin cloud passed the sun, painting gentler patterns across the floor near their toes, and Niko used that pause to breathe in and out slowly. Their bigger news still wanted to spill out, yet they remembered the careful order Mama was using and stayed patient. The kitchen smelled clean from the washed plates stacked nearby, and the close chairs let Niko see every small kind movement Mama made while she spoke.

"Third I wish for long bright hours of sun that will warm the leaves and help the blossoms open wide," Mama said next, and Niko almost opened their mouth but closed it again to hear the rest. The faint smell of cedar floated from the wooden box on the counter where Mama kept her seed packets and small garden tools, and the woody scent mixed gently with the morning air around them. Niko felt the cool smooth rim of their empty cup under their fingertips and turned it once so the handle faced the room. They listened through the whole sentence and the quiet that followed because Mama was placing each part of the wish like stones along a path. The light now rested more firmly on the tablecloth, showing its fine weave, and Niko's shoulders stayed loose even though the news inside them felt large and shining.

"Fourth comes the busy bees and the bright butterflies that will visit flower after flower," Mama added with a small smile that reached her eyes, and Niko smiled back without speaking yet. A soft wave of warmth crossed their knees when the oven clicked off nearby, raising the temperature of the air just enough to feel friendly. Niko could hear a distant bird call through the window along with the fading leaf sounds, but mostly they heard Mama's steady words landing one after another. Their feet pressed lightly against the smooth cool boards still shaded under the table, and they counted quietly to themselves to keep from interrupting. The bigger news about the perfect stone waited in their pocket like a secret, but Niko held it with care because listening felt right in this close and gentle space.

"And fifth, though every part still matters, I wish for peaceful time each evening to walk between the rows and notice what has grown since morning," Mama said, finishing the full wish for next year's garden in the order she had chosen. Niko sat very still and let the last sentence sink all the way in before they moved or made a sound. The light from the window now touched the side of Mama's hair and made it shine a little, and Niko noticed the soft settling sounds of the house around them. They felt quietly glad they had listened to every word, and the air smelled clean and ready for the day that was beginning. After a short calm pause Niko asked their one real question in a clear voice that filled the small space between the chairs. "Mama, if we plant the peas early on tall poles, will they climb high enough for me to pick them while I am standing up?"

Mama answered with the same care she had given her own long wish. "Yes, Niko, if we give them strong poles and soft deep soil they will climb high enough for you," she said, and her voice sounded warm and sure across the short distance. Niko felt the slight roughness of the tablecloth under their palm as they finally shared something small of their own after the complete listening. "I kept one smooth gray pebble from the garden path that fits exactly inside my pocket," Niko said, and Mama leaned closer to hear every quiet word. The morning light brightened another degree and warmed the side of Niko's face while they spoke, and the kitchen felt even more peaceful now that both of them had taken their turns. Niko's news had waited until the end and become a small simple gift, and that felt right in the soft mild air between them.

They remained at the table a little longer with the chairs still close, and Niko rested their hand near Mama's so their fingers nearly touched on the wood. The plain warm smell of toast that was almost ready drifted over from the counter, and Niko felt content from the crown of their head down to their toes. Outside the light grew fuller and the yard sounds began to wake, yet inside everything stayed slow and kind. Niko thought about the garden that would arrive next year with its soil and water and sun and bees and walking time, and they also thought about the gray pebble waiting safely in their pocket. Mama poured a little milk into Niko's cup with a soft liquid sound, and the temperature of the room settled into a perfect mildness that made staying easy. No one hurried at all, and the close chairs held them both inside the same quiet circle of morning before breakfast truly started.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Even though daylight now filled most of the kitchen its small steady circle of gold remained on the sill, and Niko watched it for a peaceful moment while their thoughts rested. They could still catch a trace of cedar if they breathed deeply near the counter, and the straw mat stayed steady under the fruit bowl as a simple place for things to sit. Niko and Mama needed no more words just then; they simply sat listening to the gentle house and to each other in the easy way that required no rush, while outside the fig leaves had grown still and the new day waited patiently beyond the glass. The chairs remained close, the table held their breakfast things ready, and everything felt complete and kind in the calm kitchen.
