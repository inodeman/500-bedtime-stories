The shopping list lay crumpled yet open on the smooth brown wood of the library return desk, its hurried pencil marks racing from top to bottom in uneven rows. Gita leaned in so close that the paper filled their whole view and saw how the word for flour looped into the word for berries. Soft white light from the tall front windows spilled across the desk and made the graphite shine in places while cooler blue shadows rested in the creases. Gita touched one corner with the pad of their finger and felt the paper's dry crinkle. The air around the desk held a mild indoor warmth that contrasted with the chill seeping under the door. Gita studied every shaky line and understood that this shopping list written in a hurry needed a second pair of eyes before anyone could use it well.

Outside beyond the glass the rainy steps shone dark and slick under a curtain of gentle rain that fell without wind or fuss. Gita stood at the return desk and watched Mr. Ade turn the list slowly in both hands, his kind face showing quiet puzzlement. From a little dish of dried buds on the counter rose the smell of lavender, clean and restful, drifting toward Gita like a friendly greeting. Mr. Ade muttered the first few items under his breath then shook his head. Gita stayed nearby, feeling the carpet's low pile under their shoes and hearing the muted patter of rain against the stone outside. The whole library seemed to wait in calm patience while the afternoon light grew softer.

High on the wall a tin clock marked the time with a steady bright ticking sound that carried clearly through the still air. Mr. Ade looked up at Gita and said, "I jotted this down in a rush, and now the letters dance whenever I try to read them." Gita took a small step forward so their elbow rested lightly on the desk edge. Cooler air swirled near the floor while warmer air gathered near the lamp. Gita glanced once more at the list and noticed soap written over milk and the address of the market half finished. They felt a quiet wish to make the work easier for Mr. Ade right then.

Gita spoke in a clear soft voice and said, "I can hold the list steady and read the left side if you read the right side." Mr. Ade's eyes brightened and he replied, "Yes, please, Gita, that would help more than you know." Together their hands kept the paper flat under the gentle glow of the desk lamp that cast a golden circle on the wood. Gita's free hand brushed against the strap of Mr. Ade's cloth bag and felt the neat raised ridge of a quilt stitch running along the seam, firm and tidy beneath their fingertip. Rain continued its quiet work on the steps outside, filling the entrance with a fresh damp scent that mixed with the indoor calm.

Gita began reading aloud in an even tone, "First the carrots and then the big box of tea." Mr. Ade answered at once, "And here the eggs along with three ripe pears." They went down the whole page that way, Gita taking one useful half of the job without any extra words. The light on the desk stayed steady and warm on their joined hands. When the last item was clear Mr. Ade copied the list onto a fresh card in slower careful letters. Gita watched the pencil move and felt glad inside the quiet room where book spines lined the walls like colorful friends.

With the new list finished they walked together toward the doors that opened onto the rainy steps. Cool misty air touched Gita's face as soon as they crossed the threshold and stood under the wide overhang. Silver light bounced up from the wet stone and made little rainbows in the falling drops farther down. Gita and Mr. Ade paused side by side on the top step where their shoes stayed dry. The temperature felt pleasantly cool after the warmer library hall. Mr. Ade folded the neat card and slid it deep into his pocket while Gita watched a thin shadow on the nearby wall move the width of a thumb.

Mr. Ade turned to Gita and said, "You saw what I needed and simply did it." Gita only nodded and smiled a small smile that needed no reply. They both listened to the soft continuous sound of rain striking the lower steps and the railing. The air on the covered steps carried a clean wet freshness that made Gita breathe deeply. After a peaceful minute Mr. Ade started carefully down the stairs, one hand on the rail, and Gita watched until he reached the sidewalk and raised his arm in a last friendly greeting.

Gita stepped back inside where the library air wrapped around them like a mild embrace, warmer and still. They returned briefly to the return desk and rested both palms on the smooth cool wood that still held a faint memory of the list. The high windows now showed a softer gray light as the rain continued without hurry. Gita felt the quiet settle deeper into their shoulders. They chose a low chair near the children's shelves and sat with their hands in their lap, listening to the far-off pages turning and the gentle hush that filled every aisle. Everything around Gita felt safe and slow and ready for evening.

In the reading corner beside the wide window Gita curled their legs onto the cushion and let their gaze rest on the rain-streaked glass. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Its gentle light spread a pool of honey-colored warmth across the nearby rug and touched the edges of Gita's sleeves with gold. They breathed in the peaceful room and felt their eyelids grow comfortably heavy while the last of the afternoon faded into early dusk. Outside the rainy steps emptied of footsteps and inside the library kept its kind stillness around Gita as they rested.
