The warm smell of toasted sesame drifted from a little dish near the south window, and Lior’s small hands were already busy pressing damp soil into the seed pots lined along the greenhouse bench. Soft golden light poured through the glass and rested on Lior’s wrists while they patted each pot smooth with careful thumbs. The air felt gently warm against Lior’s cheeks, and the wooden bench felt steady under their elbows. A small collar rested under a clean cloth at the far end of the bench, left there so the starch could settle before it would be ready. Lior glanced once at the quiet covered shape, then returned to the cool earth between their fingers. Auntie June moved nearby in her soft shoes, and the greenhouse held only calm sounds of leaves and light.

Lior leaned closer to one pot and felt the fine grit of soil under their nails. “Auntie June, may I lift the cloth and see the collar now?” Lior asked in a low voice that barely stirred the warm air. Auntie June came to stand beside the bench, and the light from the south window made her smile look kind. “Not quite yet, Lior,” she said. “The starch still needs its own quiet time.” Lior nodded and ran one finger along the rim of a seed pot, feeling the smooth clay edge. The greenhouse held a mild heat that made Lior’s shoulders relax. Femi appeared at the doorway with a small watering can and waved, and Lior waved back with a soil-dusted hand before turning again to the neat row of pots.

Lior wanted very much to peek, yet they kept both hands on the pots instead. “I can wait,” Lior said, almost to the seeds themselves. Auntie June rested a hand on the bench and answered, “That is a fine choice, Lior. We will let it finish in its own time.” The cloth over the collar stayed still in the gentle light. Lior breathed in the green scent of leaves and began to set the little pots in a straighter line, one after another. Cool moisture clung to the outside of each pot and felt pleasant against Lior’s palms. Outside the glass, the garden path lay quiet, and the afternoon light stayed soft and even.

Auntie June reached for her cotton apron and smoothed its worn fabric as she watched Lior work. Lior liked the way the apron hung near the bench and brushed it lightly once with the back of a hand while reaching for another pot. The texture felt familiar and kind. “Shall we give the seeds a tiny drink?” Lior asked. Femi stepped closer and set the watering can down with a soft click. “I brought just enough,” Femi said. Lior smiled and tipped the can so a thin silver stream touched the soil without soaking it. The water made a tiny dark circle in each pot, and Lior watched the circles grow slowly. Warmth from the south window settled across Lior’s hair like a light blanket.

Lior decided the collar could stay covered, and they chose a quiet task instead. They wiped a stray crumb of soil from the bench edge with a soft cloth Auntie June offered, then began to turn each seed pot a quarter turn so every side could feel the light. The wood of the bench felt smooth and slightly cool under Lior’s wrists. Auntie June sat on a low stool and sorted empty pots into a neat stack. “You are doing careful work, Lior,” she said. Lior answered, “I like helping the seeds while we wait.” The covered collar rested untouched at the end of the bench, and Lior only looked at it now and then between turns. The greenhouse air stayed mild, and a leaf brushed the glass with a whisper.

Time moved slowly in the warm light. Lior counted the pots in a soft whisper—one, two, three—and lined them again so their rims made a gentle curve. Femi helped by handing Lior a clean stick for making tiny holes, and Lior pressed the stick straight down into two pots that still needed seeds. The stick felt dry and light between Lior’s fingers. Far across the garden a gate latch clicked shut with a clear small sound, then everything grew quiet again. Lior paused to listen, smiled, and returned to the holes. Auntie June sprinkled a pinch of fine seed into Lior’s open palm, and the seeds felt like tiny smooth beads against Lior’s skin. Lior dropped them one by one into the waiting holes and covered them with a soft blanket of soil.

Lior’s shoulders stayed loose as they worked. They did not lift the cloth. Instead they brushed stray soil from the bench into a little dustpan and set the dustpan aside. The south window glowed with late light that turned the glass pale gold. Auntie June brought a small chair from the corner and Lior helped set the chair square to the little side table so it sat neat and ready. The chair legs made a soft scrape, then stood still. Lior sat for a moment and felt the calm of the room settle around them. “Is the collar nearly ready, do you think?” Lior asked quietly. Auntie June touched the edge of the cloth without lifting it and said, “Soon, Lior. It is finishing its work.” Lior nodded and rested both hands in their lap, feeling the last traces of cool soil on their fingertips.

When the light began to soften toward evening, Auntie June finally lifted the cloth. The small collar lay smooth and neat, the starch settled just as it should. Lior stood close and looked without touching yet. “It looks ready,” Lior said. Auntie June smiled and answered, “You waited well, and now it is done.” Lior felt a quiet gladness settle in their chest. Femi clapped once softly, and the three of them stood together by the bench while the last warm air of the day drifted through the greenhouse. Lior reached out at last and felt the firm, even surface of the collar with one careful finger. It held its shape beautifully. Outside, the garden had grown still, and the seed pots waited patiently in their row for morning.

Lior helped Auntie June fold the cloth and set the finished collar on a clean tray near the south window. The tray felt cool and smooth under Lior’s hands. They watered the last pot with a final gentle sip from the can, then stepped back to look at the whole quiet bench. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Lior leaned against Auntie June’s side and felt the steady warmth of the room while the evening light faded to a kind dusk. Femi waved good night from the doorway, and Lior waved back. Auntie June rested a hand on Lior’s shoulder, and together they left the greenhouse bench tidy, the seed pots safe, and the starched collar finished in its own good time.
