The smell of warm bread curled through the air while Nim’s hands were already busy patting and straightening the quilt corner that lay across the bakery counter on that soft busy morning. Flour drifted in pale clouds under the gentle window light, and Nim’s fingers sank into the padded squares as they tugged the edge smooth so it covered the wooden tray completely. Soft voices of early customers rose and fell like a quiet tide, and the counter felt sturdy under Nim’s elbows. Nim kept smoothing until every corner of the quilt sat neat, the fabric warm from the ovens nearby and wide enough that both arms could lift it if needed. The morning light turned the stitching silver for a moment, and Nim hummed a little without thinking, hands never still.

Yelena stepped close enough that Nim could feel the soft brush of a jacket sleeve, and together they watched Ms. Okonkwo move behind the counter with slow careful steps. A low clink of spoons against pans floated out, and the air held a mild heat that made cheeks pink. Nim glanced at the quilt corner again and whispered, “This quilt corner is wide enough to lift, but it will not feel fair if only one person is served first.” Yelena’s eyes opened wider in the soft light, and she nodded so that a loose curl bounced once. Ms. Okonkwo paused with a tray of golden rolls still half-hidden and simply waited, her hands resting lightly on the wood. The busy morning stayed gentle, full of shuffling feet that never hurried.

Nim lifted one edge of the quilt just high enough to peek, and the steam that rose felt damp and pleasant against their face. Underneath sat six round rolls still soft from baking, their tops shining a little. Nim set the quilt corner back down and said, “We can make smaller, even shares so everyone gets some, and Yelena is included too.” The words came out calm, and Yelena answered right away, “I can hold the edges steady if you want.” Light from the open doorway striped the floor in pale gold bars, and the counter stayed cool where Nim’s wrists rested. Ms. Okonkwo gave the smallest nod and stepped aside without adding any extra words, letting the choice simply stand as it was.

Together Nim and Yelena folded the quilt corner back in neat halves until it made a smaller soft square that left room for plates. Nim’s hands moved carefully, tearing each roll into tidy pieces that looked the same size, the crumb springy and light between their fingers. A faint coolness drifted in whenever the door swung, and somewhere outside the leaves of the fig tree stirred. The pieces lined up in two neat rows, and Nim said, “These are for you and me and anyone else who comes, all the same.” Yelena smiled and answered, “That feels right,” her voice barely louder than the soft clatter of a nearby spoon. The morning light warmed the tops of the rolls until they looked almost glowing.

Ms. Okonkwo slid two small plates forward and watched as Nim placed the even shares with steady hands. The ceramic of one plate brushed Nim’s thumb, and they noticed the cool ceramic rim against their skin for a quiet second before letting go. No one spoke for a stretch of heartbeats, only the gentle hush of the bakery filling the space. Yelena took her plate and held it close, the heat of the bread rising in tiny curls of steam that smelled sweet. Nim’s own plate felt balanced, and they both stood at the counter while the soft busy morning continued around them like a slow song. The quilt corner now rested in its smaller folded shape, still soft enough to touch.

Outside the open door the wind in the fig leaves made a dry whispering sound that drifted in and mixed with the quiet inside. Nim listened while they chewed a small bite, the bread tender and still warm. Yelena rocked once on her heels and said nothing more, simply sharing the same calm silence. Ms. Okonkwo wiped a cloth along the far end of the counter, the motion steady and kind, and never once explained what had happened. Nim felt the floor boards solid under their shoes and watched dust motes turn in a slant of light. Everything stayed gentle, the quilt corner waiting like a quiet friend at the edge of the wood.

After the plates were empty Nim carried the empty bowl that had held a little pot of soft butter over to the sink, the water there running cool for a moment as they set it down, then walked back to the counter where Yelena still waited. The air near the sink had felt fresher, and returning brought the bakery warmth again around their shoulders. Nim’s hands found the quilt corner once more and smoothed the folded edge so it looked tidy. Soft footsteps of other customers passed behind them, never loud. Ms. Okonkwo glanced over and offered only a quiet smile that needed no words at all. The light through the window had grown a little fuller, painting the quilt stitches in calm yellow.

Nim and Yelena leaned their elbows on the counter side by side, the wood smooth under their skin, while the last bits of steam faded from the air. The quilt corner stayed folded small and even, no longer hiding anything, and Nim touched one square just to feel the soft give of the batting. Somewhere a clock ticked slow and friendly. Yelena’s shoulder rested near Nim’s for a breath, and neither of them needed to speak. Ms. Okonkwo turned to greet a new customer with the same unhurried kindness, leaving the two children in their peaceful spot. The morning kept its soft busy rhythm, full of gentle sounds that never startled.

Later the bakery quieted by degrees, and Nim rested both hands flat on the counter one last time, feeling the leftover warmth. Yelena stretched and yawned a small yawn that made Nim smile. The quilt corner lay neat and ready for whatever came next, its edges no longer lifted high. Outside the light had shifted toward afternoon gold. Nim breathed in the settled air and felt ready for the slow walk home whenever it came. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
