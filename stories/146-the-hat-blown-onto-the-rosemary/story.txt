A gentle evening breeze brushed the cottage porch with a mild coolness that made the wooden rails feel smooth under open hands. Yuri walked out from the doorway and paused on the top step where the last warm light of day lay in quiet yellow patches across the boards. Yuri noticed the soft swish of leaves and the way the air carried a faint freshness from the garden beds. Just below the railing the rosemary bushes stood neat and green, and right in the middle of them rested a round hat, settled as if it had chosen the spot on purpose and remained quite unharmed. Yuri tilted their head and watched the hat for a long calm moment while the light shifted slowly along the porch floor.

Cousin Pio appeared in the hall doorway carrying a wide wooden tray stacked with clean napkins and two small bowls. The tray wobbled a little because Pio also tried to stretch one arm toward the rosemary at the same time. Yuri saw at once that the hat in the bushes made the whole task awkward for one person. The evening air still held that mild coolness against Yuri’s cheeks as they stepped closer. Pio’s shoes scuffed softly on the boards. Yuri stayed near and simply watched the careful way Pio balanced everything without any hurry or fuss.

“I can take the tray for you,” Yuri said in a low clear voice. Pio looked over with a small grateful smile and answered, “That would help a great deal, Yuri.” Yuri lifted the tray with both hands and felt its steady weight. The wood felt smooth and dry. Pio knelt by the rosemary and reached in with slow fingers so the needles would not break. A quiet breeze moved again and the light on the porch turned a softer gold. Yuri held the tray level and waited without speaking more, content to share the work in silence for a while.

Pio lifted the hat free and brushed a few green sprigs from the brim. The hat came away clean and whole. “It landed so gently,” Pio said. Yuri nodded and replied, “The rosemary kept it safe.” Together they turned toward the open door that led into the hall just inside. Yuri carried the tray first and felt the change from outdoor coolness to the milder indoor air. The floorboards inside felt a little warmer under Yuri’s feet. Pio followed with the hat held carefully in both hands, and the two of them moved side by side down the short quiet passage.

In the hall the late light filtered through a side window and made pale rectangles on the wall. Yuri set the tray on a low table and noticed a row of spoons left from earlier. Without being asked Yuri began to line the spoons up by size from smallest to largest so they rested in a neat silver curve. The metal felt cool and smooth. Pio hung the hat on a peg near the coats and smoothed the brim once more. A faint scent of soapy wool drifted from a folded scarf on the nearby chair, clean and gentle. Yuri finished the spoons and stood back, pleased by the tidy line.

A soft tinkling sound came from the far end of the hall as the house cat walked past, its collar bell giving one clear bright note. Yuri turned and watched the cat settle on a cushion. Pio said, “Thank you for taking half of everything, Yuri.” Yuri answered quietly, “I saw you needed another pair of hands.” They both smiled. Yuri ran one finger along the edge of the hat where a thin leather lace was threaded through the band; the leather lace felt firm and slightly ridged under the fingertip. The hall stayed peaceful while the light grew dimmer and kinder.

Yuri and Pio carried the bowls from the tray into the little side nook where cups and plates lived. The air inside felt pleasantly still and neither too warm nor too cool. Yuri wiped each bowl with a soft cloth until it shone. Pio folded the napkins into neat squares. No one rushed. Outside the porch the rosemary leaves made a tiny rustle that reached them faintly through the open door. Yuri glanced back once toward the hat on its peg and felt glad it had come inside unharmed after its short rest among the green stems.

They returned to the main part of the hall and sat for a moment on the long bench against the wall. The wood of the bench held a gentle leftover warmth from the day. Yuri leaned a little against Pio’s shoulder and listened to the quiet house. The cat’s breathing made a soft rhythm on the cushion. Pio hummed a slow tune under their breath, barely louder than a whisper. Yuri’s eyes followed the last pale light as it climbed higher on the wall and then began to fade. Everything felt settled and kind in the simplest way.

Yuri rose and walked once more to the doorway that looked out on the porch, checking that the rosemary still stood neat under the early evening sky. The boards outside had grown cooler now. Back inside Yuri smoothed the leather lace on the hat one last time and straightened a spoon that had slipped. Cousin Pio dimmed the brighter lamps until only a gentle glow remained. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Yuri climbed onto the bench again, tucked their feet under a light blanket that smelled faintly clean, and let their eyes close while the calm hall held them safe.
