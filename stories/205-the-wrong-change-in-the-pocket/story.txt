The baker stood already in the cottage hallway of hooks, rugs, and boots, carefully shaking loose crumbs from a long canvas bag and hanging it on a wooden peg so the soft cloth settled with a quiet sigh. Warm light from the open doorway painted golden stripes across the worn rugs, and Ximena came through with boots still damp from the path, the cool evening air brushing their cheeks like a gentle cloth. Ximena felt the small hard shape of the coin of wrong change still in a pocket pressing against their side with every step, and the baker looked up with a friendly nod while the smell of bread still clung faintly to the bag. Ximena almost curled their fingers around that pocket to hide the coin, but the sight of the baker’s calm face made them pause instead, and the hallway smelled clean and wooden under the hooks. Boots lined the wall in neat pairs, and Ximena’s own boots left tiny damp prints that faded slowly into the rug fibers.

Ximena stood very still for a moment beside a row of hanging coats that felt thick and woolly when their sleeve brushed them, and the temperature near the wall stayed pleasantly cool. The coin of wrong change still in a pocket seemed heavier now, and Ximena thought of how easy it might be to keep quiet and tuck it deeper away. Yet the baker kept working mid-task, folding a second smaller bag with careful fingers, and Ximena watched the simple motion of those hands. A soft scuff sounded as Ximena shifted their weight on the rug, and they decided the plain truth felt lighter than any secret. The hallway held the quiet creak of floorboards underfoot, and Ximena drew a slow breath that tasted of home.

“I need to tell you something important,” Ximena said, resting both palms on their knees for a steady second while the light from the doorway warmed the toes of their boots. The baker turned fully now, still holding the folded bag, and Ximena felt the coin of wrong change still in a pocket like a tiny cool circle against their leg. “When I paid at the bakery earlier, this coin came back with my change, but it is too much, and it does not belong to me,” Ximena added in a clear voice that carried gently down the hallway of hooks, rugs, and boots. The baker’s eyes stayed kind without any teasing, and Ximena’s shoulders lowered as the truth left their mouth. The air near the hanging coats held a faint woody scent mixed with the leftover warmth of the day.

The baker set the bag aside on a low bench and crouched a little so their faces met more evenly in the soft light that filtered through the open door. “Thank you for saying the plain truth, Ximena,” the baker replied, and the words sounded warm as fresh milk. Ximena reached into the pocket and drew out the coin of wrong change, feeling its smooth edges against their palm, then held it out so the metal caught a thin sparkle. Together they looked at the coin resting there, and the baker nodded slowly while a gate latch clicked shut somewhere outside in the yard. Ximena felt glad the words had been spoken, and the hallway rugs felt springy under their feet as they stood closer.

“We can mend this right here,” the baker said, taking a small notebook from a coat pocket that hung on one of the hooks, and the pages released a clean smell of fresh paper into the cool air. Ximena watched as the baker wrote a neat note about the coin and tucked the metal piece safely into a little cloth pouch that had also hung waiting. “You kept it safe until you could give it back, and that helps everything stay fair,” the baker continued, smiling without any teasing at all. Ximena nodded and felt the empty pocket now light against their side, while the texture of a damp clay wall met their fingertips when they steadied themselves near the corner. The light shifted gently as clouds moved outside, casting softer shapes across the boots lined up below the hooks.

Ximena and the baker sat for a quiet stretch on the low bench together, and the wood felt smooth and solid under them while the temperature of the hallway stayed evenly mild. “Shall we count the other coins in my bag so everything matches again?” the baker asked, opening the canvas bag once more with careful hands. Ximena helped by holding each coin for a moment and placing them in small stacks on the bench, listening to the tiny clinks that sounded clear and bright in the hallway of hooks, rugs, and boots. The baker marked the numbers in the notebook, and Ximena felt proud of the simple work they shared side by side. No one rushed, and the rugs muffled every small movement of their feet.

After the stacks looked even, the baker closed the bag again and hung it back on its hook so it swung once like a slow pendulum before settling. Ximena brushed a bit of flour dust from their own sleeve and noticed how the light now turned deeper gold across the floor. “You did well to speak up, Ximena,” the baker said once more, voice steady and kind. Ximena answered, “It feels better when the coin goes back where it belongs,” and they both smiled at the tidy hallway. Boots stood ready for tomorrow, coats waited on their hooks, and the whole space felt settled and calm around them.

They rose together and walked a few steps toward the inner door, where Ximena paused to straighten one crooked rug with the toe of their boot until the pattern lay straight again. The baker collected the notebook and pouch, tucking them safely away, and the air carried a last faint warmth from the day’s baking still clinging to the bag. Ximena felt the hallway of hooks, rugs, and boots holding them gently, every ordinary thing in its place after the mending. Outside sounds stayed distant and peaceful, and inside the light grew softer as evening settled deeper. Ximena’s hands rested easily at their sides now that the pocket held nothing extra.

The baker gave a final friendly wave before stepping toward the door, and Ximena waved back while the cool evening brushed their face one last time. Boots waited in their rows, hooks held their coats, and rugs lay flat and inviting under the quiet light. Ximena lingered a moment longer in the familiar space, feeling the day ease into rest around the simple truth they had shared. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
