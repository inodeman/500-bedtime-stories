The soft clink of a spoon against a mug drifted through the open cabin door, and Esme turned their head toward the quiet sound while the ferry swayed under their boots. Cold night air brushed Esme’s cheeks as they stepped onto the wooden deck, where a thin carpet of fresh snow still clung to the boards near the rail. Only two elbows of room remained between the metal railing and the benches, yet the first snow angel lay pressed into that narrow white patch, its wings clear and perfect under the deck lights. Esme smelled the warm tomato soup someone had carried up from the galley, the scent curling around the cold like a blanket. Neighbor Sami stood a little way off with a basket, watching without speaking, and Esme felt the smooth chill of the rail under one mittened hand while Teo hovered just outside the fun, close to the empty space beside the first snow angel.

Esme noticed how Teo’s shoulders stayed tucked inward, as if the small stretch of snow did not quite belong to them yet. The deck lights glowed pale yellow across the water, and Esme heard the gentle slap of waves against the hull mixed with the distant rattle of a cat’s collar bell from somewhere near the benches. “There is room right here if we move a little,” Esme said, voice calm and steady. Teo looked up, eyes bright in the cold, and Esme shifted one of the folding stools sideways so the open space beside the angel grew wider and kinder. Neighbor Sami only nodded once and let the children solve it, the wicker handle of the basket cool and ridged under Sami’s fingers as they waited. Esme felt the grain of the deck boards through their boots and watched their own breath float like soft clouds.

Esme knelt carefully and smoothed one wrinkle out of the cloth scarf that had fallen near the snow angel’s wing, the fabric cool and slightly damp. The motion made the space feel finished and ready rather than leftover. “You can sit right beside it,” Esme told Teo, making the words ordinary and quiet so no one would treat the spot like a prize. Teo stepped forward and settled onto the cleared board, knee almost touching the snow outline, and Esme heard the tiny creak of wood as the ferry leaned into a slow turn. Light from the cabin windows spilled in long rectangles across the snow, turning the angel’s edges silver. Esme sat opposite Teo with only two elbows of room between them and the rail, feeling the safe solid press of the metal against their back.

Neighbor Sami set the basket down without a word, and Esme reached for the thermos inside so the tomato soup smell rose again, rich and comforting in the night air. Esme poured a little into two small cups and passed one to Teo, the steam warming their faces. “It tastes better when we share the spot,” Esme said, and Teo answered, “I like being right here next to the wings.” The simple exchange settled everything. Esme noticed how the snow around the angel stayed soft and powdery under the deck light, and they pressed one careful fingertip near the edge without spoiling the shape. Cold seeped gently through Esme’s mittens, a clean winter feeling that made the soup’s heat even nicer.

Esme leaned closer so their shoulder nearly touched Teo’s, changing the pace of the evening from hurried play to slow looking. They both studied the snow angel’s outstretched arms and the little hollow where a head had rested. The ferry’s engine hummed low and steady beneath them, a sound that mixed with the soft clink of the cups. “We can make another one later if we want,” Esme offered, “but this first one already has the best space.” Teo smiled and rested a hand flat on the board beside the angel, claiming nothing loudly, simply belonging. Neighbor Sami smiled too from the rail, still silent, trusting the children. Esme felt the cool night air slide under their collar and watched the lights of the far shore twinkle like quiet stars.

Esme shifted again, sliding their own stool so Teo had even more of the two elbows of room and a clear view of the dark water beyond the rail. The motion was small and matter-of-fact, nothing announced. Snowflakes that had lingered on the railing melted under Esme’s glove, leaving tiny cold drops that they wiped away. The deck smelled faintly of wet wood now beneath the soup scent, and Esme listened to the hush of the boat cutting through the calm water. “Thank you for saving the place,” Teo said softly. Esme answered, “It was always meant for both of us.” Their words floated out and vanished into the night without needing anyone else to hear.

Esme and Teo sat shoulder to shoulder for a long while, watching the snow angel keep its shape under the yellow lights while the ferry carried them steadily forward. Esme felt the gentle rock of the deck and the solid warmth of Teo’s coat against their own. A thin ribbon of steam still rose from the cups, carrying the last of the tomato soup smell into the cold. Neighbor Sami finally moved only to tuck the wicker handle of the basket more securely against a bench leg, the texture familiar and firm. Esme noticed how the space beside the first snow angel no longer felt like an edge; it felt like the middle of something easy and shared. The cat’s collar bell sounded once more from the cabin doorway, faint and friendly, then faded.

Esme rested their chin on their knees and breathed the clean cold air, content that Teo now sat fully inside the circle of the evening rather than just outside it. The rail stayed cool under Esme’s elbow, and the two elbows of room felt suddenly generous. Light from the cabin painted soft gold across both children and the snow. Esme smoothed the scarf one last time so it lay flat beside the angel’s wing, a quiet finishing touch. No one needed to explain anything. Neighbor Sami watched the shoreline draw nearer and left the solving entirely to Esme and Teo, who had already finished it with small moves and ordinary words.

When the ferry slowed and the engines quieted to a murmur, Esme stood and offered Teo a hand so they could walk together toward the cabin door, leaving the first snow angel peaceful in its place. The night air stayed cold yet kind on Esme’s face, and the wooden deck felt solid under every step. Inside, the warmth wrapped around them at once. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Esme settled onto the bench with Teo close beside them, the earlier space on the deck still bright in their minds like a kept promise, while the gentle sway of the boat and the fading scent of soup carried the quiet night forward.
