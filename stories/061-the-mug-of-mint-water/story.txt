Grandpa Sol stood at the far end of the long table in the courtyard and carefully unfolded a clean white cloth over the wood, smoothing every wrinkle flat with both steady hands. The last rays of sunlight warmed the top of Ilaria’s head as they watched him work from a few steps away on the cool stones. A light breeze carried the fresh smell of rain on dust from the garden path where a brief shower had passed earlier in the afternoon. Ilaria listened to the quiet scrape of the cloth against the table edge and stepped closer on bare feet. “The table is almost ready for our supper, Ilaria,” Grandpa Sol said gently without looking up from his slow task. Ilaria nodded and reached out to help tuck one corner neatly into place so it would not slip later when the plates arrived.

Ilaria walked the full length of the table and counted the places set with simple plates and cups beneath the spreading branches of the old fig tree. The leaves overhead cast shifting patterns of soft green light and shadow that danced across the cloth whenever a breeze moved. Grandpa Sol set a basket of warm bread in the center, and the air near it felt pleasantly mild against Ilaria’s wrists as they adjusted a spoon that had gone crooked. Nearby on the low stone step leading toward the house rested a clear mug filled with pale mint water, its surface still and bright. Ilaria noticed how the mint leaves floated inside like tiny boats and how the glass caught the fading daylight. They stood quietly for a moment just looking at that single mug while Grandpa Sol finished lining up the napkins.

Opal arrived then through the wooden gate at the side of the courtyard, and the sound of a gate latch clicked shut with a soft metal note that carried across the quiet space. Ilaria turned toward their friend and waved, feeling the cool smooth texture of a satin ribbon that hung from Opal’s braid when they shared a quick hug of greeting. The ribbon slid lightly under Ilaria’s palm before they let go. “I am so glad you came for supper under the fig tree, Opal,” Ilaria said in a calm low voice. Opal smiled and looked around at the long table already half ready. Grandpa Sol simply gestured toward the empty chairs with a warm nod and kept setting out bowls of sliced cucumbers without any hurry at all.

Ilaria glanced once more at the mug of mint water resting alone on the step and thought how cool and refreshing it would taste after the warm day. The stones beneath the mug still held a gentle leftover heat from the afternoon, and Ilaria could feel that mild warmth when they bent near it. Soft pink light from the sky made the water gleam like a small quiet pool. Ilaria realized that if only one person drank from the mug first it would not feel fair to everyone waiting together at the table. They stood very still and listened to a single bird call once from high in the fig tree branches. Grandpa Sol finished with the bowls and wiped his hands on his apron while the courtyard grew quieter.

Ilaria picked up the mug carefully so none of the mint water would spill and carried it over to the table where two smaller empty cups waited near the bread basket. The glass of the mug felt smooth and slightly chilled against their cupped hands. “I will pour some for you and some for me so we both have the same, Opal,” Ilaria said as they tilted the mug slowly and steadily. The mint water made a gentle trickling sound as it filled first one cup and then the other until both held even amounts with matching leaves. Opal watched with bright eyes and held out one hand to steady the second cup on the cloth so nothing tipped. Ilaria set the empty larger mug aside and smiled at the two matching shares now ready.

Grandpa Sol saw what Ilaria had done with the mint water and simply nodded once, letting the choice stand without a speech as he brought over a platter of soft cheese. The air around the long table felt comfortably mild now that the sun had nearly dipped away. Ilaria passed one cup carefully to Opal and kept the other for themself, then sat down in a chair that creaked softly under their weight. “This smells so fresh and cool,” Opal said after taking a small first sip. Ilaria tasted theirs too and felt the cold liquid slide gently down while the fig leaves rustled overhead in a slow easy rhythm. Everyone began to pass the bread and cucumbers around the table in a quiet circle of hands.

Ilaria tore a piece of bread and felt its soft crust give way under their fingers while Grandpa Sol poured water into the taller glasses at the grown places. The courtyard held a peaceful hush broken only by the quiet clink of utensils against plates. The pink light faded into deeper blue, and Ilaria watched how it changed the color of the thick fig tree trunk nearby into soft shadow. “Would you like another cucumber slice, Ilaria?” Grandpa Sol asked as he held the bowl toward them with both hands. Ilaria took one and nodded thanks, then turned to Opal who was already munching happily beside them. The mint water in their shared cups stayed cool longer than expected, and Ilaria enjoyed every slow sip between bites of the simple supper.

After the plates were mostly empty Ilaria leaned back and noticed a faint fog that had formed on the side of their empty mint-water cup from the coolness inside meeting the milder air. With one dry finger they carefully traced a small neat circle in that fog and watched the clear line reappear slowly as if drawn on glass. The stones of the courtyard had grown cooler underfoot as night settled closer around the fig tree. Opal laughed a little at the circle shape and tried the same gentle motion on their own cup with a fingertip. Grandpa Sol gathered the empty bowls without any fuss and left the children to their quiet play at the long table. The scent of leftover bread mixed with the lingering mint in a pleasant way that made Ilaria feel settled and full and ready for rest.

Ilaria and Opal helped carry the last cups inside while the fig tree stood dark and still behind them in the courtyard. Inside the house the rooms felt warmer than the open air had become. Ilaria paused by the window and looked out one more time at the empty long table and the quiet step. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Grandpa Sol tucked a light blanket around Ilaria’s shoulders as they settled into a chair near Opal, and the fabric brushed gently against their neck like a quiet goodnight. Outside the night air whispered through the leaves, and Ilaria closed their eyes feeling safe and calm after the shared supper.
