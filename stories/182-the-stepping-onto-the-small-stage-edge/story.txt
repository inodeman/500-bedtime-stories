In the warm bakery a tiny spoon clinked once against a glass jar, and Remy heard it right away while standing near the counter that faced the street. Soft afternoon light slanted across the wooden boards and touched Remy’s sleeves with a gentle gold. Remy smelled rising dough and watched the ferryman set down a small basket of fresh rolls. The air felt pleasantly cool near the open window, and Remy rested one hand on the smooth counter edge. A very small stage sat just beside the counter, no taller than a step stool, built of pale pine so children could stand for a short song or a quiet hello. Remy looked at the edge of that very small stage and felt a flutter of shyness settle in their chest like a moth.

Remy stayed close to the counter and noticed how the street sounds drifted in softly, mixed with the quiet hum of the bakery oven. The ferryman smiled without hurry and wiped a crumb from the counter with a clean cloth that smelled faintly of soap. Gio stood nearby holding a napkin and whispered, “The little stage looks friendly today.” Remy nodded but kept their eyes on the pale wood edge. Warm air rose from a tray of cookies just pulled from the oven, and Remy felt the heat brush their cheeks. The glass bead hanging from a thin blue string on the counter felt cool and round when Remy rolled it gently between two fingers.

Remy listened again as a cat’s collar bell rang once from the sunny windowsill where the bakery cat stretched and blinked. The sound was small and clear, like a single bright note. Remy took a slow breath and smelled mint leaves resting in a little bowl beside the tea pots, fresh and green. The ferryman poured warm milk into a cup and said, “No rush at all, Remy. The stage is only here if you want it.” Remy felt the soft cotton of their shirt against their arms and watched the light shift on the floorboards. Gio leaned closer and added, “We can just stand here and smell the good things.” Remy almost named the smells but waited a moment longer, still shy about the edge of the very small stage.

Remy decided not to pretend the shyness was gone. Instead Remy chose the smallest brave version they could manage. Remy stepped forward until the tip of one shoe rested on the edge of the very small stage. The wood felt smooth and steady under the sole. Soft light from the street lamp outside already began to glow against the glass. Remy kept one hand on the counter for balance and spoke in a voice that could be heard across the quiet space. “I am standing on the edge now,” Remy said, the words clear and calm. The ferryman nodded once and stayed kind, offering no big show and no push.

Gio clapped once very softly and said, “That sounds just right, Remy.” Remy felt the cool air from the open door brush their ankles and smelled the mint leaves again as the bowl sat near. The bakery counter faced the street where a bicycle wheeled past with a quiet whir. Remy kept both feet now on the edge of the very small stage and spoke again. “The wood feels steady under me.” The ferryman answered gently, “I hear you well, Remy, and that is enough.” Warmth rose from the floor near the oven and settled around Remy’s legs like a blanket. Remy rolled the glass bead once more between their fingers and felt its smooth round surface before setting it back on the counter.

Remy stayed on the edge a little longer and looked out through the wide window at the gentle street. A soft breeze carried the scent of mint leaves mixed with sweet vanilla. Remy named three smells without saying any worry: “I smell mint leaves, and warm bread, and a little honey.” Gio smiled and the ferryman poured a second cup of milk that steamed gently. Remy felt the temperature of the air change as evening cooled the bakery a touch. The cat’s collar bell had already rung earlier, so now only the soft pad of paws remained. Remy spoke one more time in that same steady voice. “I like standing here for a minute.” The ferryman replied, “Take all the minutes you need.”

Remy stepped down from the edge of the very small stage after a short while and returned to the counter facing the street. The wood of the stage felt solid and kind under the last touch of Remy’s shoe. Light from the street lamps mixed with the bakery lamps and made soft circles on the floor. Remy smelled the mint leaves once more while the ferryman offered a small mint cookie on a plate. Gio took a cookie too and the three of them stood together in the quiet. Remy felt the smooth counter under their palms and listened to the distant hush of wheels on the street. The air cooled further as dusk settled, yet the bakery stayed cozy.

Remy leaned elbows on the counter and watched the ferryman tidy the basket with slow careful hands. The texture of the glass bead still lingered in Remy’s memory as cool and perfect. Soft golden light filled the room and touched every loaf and jar. Remy breathed in the remaining scent of mint leaves and felt calm settle deeper. Gio rested a hand on Remy’s shoulder for a second, light as a feather. The ferryman said nothing more about the stage and simply passed Remy a napkin warm from the pocket near the oven. Remy smiled and stayed close, listening to the quiet bakery settle into evening.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Remy sat on the low stool beside the counter facing the street and felt the last warmth of the day on their knees. The edge of the very small stage stood empty and peaceful nearby. Outside the street grew quiet and the bakery smelled of bread and mint leaves fading gently. Remy heard only the softest hush of settling pans. Gio and the ferryman stayed close without any hurry, and Remy’s breathing slowed to match the calm room. Cool night air slipped through the window and brushed Remy’s cheek like a good-night touch while the lantern light held everything steady and kind.
