The afternoon mist drifted against the school walls and cooled the threshold of the cloakroom with a gentle touch that never grew sharp. Dori stepped inside and felt that mild coolness rest on their cheeks and the backs of their hands while pale stripes of late light stretched across the wooden floorboards. The coats on their pegs hung still in the quiet air, and Dori carefully hung their own jacket so the sleeves fell straight. Beyond the open doorway the lunch mats lay in neat squares, their edges soft under the steady indoor glow. Dori walked toward them with slow steps, listening to the faint hush of their own shoes, already wondering what Uncle Idris had set out for the afternoon rest.

Warm steam carried the smell of tomato soup from a covered pot that Uncle Idris had placed near the mats, and the savory scent filled the cloakroom doorway in a comforting wave. Dori breathed it in and felt their shoulders loosen as they reached the edge of the nearest mat. Uncle Idris knelt there arranging a low basket, and Oren sat cross-legged nearby with a friendly wave. The light from the hallway lamps made the steam look golden as it curled upward. Dori settled onto the mat and ran one palm across its woven surface, enjoying the slight give beneath their fingers. “The soup smells wonderful today,” Dori said softly, and Uncle Idris smiled without hurrying.

Dori noticed the basket of buns resting in the middle of the mats and reached for its side, feeling the firm weave of the wicker handle under their fingers. The handle was smooth from long use yet still textured enough to grip easily, and Dori lifted it just enough to peek inside at the soft golden buns nestled together. Two cloth napkins lay folded beside the basket, their corners neat. Oren leaned closer with curious eyes while Uncle Idris poured a little soup into three small cups. The air near the mats stayed pleasantly warm from the soup pot. Dori counted the napkins again and felt a quiet pause form in their chest.

From somewhere down the hallway a cat’s collar bell gave one clear, gentle ring that floated into the cloakroom and faded. Dori glanced toward the sound for a moment, then returned their attention to the basket and the napkins. “There are three of us and only two napkins,” Dori said, keeping their voice calm. Oren nodded and waited. Uncle Idris stayed quiet, simply watching with kind eyes while the soup cups cooled a little on the tray. The light along the mats remained soft and even. Dori thought about how the buns would not feel fair if only one person received everything first.

Dori carefully took both napkins and the basket of buns, then began to tear each bun into smaller, even pieces that would go around. “We can make smaller shares so everyone has some,” Dori said, arranging the pieces in three little groups. Oren held out both hands to receive a share, and Dori placed the bread gently onto them. Uncle Idris accepted his portion with a quiet nod. The mats felt springy under Dori’s knees as they worked. Steam still rose in thin ribbons, carrying warmth across the small circle they made together.

Dori smoothed one wrinkle out of the cloth of the second napkin before folding it into a tidy square that could rest between the three of them. The fabric felt cool and dry under their fingertips. “This way the napkin can help all of us,” Dori explained while placing the last pieces of bun onto the open cloth. Oren grinned and bit into a soft bit of bread. The hallway light glowed steadily across the mats without glare. Uncle Idris took a slow sip of soup and let the moment stay peaceful, offering no long talk at all.

They sat together on the lunch mats and ate the even shares of buns while the tomato soup warmed their hands through the cups. Dori listened to the quiet sounds of chewing and the soft clink of spoons. Oren said, “These pieces taste just right,” and Dori answered, “I am glad we all have some.” Uncle Idris simply smiled and kept the choice as it was. The air near the open cloakroom door stayed mild and fresh. Dori felt the mat’s texture again under their legs and leaned back a little, content with the circle they had made.

After the last crumbs disappeared, Dori helped gather the empty cups and folded the shared napkin once more. The wicker basket sat empty yet ready for another day. Oren stood and stretched, then walked toward the coats while the light in the room grew softer and more golden. Uncle Idris rose without a word of instruction, simply collecting the pot. Dori stayed close to the mats a moment longer, feeling the pleasant stillness that had settled over everything. The gentle coolness from the doorway brushed their ankles like a calm reminder of the afternoon outside.

Dori walked back into the cloakroom proper and touched their hanging coat one last time before the day ended. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Its warm circle of light rested on the floorboards and mixed with the leftover scent of bread while Dori and Oren put on their jackets side by side. Uncle Idris waited near the door with the empty basket in hand, still saying nothing more. Outside the mist had thinned to a quiet shimmer, and the temperature on Dori’s face felt perfectly gentle as they stepped forward together into the rest of the soft afternoon.
