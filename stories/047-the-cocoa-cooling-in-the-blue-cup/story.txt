The ferryman stood already at the cottage windowsill of jars, trays, and drying wool, his hands moving slowly as he turned each soft length of wool so the evening air could finish its quiet work. Pale gold light spilled across the trays and made the glass jars of buttons and thread gleam softly against the wood. Cosima stepped nearer and felt that same mild warmth settle on their bare arms while they watched him straighten one last tray. The room held a gentle stillness broken only by the soft rustle of wool under his fingers. Cosima’s eyes soon found the blue cup resting on the nearest tray, its lid still in place, and they wondered how much longer the cocoa inside might need.

Cosima reached toward the blue cup but stopped short when the ferryman glanced over with a kind smile. “It is still too warm to sip,” he said in a low voice that matched the calm of the room. Cosima drew their hand back and rested it on the cool wooden edge of the sill instead. The air near the window carried a faint coolness that brushed Cosima’s wrists and made them feel settled. They could hear the distant hush of the river beyond the garden, steady and unhurried. Cosima looked at the covered cup again and decided the cocoa could wait a little longer under its lid.

“I wanted to check just once,” Cosima said, their words soft as they stood beside the trays. The ferryman nodded and kept folding a strip of wool with patient care. Cosima breathed in and caught the smell of warm bread drifting from the kitchen hearth, a comforting scent that filled the cottage and made the waiting feel easier. They stayed close to the windowsill, fingers lightly tracing the rim of an empty jar while the light shifted slowly across the drying wool. The blue cup sat unmoving, and Cosima felt the quiet choice settle in their chest like a small, steady lantern of its own.

Cosima turned from the cup and noticed an empty bowl left on the low table near the sill. They lifted it carefully, feeling its smooth weight in both hands, and carried it across the cool floorboards to the sink. Water whispered against the basin as they set the bowl down, and the soft sound followed them back to the windowsill where the ferryman still worked. Cosima returned and stood once more beside the trays of drying wool, the jars lined up like quiet friends. The cocoa remained covered, and Cosima chose not to lift the lid yet.

“Shall we wait together a little while longer?” the ferryman asked, his hands resting now on the edge of a tray. Cosima answered, “Yes, we can wait until it finishes in its own time.” They both looked out toward the garden where the fig tree stood close to the cottage wall. A light wind moved through the fig leaves and made a gentle whispering sound that drifted in through the open window. Cosima listened and felt the soft air touch their hair while they stayed near the blue cup. The wool on the trays looked almost ready, and the jars caught the last of the daylight in quiet sparkles.

Cosima’s friend Zola appeared in the doorway with quiet steps and a folded cloth in their hands. Zola smiled and placed the cloth on an empty spot among the trays without speaking at first. Cosima felt glad to have them there and shifted a little so Zola could see the covered blue cup too. The temperature in the room had grown pleasantly mild, neither warm nor cold, and Cosima’s bare feet rested easily on the smooth boards. “The cocoa is still resting,” Cosima told Zola in a hushed voice. Zola nodded and stayed beside them, watching the wool and the jars with the same calm attention.

Together Cosima and Zola helped the ferryman straighten one final tray of drying wool, their fingers brushing the soft strands that felt nearly dry to the touch. The light outside had softened into a deeper gold that painted the windowsill and made every jar glow faintly from within. Cosima kept glancing at the blue cup but left the lid undisturbed, choosing instead to smooth a corner of wool that had curled upward. The ferryman worked on Cosima’s other side, his movements slow and sure. No one rushed, and the cottage held its peaceful air like a held breath that never needed to hurry.

After a longer stretch of quiet work Cosima finally reached for the blue cup and felt the cool ceramic rim under their fingertips. The cup no longer gave off heat, and when Cosima lifted the lid the cocoa inside looked ready at last. They took a careful sip and tasted the gentle chocolate that had waited just long enough. Zola and the ferryman watched with soft smiles while Cosima held the cup close. The drying wool lay finished on its trays, the jars sat neatly in their rows, and the whole windowsill seemed to rest as Cosima took another slow sip.

Cosima set the blue cup down again and leaned a little against the sill, feeling the last of the evening air drift in. The ferryman began to gather the finished wool into a basket, and Zola helped by stacking the empty trays with care. Cosima stayed near, content that the cocoa had cooled in its own time and that they had chosen the quiet path of waiting. Outside the garden settled into dusk, and inside the cottage the soft light remained. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
