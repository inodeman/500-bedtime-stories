"Shall we sing the garden song while we tidy the hallway?" the baker said with a smile that crinkled their eyes. Violeta stood in the cottage hallway of hooks, rugs, and boots and felt the cool draft from the half-open door brush against their bare arms. Soft golden light from a lamp on the side table lit the row of hanging coats and made every brass hook gleam. Violeta took a woolen scarf from the baker's hands and looped it carefully over a low hook, listening to the quiet swish of fabric. The air held a gentle warmth drifting from the kitchen. They began the first lines together, voices blending while Violeta reached for another coat. Boots lined the wall in neat pairs and the rugs softened every step. Violeta loved how the hallway felt like a calm pause between day and night.

As the second verse began Violeta suddenly could not recall the next words about ripening fruit on the branch. Instead of stopping they covered the missed line with a low hum that rose and fell like a soft wave. The baker kept singing without pause and the humming filled the small gap as coats settled on hooks with gentle thuds. Violeta's cheeks grew warm under the hallway light. They focused on the firm leather of a boot they straightened with both hands. The hum mixed with the baker's clear tones until the verse ended. Violeta felt a small tangle inside their chest about the missed line they had covered with a hum. Still they finished hanging the last jacket and kept their gaze on the patterned rugs.

Violeta almost decided to hide the moment entirely and pretend the song had gone perfectly smooth. They shuffled their feet on the thick rug and listened to the distant clock ticking from the next room. The temperature in the hallway stayed pleasantly mild. The baker hummed a short extra tune while arranging boots by size from largest to smallest. Violeta watched tiny dust motes drift and dance in the lamp light near the window frame. For a moment they thought of saying nothing at all about the covered line. Their fingers traced the smooth curved edge of a wooden hook. Outside the garden settled into evening quiet. Violeta took one slow breath and looked toward the baker who waited with patient kindness.

The baker paused and the sweet smell of beeswax rose from a small tin they opened to polish the lower hooks. Violeta stepped nearer and breathed in the warm honeyed scent that filled the air between them. Light from the lamp caught the fresh polish and made the brass shine brighter than before. Violeta still felt tangled with the choice about the missed line covered by the hum yet the kindly silence helped. They could hear their own calm breathing. The baker rubbed the wax in slow circles without asking any questions. Violeta felt the cool metal of a hook under their fingertips as they helped wipe. The hallway rugs cushioned their stance. This quiet shared work gave them space to choose.

"I missed a line in the song and covered it with a hum," Violeta said at last, their voice steady but soft. The baker looked up and the light from the lamp warmed both their faces. "Thank you for telling me the plain truth," the baker answered gently. Violeta explained how the words about the fruit had slipped away and the hum had stepped in to fill the space. The baker set down the beeswax tin and listened with full attention. Cool air from the open door touched Violeta's neck as they spoke. Together they stood among the hooks and boots. Violeta felt the weight lift a little from their shoulders. The baker smiled once more without any teasing.

"We can mend the song right here," the baker replied warmly. "Let us say the forgotten line slowly and then try the whole verse once more." Violeta repeated the words about golden figs hanging heavy on the branch until they felt sure again. The sound of their shared voices bounced lightly off the hallway walls. Violeta noticed a leaf that had blown in through the open door and touched a leaf's soft underside against their palm. It felt cool and smooth like the finest cloth. They practiced the full verse once more without any hum covering a gap. Boots stayed quiet in their neat row. The rugs held firm under their shifting feet. Light pooled steadily around them as the mended song grew clearer and stronger.

From the garden the sound of wind in the fig leaves drifted through the open doorway in a soft rustle that rose and fell like quiet breathing. Violeta and the baker sang the whole garden song from the first note to the last, every line now in its proper place. The cool evening air carried the leafy whisper around the coats on their hooks. Violeta swayed a little with the music and felt glad of the plain truth they had shared. No one had laughed or frowned. The baker polished one last hook until it gleamed under the lamp. Temperature stayed even and comfortable in the hallway. Violeta hung a final pair of mittens and listened to the gentle outdoor sound mix with their ending notes.

Violeta noticed a small snail that had ridden in on a boot sole and now crossed one whole brick of the hallway floor at its own slow steady pace. They crouched down beside the baker to watch it move while the lamp light made its shell shine faintly silver. The snail left a thin glistening trail across the cool brick. Violeta breathed evenly and felt calm settle all through their body. The baker spoke in a whisper so as not to startle the tiny traveler. Violeta agreed with a quiet smile that even the smallest journeys mattered. Nearby rugs stayed still. The coats hung without stirring. This small shared watching finished the evening's mending in the gentlest way.

Later Violeta sat on a low stool near the boots while the baker closed the polish tin with a soft click. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Warmth from the kitchen drifted farther into the hallway of hooks, rugs, and boots. Violeta yawned and rubbed their eyes, hearing only the fading wind outside. They felt the smooth wood of the stool under their resting hands. Everything that needed mending had been mended with honest words and one shared song. The coats hung ready for morning. Boots waited patiently in their places. Violeta leaned against the wall and closed their eyes in the gentle light, ready for sleep.
