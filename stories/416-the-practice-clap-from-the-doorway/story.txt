The kind morning light touched the cottage kitchen gently, drifting in pale bands across the scrubbed boards and warming the air just enough to feel friendly on bare ankles. Bibi stepped inside and felt that mild glow settle on their cheeks while a soft draft stirred the hanging spoons. The room held a quiet readiness, pots nested and chairs tucked, and Bibi rested both palms on the table edge to sense its cool solid grain. Cousin Pio was already there by the stove, moving with unhurried care, and Bibi watched the gentle rise of steam that made the air smell clean. Outside the window the garden looked soft and still. Bibi rolled their shoulders once and decided this ordinary morning felt wide enough for trying something new with both hands.

Bibi reached for the blue mixing bowl and the wooden spoon, hearing the faint scrape as they pulled them closer across the counter. The batter for simple griddle cakes waited in a jug, and Bibi meant to stir it smooth without spilling. They stood on the little stool so their elbows cleared the rim and began turning the spoon in slow circles. The mixture felt heavy at first, pulling against the wood, yet Bibi kept going until the lumps began to fade. Light from the window caught the swirling surface and made tiny bright flecks dance. Cousin Pio glanced over but said nothing yet, only shifted a pan with a soft clink. Bibi felt the mild heat from the stove against one side of their face and kept the rhythm steady.

“I am going to try the last few turns on my own,” Bibi said, voice calm and clear in the quiet room. They gripped the spoon handle tighter and watched the batter grow silkier under the pale morning shine. A paper recipe card lay nearby, and when Bibi brushed it aside their fingertip caught the crisp paper edge that felt thin and straight. The motion stayed careful. Steam drifted up again and warmed the air near Bibi’s wrists. Cousin Pio stepped back toward the open doorway that led to the little hall, giving space without leaving entirely. Bibi finished the stirring and set the spoon down with a soft tap against the bowl rim, shoulders easing.

From the doorway came a single bright clap, just one, sharp and kind, that bounced once off the walls and faded. Bibi looked up and saw Cousin Pio still standing there, hands lowered again, a small smile ready. The clap had come right after the try, neat and certain. Bibi noticed at once that Cousin Pio had already taken care of that single encouraging sound from the doorway. The kitchen felt warmer for a moment under the steady light. Bibi’s chest rose and fell while they looked from the bowl to the doorway and back. No one else had moved. The ordinary morning held still around them both.

A quick thank-you rose in Bibi’s mind yet felt too thin for the way the clap had landed so exactly after the effort. Bibi wanted to answer with something useful that Cousin Pio had never asked for. They spotted the damp wool scarf hanging near the stove to dry and caught the clean smell of wet wool rising from its folds. Bibi lifted it carefully, shook it once so no drips fell, and draped it over the proper peg by the window where the air moved freely. The fabric felt heavier than it looked. Bibi smoothed it flat with both palms. Cousin Pio watched without speaking, eyes soft. The kitchen stayed calm and bright.

“The tin clock just gave its little mark of the hour,” Cousin Pio said, and indeed the tin clock on the shelf let out one clear tick that cut gently through the quiet. Bibi nodded and turned next to the stack of clean plates waiting on the sideboard. Without being asked they carried each plate to the table and set them in a neat row, matching the places to the chairs. The china felt smooth and cool under their fingers. Light slid along the rims as Bibi worked. They added the forks and the small napkins too, making everything ready for whenever the cakes would cook. Cousin Pio stayed near the doorway a moment longer, then came back inside fully.

“You did not have to set all that,” Cousin Pio said, voice low and even. Bibi kept placing the last cup and felt the mild warmth of the room wrap around their arms. They breathed slowly while the kettle on the stove began its quiet tick as the water inside woke. The sound was soft and regular, almost like a small heart. Bibi finished the cup and stepped back to look at the tidy table. The batter sat ready. The scarf hung straight. Everything Cousin Pio might have reached for later already waited in its place. Bibi’s hands rested at their sides, calm.

“I noticed you clapped once from the doorway after I tried,” Bibi said plainly, meeting Cousin Pio’s eyes without rush. Cousin Pio’s smile widened just a little. “That single clap told me the stirring looked steady,” Cousin Pio answered. Bibi felt the air near the window cooler where the draft entered, yet the rest of the kitchen held its gentle heat. They both stood still for a breath. No hurry pressed them. Bibi touched the table once more and felt its familiar grain. The morning light had shifted higher yet stayed kind across the floor.

The lantern by the window glowed softly. Bibi and Cousin Pio moved together toward the stove where the pan waited, and the quiet tick of the kettle kept time while the batter poured in smooth circles. The room smelled of warm flour now mixed with the earlier clean air, and Bibi felt the safe temperature settle over their shoulders like a light shawl. They listened to the soft sizzle begin and watched the pale gold light hold steady on the walls. Cousin Pio handed Bibi the turner without a word, and Bibi took it, ready for the next small careful step of the ordinary kind morning that still stretched ahead in peace.
