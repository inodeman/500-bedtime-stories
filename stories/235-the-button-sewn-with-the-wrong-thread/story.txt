The baker knelt already in the cottage hallway of hooks, rugs, and boots, rubbing a soft cloth in slow circles over a scuffed boot while the late afternoon light slanted gold across the wooden floorboards. Una stepped inside and felt the cool air from the open door brush their cheeks. The baker looked up with a gentle smile and kept polishing without pause. Una’s coat sleeve snagged on a low hook, and the mismatched button pulled free with a soft pop, dangling by a bright red thread that stood out against the quiet blue fabric. Una’s fingers closed around the button and the loud thread as if they might tuck both into a pocket and pretend nothing had happened. The hallway smelled faintly of warm beeswax from the cloth the baker still held.

Una stood very still on the braided rug, feeling the worn wool under their socks, and watched the baker set the boot aside. “I pulled the button loose just now,” Una said in a small clear voice. The baker nodded and waited without hurry. Una almost slid the bright thread and button behind a row of boots, yet the truth rose instead. “The thread does not match, and I nearly hid it,” Una added. A soft hush filled the space between the coats and the boots while outside the wind moved through the fig leaves with a dry whispering rustle that drifted in through the open door.

The baker rose and took Una’s free hand, leading them to the low bench beside the boot rack where the light fell gently. “We can mend what can be mended,” the baker said. Una sat close and felt the smooth wood of the bench under their palms. Together they spread the coat across their knees so the loose button and its loud red thread lay plain in the open. No one laughed or made a joke. The baker fetched a small sewing tin from a hook, and Una held the tin steady while the lid clicked open with a quiet metal sound. Inside lay spools of thread in every quiet color.

Una chose a soft blue spool that matched the coat exactly and handed it to the baker. “This one feels right,” Una said. The baker threaded a needle with careful fingers while Una smoothed the coat fabric flat. A single fig leaf had blown across the threshold and lay curled near Una’s shoe. Una reached down and turned the leaf over, feeling a leaf’s soft underside against their fingertip before setting it aside on the rug. The baker began to stitch with slow even pulls, and Una watched each loop of blue thread settle neatly into place. The old red thread came free in one gentle tug and rested harmlessly on the bench.

The baker paused to let Una try a stitch. Una’s hands moved carefully, guiding the needle through the thick coat cloth while the baker steadied the button. “You are doing well,” the baker said. Una felt the slight resistance of the fabric and the smooth press of the button against their thumb. Outside the light softened toward evening, and the hallway grew cooler around the boots and hanging coats. Una finished the last stitch and snipped the thread with tiny scissors from the tin. The button sat snug and quiet now, no longer loud or loose.

Una stood and slipped the mended coat back on, feeling the even weight of the button against their chest. The baker returned the sewing tin to its hook and hung the polished boots beside the others. Una noticed a small snail making its slow way across one whole brick set into the floor near the door. Together they watched in silence as the snail crossed from one edge of the brick to the other, leaving a faint silver trail that caught the fading light. Una’s breathing slowed to match the snail’s unhurried pace. The rugs underfoot felt thick and safe.

“I am glad I told you,” Una said quietly. The baker smiled and rested a warm hand on Una’s shoulder for a moment. “I am glad too,” the baker answered. They walked the length of the hallway once more, past the row of hooks heavy with coats and scarves, past the boots standing ready for morning. Una ran their fingers along a smooth wooden peg and felt how solid and ordinary everything remained. The air inside held a gentle stillness while the last of the daylight painted soft shapes on the walls.

Una helped the baker sweep a few stray threads into a small dustpan, the bristles whispering over the floorboards. Then they shook out a rug together and laid it flat again so no corner curled up. Una’s coat hung neatly now among the others, the blue button resting quiet and matched. The baker closed the sewing tin one last time and set it high on a shelf. Una felt tired in a pleasant way, as if the mending had settled something deep inside their chest. The hallway smelled clean and calm under the quiet evening light.

Una and the baker stood side by side near the window while the last pale glow faded outside. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Una leaned against the baker’s side and felt the steady warmth of the room settle around their shoulders like a favorite blanket. Boots lined the wall in peaceful rows, rugs lay smooth underfoot, and hooks held every coat in its proper place. Una’s breathing grew slow and even as the quiet filled every corner of the cottage hallway.
