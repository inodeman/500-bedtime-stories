Grandpa Sol was already kneeling on the polished floor of the school hall, mid-task with a coil of soft rope in his weathered hands, looping it carefully around the base of a wooden stand that would hold paper flowers for the gentle fair. Cool afternoon light filtered through the high windows and rested on Quinn’s shoulders like a quiet blanket as they stepped inside and set down their small satchel. The air held a gentle chill that made Quinn rub their arms once before they smiled and walked closer. Grandpa Sol looked up with kind eyes and said, “I am making sure these stands stay steady for tomorrow, Quinn.” Quinn nodded and answered softly, “I came to practice the speaking scene with Farah, Grandpa Sol.” The faint scuff of their shoes on the smooth boards sounded calm and familiar while they watched the rope slide through his fingers.

Farah arrived a moment later carrying a folded cloth banner, and together Quinn and Farah unrolled it near the low platform that would become their small stage. Warmth from the sunlight patches on the floor touched Quinn’s bare ankles as they helped Farah smooth the cloth. The scene they needed to practice was simple: one person would speak a few gentle lines about the fair’s paper birds, and the other would listen carefully for the cue word before answering with a quiet wish. Quinn felt the smooth edge of the banner under their fingertips and remembered how rushing had made the words tumble together last time. Farah whispered, “We should try it slowly today,” and Quinn replied, “Yes, let’s take our time so the cue can land right.” A soft breeze moved somewhere outside, but inside the hall stayed peaceful and still.

Quinn and Farah decided the scene would go wrong if either of them hurried the words or the listening, so they split the work into two slow jobs that could be done side by side. Quinn would speak the opening lines with a soft stage whisper, pausing after each phrase, while Farah would wait for the special cue word and then answer only when it came. Grandpa Sol stayed nearby, still working his rope, and offered only a calm count when they needed it or a simple name for the knot they might use later to hang the banner. The light shifted a little greener as clouds passed, and Quinn felt the mild coolness of the air against their cheeks while they stood ready. They began the first practice round with unhurried breath, Quinn’s voice floating just loud enough to reach Farah and no farther.

On the second try Quinn noticed a straw mat someone had placed at the edge of the platform for resting props, and they stepped onto its dry woven surface for a moment to steady their feet. The texture felt rough and friendly under their socks, like summer grass dried in the sun, and it helped Quinn stay present instead of leaping ahead. Farah listened with bright attention, hands folded loosely. Grandpa Sol murmured a soft count of “one, two, three” when Quinn glanced over, giving them a gentle pace without taking the work. The hall smelled faintly of cleaned wood and open windows, and Quinn spoke their lines again, slower still, savoring each word so Farah could catch the cue cleanly.

Outside the tall windows the wind in the fig leaves made a low continuous rustle that drifted in like a distant lullaby, and Quinn paused to hear it before starting the next round. That quiet sound settled into the practice like another soft partner. Farah waited, eyes on Quinn, and when the cue word finally arrived she answered with her own calm sentence about wishing the paper birds safe travels. Quinn smiled because the timing felt right at last. Grandpa Sol only named the knot he was finishing—“this is a simple loop knot, Quinn”—and then returned to silence so the children could keep their focus. Temperature in the hall stayed pleasantly cool, never cold, and Quinn felt steady on their feet.

They practiced once more, dividing the slow jobs cleanly: Quinn handled the speaking and the careful pauses, Farah handled the deep listening and the exact reply. Between turns Quinn spotted a small cup of water left on a nearby table for the helpers and turned the cup so the handle faced the room, making it easy for anyone to pick up later. The simple motion felt neat and finished. Soft golden light now slanted across the platform and warmed the backs of Quinn’s hands as they held them ready. Farah said, “Your whisper reached me perfectly that time,” and Quinn answered, “Your listening made the cue feel safe.” Grandpa Sol offered another quiet count when they asked, nothing more, trusting them to finish the work themselves.

Quinn and Farah ran the full scene twice without hurry, voices low and clear, the listening always patient. The paper flowers on the stands nearby seemed to lean in as if they too were part of the fair’s gentle welcome. Quinn breathed in and caught, just once, the clean smell of cedar rising from a freshly cut prop board leaning against the wall, and the scent grounded them like a calm forest corner. They kept their eyes on Farah and spoke the final line with care. Everything stayed peaceful; no one rushed, no one stumbled. The cool floor beneath the straw mat’s edge still held a trace of afternoon chill that reminded Quinn how long they had been practicing together with kindness.

At last the scene sat complete inside them, ready for the fair. Quinn and Farah folded the banner together, matching corners slowly, while Grandpa Sol gathered his leftover rope into a tidy coil. Soft evening light began to replace the brighter afternoon glow, turning the high walls a deeper blue. Quinn felt the quiet satisfaction of work shared and finished without hurry. Farah gave a small happy nod. Grandpa Sol only smiled and said the knot’s name one last time so Quinn would remember it if needed tomorrow. The three of them stood for a moment in the settling hush of the school hall, listening to nothing more urgent than their own even breathing.

Quinn walked with Farah and Grandpa Sol toward the door, the fair almost ready and the speaking scene safely practiced. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Its warm circle of light touched the edge of the straw mat and the folded banner, and Quinn felt the last cool air of the day brush their wrists as they stepped into the hallway beyond, already thinking of the quiet morning still to come.
