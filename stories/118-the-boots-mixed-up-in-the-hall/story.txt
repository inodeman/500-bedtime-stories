The boots sat mixed together in the hall just inside the library doors, two pairs tumbled into one soft heap of damp leather and mud-speckled laces. Their toes pointed every which way on the rubber mat, and little beads of rainwater still clung to the heels, catching the yellow glow from the overhead lamp. Kavi crouched low and traced a finger along one cool, slick cuff, feeling how the pairs had become braided like forgotten vines after someone hurried in from the weather.

Outside, the rainy steps shone under the porch light, each drop tapping a gentle rhythm on the stone while Kavi waited near the return desk with a small stack of picture books. The desk smelled faintly of polished wood and old paper, and the librarian’s stamp made a soft thunk each time it marked a card. Kavi’s own rain jacket hung open, still holding the outdoor chill against their arms as they watched the hall where the mixed boots waited like a quiet puzzle no one had solved yet.

Mr. Ade stood by the heap with his arms full of returned books, his wool sleeve brushing the edge of the counter as he tried to free one boot with the side of his foot. The thick knit felt soft whenever it grazed the wood, and he muttered kindly to himself while balancing the heavy stack. Kavi noticed the way Mr. Ade’s shoulders stayed careful so nothing would tip, and how the rainy steps beyond the glass still glittered with leftover puddles that reflected the porch lantern.

“Those boots got all twisted coming in,” Mr. Ade said, his voice warm and a little tired. Kavi stepped closer without a word and set their books neatly on the return desk, then knelt and began untangling the nearest lace from its neighbor. The leather felt cool and slightly sticky under Kavi’s careful fingers, and the sound of a cat’s collar bell drifted from somewhere deeper in the library stacks, a tiny bright chime that floated over the quiet carpets.

“I can hold these while you sort the pairs,” Kavi said, lifting the stack of books from Mr. Ade’s arms so he could bend more easily. Mr. Ade smiled and took one boot, matching its mate by the scuffed toe and the thin blue stripe along the sole. Together they worked in the mild indoor air that smelled now and then of cocoa drifting from a thermos someone had left open on a side table near the reading chairs.

“Thank you for noticing, Kavi,” Mr. Ade said as he slid the first pair side by side against the wall. Kavi only nodded and kept going, separating the second pair by its taller shafts and the little leaf stitched near one ankle. Rain still whispered against the windows overlooking the steps, but inside the hall stayed dry and calm, and Kavi’s hands moved slowly so no lace would knot again.

When both pairs stood straight and ready, Mr. Ade took back the books and carried them the last few steps to the return desk. Kavi wiped a single droplet from the mat with the corner of a clean tissue and then stood, feeling the warm library air settle around their shoulders. “The steps look shiny still,” Kavi said softly, glancing once more through the glass at the wet stone that curved down toward the sidewalk.

Mr. Ade placed the books in the return slot with a gentle click and turned back. “You made the job light without anyone asking,” he said, and Kavi felt the quiet pleasure of the shared work settle like a soft blanket. They walked together past the reading chairs where a single lamp glowed, and Kavi rested one hand on the smooth arm of a wooden rocker for a moment, rocking once and letting the chair stop by itself while the cocoa scent faded gently into the paper-and-wood air of the evening library.

Later at home Kavi slipped into pajamas and sat by the bedroom window, remembering the cool leather and the helpful quiet. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Outside the rain had thinned to a hush, and Kavi’s eyelids grew heavy in the mild room light while the memory of matched boots and kind hands rested easy until sleep arrived.
