A tiny paper rustle whispered near the ceiling, soft as a moth wing. Ines sat very still on the wooden bench and tipped their head until they found the sound among the hanging paper stars. The cottage room held a long tablecloth that fell almost to the floor, and the stars turned slowly in the golden candlelight. Warm air drifted across Ines’s cheeks from the open window. Ines rested both hands on the bench and felt the quiet of the room settle around them. Nia sat across the table, waiting with bright eyes. The baker stood by the sideboard with flour still pale on their sleeves. Ines smiled at the small sound and knew the evening song could begin.

Ines reached for the folded paper that held the song in a round, the special kind that folded in on itself and collapsed if anyone stared too hard at a clock. The paper felt cool and slightly stiff under Ines’s fingers in the mild evening air. Paper stars cast soft moving shadows across the long tablecloth. Nia leaned closer so Ines could see the first looping line of notes. “We have to keep it slow,” Ines said gently. The baker nodded once and stayed quiet, only watching the children with kind patience. Ines could already hear how the melody wanted to circle and meet itself again. Outside, the garden waited in deepening blue light. Ines held the paper steady so the song would not hurry itself.

Nia pointed to the middle place where the voices were meant to braid. Ines traced the curve with one careful finger and felt the room’s gentle heat rising from the floorboards. A faint scent of tomato vines floated in through the window from the garden beds beyond the cottage wall. The paper stars above them turned again, throwing pale light across Nia’s hair. “If one of us rushes, the whole round falls,” Nia whispered. Ines nodded and looked only at the notes, never at the small clock near the hearth. The baker stepped nearer and set a plate of cooled rolls on the tablecloth so no one would grow hungry. Ines breathed the garden smell and kept their eyes soft on the music.

They decided together that rushing would spoil everything, so Ines and Nia split the round into two slow jobs. Ines took the first voice and held each note like a held breath of warm air. Nia took the answering voice and waited for the exact place to enter. The candle flames made a soft glow on the long tablecloth and on the paper stars that drifted overhead. Ines felt calm settle into their shoulders. “You begin when I reach the second turn,” Ines told Nia in a low voice. The baker only listened, hands folded, offering no hurry. Ines sang the opening phrase as slowly as honey pouring, and the sound filled the quiet cottage room without pressing against the walls.

Halfway through, the notes knotted in a place neither child could name at first. Ines paused and felt the cool evening air touch the back of their neck. The paper stars hung still for a moment as if listening too. Nia frowned at the tangled measure and then looked to the baker. The baker smiled and said, “Call that knot the evening braid, and count four slow heartbeats before you step through it.” Ines repeated the name under their breath and counted with Nia, soft and even. The warm light from the candles made the tablecloth look almost golden. Ines kept their gaze on Nia’s face rather than any clock face, and the song stayed whole.

They practiced the evening braid twice more, still splitting the work so neither voice pulled ahead. Ines felt the grain of the bench under their palm while they waited for Nia’s entrance, the wood smooth yet marked with tiny lines that caught the skin just enough to notice. Soft shadows of paper stars slid across the long tablecloth like slow fish. The baker stayed near and spoke only once more. “Four heartbeats, then the name, then you meet,” the baker said. Ines and Nia both nodded. Ines sang their line again, voice low and steady in the mild room air, and Nia answered at the perfect moment so the round closed like two hands joining.

A light jingle came from the doorway as the cottage cat walked through, its collar bell giving one clear note and then quiet again. Ines kept singing without turning, letting the sound pass like a friendly visitor. The paper stars above the tablecloth turned gently in the air that followed the cat. Nia’s eyes stayed on Ines, matching breath for breath. The baker smiled and set two cups of warm milk near the edge of the cloth so the steam rose in thin white curls. Ines felt the song growing stronger because no one watched any near clock; instead they simply stayed inside the music. The round circled once more and held its shape beautifully.

When the final phrase arrived, Ines and Nia finished it together, voices meeting on the last long note that hung in the warm air like a held ribbon. The candles threw soft light across the long tablecloth and the hanging paper stars. Ines lowered the paper and felt their own quiet smile. “We did it without rushing,” Nia said. Ines answered, “We did it side by side.” The baker clapped once, very softly, so the sound was only a warm pat of hands. Outside the window the garden had gone fully dark, yet the cottage room stayed gentle and bright enough for tired eyes. Ines rested both hands on the tablecloth and listened to the last echo fade.

Ines leaned back and listened carefully for the far clock in the village rather than the near one on the shelf, just to be sure the song had truly settled. The milk tasted mild and comforting. Paper stars turned one last slow circle overhead. Nia yawned behind one hand. The baker dimmed the candles until only a softer glow remained. Ines felt the whole room grow quieter around them, full of finished music and kind company. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Ines closed their eyes for a moment and kept the round safe inside, ready for sleep.
