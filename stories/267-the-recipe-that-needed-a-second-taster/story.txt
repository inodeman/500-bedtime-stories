Grandma Nia knelt already on the thick library rug under the round window, her sleeves rolled while she arranged small plates around a covered basket in the soft afternoon light that pooled like honey across the woven circles. She had brought the tasting tray from the kitchen nook because the round window made the quietest spot for trying new recipes, and the fig tree just beyond the glass kept the air gentle. Uma watched from a cushion, noticing how the light made Grandma Nia’s silver bracelet flash each time she lifted the cloth. The rug felt springy under Uma’s knees as they leaned closer to see the golden crusts. Grandma Nia smiled without looking up and said, “Uma, these need a second opinion before we decide the final sprinkle.” Uma nodded and breathed in the calm of the room while the warm bread smell rose slowly from the basket.

Uma scooted nearer on the rug so their shoulder almost touched Grandma Nia’s, feeling the mild temperature of the sunbeam that crossed both of them like a shared blanket. Yael stood just beyond the edge of the rug, one hand resting on a low bookshelf, watching the plates but not stepping onto the circles. The recipe tasting had forgotten to call the friend over, so Yael remained close to the fun yet outside it, flipping a picture book with quiet pages. Uma noticed the way Yael’s shoes stayed planted on the wooden floorboards instead of the soft rug. Grandma Nia kept her eyes on the basket and let the children solve whatever needed solving. Uma tapped the empty space beside their own cushion and felt the faint vibration of footsteps from the hallway far away.

Uma whispered, “Yael, the window light is nicest right here,” and patted the rug firmly so the invitation stayed simple and real. Yael hesitated only a moment before crossing onto the blue circles, settling cross-legged where Uma had made room by shifting two cushions sideways. The seating changed without any loud announcement, just a quiet rearrange that gave Yael a true place among the plates. Grandma Nia passed the first small plate toward the middle and said nothing about prizes or special turns. Uma felt the cooler shadow near the bookshelf fade as Yael joined the pool of light. Outside the round window the leaves stirred, and the sound of wind in the fig leaves drifted in like a soft rustle of turning pages.

Uma slowed the whole tasting by lifting one slice carefully and holding it between them so Yael could see the steam curl up first. “We can take tiny bites together,” Uma said, and Yael answered, “That way we notice the same things.” The pace became a shared rhythm instead of a race, each child waiting while the other chewed. Grandma Nia sat back on her heels and watched the three of them form a loose triangle on the rug, her hands resting open in her lap. Uma noticed how the round window framed the green outside like a picture, and the air felt pleasantly warm against their cheeks. The plates waited patiently in the center, no one hurrying the next piece.

Uma handed Yael a plate of their own and brushed crumbs from the rim before anyone else moved. The cool ceramic rim rested smooth under Uma’s fingertips for a second, steadying the hand that offered the share. Yael took it with both hands and smiled the small quiet smile of someone who had been welcomed without fanfare. Grandma Nia murmured, “You two decide the order,” and then stayed still so the children could keep solving. Uma felt the rug’s short soft pile against their ankles as they settled deeper into the circle. Light from the window shifted a little lower, painting the plates in softer gold while the three of them tasted side by side.

Uma and Yael compared the crust and the soft middle in low voices, deciding together that a light dusting of cinnamon would suit the next batch. “It tastes like morning even though it’s afternoon,” Uma said, and Yael replied, “And the middle is almost as soft as this rug.” Grandma Nia nodded once, proud but silent, letting their words guide the tasting forward. Uma noticed how Yael’s shoulder now rested comfortably against theirs, no longer hovering at the edge. The library around them stayed hushed except for the distant tick of a clock and the gentle scrape of a chair somewhere far down the aisle. Warmth from the sunbeam lingered on Uma’s hair while they passed the last small piece between them.

Uma stood carefully, gathered the empty bowl that had held the extra slices, and carried it across the quiet floor to the little sink near the children’s corner, rinsing it under a thin stream of water before walking back. The short trip felt like part of the same calm game, and when Uma returned the bowl to the tray Yael had already stacked the plates in a neat tower. Grandma Nia accepted the clean bowl with a soft thank-you and began folding the cloth. Uma settled again on the rug, knees touching Yael’s, and felt the room’s steady temperature wrap around all three of them like a familiar sweater. Outside the round window the garden held its green silence, and inside the tasting had become something shared completely.

Uma leaned their head against the low window ledge for a moment, watching the last of the daylight slide across the fig leaves while Yael rested a book across both their laps so the pictures could be seen together. Grandma Nia packed the basket slowly, giving the children time to finish looking. Uma traced one fingertip along the edge of a remaining crumb and then brushed it away, everything finished without rush. The library lights along the far walls glowed lower now, and the rug held their weight like a gentle boat. Yael turned a page, and Uma listened to the soft paper sound while Grandma Nia hummed the same quiet tune from the beginning.

Uma stretched once on the rug and felt the day’s softness settle into their shoulders as Grandma Nia rose and offered a hand to each child. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Uma took the hand and stood, Yael rising beside them, and the three walked together past the shelves toward home, the taste of bread still gentle on their tongues and the round window light lingering behind like a quiet goodbye.
