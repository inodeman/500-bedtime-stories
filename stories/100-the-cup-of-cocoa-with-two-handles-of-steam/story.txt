A faint tick came from the tin clock on the kitchen shelf, and Jorie turned their head at once to notice the steady gentle sound filling the quiet room. Cool evening air brushed Jorie’s bare arms while golden stripes of light from the window lay soft across the floorboards. Jorie stood still and listened through several slow ticks, feeling the wooden boards firm under their feet, then smiled when the baker appeared in the open doorway. “Jorie, the picnic cloth is ready in the meadow,” the baker said in a warm voice. Jorie picked up the light basket, felt its woven handle against their palm, and followed the baker out across the yard toward the open green space where the day still held a mild glow.

On the picnic cloth spread in the calm meadow Jorie settled cross-legged and felt the sturdy fabric press evenly against their knees. A light breeze carried the mild warmth of late sun across Jorie’s cheeks and stirred the tall grass nearby. Eitan arrived along the path and sat down with a quiet grin, brushing dust from his sleeves. The baker unpacked plates and bowls with unhurried care while far-off bees hummed in the clover. Jorie watched the long tree shadows drift across the cloth and rested both hands in their lap, breathing the fresh open air and noticing how peaceful the whole meadow felt under the soft pink sky.

The baker then lifted a wide cup of cocoa sending up steam for two noses and placed it gently in the center of the cloth. Jorie leaned closer and felt the gentle rising heat touch their face like a soft veil. Twin curls of steam floated upward in the clear meadow light, looking ready for two friends at once. Eitan peered at the rich brown surface and licked his lips. “It smells wonderful,” Eitan said softly. Jorie smiled at the dancing steam and the way the light made the wide cup gleam, while the baker set a clean spoon beside it and stepped back without another word.

Jorie looked from the steaming cup to Eitan and then to the baker. The air around them had begun to cool as the sun slipped lower. Jorie knew the wide cup of cocoa sending up steam for two noses would not feel fair if only one person was served first. They reached for the linen napkin folded at the edge of the cloth and felt its crisp smooth texture under their fingertips. Jorie unfolded it slowly across their lap and said, “We can make smaller shares so everyone has some.” A bright smell of orange peel drifted from a small dish of zest the baker had brought along, and Jorie breathed it in while deciding exactly how to share.

With careful hands Jorie guided the spoon and divided the cocoa into two smaller even portions that filled the waiting bowls. The warm liquid made a soft pouring sound as it settled, and fresh steam rose again for both noses now. Eitan held his bowl steady with both hands. “Thank you, Jorie,” Eitan said with a bright glance. Jorie passed one bowl safely across the picnic cloth and kept the other close. The baker watched in silence, gave a single quiet nod, and let the choice stand without a speech. Soft evening light touched the two bowls and made the surface of each one shimmer.

Jorie blew gently across their share and felt the steam tickle the tip of their nose. The meadow grass whispered as a light wind passed through the stems. “It is just right for both of us,” Jorie said to Eitan. They took a small sip and tasted the sweet cocoa mixed with a faint bright note of orange peel that floated on top. Eitan sipped too and closed his eyes for a moment in quiet pleasure. The baker poured clear water from a jug into his own cup nearby. Jorie rested their free hand on the picnic cloth, feeling content as the sky deepened from pink to a calm blue overhead.

After several more slow sips Jorie set the bowl down and watched a ladybug crawl along a single blade of grass at the very edge of the cloth. Cooler air touched the back of Jorie’s neck as night drew nearer. Eitan pointed toward the distant hills and said, “The colors are pretty tonight.” Jorie answered with a soft hum of agreement and stretched their legs out carefully so the cloth would stay smooth. The baker packed the empty wide cup away with slow steady movements. Jorie listened to the far-off call of a dove and felt the deep peace of the calm meadow settle around them all like a gentle blanket.

Jorie helped fold the picnic cloth by holding two corners while Eitan held the opposite ones, and the sturdy fabric slid cool and clean through their fingers. A small lantern the baker had set near a low wooden fence cast a mild glow that framed the path home like a quiet window of light. Jorie walked slowly beside Eitan over the soft earth, hearing their paired footsteps and the rustle of the basket the baker carried close behind. The evening air smelled clean and grassy as they left the meadow. Inside the house again the familiar room welcomed them with its still shapes and mild indoor warmth.

Jorie sat on the edge of their bed and drew the blanket up to their chin, feeling its soft weight settle evenly across their shoulders. The pleasant day of the picnic rested gently in their mind while they closed their eyes and breathed in the quiet. Outside the last birds had grown silent under the darkening sky. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Jorie let their thoughts drift like slow steam and drifted toward sleep while the whole house held its calm night stillness around them.
