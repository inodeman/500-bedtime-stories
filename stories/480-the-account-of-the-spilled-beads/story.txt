The thin paper of the account rested right by the pillow seam, its corners curling just a little in the warm circle of lamp light that made the ink shine like wet stone. Isla could see the careful loops that told how a whole jar of beads had tipped and scattered, and then the part about kneeling to gather every single one from under the rug fringe and the chair legs. The air in the bedroom felt gently heated from the lamp bulb, and Isla smoothed one finger along the paper edge while Auntie Luz settled on the quilt with a soft sigh.

Auntie Luz lifted the account and cleared her throat so the first words could begin. “The jar tipped on the table and the beads poured out in a bright rush,” Auntie Luz said, her voice low and even. Isla heard the steady tick of a tin clock on the shelf and almost opened their mouth with bigger news about the afternoon, yet they pressed their lips together and kept listening. The lamp cast a buttery glow across Auntie Luz’s hands as she held the paper steady.

The sweet sharp scent of orange peel drifted from the little bowl on the nightstand where Auntie Luz had set the spirals earlier, and Isla breathed it in while the story moved on. Auntie Luz read how the beads had rolled under the couch and into the dust along the baseboard, some hiding like tiny moons. Isla wanted to jump in with the big thing that had happened outside, but they stayed quiet and watched the way the lamp light warmed the paper. Their toes wriggled under the blanket, feeling the familiar weave.

“We looked for a long time and called softly so we would not miss any,” Auntie Luz continued, turning the page with care. Isla touched the folded linen napkin that lay near the water glass, its crisp dry weave cool against their palm, and used that small steady feeling to keep still. The beads in the account were blue and green and one special silver one, and the finding started with a glint near the door. Isla’s shoulders stayed relaxed in the mild room air while they waited for every detail.

Auntie Luz described how Zev had spotted three beads caught in the pattern of the rug and had placed them gently into a cup. “Then we found four more behind the plant pot where the light barely reached,” Auntie Luz said. Isla almost spoke then about their own bigger news, yet the calm rhythm of the words held them, and they only nodded so the story could keep going. A faint cool draft from the window edge brushed Isla’s cheek like a polite reminder to stay patient.

The account told next of crawling slowly and listening for the tiny click of bead against wood. Isla heard their own quiet breathing mix with the room sounds and kept their eyes on Auntie Luz’s kind face in the soft lamp glow. No bead was left behind in the telling, and the paper made it plain that each one returned to the jar with a soft plink. Isla’s hands rested open on the quilt, warm now from being tucked near their body.

“At last the silver bead shone under the table leg and we knew the jar was full again,” Auntie Luz finished, reading the final sentence all the way through without hurry. Isla waited until the very last word settled in the quiet air, the lamp light steady on the page. Only then did Isla lean a little closer, ready for their turn after such careful waiting.

“How did the silver one stay so bright after sitting on the floor?” Isla asked, one real question that had grown while they listened. Auntie Luz smiled and answered that the silver bead had a smooth coat that dust could not cling to for long. Isla thought about that for a slow breath, the room feeling pleasantly warm around their shoulders, and then decided the bigger news could wait for morning.

Isla shared something small of their own, saying, “I kept a smooth pebble in my pocket today and it felt nice when I held it.” Auntie Luz nodded as if that mattered just as much. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Isla snuggled deeper into the quilt while the lamp hummed its tiny heat and the whole room settled into the kind of stillness that made eyelids heavy in the gentlest way.
