The open notebook rested right under the warm circle of lamp light on the quilt, its cream pages showing the looping handwriting that made up the account of the spilled beads that included how they were found. Isla’s fingertip rested on the first line and felt the raised dry ink, cool and slightly grainy against their skin while the paper itself gave off a dry whisper when the page shifted. Golden light from the bedside lamp lit every curve of the letters and threw a soft shadow across the margin. Isla breathed slowly and noticed how still the room had become, ready for whatever came next.

Auntie Luz settled deeper onto the edge of the bed so that her skirt brushed the quilt with a quiet shush. She smoothed a linen napkin across her lap with slow fingers, the cloth feeling crisp and cool under her palm before she set a small bowl of fruit peels on it. “This is the account I wrote after the beads spilled that afternoon,” Auntie Luz said in a low voice that matched the lamp glow. “I want to tell it just as it happened, Isla, from the first roll to the last find.” Isla felt a quick bubble of bigger news rise in their chest and almost spoke, yet the soft light on Auntie Luz’s face and the gentle weight of the quilt kept them quiet for now.

The tin clock on the shelf marked each second with a small bright tick that threaded through the quiet bedroom air. Auntie Luz began the telling while Isla sat cross-legged and listened with their whole body. “The beads were blue and green glass, round as raindrops,” Auntie Luz said. “They poured from the open tin when the table wobbled, and they raced under the chair legs and into the soft rug fringe faster than anyone could catch them.” Isla watched the lamp light dance on the notebook pages and smelled the bright citrus note of orange peel rising from the napkin bowl, sharp and sweet at the same time. Their own news pressed at their lips again, yet they pressed their lips together and stayed still.

Auntie Luz turned a page with a faint paper rustle and kept going. “We got down on our knees right away,” she said. “The light from the window made little sparkles on every bead we could still see, and we picked them up one by one, dropping them back into the tin with tiny plinks that sounded like rain on a pail.” Isla felt the cool air from the slightly open window touch their cheek and heard the continuing tick of the clock under the words. The bigger news still waited inside them like a held breath, but Isla only nodded and kept their eyes on Auntie Luz’s hands as the story unfolded without hurry.

“Under the low shelf we found a whole cluster that had rolled together,” Auntie Luz continued, her voice steady and warm. “They felt smooth and cool when we scooped them up, and the dust from the floorboards left a soft gray smudge on our fingertips that we wiped away on our sleeves.” Isla shifted on the quilt and felt its thick cotton texture press against their bare ankles while the lamp threw a steady honey-colored circle across both of them. The wish to interrupt grew stronger for a moment, yet Isla folded their hands and let the full account keep coming, sentence after careful sentence.

Auntie Luz smiled and glanced once at Isla before reading the next part aloud from the notebook. “Behind the curtain hem we discovered three more that had tucked themselves into the fold like sleepy seeds,” she said. “We laughed a little because they looked so snug, and the afternoon light through the cloth made them glow from inside.” Isla could feel the gentle heat of the lamp on their shoulder and smell the lingering orange peel sweetness still floating near the napkin. Their own small piece of news stayed patient while they listened all the way, never rushing the soft rhythm of Auntie Luz’s telling.

“The very last bead hid inside the turn of a mitten left on the floor,” Auntie Luz said, turning the final page. “We only found it when we picked the mitten up to put it away, and it tumbled out with a single bright click against the wood. That completed the whole account of the spilled beads that included how they were found.” Isla sat very still in the calm bedroom air, the quilt warm under them, and waited until the last word had settled. The bigger news they had wanted to share first now felt less urgent because the story had been given all the way to its quiet finish.

Isla took one soft breath and asked their real question. “Did the beads look different after they were all gathered again, Auntie Luz?” Auntie Luz closed the notebook gently and answered, “They looked even brighter, Isla, because we had looked so carefully for each one.” Only then did Isla share the small thing of their own. “Today I lined up my crayons by color on the windowsill and the red ones caught the late light,” Isla said, voice quiet as the lamp. The two of them sat together while the room held its gentle warmth and the quilt stayed cozy around Isla’s legs.

Isla leaned their head against Auntie Luz’s arm and felt the steady rise and fall of breathing while the lamp light stayed kind and even. The orange peel scent had faded to almost nothing, the tin clock kept its soft tick, and the linen napkin lay folded neat on the bedside table. Isla closed their eyes halfway and listened to the ordinary peace of the bedroom at lamp time. The lantern by the window glowed softly.
