Evening air drifted cool and gentle through the open window of the cottage room, lifting the edge of the long tablecloth so that it brushed the floor like a slow wave and setting the paper stars to turn on their thin strings. Kei sat close to the table and felt the mild breeze touch their cheeks while the soft golden light of late day stretched across the cloth in long quiet bars. Kei watched one paper star turn and another follow, and the room smelled clean and still. Kei rested both hands flat on the tablecloth and listened to the hush that filled the corners. Outside the garden waited under a kind sky, and Kei smiled at the way the light warmed the backs of their hands without ever growing bright or sharp.

Kei looked at the neat row of empty glass jars waiting on the cloth and the bright seed packets stacked beside them, each packet ready to be read. Jasper sat on the other side of the table with a careful pencil and a small stack of blank labels, and the baker stood nearby wiping flour from their hands onto a soft towel. Kei heard the quiet creak of the baker’s chair as it settled. “We can label every jar before the light goes,” Kei said, and Jasper nodded. The warm scent of fresh bread still clung faintly to the baker’s apron, and Kei felt safe in that ordinary kitchen smell while the paper stars hung still for a moment above them all.

Kei picked up the first packet and began to read the name of the seeds aloud so Jasper could write it cleanly, but the letters felt ready to blur if anyone hurried. The baker leaned in and said, “If we rush the writing the names will tilt and the jars will mix.” Kei set the packet down slowly and felt the cool glass of an empty jar under their fingertips. Jasper held the pencil still. Kei understood that the work needed patience more than speed, and the late light through the window stayed gentle on the long tablecloth while the paper stars cast tiny soft shadows that never moved too fast.

Kei looked across at Jasper and spoke again in a calm voice. “We can split it into two slow jobs,” Kei said. “I will read every packet twice and hold it steady. You will write each label and press it on only when both of us agree.” Jasper answered, “That feels right,” and the baker smiled without taking over. Kei felt the smooth paper of the first packet between their fingers while the room stayed quiet enough to hear a single soft breath. The paper stars hung like quiet friends above the long tablecloth, and Kei kept their shoulders easy so the work could begin without hurry.

Kei read the first packet carefully and then read it once more, letting every letter settle, while Jasper wrote the name in clear slow loops. A soft drift of air from the garden carried the green smell of tomato vines through the open window and across the table, and Kei breathed it in once and smiled because it belonged to the same calm day. Kei passed the finished label to Jasper, who pressed it straight onto the jar. The baker counted quietly under their breath and then offered only a simple number when Kei asked how many jars remained. Kei kept reading at the same gentle pace, and the light on the tablecloth never flickered.

Kei shifted on the stool and rested one elbow against the edge of the wooden bench that ran beside the table, feeling the fine grain of the bench under their skin like tiny smooth rivers. Jasper wrote the next name with the same care, and Kei checked every letter before the label touched the glass. “This one is for the tall sunflowers,” Kei said softly, and Jasper repeated the words so the letters would match. The baker stood back and let them keep their two slow jobs, only reaching now and then to slide a finished jar farther down the long tablecloth where the paper stars watched from above. Kei felt the grain of the wood again and knew the bench held them steady while the work continued.

Somewhere near the doorway a small cat moved, and the clear ring of a cat’s collar bell sounded once like a tiny silver note before the room returned to hush. Kei glanced toward the sound and then returned to the next packet, reading the name of the beans twice while Jasper waited with the pencil ready. The baker said, “You have seven jars left, and the knot on that string of labels is called a simple loop if you need a name for it.” Kei nodded and used the loop the baker had named so the remaining labels stayed neat in a soft coil. Jasper pressed another label firm and straight, and Kei felt the quiet pride of matching names without any rush at all.

Kei and Jasper kept their two slow jobs until the last packet lay open and the last jar received its clear label. The baker offered one final count of the finished row and then stepped back so Kei and Jasper could set every jar in a gentle line along the long tablecloth. “We finished them together,” Kei said, and Jasper answered, “Every name is right.” The paper stars turned once more in the cooling air, and Kei brushed a finger along the cool rim of the nearest jar just to feel that the work was complete. The light in the room had grown softer and more golden, and Kei listened for a moment to the far clock ticking somewhere deeper in the cottage rather than the nearer one on the shelf, letting the distant sound settle the evening.

Kei sat back on the stool while Jasper stacked the empty packets and the baker folded the soft towel. The room held the quiet of finished careful work, and the long tablecloth lay smooth beneath the row of labeled seed jars that waited for another day. Kei felt the mild air on their arms and watched one paper star turn in the last of the light. The lantern by the window glowed softly. Kei breathed once more and let their hands rest open on the cloth while the whole cottage seemed to settle around the three of them in perfect ordinary peace.
