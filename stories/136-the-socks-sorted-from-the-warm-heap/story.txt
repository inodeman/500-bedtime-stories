The warm heap of socks rested in a soft, rounded pile right where the cottage porch met the hall just inside, still holding the day's gentle heat. Brisa leaned near enough to see every cuff and toe, the stripes and the plain ones all mixed so that no two matched yet. Warmth rose against their cheeks like a quiet breath from the wool itself. Evening light slipped across the top of the heap and made some of the fibers shine a little. Brisa stayed close for a long moment, listening to the soft hush that filled the doorway between porch and hall, and feeling how the pile seemed almost alive with leftover coziness from the drying rack near the stove.

Cousin Pio sat on a low stool just inside the hall with an open basket at his knee and another basket waiting empty beside him. He picked up a red sock, then a gray one, then set them both down again while the warm heap stayed large and awkward. Brisa stood in the cool air that drifted from the porch boards and watched his careful hands. The floor under Brisa's feet felt smooth and slightly chill where the outside air reached, while the hall itself held a milder temperature around Pio. Brisa noticed the small pause in his shoulders and understood without being told that one person alone would take a long while with so many unpaired socks.

"I can take the socks nearest the porch," Brisa said, kneeling down opposite Cousin Pio so that the warm heap lay between them like a shared puzzle. Pio looked up and his face brightened. "That helps more than you know, Brisa," he said. Brisa began lifting socks from their half, feeling the soft leftover warmth in each one against their palms. They set possible matches in short, neat rows along the porch-side boards. The wood pressed firm and cool under their knees, and the light from outdoors still touched the edges of the work space with a pale gold that slowly turned quieter.

Side by side they sorted without rushing. Brisa held up a thick blue sock with a white toe, and Cousin Pio searched his side until he found the twin. "Here it is," Pio said, passing it across. Brisa folded the pair together and placed them in the finished basket. As they turned a heavier pair that had dried more slowly near the open door, the smell of wet wool rose once, mild and clean like a sweater after soft rain, then drifted away into the ordinary cottage air. Brisa breathed it gently and kept matching sock to sock, their hands steady and sure.

A tin clock on the hall shelf counted the quiet minutes with its steady little sound while Brisa and Cousin Pio worked through the pile. Brisa discovered two green socks with tiny leaves stitched near the cuffs and pressed them side by side. "These two belong together," Brisa said. Pio smiled and tucked them into the basket that was growing fuller. Soft indoor light mixed with the fading porch glow across Brisa's wrists as they reached and sorted. The heap shrank in a satisfying way, and Brisa felt the simple pleasure of making order without any hurry or fuss.

Near the doorframe a small paper list from earlier chores still waited on a shelf edge, and Brisa's thumb brushed a paper edge as they stretched for a sock that had slid that direction. The edge felt thin and dry under their skin for just a second before they guided the sock back into the sorting space. Cool air from the porch mixed with the warmer breath of the hall, carrying a faint scent of grass from outside. Brisa kept doing their useful half of the job as if sharing were the most natural path, never pausing to ask if anyone noticed.

When only a handful of socks remained from the warm heap, Brisa and Cousin Pio slowed down and checked each one twice. Pio lifted a brown sock with a sturdy heel. Brisa found its partner half-hidden under the rim of a basket and held it up. They both smiled. The floorboards felt smooth and familiar under Brisa's palms when they leaned forward to gather the last few. Outside on the cottage porch the light had softened into a sleepy blue-gray, and inside the hall the shadows stretched long and kind across the rug.

They settled the final pairs into the basket together and sat back for a breath. Somewhere deeper in the cottage a kettle began its quiet tick on the stove, and Brisa paused to breathe slowly while that soft sound started, feeling their chest rise and fall in the calm. Cousin Pio carried the basket a few steps farther into the hall and set it where morning feet would find the pairs ready. Brisa stood and brushed a fleck of lint from their sleeve. No one asked to be praised, and Brisa did not wait for any words of that kind. The awkward heap had become only neat, friendly pairs waiting for tomorrow.

Later Brisa rested on a cushion near the window in the hall with a light blanket draped over their legs, listening to the cottage grow still around them. The lantern by the window glowed softly. "Good night, Brisa," Cousin Pio called from the stairs, and Brisa answered, "Good night," in a low, sleepy voice. The porch beyond the door lay quiet and cool under the evening, all the socks were sorted and safe in their basket, and Brisa felt the calm of the finished work settle over them like a warm cover. They closed their eyes and let the peaceful house hold the silence while the last of the day's light faded from the boards.
